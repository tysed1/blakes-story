# CHAPTER V — VELOCITY

> **Belle Grove · Hollow Ridge · off the map (Chattanooga, Knoxville, Birmingham, Jacksonville, Savannah) · Laurel City · Tannersville · Tuesday, December 24, 1974 – Friday, May 2, 1975**
> Main-story runtime: ~6.5 hours · Playable: Dean Holloway, Ellis Blake, Cal Mercer, Riley
> **This chapter contains the game's midpoint.**

## Chapter thesis

Momentum still feels steerable. Velocity doesn't. In these four months The Blakes go from a bar band with a 45 to a regional draw with a record offer, and every yes creates the next obligation before anyone has finished feeling the last one. That's how this game avoids the montage version of a fame story: the player experiences the mechanism. One yes creates another yes.

And at the end of it, Ellis asks Clara a question she can't answer, and the camera shows the player what it has been politely not showing for five chapters.

## World state at open

- Christmas Eve 1974. Carter announced for president twelve days ago and Hollow Ridge thinks it's the funniest thing it's heard all year.
- The 45 is on consignment in one Laurel City record store and on WLSU's playlist. Martin Keller is about to mail copies to thirty college stations in the Southeast.
- The Blakes own an Econoline in eighths and have six dates booked, February into April.

## COLD OPEN

Black. A crowd, maybe four hundred people, chanting something, and at first we can't make it out.

Then we can.

**CROWD:** *BLAKES. BLAKES. BLAKES.*

Cut to a cramped backstage hallway. Cinderblock painted black. Posters. The HUD, if the player looks: **FRIDAY**.

Ellis is completely still, Loretta hanging from him. Dean is pacing like something in a cage, twirling a stick through his fingers and dropping it and picking it up. Riley is tuning. Cal is checking the battery in a tuner that doesn't have a battery.

The chant gets louder.

**ELLIS:** Are they doing that for us?

Dean looks at him as though he's an idiot.

**DEAN:** No, Ellis. There's another Blakes backstage.

**CAL:** There could be.

**DEAN:** Don't ruin this.

Ellis looks toward the curtain. He smiles. Then:

**CLARA** *(beside him):* You like it.

Clara is sitting on an equipment case against the wall with her boots on another one.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** A lot.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** You're grinning like a possum.

She studies him.

**CLARA:** Careful.

He looks back toward the curtain. When he looks back at the case, she's gone.

**STAGE MANAGER:** Two minutes!

**CHAPTER V**
**VELOCITY**

Cut to black. A card:

**THREE MONTHS EARLIER**

## MISSION 1 — CHRISTMAS EVE

**Playable:** Dean → Ellis · **Where:** the Holloway house, Belle Grove → US 19 → Cold Branch Road · **When:** Tuesday Dec 24, 1974, 6:30 p.m. – midnight · **Length:** ~35 min

### The party (Dean)

The Holloways' Christmas Eve party: sixty guests, a fifteen-foot tree in the front hall, a bartender in a red vest, a pianist playing carols in the living room, Richard's law-and-accounting friends, three men from Row publishing houses, the Episcopal rector, the neighbors, and two dogs in bows.

Dean invited the whole band. Riley is in Linwood with her family. Cal is at the Christmas Eve service at his parents' Baptist church. Ellis came, because Dean called Roy's garage on Monday and said *you'll come to this* in a voice that didn't take no, and Ellis understood without being told that Dean was making sure he wasn't alone on Christmas Eve.

He's wearing his one good shirt and a sport coat Dean loaned him that's too short in the arms. He's holding a cup of eggnog like it's evidence.

The player, as Dean, circulates. Dean is good at this party, the way he's good at every party. He charms three of his father's partners in a row. He tells the rector a joke the rector likes. He gets a sip of bourbon from the bartender, who's known him since he was eleven. And he keeps glancing at Ellis.

### Patty

Patty Holloway (fifteen, in a green velvet dress she hates, hiding by the staircase with a plate of cheese straws) is the one who finds Ellis, because she's also trying to be invisible and he's doing it better.

The player (still Dean, across the room) watches this. Dean can drift close enough to hear it.

**PATTY:** You're the singer.

**ELLIS:** Allegedly.

**PATTY:** Dean says you're a genius.

**ELLIS:** Dean says a lot of things.

**PATTY:** He says *I'm* a genius.

**ELLIS:** Are you?

**PATTY:** In algebra, no. In everything else, obviously.

**ELLIS:** That's how it usually works.

Patty laughs, surprised into it. Ellis talks to her like a coworker. He asks what she thinks of the party. She says it's a museum of people who agree with each other. He asks if she wants to leave. She says every year. He tells her the trick at parties like this is to carry something, because people who are carrying something look like they're on their way somewhere and nobody stops them, and hands her the bowl of pecans off the sideboard, and she carries it around the house for twenty minutes, delighted, and nobody stops her.

Dean watches this. He has never seen Ellis gentle.

> **Replay layer.** Ellis's sister would have been sixteen this Christmas. He's gentler with a fifteen-year-old girl than with anybody in the game. Dean doesn't understand why until much later.

### Richard

Richard Holloway finds Ellis in the front hall. He's heard from Dean that Ellis is a mechanic, and he's troubled by his Lincoln.

**RICHARD:** Dean says you're good with engines.

**ELLIS:** Some.

**RICHARD:** Mine makes a sort of whistle. At idle. The dealership says it's nothing and charges me forty dollars to say it.

**ELLIS:** Might could hear it, if you'll start it.

They go out to the driveway, Richard in his cardigan, Ellis in the too-short sport coat. Richard starts the Lincoln. Ellis listens, walks around it, lifts the hood, listens with his head cocked, and pulls a cracked vacuum hose off a fitting behind the carburetor with two fingers.

**ELLIS:** There's your whistle. Forty cents at Western Auto.

Richard is delighted: really, childishly delighted, in a way the player has never seen him.

**RICHARD:** You could work anywhere, son.

**ELLIS:** I work for Roy.

**RICHARD:** Roy's a lucky man.

Dean is watching this from the front step with his drink. He goes inside.

### The pool house (Dean)

The pool house behind the covered pool: wicker furniture, a wet bar, a cold fireplace. Dean does a line off the glass top of the bar. Ellis comes in a few minutes later with two beers from the kitchen and hands him one.

**ELLIS:** Your dad's nice.

**DEAN:** He's nice to you.

**ELLIS:** What's the difference?

**DEAN:** You're not his.

Ellis sits on a wicker couch.

**ELLIS:** I'd trade you.

**DEAN:** You would not.

**ELLIS:** I'd trade you in a second.

A pause. Dean looks at him. He realizes Ellis isn't joking.

**DEAN:** He'd make you an accountant.

**ELLIS:** I'd be a hell of an accountant.

Dean laughs. It lands anyway. Dean has never had to look at his father through somebody else's eyes before, and these eyes were hungry.

> **Seed.** Each envies the other's father. Neither can have the other's life. That will come back.

### Leaving (switch)

Ellis leaves at ten. At the front door he thanks Carol Holloway.

**ELLIS:** Thank you for having me, ma'am.

**CAROL:** You come back. You hear me?

**PATTY** *(from the stairs, still holding the pecans):* Bye, Ellis.

**ELLIS:** Keep carrying those.

The camera follows Ellis out the front door, down the brick walk under the Christmas lights. Stutter. Control transfers to Ellis.

### The drive home

The Valiant is cold. The player starts it. And warm air comes out of the vents, which it hasn't done in more than a year. (The heater blend door has been stuck since last winter.) A small particle effect of warmth on the windshield. Ellis puts his hand in front of the vent.

West on the Tanner Valley road. Christmas Eve: the Church of God marquee reads JOY TO THE WORLD — SERVICE 11 PM. A house with a lit plastic nativity in the yard and a lit Santa on the roof who appears to be looking down at it. The radio: a Laurel City station playing carols, then a DJ reading greetings, then "Blue Christmas."

> **Observe** (the Santa on the roof): *Dec 24. Plastic Santa on a roof, looking down at the manger like he's thinking about making an offer.*

Hollow Ridge: the Methodist candlelight service letting out onto the church steps, people Ellis has known his whole life holding white candles in paper collars and hugging in the cold. Nobody sees the Valiant go by. Or they all do.

### Home

The kitchen light is on. Wayne is at the table in his undershirt with a cup of coffee and the Laurel City paper.

There's no tree. There hasn't been one since Grace. But on the kitchen window, hanging from the sash lock by a loop of yarn, there's a construction-paper star with a glitter border half gone and, in green crayon, **GRACE — 2ND GRADE**.

Ellis sees it. Wayne sees him see it. Neither says anything about it. It's up. It's been up every Christmas Eve, two years running. Neither of them has ever seen the other hang it or take it down.

**ELLIS:** Heater's working.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

**ELLIS:** You fix it?

**WAYNE:** Blend door was stuck.

That's the present.

Ellis takes an envelope out of his coat and puts it on the table next to Wayne's cup. Wayne opens it. Inside: a book of four tickets from the Laurel City Engineers, *OPENING HOMESTAND — GOOD FOR ANY APRIL HOME GAME — GRANDSTAND*. Twelve dollars. About half a week's groceries.

Wayne looks at it for a long time.

**WAYNE:** That's April.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

He puts the tickets in his shirt pocket and buttons the flap.

**WAYNE:** We'll go.

That's all.

### Midnight

Ellis's room. Lamp off. Clear, cold night; the window's frosted at the corners. Clara is sitting on the sill with her knees up.

**CLARA:** He hang it?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** Good.

She watches him work his boots off.

**CLARA:** You okay?

**ELLIS:** Fine.

**CLARA:** Liar.

She nods at Dean's sport coat, hung over the chair.

**CLARA:** Bet you looked like a scarecrow at a wedding in that.

**ELLIS:** Patty Holloway said I looked distinguished.

**CLARA:** Patty Holloway is fifteen.

A long quiet.

**CLARA:** Merry Christmas, Ellis.

**ELLIS:** Merry Christmas.

> **Replay layer.** "He hang it?" A family friend might know the Blakes hang a paper star. A fourteen-year-old would want to know her father still hangs hers.

## MISSION 1b — SATURDAY BACH

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** the Riley house → Linwood Presbyterian Church, Linwood (Laurel City) → the church lot · **When:** Saturday Dec 28, 1974, 9:30 – 10:40 a.m. · **Length:** ~15 min

### The kitchen

The Rileys' kitchen in Linwood, the Saturday after Christmas. The leaf is still in the dining table. Tommy is asleep upstairs; the Navy has taught him to sleep through anything. Tom Riley is at the counter in his bathrobe with the crossword Riley already finished in pen, checking it.

**RILEY:** Where's Mom?

**TOM:** Church.

**RILEY:** It's Saturday.

**TOM:** She's gone to pull out all the stops.

He waits, delighted with himself.

**RILEY:** That isn't a pun. That's a description of what she's doing.

**TOM:** I've been saving it since October.

Riley takes her coat and the Datsun keys off the hook. She tells her father she's going to the drugstore. He tells her 14 across is wrong. It isn't.

### The sanctuary

Linwood Presbyterian: red brick, a white steeple, the lot empty except for her mother's green Buick. Riley goes in by the side door near the Sunday school rooms, the one that's never locked, and slides into the back pew in her coat.

White walls. Clear windows with a band of colored glass at the top, and the winter sun lying across the pews in long bars. The Christmas poinsettias are still banked on the chancel steps, dropping leaves. Up front and to the left, the organ console, and her mother at it with her back to the room.

Joan is playing Bach: a chorale prelude from the *Orgelbüchlein*, "Das alte Jahr vergangen ist," the one for the turning of the year. The tune goes high and slow on top, and underneath it the inner voices step down in half steps, carefully, like people going down a staircase in the dark. She plays it slower than she would on a Sunday, with her head down.

She doesn't know Riley is there.

### Listening

The player can simply listen. There's no timer. When the prelude ends Joan sits for a moment with her hands in her lap, then starts it again from the top.

Three Observe cues come up, one at a time, if the player looks around. This is Riley's notebook, not Ellis's; the button is the same.

> **Observe** (the window light): *Dust in the sun, taking its time, like it knows the building's empty.*

> **Observe** (the hymn board): *The hymn board still has last Sunday on it.*

> **Observe** (under the organ bench): *Her good shoes under the bench, side by side, like two people waiting outside an office.* Joan plays the pedals in her stocking feet. She always has. On Sundays the console hides it, and nobody in the congregation knows.

### The verse

Riley has a folded handout in her coat pocket, the spring syllabus for Landry's Kant seminar, unread. She turns it over and uncaps her pen.

The interaction is Ellis's songwriting interaction turned around: the details the player observed come up as options, and Riley tries them against the melody her mother is playing. The first line she writes is *My mother's hands are older than her shoes.* She looks at it and crosses it out. Then:

> *She plays for the Lord at a quarter to eleven*
> *and for nobody at all on Saturday.*

That one stays. Whatever the player observed goes in the margin beside it, for later.

Riley has written funny songs before. She sits and looks at this one for a while.

### Before the shoes

Joan reaches the last chord and holds it, the way she holds the last chord on Sunday so the congregation knows to stand. Nobody stands.

When her hands come off the keys she'll turn on the bench and reach for her shoes. Riley has watched her do it since she was six. The player gets a prompt to leave as the chord starts, and Riley is out the side door and down the steps before the chord ends. Her mother never turns around.

### The Datsun

The church lot. The Datsun's heater is taking its time. The syllabus is on Riley's knee.

Three options come up on the dashboard, and the player can try all of them:
- *Friday rehearsal.* Riley rehearses it to the rearview mirror: "I've got one. It's short. It's about my mother. Dean, if you say one word about organs—" She stops, because she can already hear the word. The option greys out.
- *Just Ellis.* She gets as far as "It's about my mother," and thinks of the one-word answer she got in Room 12 when she asked about his. The option greys out.
- *Not yet.*

*Not yet* is the only one that holds. Riley folds the syllabus into her Kant textbook, where it stays.

The heater finally gets warm as she turns into her parents' street.

> **Design note.** Riley's song, Riley's scene. Ellis isn't in it, and the band is only a thing she might tell. The choice at the end has one answer on purpose: Riley keeps things, too. In Chapter VIII the label takes this song from her, and the player should remember writing it in the back pew.

## MISSION 2 — LETTERING

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** the state capitol, Laurel City → Marlon's lot, Hollow Ridge · **When:** Tuesday Jan 28, 1975 · **Length:** ~20 min

### The capitol

The Georgia capitol dome in Laurel City, gold leaf in the winter sun. Riley followed a chartered bus down in her Datsun, so she could make rehearsal after: forty women from the Tannersville College caucus, the Laurel City League of Women Voters and a Baptist women's group that surprised everybody by showing up, come to sit in the senate gallery while the Equal Rights Amendment comes to a vote.

The player sits in the gallery as Riley. Below, men in suits make speeches: one about the family, one about the draft, one about bathrooms, one about God. On the far side of the gallery, a row of women in red STOP ERA buttons knit and do not look at Riley's row.

The vote is taken by voice and then by roll. It fails, by a lot.

The women on Riley's side of the gallery sit very still. One of the Baptist women says, loud enough for the floor to hear, *"Well, that's a shame,"* and a senator looks up.

### The van

Riley drives back up the Gap to Hollow Ridge, furious. She has rehearsal. When she pulls into Marlon's lot the band is standing around the van because Dean has bought a can of cream enamel and a stencil brush.

Riley takes the brush out of his hand.

**DEAN:** Hey—

**RILEY:** I'm doing it.

She cuts a stencil out of a Frosted Flakes box with Cal's pocketknife (block letters, measured with a ruler because she's too angry to freehand it) and paints THE BLAKES across the Econoline's two rear doors, small, in cream, level. The player does it: a short, meditative, satisfying painting task. It's better than anything Dean would have done.

Dean, watching:

**DEAN:** How'd it go at the capitol?

**RILEY:** They voted it down.

**DEAN:** Well. Maybe next time, Professor. Maybe you should wear a dress.

It's a dumb joke. He means nothing by it. The paintbrush stops.

**RILEY:** Say that again.

Dean hears it now.

**DEAN:** I didn't—

**RILEY:** Forty women rode a bus for an hour and a half to sit in a balcony and watch thirty-three men vote on whether I get to be a person in the Constitution, and you want to know what I was *wearing*.

**DEAN:** That's not what—

**RILEY:** Go ahead. Tell me what you meant.

Dean opens his mouth, and to his credit, closes it.

**DEAN:** I meant it was a stupid thing to say. It was a stupid thing to say. I'm sorry.

Riley looks at him. He means it. She goes back to painting.

**RILEY:** Hand me the rag.

He does. That's the whole fight. Ellis and Cal have stayed completely silent and very still, like men in a field with a bull.

### The letters

When it's done, Ellis walks around the van and looks at the rear doors for a long moment.

**ELLIS:** Who did that?

**DEAN:** Artistic vandalism.

**RILEY:** I did.

Ellis looks at Cal.

**CAL:** Spelling's correct.

Ellis shakes his head. He doesn't ask them to paint over it. He hated the name, and he lets it stay.

> **Ledger:** *1/28/75 — Maestro Echoplex EP-3, used (Vale) — $150.00 from band fund.* Cal bought it that morning. It sits in the van in its gray case, and from now on "the loud part" can happen live.

## MISSION 3 — SIX DATES

**Playable:** Cal · **Where:** Tannersville → the map edge → Chattanooga (off-map) · **When:** Friday Feb 7, 1975 · **Length:** ~35 min

The first of Eddie's six dates. The van heads northwest out of the Tanner Valley. The player is Cal, driving.

Within about forty-seven minutes of leaving Vale's, Cal's beautiful schedule means nothing. Dean forgot his stage shirt (the satin one; he refuses to play in anything else). Riley needs a post office to mail a paper. Ellis left notebook 46 at Roy's garage and can't play without it (he can; he won't). The van is making a noise Cal has never heard before, from somewhere behind the dashboard. They're forty minutes behind.

**DEAN:** This is touring.

**CAL:** We've been touring for forty-seven minutes.

**DEAN:** And we're already making memories.

**CAL:** I'm making an ulcer.

### Leaving the map

This is the player's first drive off the edge of the map, and the game makes it feel like leaving home. The Tanner Valley corridor narrows into a river gorge on the northwest side. A green highway sign: **CHATTANOOGA 58**. A barn roof with SEE ROCK CITY painted on it in giant letters. The radio loses the Tannersville stations one by one.

> **Observe** (the barn roof): *Feb 7. SEE ROCK CITY, on a barn that has never been anywhere.*

Then the transition, a road cut with no loading screen: The van rounds a bend in rain-light and the world beyond is a compact off-map space: a single stretch of Chattanooga's riverfront, a club on a side street, a motel, a diner, a gas station, the river.

> **Design note — off-map spaces.** Regional tour towns are dense mission zones: one street, one venue, one motel, one diner, one landmark. The van is the connective tissue. The player can drive the stretches between, which are condensed (a few minutes of highway, a stop or two, conversation) but never skipped entirely. Leaving the map should always feel like leaving; coming back over the map edge onto the Tanner Valley road should always feel like coming home.

### The tour loop (introduced)

Across the six dates, touring becomes a loop rather than a series of cutscenes:
- **Drive** (whoever's at the wheel; usually Cal).
- **The van hub:** change the radio (Tully's portable FM, when he's along; the van's AM when he isn't); talk; practice; write; sleep; play cards on an amp case; read; argue about the map.
- **Stops:** diners, boiled-peanut stands (Ellis ranks them in a list that becomes an in-game collectible), roadside attractions (Rock City, a snake farm, a Stuckey's with pecan logs), record stores in each town (the band checks for their 45; it's there in two of six).
- **Gig day:** load-in, soundcheck, the show, load-out.
- **Motels:** who rooms with whom (the player can influence it; it's remembered).
- **Small unscripted events:** a hitchhiker, a flat, a speeding ticket, a waitress who wants an autograph for her nephew, a church choir practicing through a motel wall.

### Chattanooga

A club called the Hi-Fi on a side street by the river: three hundred capacity, maybe a hundred and fifty tonight. Support for a Knoxville band. The show goes well, not spectacularly.

But something has changed. A handful of people know "No Name," not the whole song, just the chorus. They sing it back on the last time through. Ellis nearly misses his own line.

Afterward, loading out:

**ELLIS:** Did you hear that?

**CAL:** Hear what?

**ELLIS:** They knew it.

Riley smiles.

**RILEY:** Yeah.

**ELLIS:** How?

**CAL:** Martin mailed the 45 to thirty college stations. The one here's played it for three weeks.

**ELLIS:** When did Martin do that?

**CAL:** After Christmas. He asked me not to tell you.

**ELLIS:** Why?

**CAL:** He said you'd make it weird.

**ELLIS:** That's fucking weird.

**DEAN:** That's fucking *fantastic.*

### First autograph (a real one)

Outside the Hi-Fi's side door, a young man in an army-surplus coat waits with the 45's white sleeve.

**FAN:** You Ellis?

Ellis looks behind himself.

**ELLIS:** Depends.

The fan holds out the sleeve and a pen.

**FAN:** Would you sign this?

Ellis stares at it.

**ELLIS:** Why?

The fan laughs; he thinks Ellis is joking. Ellis isn't.

**FAN:** Because you made it.

Ellis looks at his own name, stamped crooked by Dean. He signs it, *Ellis Blake*, in the same parts-order handwriting. It looks terrible. The fan leaves thrilled.

**RILEY:** You spell your own name right?

**ELLIS:** Barely.

She takes his hand. They walk to the van.

### Where's Dean?

Load-out. No Dean.

**CAL:** Where is he?

**ELLIS:** Reckon he's in the bathroom?

**RILEY:** For twenty minutes?

Cal knows. The player, as Cal, searches the Hi-Fi's backstage: a small exploration sequence through a kitchen, a storeroom, a stairwell. Eventually Cal finds Dean in the headliner's dressing room: a party, coke on a hand mirror, bourbon in paper cups, Dean talking like he's known everyone for years.

**DEAN:** *Cal!*

**CAL:** We're leaving.

**DEAN:** Meet Gary. Gary opened for Skynyrd.

**GARY:** Twice.

**DEAN:** Basically family.

**CAL:** We're leaving.

**DEAN:** Five minutes.

**CAL:** Now.

Dean's smile goes.

**DEAN:** I'm not twelve.

**CAL:** Then stop making me come find you.

That lands. The room goes awkward. Dean stands up.

**DEAN:** Fine.

### Outside

Dean follows Cal into the alley.

**DEAN:** You embarrassed me.

**CAL:** You disappeared.

**DEAN:** I was talking to people.

**CAL:** You were doing coke with people.

**DEAN:** Those aren't mutually exclusive.

Cal stops.

**CAL:** We're working.

**DEAN:** We already played.

**CAL:** Touring is work.

Dean laughs.

**DEAN:** Jesus Christ.

**CAL:** What's funny?

**DEAN:** You finally get into a rock band and you're trying to turn it into a savings and loan.

**CAL:** And you're trying to turn it into an excuse.

Silence.

**DEAN:** For what?

**CAL:** Whatever you'd be doing anyway.

Dean's face changes. This time Cal has hit the actual nerve instead of the one next to it.

**DEAN:** Fuck you.

He walks to the van.

### Later

Everyone in the van. Nobody talks. Five minutes. Ten.

**ELLIS:** Anybody hungry?

**DEAN:** Starving.

The conflict dissolves, for now, into food. They stop at a Krystal and eat eleven hamburgers between them in the parking lot at 1 a.m.

## MISSION 4 — DIAL

**Playable:** Dean → Ellis · **Where:** the road to Knoxville → Knoxville (off-map) · **When:** Thursday Feb 13 – Friday Feb 14 · **Length:** ~25 min

### Dean drives

Chattanooga to Knoxville, a long afternoon on US 11 in rain. The heater only works on the passenger side. Cal fell asleep in the passenger seat before they cleared Chattanooga. Riley is studying in the back with a flashlight. Dean is bored, which is dangerous. He convinces Ellis to let him drive while Cal sleeps.

Stutter. Dean at the wheel.

He drives normally for almost five minutes. Then:

**DEAN:** You know what this van needs?

**ELLIS:** Brakes?

**DEAN:** A radio that doesn't suck.

He starts spinning the AM dial violently. Country. Static. A preacher. Static. A soul station out of Chattanooga. Static. A Top 40 station playing "Please Mr. Postman." Static.

Riley passes Tully's portable FM up from the back (Tully isn't on this run; he left the radio). Dean sets it on the dash and spins that dial instead. Static, classical, static, a college station with a DJ who sounds about twelve, a rock station—

Dean stops.

A DJ is introducing a record. The DJ is saying *The Blakes.*

Everyone freezes. Cal wakes up.

**CAL:** What?

**RILEY:** *Shh.*

**DJ** *(radio):* —four-piece out of north Georgia, been getting a lot of phone calls about this one. This is "No Name."

Their song starts. Inside their own van.

Nobody speaks for twenty seconds.

Then Dean screams.

**DEAN:** *WE'RE ON THE FUCKING RADIO!*

The van swerves.

**CAL:** *WATCH THE ROAD!*

**DEAN:** *WE'RE ON THE RADIO!*

**CAL:** *WE'RE GOING TO BE IN THE OBITUARIES!*

Riley is laughing too hard to breathe. The player (still Dean) has to keep the van on a wet two-lane road while everyone yells.

### Something strange (switch to Ellis)

Stutter. The camera slides back to Ellis on the wheel well, listening. His own voice, coming through a cheap portable speaker, small, real.

Halfway through the second verse, Ellis hears another voice, quiet, buried behind his vocal: a girl's voice, singing a harmony he doesn't recognize, a different line from Riley's, lower, older, the way you'd harmonize to a record you'd heard a hundred times through a bedroom wall.

Ellis leans forward and turns the radio up. Riley, pleased, thinks he's listening to her.

He listens hard. The player hears it too, faintly, and then, as he strains toward it, it's gone. Only Riley's part is there, the one she recorded at WTCR.

Ellis sits back against the wheel well and doesn't say anything to anybody.

> **Design note — ideas of reference.** The player hears the phantom harmony because the player is in Ellis's perception. Played back later (the band's own copy of the 45, on a turntable, in Chapter VI) there is only Riley. The game never calls attention to the difference.

### Knoxville

A college bar by the University of Tennessee campus, Valentine's night. The show is packed with students because the campus station has played the 45 every day for a week. People sing the chorus of "No Name" and, to everyone's astonishment, the first verse.

Before the show, the player (as Ellis) can see Cal at the pay phone in the bar's back hallway, turned toward the wall, the receiver cupped close. Only fragments carry: *"...Sunday. Late. ...I will. ...Me too."* Dean passes on his way to the bathroom.

**DEAN:** Who's that?

**CAL** *(covering the mouthpiece):* My mother.

**DEAN:** On Valentine's Day?

**CAL:** She's very lonely.

Dean laughs all the way down the hall. Cal waits until he's gone and says one more quiet thing into the phone and hangs up.

> **Replay layer.** Cal met Theo Marchand at the Lantern in December. Nobody in the band knows. The player doesn't either, yet.

## MISSION 5 — TWO CARS

**Playable:** Ellis ‖ Riley (alternating) · **Where:** Birmingham (off-map) → US 78 → the map edge → Laurel City's half-built expressway → the Laurel Gap corridor in rain; in parallel, Riley's Datsun on the same roads · **When:** Wednesday Feb 26, 11 p.m. – Thursday Feb 27, 9 a.m. · **Length:** ~40 min

### The problem

Riley is falling behind at school. She's too capable to fail, but the compensating is eating her. She studies in the van, in diners, in motel rooms, backstage, during soundchecks, and it isn't enough.

Birmingham, Wednesday. Their show ends around eleven. Riley has a midterm in Tannersville at nine Thursday morning, in Landry's Kant seminar. It's roughly four hours away if nothing goes wrong. She drove her Datsun to Birmingham separately so she could leave the second the set ended.

At eleven-fifteen, in the club's back lot in a cold drizzle, she's so tired she's swaying.

**ELLIS:** I'll drive you.

**CAL:** We've got load-out.

**ELLIS:** Dean can—

Cal looks at Dean. Dean is sitting on the van's bumper talking very fast to a stranger about Evel Knievel.

**ELLIS:** Right.

**RILEY:** I'll drive myself.

**ELLIS:** You're asleep standing up.

**RILEY:** So are you.

**CAL:** I'll take her. In her car.

**RILEY:** Then who's driving the van?

**CAL:** Ellis.

Everyone looks at Ellis.

**ELLIS:** I can drive.

A strange beat. Nobody knows why it feels strange except Ellis, and the player, who has a faint unease they can't name.

**RILEY:** You sure?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

Behind Riley, in the drizzle under the club's back-door light, Clara is standing with her arms folded.

**CLARA:** Not in the rain.

Ellis looks at her. His expression changes, very slightly.

**CAL:** You with us?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

A pause.

**ELLIS:** I'll drive.

Load-out takes twenty minutes. Ellis gets the van running while the others carry, and for a minute he's alone in the driver's seat with the door open and the wipers off.

Clara is leaning on the open door with her arms folded on top of it.

**CLARA:** Slow down.

**ELLIS:** I'm parked.

**CLARA:** Then you're doing it right. Keep it under forty-five. Hear?

**ELLIS:** Yes ma'am.

Dean climbs in the passenger side, still talking about Evel Knievel. Clara steps back from the door into the drizzle and stays there under the back-door light, arms folded, while the van pulls out of the lot.

### Two journeys

The mission splits. The player alternates between two vehicles on the same roads, with a switch every few minutes triggered by passing landmarks (a truck stop, a river bridge, the state line, the map edge).

### Riley and Cal (the Datsun)

Cal drives Riley's orange Datsun 510 with his knees nearly at the steering wheel. Riley in the passenger seat with index cards. Kant: the categories, the antinomies, the thing-in-itself. The player can quiz her (a small interactive flashcard game in which Cal reads the cards badly on purpose to keep her awake).

**CAL:** You're going to make yourself sick.

**RILEY:** I'm fine.

**CAL:** That's Ellis's answer.

Riley looks up.

**CAL:** Don't steal it.

**RILEY:** I'm not quitting school.

**CAL:** Didn't say you were.

**RILEY:** Everybody thinks I will.

**CAL:** I don't.

**RILEY:** Really?

**CAL:** You'd have to decide something first.

**RILEY:** Asshole.

Cal smiles. Then, eyes on the road:

**CAL:** You love him?

Riley freezes.

**RILEY:** Jesus, Cal.

**CAL:** I'm just asking.

**RILEY:** You don't "just ask" anything.

**CAL:** I know.

A long quiet. The wipers.

**RILEY:** I don't know.

**CAL:** Okay.

**RILEY:** That's it?

**CAL:** What do you want me to do with it?

She laughs. Then, after a while:

**RILEY:** Do you think he's okay?

Cal's expression changes slightly.

**CAL:** Be specific.

Riley pushes her glasses up with a knuckle and waits him out.

He thinks about it honestly.

**CAL:** I think Ellis has spent a long time learning how to look okay.

Riley looks out at the dark. That line stays with her.

### Ellis and Dean (the van)

The van. Dean is coming down: quieter, jaw tight, one knee jumping. Ellis is driving, both hands on the wheel. Very tight. The player feels it: the steering is slightly stiffer when Ellis drives at night; the camera sits closer.

Dean notices.

**DEAN:** You good?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

Headlights come toward them on the two-lane. Ellis flinches: a small lift of the right hand off the wheel, a turn of the shoulder. The van drifts toward the white line on the right. He corrects.

**DEAN:** You want me to drive?

**ELLIS:** No. I'm fine.

Ellis is already under the speed limit. He looks at the speedometer anyway. Forty-two.

**DEAN:** You're doing forty-two, Grandpa. Mailboxes are passing us.

**ELLIS:** They're welcome to.

Dean looks at him, and doesn't push.

### The rain

East of the state line the drizzle becomes rain. The van comes over the map edge, through Laurel City on its half-built expressway, and onto the Laurel Gap corridor, into the mountains, past midnight, on a road with no shoulder that bends every quarter mile.

The wipers. The road black and shining. Headlights blooming. Ellis's breathing changes, and the gameplay changes subtly with it: steering heavier, the sound of the rain louder than it should be, the radio fading out on its own, headlights from oncoming cars wider and whiter than in any other driving in the game, all of it subjective.

On a long right-hand curve, a pickup comes around the bend with its high beams on.

Then, for twelve frames, too fast to read:

A small hand flat on a dashboard. A child's hand, a teenager's, green felt-tip ink on the knuckles. Rain on a windshield. A different windshield.

Gone.

Ellis jerks the wheel. The van lurches right; the right front tire drops off the pavement edge with a thud; gear crashes in the back.

**DEAN:** *Whoa—*

The player (as Ellis) has control. The van can be brought back onto the road; the game makes it hard but not impossible, and if the player overcorrects the van fishtails once and stops. Either way, it ends at the side of the road in a gravel pull-off, headlights pointing at a guardrail, engine running, wipers going.

Silence. Dean lets go of the door handle one finger at a time.

Ellis gets out.

### Roadside

Rain on the van roof. Ellis standing by the guardrail breathing. Dean follows him out.

**DEAN:** What happened?

**ELLIS:** Nothing.

**DEAN:** You nearly put us in a ditch.

**ELLIS:** I said nothing.

Dean doesn't joke. That's rare.

**DEAN:** Okay.

A pause.

**DEAN:** You want me to drive?

**ELLIS:** You're high.

**DEAN:** Not anymore.

Ellis looks at him. Dean is coming down: not ideal, not what he was. Ellis looks at the road.

**ELLIS:** We're waiting.

So they wait. Two young men sitting on the hood of a green van in a gravel pull-off on a mountain road in the rain, the engine ticking, the rain letting up a little at a time.

### Being forty

**DEAN:** My old man thinks I'm ruining my life.

**ELLIS:** Maybe we both are.

**DEAN:** Speak for yourself.

Ellis almost laughs.

**DEAN:** You know what scares me?

**ELLIS:** Your reflection?

**DEAN:** Fuck you.

A pause.

**DEAN:** Being forty.

**ELLIS:** That's your fear?

**DEAN:** Forty. Same house. Same street. Same people at the same Christmas party. Talking about some shit I almost did when I was twenty.

Ellis looks at him.

**DEAN:** I'd rather fuck it all up trying.

**ELLIS:** You ever think ordinary might be nice?

**DEAN:** No.

**ELLIS:** Me neither.

That isn't entirely true. Some part of Ellis wants ordinary more than anything: Grace alive, Wayne normal, supper, a sister stealing his records. He can't say it.

They wait another twenty minutes. Then Dean drives the rest of the way, carefully, which is new, and Ellis sleeps sitting up in the passenger seat with his forehead against the cold window.

> **Replay layer.** The hand on the dashboard is Grace's. She drew on her knuckles in green felt-tip. Ellis just swerved right on a wet curve toward a pickup with its high beams on. The player, on a first playthrough, has seen a strange flash and a tired boy nearly go off the road.

### The exam (Riley)

Morning. Stutter to Riley. Tannersville College, 8:52. She hasn't slept. The Datsun is parked crooked. Cal is asleep in it.

She takes the exam. The gameplay narrows to simple interactions (choosing answers, writing a short essay by selecting among arguments), and her vision drifts: the words on the page swimming slightly, a line she has to read three times. She catches herself. She finishes.

A week later, in her campus mailbox: the exam, graded. **B−.** Landry's handwriting in the margin: *You know this. You were somewhere else.*

For most students, a B-minus is fine. For Riley it's a warning. She folds it in half and puts it in her bag and doesn't tell Ellis.

## MISSION 6 — THE KITCHEN TABLE

Between dates, Dean goes home. His father has been waiting, and on the dining-room table, squared to the edge, are three things: a letter from Tannersville College placing Dean Holloway on academic probation; a typed offer from Caldwell, Moss & Reeve, CPAs, for a summer internship beginning June 2; and a pen.

**RICHARD:** Deadline's Friday.

**DEAN:** I'm not doing it.

**RICHARD:** You haven't even considered it.

**DEAN:** I've considered it my whole fucking life.

**RICHARD:** Language.

Dean laughs.

**RICHARD:** I'm worried about watching you throw away every opportunity you've been given.

**DEAN:** Given.

**DEAN:** You mean picked.

**DEAN:** School. College. Job. House. You picked all of it.

**CAROL** *(from the kitchen doorway):* Dean—

**DEAN:** I don't want your life.

**RICHARD:** You think every man who gets up and puts on a tie dreamed about amortization tables?

**DEAN:** Exactly.

**DEAN:** Then I don't want to grow up.

**RICHARD:** I know.

The basement. Dean closes the door and sits on the drum throne with his back to the kit. For once, no smile.

The player has no choice here. Dean uses.

A knock on the basement door. His mother's voice: *"Dean? Honey?"* He doesn't answer. A pause. Then the sound of a plate being set down on the top step, and her footsteps going away.

After a while he goes up and gets it. Pot roast, green beans, a roll. He eats it sitting on the stairs.

## MISSION 7 — STILL HERE

**Playable:** Ellis → Riley · **Where:** the Blake house; Marlon's · **When:** Monday Mar 10 – Friday Mar 14 · **Length:** ~25 min

### Late

Hollow Ridge. Past one in the morning. Ellis can't sleep. Clara is on his floor going through his milk crates of records, pulling each one out and looking at the back.

She holds one up: a Louvin Brothers album, *Tragic Songs of Life*, the one with Ira and Charlie in suits on the cover. It's Wayne's. It's been in Ellis's crate for two years.

**CLARA:** This one's terrible.

**ELLIS:** You like that record.

**CLARA:** No I don't. Look at them. Two men selling burial insurance.

**ELLIS:** You literally told me—

He stops. When? He can't remember. He can remember her saying it, the exact tone, and he cannot remember when.

**CLARA:** Told you what?

**ELLIS:** Strike that from the record.

She smiles and puts it back. Ellis writes.

> **Replay layer.** Grace sang harmony with him on this record through a bedroom wall and swore she hated it.

### Kitchen

Thursday morning. The Engineers ticket book is under a magnet on the refrigerator, next to the Feed & Seed calendar. Wayne is pouring coffee.

**WAYNE:** Saturday the twelfth. That's opening day.

Ellis, at the sink rinsing a cup, goes very still.

**ELLIS:** The twelfth.

**WAYNE:** That's opening day.

**ELLIS:** Okay.

That's all. Wayne takes his coffee to the truck.

### The song

The lyric isn't explicitly about Clara, and the player shouldn't be able to tell for sure. It's about someone who shows up when everyone else has gone, who knows what you haven't said and never changes, who is realer in the dark, and whom you can't introduce to anyone.

And the first verse is their first conversation in Chapter I, almost word for word:

> *You sat on a crate in the alley in the cold*
> *and said the second one was better.*
> *I said nobody asked.*
> *You said I did, by playing it at all.*
>
> *Everybody in this town can see my business.*
> *Nobody here knows you.*
> *And I'm still here.*
> *And you're still here.*

He calls it "Still Here."

The player writes the rest of it with him: a songwriting interaction in which Ellis offers lines (some drawn from Observe entries the player has collected; if they collected the ice-machine line or the Stony Knob line, those images appear) and the player picks the ones that stay. There's no wrong choice. The chorus is fixed.

> **Design note.** Players will recognize the first verse from the alley. They'll think Ellis wrote a song about a friend from somewhere else, which is what "Nobody here knows you" sounds like. After the midpoint they'll understand the line was literally true: nobody but Ellis has ever met her. In Chapter VII sixteen hundred strangers sing it back to a man none of them know.

### Rehearsal

Friday, Marlon's, afternoon. Ellis plays it for them alone on Loretta.

Riley loves it. Dean loves it and says so four times. Cal doesn't react right away.

**ELLIS:** Well?

**CAL:** It's good.

**ELLIS:** That's all?

**CAL:** Who's it about?

**ELLIS:** Nobody.

**CAL:** Clara?

Ellis looks at him.

**ELLIS:** Maybe.

**CAL:** Can I meet her?

Silence.

**ELLIS:** What?

**CAL:** You talk about her all the time. I'd like to meet her.

Ellis gets defensive, instantly.

**ELLIS:** Why?

**CAL:** Because apparently she's at every show.

**ELLIS:** So?

**CAL:** So I've never met her.

**ELLIS:** That's not my problem.

Riley looks between them.

**CAL:** Didn't say it was.

**ELLIS:** Then what are you saying?

Cal realizes he's pushing too hard. He looks down at his bass.

**CAL:** Play me the bridge again.

**ELLIS:** Play it yourself.

He puts Loretta down and walks out the back.

### Outside (Riley)

Stutter. Riley follows him out to the back steps and sits down beside him, not too close.

**ELLIS:** What.

**RILEY:** Why are you angry?

**ELLIS:** I'm not.

**RILEY:** You're outside.

**CLARA:** Eight hundred witnesses, Ellis.

## MISSION 9 — THE EXIT

**Playable:** rotating · **Where:** the Row → the Exit, Tannersville · **When:** Friday Apr 4 · **Length:** ~35 min

### The Row changes its mind

Tannersville. The record store on the Row where, in December, the owner told Cal *we don't carry that kind of thing*. Its front window now has a hand-lettered card that says **LOCAL — AS HEARD ON WLSU & WTCR**, and under it, standing up in its white sleeve, the 45. THE BLAKES, stamped crooked.

Dean sees it first and stops everyone on the sidewalk. Nobody says anything.

Riley touches the glass.

**ELLIS:** When did that happen?

**CAL:** Tuesday. She called Vale's and asked for twenty-five.

**DEAN:** We're international.

**CAL:** We're on the Row.

**DEAN:** Foreign country.

Ellis looks at the record in the window for a long time.

**ELLIS:** I'd like to file a complaint about the stamping.

**DEAN:** It's artisanal.

**CAL:** It's crooked.

**DEAN:** Artisanally.

### The Exit

The Exit is Tannersville's one real rock listening room, a converted storefront near the college with tables, a small stage and a reputation for booking people before anyone else will. Four hundred people tonight, maybe more. This is the cold open. The chant in the hallway. *"Are they doing that for us?"* *"Careful."*

It's their homecoming: the city three of them live in, the city whose music business looked at them and said *that kind of thing*. Hannah is here with the whole third floor of her dorm. Martin Keller is at the soundboard, beside the house engineer, because of course. Mr. Vale is at a table in the back. Walt Mercer is not here, and neither is Dot, and Cal checks the door three times.

### "Still Here"

They play it third. The room becomes completely silent.

Ellis sings about somebody nobody here knows. The words never say more than that, and on replay they don't need to. Riley harmonizes. Cal's bass carries the melody on the bridge. Dean barely touches the kit until the last third.

Then everything opens.

The song ends. Two seconds of silence. Then enormous applause.

Ellis looks overwhelmed. For years his most private, impossible experience has isolated him. Now four hundred strangers are applauding a song made of it. They don't know what it's about. They feel it anyway. To Ellis, that distinction almost doesn't matter.

### After

At the edge of the stage, while the band is packing up, a woman about Ellis's age (a student, a coat over her arm) waits until he looks at her.

**WOMAN:** That last one. The quiet one.

**ELLIS:** Yeah?

**WOMAN:** I don't know what it's about.

Ellis smiles.

**ELLIS:** Neither do I.

**WOMAN:** It feels like missing somebody who's still standing next to you.

Ellis's face changes. The woman walks away.

**CLARA** *(behind him):* You bowed at her.

**ELLIS:** I nodded.

**CLARA:** Like a headwaiter.

She watches the woman go out the door.

**CLARA:** She understood.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** Nobody understands.

Ellis turns. That's unusually possessive.

**ELLIS:** She just did.

Clara looks almost hurt.

> **Design note — Clara changes.** Until now Clara has mostly protected Ellis: comforted, teased, encouraged. Now jealousy appears, and it isn't romantic. As Ellis forms stronger connections outside himself (Riley's harmony in Grace's place, an audience that understands without being told, success), the thing that's protected him is threatened. If Ellis stops needing Clara, what happens to her? Clara isn't thinking this; she's part of Ellis. It means some part of Ellis is terrified of letting go of the guilt that has held him together. So Clara pulls. Gently.

### Eddie

Eddie Farris finds them in the Exit's back alley, where Dean is taking an SX-70 of the dumpster because it has the club's name on it. Eddie counts the band's money into Cal's hand under the alley light.

**EDDIE:** Three eighty-five at the door, less my fifteen. Three twenty-seven to you.

**DEAN** *(watching his Polaroid develop, not looking up)*: Three twenty-seven twenty-five.

Eddie looks at him. Then he digs in his pocket and puts a quarter on top of the stack.

**EDDIE:** I've got something.

**DEAN:** If it's another six dates, yes.

**EDDIE:** It's four. Support. Galveston.

Galveston is a Texas boogie band with a hit on the radio right now. Real crowds: two, three thousand a night.

**EDDIE:** Jacksonville on the twenty-fourth. Columbia. Macon. Savannah on the twenty-ninth. Then you come home.

Dean doesn't need to say yes. Everybody watches him not need to.

## MISSION 10 — OPENING DAY

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** the Blake house → US 19 → Engineers Park, Laurel City → US 19 at night → the Blake house · **When:** Saturday Apr 12, 1975, 8:00 a.m. – 11:30 p.m. · **Length:** ~40 min

> The HUD shows **SATURDAY · APRIL 12**.

### The pie

Morning. Ellis comes into the kitchen. Wayne's at the table with coffee. On the counter by the stove, under a dish towel: a buttermilk pie in a glass plate, and propped against it a card with a dogwood on the front. The player can read it:

> *Thinking of you both today. Ada T.*

Ellis looks at the pie. At the card. At Wayne, who is reading the sports page with enormous concentration.

In March, Wayne had said, looking at the ticket book on the refrigerator, *"We'll go Saturday the twelfth. That's opening day."* And Ellis had gone very still and said *"The twelfth,"* and Wayne had said *"That's opening day,"* again, and that was all. The player saw it (a two-line exchange in the kitchen in Mission 7's week) and didn't know why it mattered.

**WAYNE:** We going or not?

**ELLIS:** We're going.

> **Replay layer.** April 12 is the day Grace died. It's the second anniversary. Ada Tolliver (whose farm Grace was driving home from) sends a pie every year. Nobody in this house has ever eaten one. Wayne chose this date for the ballgame out of the whole month. He couldn't say why. He didn't have to. On a first playthrough, the player sees a pie, a card, and two men not mentioning something.

### The drive

Wayne drives the F-100. Ellis in the passenger seat. The radio on WLC-AM, the Engineers' pregame show: the manager, the lineup, a man selling tires. US 19 through the Laurel Gap in April: dogwoods white on the ridges, redbud, the lakes high with spring rain, the Starlite at the truck stop with a sign that says HAPPY OPENING DAY ENGINEERS.

They don't talk much. They don't need to. It isn't hostile.

### Engineers Park

An old brick ballpark near the rail yards in Laurel City, built in 1929: a green grandstand roof on iron columns, a scoreboard someone updates by hand, the smell of popcorn and creosote from the tracks behind left field. Double-A baseball. About three thousand people on opening day. Bunting on the rail.

They sit in the grandstand along the first-base line. Wayne buys hot dogs and two Cokes and a program and refuses Ellis's money.

The anthem. A local high-school girl sings it, and strains at the top, and the crowd claps anyway.

**WAYNE** *(quietly, while everyone's sitting back down):* Your mother could sing that. Everybody strains at the top. She didn't.

Ellis looks at him. Wayne is looking at the field.

**ELLIS:** You never told me that.

**WAYNE:** You never asked.

**ELLIS:** I didn't know to ask.

Wayne considers that, and seems to find it fair.

### The game

The player watches a ballgame: nine innings, compressed and skippable, with things to do between pitches (keep score in the program; buy a beer from a vendor; heckle the umpire, which Wayne does too, specifically and profanely; watch the kid three rows down with a glove).

In the fifth, Wayne tells a story, apropos of nothing, looking at the field.

**WAYNE:** I brought Grace here once. Nineteen and seventy.

Ellis goes still. Wayne doesn't look at him.

**WAYNE:** Hated it. Said it was the most boring thing she'd ever been made to do in her life. Ate three hot dogs. Three. Fell asleep in the seventh on my coat. Woke up in the ninth and asked me who won the football.

Ellis laughs. He can't help it.

**ELLIS:** The football.

**WAYNE:** The football.

Wayne laughs too. The first time the player has heard him laugh. It sounds like it hurts his chest a little.

In the seventh, a foul ball comes back into the grandstand two rows in front of them. Ellis stands and reaches; it goes off his fingertips and a boy of about ten catches it barehanded.

**WAYNE:** You'd have dropped it.

**ELLIS:** I did drop it.

**WAYNE:** You'd have dropped it anyway.

> **Observe** (the scoreboard): *Apr 12. The scoreboard man leans out of his little window to hang a zero, like somebody hanging out wash.*

The Engineers lose, 4–3, on an error in the eighth.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

This is their best day in the game. They never once mention the date.

### The drive home

Night. US 19 back up through the Gap, Wayne driving. Two-lane in the mountains. The radio playing the postgame and then a country station. The player, as Ellis, can look out the window, fiddle with the radio, or just sit.

An oncoming car comes around a curve with its high beams on.

Ellis flinches: his right hand comes up off his knee toward his face, his shoulder turns toward the door. It's small. It's over in a second.

Wayne sees it.

He doesn't say anything. He takes his foot off the gas. The speedometer drops to forty. He moves the truck over until the right tires are riding just inside the white line, and he puts his other hand on the wheel, ten and two.

He drives that way the rest of the way home: forty miles an hour, both hands, keeping right. Forty minutes of it. Cars stack up behind them on the straight stretches and pass. Wayne doesn't speed up. Neither of them says one word about it.

> **Design note.** The player can't control this; they're the passenger. That's the point. This is the most loving thing Wayne does in the game, and it's forty minutes of a man driving slowly.

### Home

The kitchen. The pie is still on the counter under the dish towel. Wayne takes it, carries it out onto the porch, and sets it down on the boards in front of Tater.

Tater regards it. Then eats it, plate and all, very nearly.

Wayne comes back in, washes his hands, and goes to bed.

**WAYNE** *(from the hall):* Night.

**ELLIS:** Night.

His office.

**RILEY:** I'm asking.

**RILEY:** Yes.

**LANDRY:** Riley, I meant what I said in December.

The dorm room. Riley on her bed with the Edinburgh envelope. Hannah at her desk with lesson plans.

## MISSION 12 — SAIGON

Ellis is increasingly distracted in a way nobody can name. **CAL:** Dean.

**CAL:** *Dean.*

**DEAN:** *What?*

**DEAN:** Jesus.

**CAL:** Soundcheck.

**CAL:** Twenty minutes ago.

**DEAN:** Fuck.

**CAL:** How long have you been awake?

**DEAN:** I slept.

**CAL:** When?

**DEAN:** Just now.

**CAL:** Before that.

**DEAN:** I don't know.

**CAL:** Sunday? Saturday?

**DEAN:** What do you want me to say?

**CAL:** That you'll stop.

Dean looks at him. Then laughs.

**DEAN:** Oh, fuck off.

**DEAN:** So am I.

**CAL:** You're missing soundchecks. You don't sleep. You disappear after every show—

**DEAN:** And then I play.

**CAL:** That isn't the point.

**DEAN:** Don't tell me how to do my job.

**DEAN:** Don't—

Cal hits him. One punch, to the mouth. Dean stumbles back against the van.

Silence.

**ELLIS** *(from the loading door):* *What the fuck?*

Riley runs out past him. Dean touches his lip. Looks at the blood on his fingers. Looks at Cal. Then laughs.

**DEAN:** There he is.

**CAL:** Fuck you.

Dean swings.

### The fight

It's ugly and sloppy, nothing heroic: two friends trying not to seriously hurt each other while angry enough to forget that. The player, as Cal, has limited, clumsy inputs (grab, shove, block) and no way to "win." Cal hits Dean's shoulder. Dean catches Cal's ear. They go down against the van's bumper. Tully comes out of the loading door at a run and doesn't intervene; he just stands close enough to catch somebody's head before it hits the pavement.

Ellis pulls Dean off. Riley gets between Cal and them.

**DEAN:** *Let go of me.*

**ELLIS:** *Stop.*

**CAL** *(bleeding from his nose)*: You're going to kill yourself.

That sentence ends it. Dean goes still in Ellis's grip.

**DEAN:** Fuck you.

**CAL:** Yeah.

**DEAN:** You don't know anything about me.

**CAL:** I know you're my friend.

Silence. It's Cal's first explicit emotional admission in the game, and he says it bleeding from his nose in a parking lot in Savannah.

Dean looks away.

### The show

They play anyway. Of course they do. Cal with a swollen nose and a bruise coming up under one eye; Dean with a split lip that opens again on the second song. Three thousand people in a civic auditorium who came to see Galveston think it looks tough. They cheer when Dean spits blood into a towel between songs.

They have no idea. That's a recurring motif of fame: the audience turns private damage into image.

### After

Backstage, Dean sits alone on a road case in a hallway under a fluorescent tube, holding a paper towel to his mouth. Cal comes down the hall with a Ziploc bag of ice from the concession stand. No apology speech. He hands Dean the ice.

Dean takes it.

**DEAN:** You hit like shit.

**CAL:** You bleed like a bitch.

Dean smiles slightly and winces.

**DEAN:** I'm not stopping.

**CAL:** I know.

**DEAN:** You gonna hit me every time?

**CAL:** Depends.

**DEAN:** On what?

**CAL:** How annoying you are.

**DEAN:** We're fucked.

**CAL:** Completely.

Cal sits down on the case next to him. They stay there until the stage manager comes looking for the headliner's drummer and finds the wrong one.

### The motel (switch to Dean)

Past midnight. A motel on the Ogeechee Road outside Savannah, two stories around a pool that's been drained for repairs. The lobby has a television on a shelf, a coffee urn, a rack of brochures for Fort Pulaski. The night clerk is watching the news.

Stutter. Dean, coming into the lobby for coffee with the ice bag still on his lip.

On the television, the late news from Saigon: a correspondent's voice on a telephone line from the fleet, crackling, over a still photograph of him; a map of South Vietnam with the arrows closing in; the words FINAL EVACUATION across the bottom of the screen. The anchor saying the last Americans are being flown out of Saigon tonight by helicopter.

Tully is standing in front of the television with a coffee in his hand he hasn't drunk. He's been standing there a while.

Dean stops.

Tully watches the map until the anchor moves on to the weather. Then he puts the coffee down on the brochure rack, very carefully, and walks out of the lobby into the parking lot.

Dean looks at the television. At the door. The player, as Dean, can go back to his room. The prompt is there. Or follow.

If the player follows: Tully is sitting on the curb at the far end of the lot under a sodium light, by the van, with his forearms on his knees.

Dean sits down on the curb next to him.

And for the only time in the game, Dean doesn't say anything. The player's input is disabled except for one option, **Stay**, and it's already selected. The camera holds on the two of them from behind, across the lot. A truck on the Ogeechee Road. The ice machine. A dog somewhere.

After a very long time, Tully says one thing.

**TULLY:** I was at Loc Ninh.

That's all. Dean doesn't ask what that means. He stays until Tully gets up.

## MISSION 14 — SOUTHERN STAR

> 1142 Linden Street, Laurel City

**EDDIE:** A label.

**ELLIS:** What?

Dean collapses laughing.

**ELLIS:** Fuck you.

But Ellis is pale. This is different. A show can disappear. A record lasts.

### Linden Street

Southern Star is a converted Victorian house on a side street off Tenth: a porch with rocking chairs nobody rocks in, a receptionist in the front parlor, two offices upstairs, a gold record on the wall from 1971 by a band nobody's heard of since, cigarette smoke, coffee, a Christmas cactus.

**VANCE:** I don't know what you are.

**DEAN:** We've been hearing that.

**VANCE:** That's why you're here.

He talks for ten minutes about what's wrong with the business: every band sounds like the Allmans or like Skynyrd; the labels in Macon and here want boogie; the national companies want the next Eagles. The Blakes don't sound like anybody. That's commercially risky and possibly valuable.

**DEAN:** Dad. Put the paper down.

**CAL:** What?

**DEAN:** We're getting a record.

**CAL:** We're being offered a contract.

**DEAN:** Same thing.

**CAL:** Those are catastrophically different things.

Vance smiles.

**VANCE:** I like him.

**DEAN:** Everybody says that till about hour three.

### Take it home

Ellis has been quiet the whole meeting. Vance notices.

**VANCE:** You're the one who writes the words.

**ELLIS:** Allegedly.

Vance slides the contract across the desk.

**VANCE:** Take it home. Get somebody who understands contracts to read it before you sign anything. Anybody who tells you different is robbing you.

**CAL:** We will.

**DEAN** *(on the porch, afterward):* Do we know anybody who understands contracts?

Cal looks at him for a long moment.

**CAL:** We will.

### Outside

On the sidewalk on Linden Street, everybody erupts at once. Dean grabs Riley and spins her; Riley screams. Cal actually laughs. Eddie grabs Ellis by both shoulders.

**EDDIE:** Do you understand what just happened?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

He doesn't.

**RILEY:** We're making a record.

He looks at her. The fear goes out of him for a moment.

**ELLIS:** We're making a record.

He smiles, hugely. They kiss again. Ellis glances across the street: on the porch of a boarding house, Clara is watching. Expression unreadable.

Clara opens her mouth. Nothing comes. She looks at him as if he's asked her what year it is and she has never once been told.

**CLARA:** Why are you doing this?

**ELLIS:** Because I don't remember.

**CLARA:** Of course you do.

**ELLIS:** Then tell me when.

**CLARA:** El.

He freezes.

**ELLIS:** What did you call me?

**CLARA:** Ellis.

**ELLIS:** You called me—

**CLARA:** You know.

That phrase. *You know.*

Ellis's breathing changes. The screen doesn't distort; the sound does, just slightly, like a room with the doors closing. Then twelve frames, faster than on the mountain road:

A windshield, broken into a web, rain coming through it. A girl's laugh, from a car seat, before. A green felt-tip hand. Headlights.

Gone.

Ellis bends over with his hands on his knees in the middle of Depot Street.

### Clara changes tactic

She comes toward him. Gentle again.

**CLARA:** Hey.

**ELLIS:** Don't.

**CLARA:** You're tired.

**ELLIS:** Don't touch me.

She stops.

**CLARA:** Okay.

**ELLIS:** You're messing with me.

**CLARA:** No.

**ELLIS:** Then tell me when.

And Clara starts crying.

This undoes him. In five chapters Clara has never cried.

**CLARA:** Why are you doing this to me?

And now he feels guilty. He softens immediately.

**ELLIS:** I'm sorry.

The defense protects itself through Ellis's guilt. He steps toward her.

**ELLIS:** I'm sorry. I'm sorry.

He says it the way people say it who have been saying it for years. The player may notice it's the phrase Riley isn't allowed to say to him.

Clara puts her arms around him. From Ellis's side: real. Warm. A person. Her hair against his face, the jacket, the horse patch pressed against his shirt.

A prompt comes up, the same one the game uses for holding a long note: **Hold.** The player holds.

### The pull-back

While the player holds, the camera begins to move. Slowly. Backward. Away from them.

Across Depot Street. Past the front of Marlon's with its sign dark. Up, a little, the way a camera on a crane would move. Past the depot. Farther.

Until Ellis is a small figure under the one streetlight on Depot Street, in Hollow Ridge, Georgia, at twenty to two in the morning.

Alone.

His arms wrapped around nothing. His head bowed onto nobody's shoulder. His hand holding the back of a jacket that isn't there.

No character sees this. Only the player, who is still holding.

> **This is the first objective camera shot in the game that shows the truth.** No horror sting, no distortion, no music at all: just a freight horn, very far down the valley, two longs, a short, a long. The player has spent five chapters (twenty-five hours) seeing Clara through Ellis. Now the camera stands across the street, and she isn't there.

### Do not explain it

No text, no diagnosis, no flashback, no voice-over.

Let the player sit in it. The wide shot lasts exactly as long as the player keeps holding. There's no timer; the player chooses when to let go. When they release, far off under the streetlight, Ellis's arms open around nothing and come down to his sides. Then the camera returns to Ellis, close, and Clara is there again, stepping back from him and wiping her face with her jacket cuff, because the camera is with Ellis again, and the player now knows exactly what that means.

Fade.

**CHAPTER V COMPLETE**
**VELOCITY**

## Chapter V — design summary

### Momentum became velocity
From Christmas to May: six dates became ten, a 45 on one college station became thirty, a Laurel City club became a sold-out headliner, and a record store on the Row that wouldn't carry them put them in the window. A label wants them. Every success created the next obligation before anyone could process the last. The player experienced the mechanism, not a montage.

### The midpoint
The player now knows Clara isn't physically there. The game deliberately hasn't said who she is. The mystery has changed shape:
- Chapters I–IV: *Who is Clara?* (mostly unasked)
- End of Chapter V: *What is Clara?*
- Coming: *Why Clara?* Then: *Who is she built from?* Then: *What happened to Grace?* Then: *What did Grace say?*

Each answer makes a worse question.

Hundreds of earlier moments change meaning at once: Cal wasn't forgetful; Riley didn't miss her; Marlon wasn't distracted; Wayne wasn't paranoid; the empty passenger seats, the photographs, the untouched food, the dog, the deputy. It was all there.

But Clara must not become a monster now, or a hallucination working against Ellis. She's made of him: love and guilt and memory. Her growing possessiveness is Ellis's fear that moving forward means leaving Grace behind. That's harder to watch than any villain would be.

### Ellis and the audience
This chapter establishes the addictive thing fame gives Ellis, which has nothing to do with money: understanding. He writes something nobody in his life understands, and four hundred strangers are silent for it. For someone this isolated, that's intoxicating. The stage is becoming the one place his inner world produces an outer response that makes sense. Later, that will be dangerous.

### Riley
Over Christmas, in the back pew of her mother's church, she wrote the first verse of "Sunday Clothes" and told nobody: *not yet*. She missed an exam because she chose to. Landry only asked whether it was worth it; she wrote *own the cost* on the back of the grade report herself. She put Edinburgh in a drawer. That preserves her agency: nobody made her. She'll make choices she doesn't regret that cost her dearly, and the game will let both be true.

### Dean
Recreation → regulation. He used alone in a basement after his father said *I know*, and a plate of pot roast appeared on the stairs. He still plays brilliantly. He's still lovable. He also shoved his best friend and got hit for it. And without looking up from a Polaroid he corrected Eddie's fifteen percent to the quarter, and nobody in the alley but Eddie noticed. Substance problems don't delete the rest of a person, which makes the trajectory worse to watch.

### Cal
He's become indispensable, and that's corrosive. Everybody asks him everything, so he believes if he stops paying attention something bad happens, and he's often right: the van, Dean, money, gear, Riley's exhaustion, Ellis drifting. "I know you're my friend," bleeding. He's also on a pay phone on Valentine's Day saying *me too* to somebody who isn't his mother.

### Wayne
The best day he and Ellis have had in two years happened on the anniversary of Grace's death, at a ballgame he chose the date for. He drove home at forty miles an hour with both hands on the wheel because his son flinched at a headlight. He fed the pie to the dog. He walked into Marlon's. He said Grace's name without using it as a weapon. The tragedy the game is building is that these two keep getting within inches of each other.
