# CHAPTER IV — MOMENTUM

> **Laurel City · Tannersville · Hollow Ridge · the Tanner Valley · Saturday, December 7 – Monday, December 23, 1974**
> Main-story runtime: ~5.5 hours · Playable: Cal Mercer, Riley, Dean Holloway, Ellis Blake

## Chapter thesis

There's a point in every dream where it stops being something you talk about and starts rearranging your life. The Blakes reach it this month. Nobody quits a job, drops out or signs anything. Riley moves an assignment. Cal keeps Saturdays open. Dean spends money on a van instead of whatever future his father imagines. Ellis stops asking whether they'll play next week. The band has moved, quietly, from something they do to something they are. Because nearly everything happening to them is good, none of them recognizes momentum as something that can hurt you.

## World state at open

- December: Christmas lights strung across Main Street in Hollow Ridge, a plywood nativity on the Methodist lawn, tinsel on Marlon's register next to the WIN button. Dead week coming at Tannersville College: the week before finals.
- The Blakes: a college-radio recording, one Laurel City show, a letter from a stranger, and a decision to make "something real." About $370 in the band fund.
- WTCR has played "No Name" twice more. Nobody else has.

## COLD OPEN

Black. A mechanical clunk: a tape machine going into record.

Ellis sings one line of "No Name." Stop. Rewind whir. Clunk. The same line. Stop. Rewind. Again, harder.

Stop.

**CAL** *(from outside the booth, talkback):* You're squeezing it.

**ELLIS:** I'm singing it.

**CAL:** You're strangling it.

**ELLIS:** You want to fucking sing it?

**CAL:** No.

A beat.

**CAL:** I want you to.

Silence. Then Riley laughs somewhere in the control room.

**RILEY:** He's got you there.

**ELLIS:** Why did I join a band?

**DEAN:** You didn't. I joined one for you.

**ELLIS:** Right. Fuck you especially.

**DEAN:** Love you too.

Another voice on the talkback, older, flat, a Laurel City accent:

**HOYT:** Y'all paying for another hour?

Silence. Cal looks at the clock on the control-room wall: a big round Simplex with a red sweep hand. He looks at a bank envelope on the console with *BAND* written on it in his handwriting. He looks through the glass at Ellis.

**CAL:** One more.

**ELLIS:** Thought I was strangling it.

**CAL:** Then stop.

The tape rolls. Ellis closes his eyes. And sings.

## MISSION 1 — EIGHT HOURS

**Playable:** Cal (with brief switches) · **Where:** Mockingbird Sound, Laurel City · **When:** Saturday Dec 7, 9:00 a.m. – 5:10 p.m. · **Length:** ~55 min

The Blakes have rented eight hours at **Mockingbird Sound**, a small commercial studio in a converted appliance store two blocks off Tenth Street in Laurel City: jingles, gospel groups, a Southern rock band once. Thirty dollars an hour for the room, the engineer and an eight-track Scully, plus sixty for the tape. Eight hours is all they can afford. That means every minute costs money, visibly: the Simplex clock is on the control-room wall and the player sees it constantly, the way you see a taxi meter.

### The studio

Wood paneling. Heavy soundproof doors with pull handles like a meat locker. Brown shag carpet worn to the backing in a path from the door to the drum riser. Ashtrays everywhere. Patch bays. A battered Baldwin upright in the corner and a Hammond organ with a cigarette burn on the lid. A microphone cabinet holding four microphones worth more than Ellis's car.

Dean walks into the live room and stops. For once, quiet.

**DEAN:** Holy shit.

**CAL:** Don't touch anything.

**DEAN:** Why does everybody say that to me?

**HOYT** *(Hoyt Barnes, the engineer: forties, flattop haircut, a short-sleeve dress shirt, ex–Air Force radar technician, hands like a watchmaker's):* Because you look like you'd touch things.

Dean considers it. His hand is resting on the Hammond.

**DEAN:** I'd like the record to show I haven't touched anything.

### Why this is Cal's mission

This is Cal's environment. He isn't a producer, but there are finally enough controls in one room that his way of hearing becomes useful instead of irritating. Placement matters. Timing matters. Tuning matters. The difference between almost right and right matters, and here it can be fixed. Cal is thrilled. He expresses it by becoming more serious.

### The money

They set the band envelope on the console: Marlon's Fridays, the Blind Tiger, the car lot, WTCR's thirty-dollar honorarium, and forty dollars Dean put in with a note in the ledger that says LOAN (D.H.) in Cal's handwriting. Cal has tracked every dollar.

Dean reads the ledger over his shoulder.

**DEAN:** You wrote down the chili dogs?

**CAL:** They came out of band money.

**DEAN:** There were four.

**CAL:** There were seven.

Dean glances at the column without seeming to read it.

**DEAN:** Seven at thirty-five cents is two forty-five. You wrote two sixty.

Cal runs his pen down the figures. He adds them again. It's two forty-five.

**CAL:** How did you—

**DEAN:** Lucky guess.

**RILEY:** You ate three yourself.

**DEAN:** I was performing.

**CAL:** You were sitting on an amplifier.

**DEAN:** Recovering.

**ELLIS:** Can we make the fucking record before Cal charges Dean interest?

Cal closes the book.

**CAL:** Three songs.

**RILEY:** Three good songs.

**ELLIS:** One great one.

Riley looks at him. That was her line at the Starlite. He remembered. She smiles.

### The three songs

- **"No Name."** Ellis's original. Still unnamed officially. Everyone calls it "No Name" now, which Dean hates, because he meant it as a joke.
- **"Low Water."** The first song all four of them wrote.
- **Song three**: unfinished. This was the dangerous choice. Cal wanted something they'd already played at Marlon's. Ellis insisted the third song should be something nobody had heard, the song he's been writing since the night on Stony Knob. Riley agreed. Dean agreed because Dean agrees with anything reckless. Cal lost three to one and is still angry about democracy.

### Recording gameplay (Cal)

The player, as Cal, helps set up: the bass through the Ampeg into a microphone and a direct box both; moving Ellis's Silvertone amp off the floor onto a chair so it stops sounding like a cardboard box; hearing Dean's cymbals bleed into Riley's vocal microphone and moving her behind a gobo. The mechanics are accessible, not a technical simulation. The point is to make Cal's ears feel like a superpower.

When the band runs the first song, the player can pick problems out of the mix by listening: the crash washing over Riley; a cable crackling when Ellis moves; Ellis's amp buzzing on the low E; the room ringing at a frequency Cal can find and Hoyt can notch out.

Hoyt watches Cal do this.

**HOYT:** You done this before?

**CAL:** No.

**HOYT:** Could've fooled me.

**CAL:** I fix amplifiers.

**HOYT:** Same disease.

Nobody outside the band has noticed Cal's ears before. He loves it quietly.

### "No Name"

**Take one** goes badly. Dean comes in too hard. Ellis forgets the new second verse and sings the old one. Riley's guitar is out of tune after the cold car ride.

**CAL:** Again.

**DEAN:** That one had character.

**CAL:** That one had mistakes.

**DEAN:** Same thing, sometimes.

Cal looks at him. From the console:

**HOYT:** Kid's not entirely wrong.

**DEAN** *(pointing at Hoyt)*: Professional opinion.

**Take two:** better. **Take three:** better. **Take four:** technically excellent. Nobody drops anything.

They listen to the playback in the control room.

Something's missing.

**ELLIS:** It sounds dead.

**CAL:** It's clean.

**ELLIS:** I rest my case.

This is the first real aesthetic disagreement in the band. Cal wants precision. Ellis wants danger.

**RILEY:** Play it live.

**HOYT:** You've been playing it live.

**RILEY:** No. No headphones. In the room. Facing each other.

**CAL:** Bleed.

**RILEY:** Good.

Cal looks betrayed. Riley smiles.

**RILEY:** Character.

Dean is delighted.

They rearrange the live room. It's Ellis who places them: amps pointed at the walls, the four of them in a square, facing inward, close enough to see each other's hands.

**ELLIS:** Like a singing.

**RILEY:** Like a what?

**ELLIS:** Nothing. Face each other.

> **Replay layer.** Sacred Harp singers sit in a hollow square facing each other and sing at full voice. Ellis's grandmother took him every summer. This is where he learned that music is something you do facing the people you're doing it with, not facing whoever's listening.

They play it live. Ellis makes a tiny mistake on the turnaround (a chord held one beat too long). Dean hears it and holds with him. Riley follows. Cal compensates underneath. Because everybody responds, the mistake becomes something: a breath in the song that wasn't there before, and it's the best part.

They finish. Silence in the control room.

**HOYT:** That's your take.

Cal wants to object. He listens to the playback, twice. He can't. It's the take.

### The sound of The Blakes begins (switch to Ellis)

Song two, "Low Water," is where something happens that none of them understands yet.

Hoyt has a Maestro Echoplex on a shelf, a tape-loop echo in a gray metal case with a cartridge of tape you can see going around. Ellis stares at it between takes.

**ELLIS:** What does that do?

Stutter. Control transfers to Ellis.

Hoyt patches it in. The player plays one note on the Silvertone. The note comes back. And back. And back, a little darker each time, like somebody walking away down a hall.

Ellis looks at Riley through the glass. Riley already understands. She plays a note on the Martin against the repeats, a different one, and the two collide into something neither guitar can make alone. They begin building it: Ellis's repeats, Riley's long ringing chords, the whole thing hanging in the air of the live room.

**CAL** *(talkback):* We're paying by the hour.

**ELLIS:** Listen.

**CAL:** I am listening. To money disappearing.

Then Dean, without being asked, starts playing only the floor tom. Boom. Space. Boom. Space.

Eventually Cal gives up and comes in on bass, one note, low.

Hoyt reaches over and pushes the Echoplex's repeat knob up.

For perhaps thirty seconds they make something that doesn't sound like any local band they've heard, or like anything they have a name for: dark and huge and dreamlike and heavy and beautiful.

Hoyt stops the tape afterward.

**HOYT:** What the hell was that?

Nobody answers, because nobody knows.

> **Design note — the embryo.** Years later, people will retrospectively call this sound space rock, shoegaze, alternative metal, post-rock. In December 1974, The Blakes know only that it sounds like them. It becomes the middle section of "Low Water" and stays there. The band starts calling it "the loud part," even though what defines it is how quiet it gets first.

### Dean (switch)

During the heavy section the camera swings behind the kit. Micro-stutter. Dean.

Dean has discovered that his job isn't filling every second. Cal's criticism in October actually took. The player feels it in the controls: the kit is quiet, the tension builds, and the Room shows the band leaning toward him, waiting.

He waits. And waits. And waits.

Then he hits, and the room explodes.

Dean's grin says the whole thing: restraint makes the chaos bigger.

### Riley (switch)

Riley overdubs a nearly whispered harmony on the loud part, then a second whisper a third above it. Hoyt pans them left and right. Her voice surrounds Ellis's instead of sitting next to it.

Ellis listens to the playback through the control-room speakers.

**ELLIS:** That's terrifying.

**RILEY:** Good terrifying?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**RILEY:** Then keep it.

They keep it.

### Song three

They have one hour and fifty minutes left. The third song doesn't have a finished lyric.

**CAL:** We should cut it.

**ELLIS:** No.

**CAL:** We don't have it.

**ELLIS:** We will.

**CAL:** When?

Ellis points at the Simplex.

**ELLIS:** Four-thirty.

**CAL:** That isn't how songwriting works.

**RILEY:** Sometimes it is.

Cal looks between them.

**CAL:** You two are exhausting.

**DEAN:** And yet you're still here.

**CAL:** Against medical advice.

### Ellis and Riley write

The others go out for coffee at the drive-in on the corner. Ellis and Riley stay in the live room with their guitars.

This is a playable writing scene. Ellis plays a progression. Riley changes a chord. Ellis changes it back. Riley changes a different one. They argue quietly. They settle. Then words: Ellis sings a line; Riley shakes her head; Riley offers a line; Ellis shakes his.

The song is about the mountain and the radio: a signal that only lands in one place, if you drive high enough and hold still. The verses keep describing the drive.

**RILEY:** You're writing around it.

**ELLIS:** Around what?

**RILEY:** Whatever the song's actually about.

**ELLIS:** You don't know what it's about.

**RILEY:** Neither do you.

That lands. Ellis looks at his notebook. A line comes. He writes it:

> *There's a pull-off at the top where my daddy parks his truck*
> *and turns the dome light on and waits for somebody to find him.*

Riley reads it. She doesn't say anything. She plays the next chord, the one that makes the line fall into the chorus.

> **Replay layer.** The player saw Wayne's dome light on Stony Knob in Chapter III. Ellis just put his father in a song, and Riley doesn't know it's true.

### The third take

One take. They can't afford two, and everyone knows it. Cal's hands are tight on the neck. Dean waits for Ellis. Riley watches Ellis.

**HOYT:** Rolling.

Ellis breathes. Then music.

The performance isn't perfect. Dean almost loses the transition into the last chorus and Cal pulls him back. Riley's harmony cracks slightly on the high note. And Ellis's voice breaks on the last line, on *find him*, like something gave way.

Nobody stops.

They finish. The tape keeps running. Nobody speaks.

**HOYT:** Time.

**CAL:** Playback.

**HOYT:** You're over.

**CAL:** Thirty seconds.

Hoyt looks at them. Then he rewinds.

They listen. When Ellis's voice cracks on the playback, Ellis winces.

**RILEY:** Leave it.

**ELLIS:** It broke.

**RILEY:** I know.

Cal listens again. The clock has gone past five. He's paying for this minute personally.

**CAL:** Leave it.

Ellis looks at him. If Cal says to leave the imperfection, it stays.

**ELLIS:** What do we call it?

**DEAN:** "Stony Knob."

**ELLIS:** How do you know about Stony Knob?

**DEAN:** It's in the second verse, genius.

### The master

Hoyt hands Cal the one-inch multitrack in its box and the quarter-inch mono mixdown, the master, plus a reference copy. Cal holds the master like a transplant organ. Dean reaches for it.

**CAL:** Don't.

**DEAN:** Still?

**CAL:** Especially now.

> **Ledger:** *12/7/74 — Mockingbird Sound — 8 hrs @ $30 + tape $60 = $300.00. 10 min over, $5.00 (C.M. personal).*

## MISSION 2 — FIVE HUNDRED COPIES

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** Marlon's; Tanner Custom Records on the Row · **When:** Monday Dec 9 · **Length:** ~20 min

### A record

Carla Vickery's letter asked where to buy the record. There isn't one. You can't sell a reel of tape.

Cal has done the arithmetic: a custom pressing plant on the Row, **Tanner Custom Records**, which presses gospel quartets, country demos and high-school choirs, will cut lacquers from the master, plate them and press **500 seven-inch 45s** with printed labels in plain white sleeves for **$412**, ready in a week.

They have $112 left.

Riley, as the player, runs the problem: she can talk to each of the others (the answers are funny and useless), count what she has, and walk down to Marlon's.

### Marlon

Monday afternoon at Marlon's. Marlon's doing the week's books at the end of the bar with a pencil and an adding machine.

**RILEY:** Mr. Pettit, we need three hundred dollars.

**MARLON:** So do I.

**RILEY:** To press records. Five hundred of them. We'd sell them at the shows. And the college station would—

**MARLON:** How many Fridays is three hundred dollars?

Riley does it in her head: after the band fund comes out, a little over two Fridays of their shares.

**RILEY:** Two. Two and a half.

Marlon looks at her over his glasses for a long time. Then he opens the register, counts out three hundred in twenties and tens, and pushes it across the bar. With the hundred and twelve in the envelope it comes to four hundred and twelve, to the dollar.

**MARLON:** Against the Fridays.

**RILEY:** Thank you.

**MARLON:** Don't thank me. Play better.

She's at the door when he says, without looking up from the books:

**MARLON:** Tell him I want one. The first one.

He doesn't say who "him" is. He doesn't have to.

> **Replay layer.** Marlon invited sixteen-year-old Ellis to play this bar on April 12, 1973. He's never said it. He'll never say it to Ellis. He just keeps paying.

### The label

At Tanner Custom Records (a cinderblock building behind a publisher's house on the Row that smells like hot vinyl) Riley fills out the label copy on a form in block capitals with Cal standing over her.

> **THE BLAKES**
> **A: NO NAME** (E. Blake)
> **B: LOW WATER** (Blake–Riley–Mercer–Holloway)

**CAL:** Put all four on "Low Water."

**RILEY:** I did.

**CAL:** I'm checking.

**RILEY:** You watched me write it.

**CAL:** I'm still checking.

> **Design note — credit.** This small form matters later. "Low Water" is credited to all four from the beginning. When the press starts writing that Ellis Blake writes all The Blakes' songs, there is a 45 label that says otherwise.

### The sleeve

The plant's sleeves are plain white paper. Riley wants something on them. She has two ideas.

The first is the Polaroid from the Starlite. She brings it up at Vale's that evening. It's in the frame of Ellis's mirror; he answers without needing to look at it.

**ELLIS:** No.

**RILEY:** Why?

**ELLIS:** It's ours.

It surprises everyone slightly. He means *private*, and Riley understands. She puts the idea away.

The second idea is a rubber stamp. She has one made at the office-supply store on River Street (THE BLAKES in a typewriter face, $4.50) and an ink pad, and the player gets a small, satisfying task: stamping sleeves on the floor of Vale's back room with Dean, who stamps them crooked on purpose, and Tully, who stamps them perfectly straight and very slowly, and Cal, who takes Dean's stamp away. At the bottom of each sleeve, in smaller stamps Riley buys the next day: *BOOKING — VALE MUSIC — TANNERSVILLE* and the store's phone number.

## MISSION 3 — FOUR HUNDRED DOLLARS AND A TERRIBLE IDEA

**Playable:** Dean · **Where:** Marlon's → the South Fork → Laurel Gap → the Tanner Valley · **When:** Wednesday Dec 11 · **Length:** ~35 min

### The problem

Mr. Vale needs his van back for Christmas deliveries. The band has three shows this weekend in three places. There are four people, a PA, a drum kit, three amplifiers, and one Plymouth Valiant, one Chevelle with a trunk the size of a suitcase, Riley's Datsun and Cal's Dodge Dart.

Dean has been reading the classifieds. This is his moment. He slaps the *Banner* and the *Laurel City Courier*'s trader pages on Marlon's bar, four ads circled in red.

**DEAN:** Gentlemen.

Riley looks at him.

**DEAN:** And Riley.

**RILEY:** Strong recovery.

**DEAN:** We're buying a van.

Cal reads the first ad.

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** You haven't seen it.

**CAL:** "Runs most days."

**DEAN:** Honesty.

**CAL:** Warning.

### Money

Dean, it turns out, has two hundred dollars: a savings certificate from his grandmother that matured on his twentieth birthday, money his father has never touched. He says this looking at Ellis, carefully, and Ellis nods once.

Cal puts in a hundred. Riley fifty. Ellis puts in fifty, which is everything he has after rent and Carson's four dollars. He doesn't buy strings this week. The player sees it on the money screen and can't change it; Ellis insisted.

> **Ledger:** *12/11/74 — Van fund — D.H. $200, C.M. $100, R. $50, E.B. $50. Ownership by share: D.H. 1/2, C.M. 1/4, R. 1/8, E.B. 1/8.*
> Dean will spend two years claiming he owns the engine.

### Van shopping

A genuinely fun open-world sequence across the map: four vehicles in four places, driven to in Dean's Chevelle with all four of them crammed in, arguing. Each van is terrible in a different way.

**Van one** (a farm off the South Fork road): a gorgeous 1971 Dodge Tradesman, candy-apple red, a mural of a sunset on the side. Cal listens to it idle for three seconds.

**CAL:** No.

**SELLER:** You haven't driven it.

**CAL:** Don't need to.

**DEAN:** Can we drive it anyway?

**CAL:** Why?

**DEAN:** Research.

They drive it. A hundred yards down the road, white smoke pours out from under the doghouse cover between the front seats, filling the cab. They all pile out coughing.

**DEAN:** Research concluded.

**Van two** (a used-car lot in Laurel Gap): runs perfectly. Riley opens the back doors, looks in, and closes them.

**RILEY:** No.

**ELLIS:** What?

**RILEY:** Get in.

Ellis opens the doors, leans in, and closes them.

**ELLIS:** No.

**DEAN:** What happened in there?

**CAL:** Don't ask questions you don't want answered.

**Van three** (a Church of God in the Tanner Valley, selling its old youth van): a white '65 Chevy with FIRST CHURCH OF GOD YOUTH still showing through the new paint. Dean loves it. Cal hates the mileage (it has been around the odometer once already). Riley thinks it's hilarious.

**ELLIS:** We are not driving around with "Church of God Youth" under the paint.

**DEAN:** Rebrand.

**Van four** (a man named Otis Crump in a hollow off the Laurel Gap road): a **1966 Ford Econoline** cargo van, faded dark green with a cream roof. Flat-nosed, the engine between the front seats under a cover. A dented rear door. The engine sounds questionable but honest. The inside is bare metal, the floor scratched down to primer, one seat repaired with electrical tape.

Cal crawls underneath it.

**OTIS:** Your buddy always do that?

**ELLIS:** Unfortunately.

Cal comes back out.

**CAL:** Transmission's leaking.

**OTIS:** Little.

**CAL:** Rear tires are finished.

**OTIS:** Little.

**CAL:** The fan belt's cracked in four places.

**OTIS:** Little.

**CAL:** Your definition of little is fascinating.

### Haggling (Ellis's specialty)

Stutter to Ellis. This becomes his specialty in the band, because he grew up around men selling each other machinery on porches.

A haggling mechanic: Otis wants $550. Cal supplies defects. Ellis turns defects into money in a language Otis respects: facts, delivered slowly, with long silences. Dean keeps nearly ruining it by saying how much he loves the van. Riley physically removes Dean to the yard, where he pets Otis's dog and tells the dog how much he loves the van.

**ELLIS:** Reckon that belt'll make it to the state line?

**OTIS:** Which state?

**ELLIS:** Any of 'em.

The player can push too hard (Otis gets offended and the price goes up) or not hard enough. The best result is $400. Every price between $400 and $475 is achievable and remembered in the ledger (Dean covers any overage; the ledger re-cuts the shares).

> **Observe** (Otis's porch): *Dec 11. Otis Crump says "little" the way a preacher says "amen."*

They buy it.

### Home

The four of them stand in the empty cargo area. Dean spreads his arms.

**DEAN:** Home.

**CAL:** Absolutely not.

**DEAN:** We'll live here.

**RILEY:** You can.

**ELLIS:** Reckon something already died in here.

**DEAN:** History.

**CAL:** Mold.

> **The van.** This is one of the most important objects in the game. Players will spend dozens of hours inside it. It needs history, and it starts getting some immediately.

### Customization

Back at Marlon's lot, the player helps outfit it. The van's personality is canonical, so the choices are few. They install plywood racks for amplifiers, moving blankets, a remnant of orange shag carpet Riley gets free from the Tannersville carpet outlet, a Coleman cooler, Cal's toolbox bolted behind the driver's seat, a bench from a junked Chevy bolted to the floor behind the driver (Dean's), a curtain for the back windows made from a bedspread, a cheap dome light, and a place for guitar cases that doesn't let them slide.

Dean tries to paint THE BLAKES on the side in enormous letters with a can of spray paint. Ellis catches him with the can shaken and the cap off.

**ELLIS:** Absolutely fucking not.

**DEAN:** Promotion.

**CAL:** The police don't need help identifying us.

**RILEY:** For once, Cal has the fun answer.

Nothing gets painted. (Yet.)

When they try the heater, the cab fills with a smell so bad Riley opens her door at twenty miles an hour. Tully takes the heater box apart in Marlon's lot and removes a mummified possum.

**TULLY:** Found your smell.

**DEAN:** Can we keep him?

**CAL:** No.

### First drive — the keys

Cal drives first. Obviously.

**DEAN:** I bought fifty percent.

**CAL:** You can sit fifty percent closer to the front.

**DEAN:** You're drunk with power.

Cal drives carefully for twenty minutes, east on the Tanner Valley road. They stop at a Pure Oil station for gas. Cal goes inside to pay.

Dean looks at Ellis. Ellis knows.

**ELLIS:** Don't.

**DEAN:** I'm moving it six feet. To the air hose.

**RILEY:** The air hose is behind us.

Dean starts the van. Cut to the van flying down a two-lane county road through pasture, Dean laughing, Ellis braced against the dashboard with both hands, Riley in the back hanging onto a rack.

**RILEY:** *SIX FEET?*

**DEAN:** *I UNDERESTIMATED!*

Stutter. Control transfers to Dean.

The player gets the first proper van-driving sequence: winding road, soft suspension, the whole van leaning like a boat, gear sliding in the back, the heater now possum-free and roaring.

**ELLIS:** *CAL'S GONNA FUCKING KILL YOU.*

**DEAN:** *ONLY IF WE GO BACK!*

They come around a blind bend. A black Angus cow is standing in the middle of the road. Dean brakes hard. Everything in the back slides forward. The van stops ten feet from the cow.

Silence. The cow looks at them.

**RILEY:** Give me the keys.

**DEAN:** Reasonable.

### Cal

They drive back to the Pure Oil. Cal is standing beside the gas pump with his hands in his jacket pockets. No expression at all.

Dean gets out and holds out the keys.

**DEAN:** Before you say anything—

**CAL:** Get in the back.

**DEAN:** Yep.

He does. Cal adjusts the mirror and drives them home at exactly the speed limit.

## MISSION 4 — THREE TOWNS

**Playable:** Cal → Riley → Ellis → rotating · **Where:** a roadhouse in the Laurel Gap; the Tanner Valley road; the Tanner Valley Motor Court; Tannersville College; the Blind Tiger, Laurel City; the Starlite, Laurel Gap · **When:** Friday Dec 13, 6 p.m. – Sunday Dec 15, 4 a.m.; Monday Dec 16, 4:10 a.m. (Dean) · **Length:** ~72 min

The 45 isn't back yet. But WTCR plays the tape every week now, and Martin's contact at the Blind Tiger wants them back, headlining a Saturday night. Riley's campus wants a band for the Dead Week dance, the Saturday before finals, in the afternoon. And a roadhouse in the Laurel Gap called the Holler House needs somebody Friday because its regular band is in jail (it's always jail).

Three shows in thirty hours. Their first miniature road run.

### Friday — loading (Cal)

Cal has made a checklist on a clipboard: every cable, every stand, every spare tube, fuse and string, a flashlight, a roll of electrical tape, a first-aid kit, the ledger.

Dean mocks the checklist at length. Thirty minutes later:

**DEAN:** Anybody seen my hi-hat clutch?

Cal pulls it out of a cigar box labeled *DEAN — SMALL PARTS* and hands it over without looking up.

**DEAN:** I love your lists.

**CAL:** Say it louder.

**DEAN:** Never.

### Road gameplay

The van becomes a moving social hub. Whoever isn't driving can, as the player moves through the cabin: change stations (the van's AM radio, plus a portable FM Tully brings); practice lyrics; smoke with the window cracked; sleep; talk; play cards on an amp case (Dean cheats at gin; Cal lets him, then wins anyway); read (Riley has three books); tune; argue about directions. Conversations begin around the player on their own; sometimes the player can join, and sometimes they just listen. The van starts to feel like home.

### The Holler House

A cinderblock roadhouse off US 19 with a gravel lot full of pickups, a bouncer named Big Wendell, chicken wire in front of nothing (it's just decoration now, Wendell says, mostly). The crowd is rough and not hostile, just uninterested. The Blakes earn them slowly over an hour of covers. Nothing special happens.

That's deliberate. Some gigs are just gigs.

### Load-out, rain

Rain, of course. Everybody carries equipment through mud. Riley's hair is soaked. Cal is furious because somebody set a speaker column directly on wet ground.

**DEAN:** Rock and roll.

**CAL:** Pneumonia.

**ELLIS:** Same thing.

### The van overheats

Thirty miles later, on the Tanner Valley road heading east toward Tannersville for the next day, the temperature needle climbs. Cal sees it before anybody. He pulls over at a wide spot. Steam from under the engine cover.

Midnight. The rain slowing. Nothing for miles in any direction but a barbed-wire fence and a field.

**DEAN:** I thought you inspected it.

**CAL:** I inspected a van sold to me by a man named Otis in a hollow for four hundred dollars.

**DEAN:** So what you're saying is—

**CAL:** Finish that sentence and you walk.

Dean doesn't finish the sentence.

### Roadside repair

A hands-on sequence where each of them helps differently.
- **Cal** diagnoses: the lower radiator hose has split along a seam.
- **Ellis** actually knows enough to assist, and the player (switching to Ellis) does the physical work: draining what's left, taking off the old hose with frozen fingers, wrapping the split with electrical tape as a stopgap while Cal explains exactly why it won't hold.
- **Riley** holds the flashlight, and asks what they're doing, and within ten minutes actually understands what a thermostat is for, and says so, and Cal is quietly impressed.
- **Dean** is initially useless. Then he disappears.

**CAL:** Where'd he go?

**ELLIS:** Probably toward civilization.

**RILEY:** There isn't any.

Twelve minutes later: headlights. An old Chevrolet pickup pulls up. Dean jumps out of the passenger side. Behind the wheel is a farmer in a hunting cap, Mr. Gentry, who has a length of radiator hose in his barn that's close enough, two jugs of water, a hose clamp, and a thermos of coffee his wife sent.

**DEAN** *(triumphant):* Who's useless now?

**CAL:** Still mostly you.

But Cal smiles, and he pours Mrs. Gentry's coffee for everybody before he takes any.

### The Tanner Valley Motor Court

They reach the cheapest motel for twenty miles at about two-thirty in the morning: eleven units in an L around a gravel lot, a neon VACANCY with the first V burned out, an ice machine that sounds like a small airplane.

Only two rooms are free. One has one bed. One has two.

**DEAN:** I call floor.

Everyone looks at him.

**DEAN:** What?

**CAL:** You volunteered for the floor?

**DEAN:** I can sleep anywhere.

**RILEY:** That's the most believable thing you've ever said.

They divide awkwardly. Cal and Dean in number 11, the one bed (Dean on the floor, as promised, where he falls asleep in forty seconds). Riley and Ellis in number 12, with two beds.

Nobody comments. Which makes it more awkward.

Tully sleeps in the van with the gear. He prefers it.

### Room 12 (Riley)

The game slows down. Stutter to Riley.

An ugly motel room. Floral bedspreads that don't match. Wood-grain paneling. A lamp with a shade scorched on one side. A Philco television bolted to the dresser. An air conditioner under the window that rattles though it's December and off.

Riley puts down her bag. Ellis looks at the beds.

**ELLIS:** I'll take that one.

**RILEY:** They're identical.

**ELLIS:** Then I've chosen well.

Neither of them knows why they're nervous. They've spent dozens of hours together. This is different.

She showers. (The game cuts away; it isn't that kind of scene.) She comes out in sweatpants and a Tannersville College t-shirt with her glasses on and her hair wet. Ellis is sitting in the chair by the window writing in his notebook.

**RILEY:** You're always doing that.

**ELLIS:** Doing what?

**RILEY:** Writing after shows.

**ELLIS:** When else would I?

**RILEY:** Normal people sleep.

**ELLIS:** Dean is snoring through a cinderblock wall and Cal is probably alphabetizing his socks.

**CAL** *(muffled, through the wall):* *I CAN HEAR YOU.*

Riley bursts out laughing.

**ELLIS:** See?

### Riley's studying

She pulls a textbook from her bag: *Philosophical Problems of Space and Time*. Her philosophy final is Monday at eight.

**ELLIS:** You've got to be kidding.

**RILEY:** Monday.

**ELLIS:** It's Saturday morning.

**RILEY:** Which leaves me Sunday.

She reads. He writes. Silence. Five minutes.

**RILEY:** Stop tapping.

He stops. Thirty seconds. Tap. Tap.

Riley looks at him.

**ELLIS:** Sorry.

She throws a pillow at him. He catches it and throws it back. It escalates into something stupid: both of them on their feet, pillows, a lamp nearly lost, laughing too loud at three in the morning. For thirty seconds they're children.

**CAL** *(through the wall):* *It's three in the morning.*

They collapse, laughing, on Riley's bed, because it's the closer one. Then quiet. They're sitting closer than before.

### Names

**ELLIS:** Is Riley your first name?

**RILEY:** Last.

**ELLIS:** What's your first?

**RILEY:** Guess.

**ELLIS:** Loretta.

**RILEY:** That's your guitar.

**ELLIS:** Worth a shot.

**RILEY:** Margaret.

**ELLIS:** Margaret.

**RILEY:** Nobody calls me that.

**ELLIS:** What's your mama call you?

**RILEY:** Maggie.

**ELLIS:** Maggie.

**RILEY:** Don't.

**ELLIS:** Okay.

**RILEY:** What's yours call you? Your mom?

**ELLIS:** Ohio.

Riley waits for the rest of the sentence. There isn't any. So she gives him hers.

**RILEY:** Seventh grade the softball coach called everybody by their last names and I decided I liked being somebody who does things.

**ELLIS:** Riley does things.

**RILEY:** Riley does things.

And he never calls her anything else, in the whole game.

> **Replay layer.** In his notebooks, poems about her are headed "M." She'll find that out after.

### Grace

**RILEY:** What was Grace like?

Ellis goes guarded. Riley sees it immediately.

**RILEY:** You don't have to—

**ELLIS:** Annoying.

Riley smiles a little.

**ELLIS:** Really annoying.

A pause.

**ELLIS:** Followed me everywhere.

**RILEY:** Younger?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**RILEY:** How much?

Ellis looks at the window.

**ELLIS:** Enough.

He still withholds her age. Riley lets him.

**ELLIS:** She'd steal my records. Didn't even like half of them. Just knew it made me mad.

A pause.

**ELLIS:** She'd sing along to them anyway. Harmony. On stuff that didn't have any. She'd just make one up.

Riley smiles. So does he.

**RILEY:** Sounds like a little sister.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

Then, almost by accident:

**ELLIS:** She'd have liked you.

Riley looks at him. He hears what he said.

**ELLIS:** Probably.

**RILEY:** I'd have liked her.

He can't answer.

> **Replay layer.** "She'd make one up." Riley's harmony on "No Name," in Chapter I, is a harmony somebody made up for him once before.

### Almost

The closeness shifts. She looks at him. He looks back. The player expects the kiss.

Ellis stands up.

**ELLIS:** I need some air.

She's disappointed and hides it.

**RILEY:** It's thirty-one degrees.

He goes. Ellis wants closeness, and when it becomes real enough to lose, he leaves.

### Switch — through the glass

The camera stays with Riley at first. She watches the door close. Then, through the window, she sees Ellis walking along the concrete walkway under the eave toward the ice machine. Stutter. The focus pulls through the glass. Control becomes Ellis.

### The ice machine

The ice machine is roaring in its alcove. Ellis stands in front of it without getting any ice. Clara is sitting on the walkway railing beside it, heels hooked on the lower rail.

**CLARA:** Smooth.

**ELLIS:** Go away.

**CLARA:** You ran away.

**ELLIS:** I came outside.

**CLARA:** From inside.

**ELLIS:** That's generally how outside works.

**CLARA:** You didn't get any ice.

**ELLIS:** I'm fixing to.

She smiles.

**CLARA:** You're scared.

**ELLIS:** Of Riley?

**CLARA:** Of liking Riley.

He doesn't answer.

**CLARA:** She isn't going anywhere tonight.

**ELLIS:** That's not what I'm worried about.

Clara looks at him. She understands. Of course she does.

> **Observe** (at the ice machine): *Dec 14. 3:40 a.m. The only thing awake in the Tanner Valley Motor Court is the ice machine and me and the girl in Room 12 pretending she's asleep.*
> (This line becomes the first verse of "Ice Machine." If the player doesn't collect it, Ellis writes it anyway in the notebook between missions.)

When he goes back in, Riley is asleep, or pretending. He lies down on the other bed in his clothes.

### Saturday afternoon — the Dead Week Dance

Tannersville College's student union ballroom, three in the afternoon, the Saturday before finals. Streamers. A punch bowl someone has already spiked. A few hundred students with one weekend left before exams and ready to be happy about anything.

It's daylight, and it's Riley's world. She shines here, and the game lets the player feel it: as Riley, her Room reads the crowd and she knows half of them by name. Hannah dances right in front of her. Russell and Pam and Deb from the oak tree. Martin Keller at the soundboard, because of course.

About halfway through the set, the player may notice Professor Landry at the back of the ballroom by the door, in his tweed jacket, holding a paper cup. He stays for one song ("Low Water," Riley's verse). When Riley looks up from her guitar he raises the cup an inch. When she looks up again he's gone.

Ellis sees that Riley belongs here, and something in him registers it: a life she has that doesn't need the band.

### Saturday night — the Blind Tiger, headlining

Laurel City again. The chalk sign on Tenth Street:

**SATURDAY — THE BLAKES**

No "with." Their name alone, for the first time. Dean takes a Polaroid and puts a matchbook in his pocket.

At the door, a man in a leather jacket stops Ellis.

**MAN:** The Blakes, right?

Ellis stops. Nobody has ever recognized the whole band outside the places they play every week.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**MAN:** You played here last month.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**MAN:** Good shit.

He walks off. Ellis stares after him.

**DEAN:** Famous.

**CAL:** One man.

**DEAN:** One fan at a time.

### Carla

Before the set, at the bar, a woman in a car coat with a beauty-shop smock underneath comes up to Ellis. She's nervous about it.

**CARLA:** I'm the one who wrote the letter.

**ELLIS:** The kitchen light.

**CARLA:** Who's the kitchen light for?

**ELLIS:** Somebody in Ohio.

She nods as if that's a whole answer. For a hairdresser it might be.

**CARLA:** Is there a record?

**ELLIS:** There's going to be. Next week.

She opens her purse and takes out a dollar bill, folds it once and holds it out.

**CARLA:** Save me one.

Ellis looks at the dollar. He takes it and puts it in the back of his notebook.

**ELLIS:** Yes ma'am.

> **Object.** The first preorder in the band's history is a dollar bill from a hairdresser, kept in the back of notebook 44.

### The show

The strongest crowd yet. Several people know "No Name" from the tape; one of them sings along badly on the chorus. The Echoplex section of "Low Water" doesn't exist live yet (they don't own an Echoplex), so the loud part is just the band getting very quiet and then very loud, and it works. Some people don't know what to do with it. Others are hypnotized.

Afterward, Artie Szabo, the booker:

**ARTIE:** That long weird one.

**ELLIS:** Yeah?

**ARTIE:** Do more of that.

Cal and Ellis exchange a look. They're finding out what they sound like by watching what makes a room go still.

### The afterparty

Dean finds one. Obviously. This time it finds them. Back at the Tanner Valley Motor Court (they paid for two nights), a few carloads from the Blind Tiger who were headed east anyway follow the van up the highway: musicians, students, a couple of strangers, two girls who were at the Dead Week Dance and drove over. They bring a guitar, beer, cocaine, weed, records, and an eight-track player somebody plugs into the ice-machine outlet. The party has followed The Blakes, instead of the other way around. People came back because of them.

Dean is the center of it within minutes. He's using more openly now, more than once and in front of people, though nobody is counting but Cal. This time Cal doesn't say anything in front of anyone.

### Riley and Ellis, outside

Riley is smoking a joint on the walkway outside Room 12 with the door half open behind her. Ellis comes out and leans on the railing beside her. They're both close to sober.

**RILEY:** You going to run away again?

**ELLIS:** Maybe.

**RILEY:** Good to know.

**ELLIS:** I wasn't—

**RILEY:** Relax.

She isn't angry.

**ELLIS:** I'm bad at that.

**RILEY:** I've noticed.

They stand there. Music muffled behind them. The gravel lot. Cold air. A pulpwood truck downshifting on the highway. No romantic score.

### The first kiss

**ELLIS:** What happens if this gets weird?

**RILEY:** This is already weird.

**ELLIS:** I mean the band.

Riley understands.

**RILEY:** Then we deal with it.

**ELLIS:** Cal will kill us.

**RILEY:** Cal wants a schedule. He'll adjust.

Ellis laughs. Riley puts the joint out on the railing, carefully, like it's a job.

**ELLIS:** What?

Riley kisses him, small and uncertain. She pulls back.

**ELLIS:** ...

**RILEY:** You think too much.

**ELLIS:** Nobody's ever accused me of that.

She kisses him again. This time he kisses her back.

The camera holds still and the score stays out of it: two young people who have wanted to do this for weeks and finally did, on a motel walkway next to an ice machine.

### Dean opens the door

The door to Room 11 flies open behind them.

**DEAN:** *OH, THANK GOD.*

Riley pulls away.

**ELLIS:** How long have you been at that door?

**DEAN:** I owe Tully five dollars.

**ELLIS:** I'm going to kill you.

From the dark of the van, parked in the lot, Tully's voice:

**TULLY:** Told you.

Dean slams the door. From inside:

**DEAN:** *WORTH IT!*

Riley is laughing. Ellis wants to die.

> **Design note.** Don't let the romance become precious. These are friends. The kiss and the five-dollar bet are the same scene on purpose.

### Four in the morning (Dean)

Monday, 4:10 a.m. The van dropped everybody off by Sunday supper. Dean got in the Chevelle and drove around the Gap until the only lit thing left was the Starlite.

Two truckers asleep sitting up. The pie case humming. Dean in a booth with coffee he hasn't touched, knee going, jaw tight.

Lynette sets a sheet of manila paper on the table on her way past. Crayon: a green box on four black circles, four stick people standing on its roof, a yellow sun with teeth.

**LYNETTE:** Bobby. I told him you bought a truck.

**DEAN:** Van.

**LYNETTE:** He doesn't care.

**DEAN:** Why are we on the roof?

**LYNETTE:** He says that's where a band goes.

**DEAN:** Which one's me?

**LYNETTE:** The one falling off.

Dean laughs, too loud for four in the morning. One of the truckers opens an eye.

**DEAN:** Can I keep it?

**LYNETTE:** It's going on my refrigerator. *(She takes it back and fills his cup.)* Drink that or go home.

He drinks it.

## MISSION 5 — MONDAY

**Playable:** Riley → Dean → Cal → Ellis (relay) · **Where:** across the map · **When:** Monday Dec 16 · **Length:** ~20 min

This mission shows the cost of momentum without a single catastrophe. It's a relay of short scenes joined by switches, four lives resuming after a weekend.

### Riley — 8:04 a.m.

Campus, running. The philosophy final started at eight. She enters the exam room four minutes late. Professor Landry looks at the clock, then at her, and says nothing, which is worse. She sits. Opens the blue book. Writes her name.

Then she remembers the take-home paper that's also due today, the one she finished Friday before the Holler House. It's in the nightstand drawer of Room 12 of the Tanner Valley Motor Court, twenty-two miles away.

She puts her forehead on the desk for one second. Then she sits up and writes the exam.

After, at the door, she tells Landry about the paper. He looks at her for a long moment.

**LANDRY:** Friday. Nine a.m. Not a minute later.

**RILEY:** Thank you.

**LANDRY:** Don't. You'll be writing it again from memory.

### Switch — Dean

A car horn outside the building. The camera moves through the window. Dean's Chevelle at the curb. Stutter.

Dean hasn't been home since Friday. He drives to Belle Grove, radio loud, sunglasses on in December. He pulls into the drive as his father is coming out the front door with his briefcase. Richard stops on the step.

**RICHARD:** What time did you leave Friday?

**DEAN:** Time's sort of subjective.

Richard comes down the steps and looks at his son's face. At his eyes.

**RICHARD:** Are you high?

Dean's smile goes.

**DEAN:** Good morning to you too.

Richard sets his briefcase down on the step, which he never does.

**DEAN:** I'm twenty.

**RICHARD:** You're living in my house.

**DEAN:** There it is.

**RICHARD:** What?

**DEAN:** The invoice.

He walks past his father into the house. Richard stands in the driveway with his briefcase. He is genuinely frightened. Dean hears only control.

### Switch — Cal

Dean goes inside. The camera stays in the driveway. A delivery truck passes on the street and fills the frame. Cut, stutter: Vale Music.

Cal opens the shop exactly on time. He looks awful: three hours' sleep on a motel floor after Dean took the bed back at five.

**VALE:** Good weekend?

**CAL:** No.

**VALE:** Band good?

Cal pauses.

**CAL:** Yeah.

**VALE:** Then good weekend.

Cal thinks about this and realizes it's correct.

### Switch — Ellis

The bell over Vale's door. Ellis, in coveralls; Roy sent him east with parts again.

**CAL:** You're not on the schedule.

**ELLIS:** I don't work here.

**CAL:** That's why it's strange.

**ELLIS:** I need strings.

Cal gets them. Black Diamond mediums. Ellis puts three-fifty on the counter. Cal counts it, pushes a dollar back.

**ELLIS:** Discount?

**CAL:** Band rate.

**ELLIS:** We have a band rate?

**CAL:** We do now.

Ellis leaves. The camera follows him out.

### Wayne

Ellis gets home at six. Wayne has Friday's *Banner* open on the kitchen table to the weekend listings: a column of tiny type, and one line circled in pencil.

> *TANNERSVILLE COLLEGE — Dead Week Dance, Student Union, Sat 3 pm, The Blakes (Hollow Ridge)*

Wayne holds it up.

**WAYNE:** That you?

**ELLIS:** Allegedly.

**WAYNE:** Thought you hated the name.

**ELLIS:** I do.

**WAYNE:** Then change it.

**ELLIS:** Little late.

**WAYNE:** Never too late to stop doing something stupid.

**ELLIS:** We played three places this weekend.

**WAYNE:** Congratulations.

**ELLIS:** You don't mean that.

**WAYNE:** No. I don't.

Ellis looks at him.

**WAYNE:** You worked Saturday?

**ELLIS:** Roy switched me.

**WAYNE:** So no.

**ELLIS:** I switched with Dale.

**WAYNE:** This is how it starts.

**ELLIS:** How what starts?

**WAYNE:** You deciding everything else matters less than whatever's in your head that week.

**ELLIS:** It's not a week anymore.

**WAYNE:** That supposed to make me feel better?

**ELLIS:** No.

**WAYNE:** Good.

Ellis turns to go. Wayne, to the newspaper, almost to himself:

**WAYNE:** Your mother used to say that.

Ellis stops. It's rare for Wayne to mention his mother at all. Wayne realizes he's opened something.

**ELLIS:** Say what?

**WAYNE:** Hm.

**ELLIS:** No. You said it. Say what?

**WAYNE:** Go wash up.

**ELLIS:** Dad.

Wayne slams his palm on the table.

**WAYNE:** *I said go wash up.*

Ellis stares at him. Then goes. The water in the bathroom runs a long time.

> **Replay layer.** "It's not a phase, Wayne. It's not a week." Lorraine said it about her singing, the year before she left.

## MISSION 6 — THE GIRL NOBODY KNOWS

**Playable:** Cal → Ellis · **Where:** Marlon's, afternoon; the alley behind it · **When:** Tuesday Dec 17 · **Length:** ~20 min

Marlon's, 2 p.m., closed. Dean has a dentist appointment his mother made. Riley's rewriting her paper. Cal and Ellis are working alone at a table by the stage with a bass and Loretta. They've become surprisingly comfortable doing this.

### The song

Ellis has a new lyric (the motel song, "Ice Machine") and plays Cal the verse.

**CAL:** Who's "you"?

**ELLIS:** Nobody.

**CAL:** "You, pretending." There's a "you" in every line.

**ELLIS:** It's a song.

**CAL:** Usually about something.

**ELLIS:** Not always.

**CAL:** Bullshit.

Ellis looks at him.

**ELLIS:** You steal that from me?

**CAL:** Improved it.

### Clara

Ellis tunes the B string.

**ELLIS:** Clara liked the verse.

Cal stops tuning.

**CAL:** Clara who?

**ELLIS:** Just Clara.

**CAL:** The girl from Marlon's.

**ELLIS:** You remember.

**CAL:** No. I remember you saying her name.

**ELLIS:** Right.

**CAL:** Which one was she?

Ellis describes her casually: dark hair to here, a jean jacket, always sits with her feet up on something, *she was by the cigarette machine at the Thanksgiving show.*

Cal listens. He can't place her. He's good at remembering who's around a stage; he watches crowds the way he watches meters.

**CAL:** Was she at the Blind Tiger?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CAL:** In November?

**ELLIS:** Both times.

Cal thinks.

**CAL:** Huh.

**ELLIS:** What?

**CAL:** Which side of the cigarette machine?

**ELLIS:** You've got a weird face.

**CAL:** You say that constantly.

They go back to the song. No ominous cue.

### Riley arrives

Riley comes in around three with her paper done in longhand and a question about the bridge. Ellis goes across the street to the Gulf station for food.

Cal and Riley alone.

**CAL:** Who's Clara?

**RILEY:** I don't know.

**CAL:** You've never met her?

**RILEY:** No.

**CAL:** Ellis talks about her like she's always around.

**RILEY:** Friend from Hollow Ridge?

**CAL:** Apparently.

**RILEY:** Why?

**CAL:** You see anybody by the cigarette machine at Thanksgiving?

**RILEY:** Dean. Twice.

Riley studies him.

**RILEY:** You're doing your thing.

**CAL:** What thing?

**RILEY:** The face.

**CAL:** Apparently everybody hates my face.

Riley smiles. The conversation ends. Cal files it, and goes back to tuning the bass.

### Behind Marlon's (switch to Ellis)

Riley goes to the stage. Cal watches the door. The camera finds, through the storeroom window, Ellis sitting on the back steps. Stutter. Ellis.

He's eating fries out of a paper boat from the Gulf station's grill. Broad daylight. Half past three. No drugs, no alcohol. He slept last night. He worked all morning.

Clara is sitting beside him on the step. Completely normal.

**CLARA:** Cal doesn't like me.

**ELLIS:** Cal doesn't like anybody.

**CLARA:** He likes you.

**ELLIS:** Debatable.

Clara reaches over and takes a fry. (The camera frames it from behind her shoulder, her hand going into the paper boat. We never see the boat afterward.)

**CLARA:** You and Riley kissed.

Ellis stops chewing.

**ELLIS:** How the hell do you know that?

Clara smiles.

**ELLIS:** Seriously.

**CLARA:** You look different.

**ELLIS:** No I don't.

**CLARA:** You've been smiling at a French fry for five minutes.

He shakes his head. On a first playthrough: she knows him well. On replay: Ellis knows himself.

### Marlon

The back door opens. Marlon, in his shirtsleeves, with a case of empties.

**MARLON:** You coming back in, or are you and the fries eloping?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

Marlon has the door propped with his hip and his eyes on the case of empties. From where he stands, the door hides the far end of the step.

**MARLON:** Who're you talking to?

The question lands. Ellis doesn't panic.

**ELLIS:** Myself.

**MARLON:** Bad company.

**ELLIS:** I'll take that under advisement.

Marlon goes back in with the empties. Clara is still sitting there, grinning.

**CLARA:** He's funny.

**ELLIS:** Sometimes.

Nothing in Ellis's reaction suggests he thinks Clara isn't physically there. The player can rationalize it: she was sitting on the far side, the door hides her from Marlon's angle, he only heard Ellis talking. The game preserves the ambiguity.

## MISSION 7 — DEMAND, AGAIN

**Playable:** Dean → Cal · **Where:** Tanner Custom Records; Vale Music; Tenth Street, Laurel City; the Row, Tannersville · **When:** Monday Dec 16 → Wednesday Dec 18 · **Length:** ~20 min

### The records

Cal picks up two cardboard boxes of 250 each from the pressing plant in the Dart. At Vale's, in the back room, all four of them (and Tully, and Mr. Vale in the doorway) watch Cal cut the tape on the first box with his pocketknife.

Five hundred records in white sleeves stamped THE BLAKES, slightly crooked on the ones Dean did. Black vinyl. White labels:

> **THE BLAKES — NO NAME** (E. Blake)

Nobody says anything. Then Mr. Vale holds out his hand for one, takes it to the store turntable out front, and drops the needle. The whole band follows him out and stands in the aisle between the guitars while it plays through Vale's display speakers. Two customers stop browsing.

It's a record. Their record.

Ellis takes one copy and a sleeve and walks out without saying anything. (He mails it that afternoon at the Tannersville post office to Carla Vickery in Laurel City, with a note in carpenter's pencil: *You were first. Paid in full. —E.B.* The player sees him do it and can read the note. He keeps the dollar.)

Marlon gets the first one off the stack. Ellis drives it to Hollow Ridge himself.

### Tenth Street (Dean)

Dean takes ten copies to Laurel City. Stutter to Dean.

**Tenth Street Records** is a narrow shop with bins made of fruit crates, a hand-lettered LOCAL box that holds two copies of somebody's poetry album, and an owner named Mitch with a gray ponytail and a Georgia Bulldogs cap.

**MITCH:** Nobody buys local records.

**DEAN:** Nobody's had ours.

**MITCH:** You always like this?

**DEAN:** I'm usually worse.

Mitch takes five, on consignment, a dollar each to the band. Dean tries to leave ten. Mitch hands five back.

**MITCH:** Five.

**DEAN:** You'll call.

**MITCH:** I will not call.

### The Row (Cal)

Cal takes five copies to the record shop on the Row in Tannersville, the one every session player uses, where Cal buys all his records. The owner, who has known Cal for five years, puts the needle down on "No Name," listens to thirty seconds, lifts it.

**OWNER:** Cal, honey, we don't carry that kind of thing.

**CAL:** What kind of thing?

**OWNER:** That kind.

Cal takes his five records back. The Row has made its position clear. Laurel City will embrace The Blakes long before Tannersville does.

> **Design note — the camouflage.** Ellis is sober, at home, in daylight, after an ordinary day, and Clara is fully present until Wayne's boots hit the porch. No drugs. No exhaustion. She clears out before he comes in, the way any friend of Ellis's who doesn't get along with his father would, so nothing here looks like evidence. "You on something?" looks like Wayne being an asshole. That's exactly what the scene should do. On replay it removes the easy explanation: the drugs didn't make Clara.

## MISSION 10 — SOMETHING GOOD

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** Tannersville College · **When:** Saturday Dec 21, 11:45 p.m. – 2:30 a.m. · **Length:** ~20 min

Night. The college is emptying for Christmas; most of the dorm has gone home. Hannah left this morning for Macon. Riley is at her desk, finally finishing the paper due at Landry's office Monday (he extended it once more, sighing), with Gerald the spider plant for company.

The hall phone rings. Nobody else is left to answer it.

**RILEY:** Hello?

**ELLIS** *(on the phone; background noise: wind, a truck going by)*: It's me.

**RILEY:** It's midnight.

**ELLIS:** I know.

**RILEY:** I'm writing.

**ELLIS:** I know.

**RILEY:** Then why—

**ELLIS:** Can I come over?

He sounds like he's been standing at that pay phone a while.

**RILEY:** Yes.

### The practice room

He's there in forty minutes (the Valiant, the pay phone at the Hollow Ridge Gulf station, the Tanner Valley road at night in December; the player doesn't drive this, it's a cut). Sober, and hurt.

Visitation hours ended at eleven. Riley takes him to a practice room in the music building instead, one of the little cells with an upright piano and a window, because she's a music minor and has a key.

Ellis sits down on the piano bench and picks up her Martin and plays. Wrong. He stops.

**RILEY:** What happened?

He doesn't answer. Riley waits.

**ELLIS:** Nothing happened. That's what happens. Nothing, all day, every day, and then—

He stops. Riley stays quiet. The player, as Riley, has the option to ask or wait. Waiting is the right choice and the game doesn't say so; if the player asks, Ellis shuts down and the scene becomes smaller (he says only "I was there" and leaves at two). If the player waits:

**ELLIS:** Grace died in a car wreck.

This is the first new truth in a month.

**RILEY:** Okay.

No *oh my God*, and no pity on her face. He's grateful for that.

**ELLIS:** I was there.

Riley waits. Ellis can't go further. She nods, once.

Ellis looks at her.

**ELLIS:** That's all you're gonna say?

**RILEY:** Do you want me to ask?

**ELLIS:** No.

**RILEY:** Then no.

He looks down. She isn't going to pry it out of him to make herself feel better. He didn't know anybody did that.

**ELLIS:** Wayne thinks I'm forgetting her.

**RILEY:** Are you?

He almost laughs.

**ELLIS:** No.

**RILEY:** Then Wayne's wrong.

**ELLIS:** He's not wrong about everything.

**RILEY:** Nobody is.

> **Parallel, unmarked.** Riley consoles by argument ("Are you?" "Then Wayne's wrong."). Clara consoles with orders. They increasingly fill the same place in Ellis's life, one outside him and one inside. That will matter.

### They play

Riley sits down at the piano. Ellis takes the guitar. Quiet, more a conversation than a song. The player controls Riley's voicings; Ellis follows. Eventually it turns into the loud part of "Low Water," played very softly: Riley holding one chord with the sustain pedal down, Ellis letting the guitar ring against the piano's strings until the room hums with it.

They don't need to talk. Ellis does anyway, to the guitar.

**ELLIS:** I keep waiting on somebody to send a bill.

**RILEY:** For what?

His eyes go around the little room: the piano, her Martin in his hands, her.

**RILEY:** Name one thing you've earned. I'll wait.

He tries. He can't. The piano is still humming under her foot, one long chord with nowhere to go.

**RILEY:** You eat today?

### No "I love you"

Don't rush it. Riley takes his hand. Ellis stays. She has a sleeve of saltines in her coat pocket, and they split it on the piano bench.

He drives home at two-thirty. The player drives this one: the Tanner Valley road west in the dark, the Church of God marquee (MERRY CHRISTMAS — JESUS IS THE REASON), the switchbacks, Hollow Ridge's Christmas lights still on over Main Street for no one. No Clara in the car. The radio off.

## MISSION 11 — LEDGER

**Playable:** Cal → Ellis · **Where:** Vale Music; Hollow Ridge Auto & Service · **When:** Sunday Dec 22, night; Monday Dec 23, morning · **Length:** ~10 min

### Cal

Vale Music, closed, Sunday night. Cal at the counter under one lamp with the green ledger open, bringing it up to date. The player enters the lines: each envelope, receipt and consignment slip on the counter goes into its column in Cal's hand, and the page won't total until every figure is right.

> *12/19/74 — The Lantern, Laurel City — $100.00. Band fund $20.00. Shares $20.00 x4.*
> *12/20/74 — Tenth Street Records — 25 x 45s consigned @ $1.00. Paid $10.00 (10 sold).*
> *12/20/74 — Marlon's — Friday. $200.00. Toward Marlon loan $50.00 (bal. $250.00).*

Below, the player writes:

> *NEXT: Chattanooga — Feb 7.*

Then there is nothing left to enter. Whenever the player stops, Cal's pen taps twice on the page, the way it does before he speaks, and moves to the margin by itself, and writes in small letters:

> *Clara?*

He looks at it. He puts one line through it. He hasn't solved anything. He feels ridiculous.

He closes the book.

> **Object — the ledger margin.** Cal's *Clara?* with one line through it is one of the smallest details in the game and one of the most important. It comes back after Ellis dies.

### Ellis

Monday morning, Dec 23. Hollow Ridge Auto & Service. Ellis is under a Chevrolet on the creeper.

Roy bought a radio for the garage after Evelyn took hers back, a Panasonic AM/FM, and stands it in the office window, where it can just pull in WLSU 88.5, the state university's student station in Laurel City, a real signal, thousands of watts, that reaches all the way up the Gap on a clear morning.

**DJ** *(radio):* —and somebody left this at the station on Friday with a note that says *play this or else*, which is not how we usually take requests, but I'm going to allow it. The Blakes, out of Hollow Ridge. It's called "No Name."

From under the car, only Ellis's boots are visible. They stop moving.

Roy walks over to the window and turns the radio up without saying anything and goes back to the Buick.

Under the Chevrolet, in the dark, with oil dripping past his face, Ellis is smiling.

Cut to black.

## Chapter IV — design summary

### What changed
The Blakes are no longer only a Hollow Ridge bar band. They have a recording, a 45 in two cities, a van they own in eighths, a sound nobody has a name for, a promoter's phone number, and six dates. The most important change: every member has now given something up for the band.

### The characters
**Ellis.** The future has started competing with the past. He wants the dates, and Laurel City, and Riley, and people hearing the music. Wayne sees him moving forward and hears forgetting. So Ellis gets two messages at once. The band says *come with us.* Wayne says *how dare you leave her.* And Clara offers the compromise his mind came up with: *take me with you.* He promised. Asked about his mother, he gives the answer his father gave him: Ohio.

**Riley.** She kissed him first. She's still finishing papers at midnight. She saw a woman who makes a living in a folk trio and it frightened her. Landry told her she's allowed more than one ambition and went back to his grading; she's still trying to keep two.

**Cal.** Recording gear made his ears an asset. He met a man at a soundboard who knew what he'd ask for before he asked. He has two anomalies now (a girl nobody can place, and the note he missed at the Lantern watching Ellis smile at an empty mezzanine) and a word in a margin with a line through it.

**Dean.** The world has started rewarding his worst habit. At the rooftop party he found people who do what he does and function. He chose the band over dinner at the club and felt wonderful. His father heard a joke and laughed at him; Dean heard the invoice. He totaled the chili-dog tab in his head and called it a lucky guess. At four in the morning, the only person who could make him drink his coffee was Lynette, and the only picture of the band anybody has drawn yet is Bobby's, with Dean falling off the roof.

**Wayne.** "She know?" Then, softer: "You want me to tell her?" He found the only thing that frightens his son more than him. Then he waited up. It was Thursday.
