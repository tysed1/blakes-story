# CHAPTER VII — STRANGERS KNOW YOUR NAME

> **Hollow Ridge · Laurel City · Tannersville · the Southeast · New York City · Monday, September 8 – Saturday, December 13, 1975**
> Main-story runtime: ~7 hours · Playable: Ellis Blake, Riley, Cal Mercer, Dean Holloway, in rotation

## Chapter thesis

Everyone loses ownership of something. The record goes out into the world and belongs to whoever buys it. A song made from a private conversation becomes something sixteen hundred strangers sing. A magazine decides who Ellis Blake is and prints the worst day of his life as a biography. Riley's song is credited to Ellis. Cal's sentence about the band is used to prove it's Ellis's band. Dean's father takes back the car, the allowance and the pretense. By December the four of them are famous in a way that has nothing to do with what they are to each other, and the first thing they break is a promise they made in a van.

The player spends most of this chapter enjoying it. That's the point. Fame in this game has to be fun before it costs anything, or the cost means nothing.

## World state at open

- *Borrowed Stone* is pressed and a week from release. Southern Star has ordered 5,000 copies, which Harold Vance calls "optimistic" and his boss calls "the tax shelter."
- The Blakes are known in Laurel City, a curiosity in Tannersville, and a subject of opinion in Hollow Ridge.
- Ellis still works at Roy's four days a week. Riley has moved out of the dorm into a second-floor apartment on Cutler Street, near the college, with Gerald and a mattress on the floor. Cal lives above the dry cleaner. Dean lives at home in Belle Grove, allegedly.
- Autumn 1975: Ford survives two assassination attempts in September. The Reds and the Red Sox in October. "Fame" and "Rhinestone Cowboy" on the radio; *Wish You Were Here* in college dorms; CB radio in every truck at the Starlite; *One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest* opens in November.

## COLD OPEN

The Bowery, New York City. A Saturday afternoon in December. The light is going. It has just started to snow, the dry kind that doesn't stick.

A brick wall layered with torn posters: a boxing card, a Bicentennial ad already peeling. Four people stand against it in a row, and a man with two cameras around his neck is walking backward into the street to get them all in.

They're cold. Riley has her father's Navy peacoat buttoned to the throat and her glasses on. Cal is in a borrowed overcoat that's too short in the sleeves, over a pressed shirt. Dean is in a satin baseball jacket over a Hawaiian shirt, bouncing on his toes. Ellis is in a black leather jacket that still smells like somebody else.

**PHOTOGRAPHER** *(Denny Ostrow, Rave's staff photographer, forties, a Bronx accent and no patience)*: Closer. Closer. You're a band, not a bus stop.

They shuffle together.

**PHOTOGRAPHER:** Don't smile.

Dean smiles.

**PHOTOGRAPHER:** The drummer can smile. Nobody else.

**DEAN:** Thank you.

**PHOTOGRAPHER:** It wasn't a compliment.

A cab honks. Somebody on the sidewalk says *Is that anybody?* and somebody else says *No.*

The player is Ellis, and the only input is the camera: where Ellis looks. The lens. The snow. Riley. The street.

Across the Bowery, by a newsstand with its shutters half down, someone in a faded jean jacket is standing in the snow without a coat, watching.

**PHOTOGRAPHER:** Hold it.

Before the shutter fires, the frame freezes and drains to black.

**STRANGERS KNOW YOUR NAME**

> **Design note.** The cold open is the end of the chapter, played three months early and interrupted. Mission 19 plays it through, and where the player has Ellis look becomes the photograph.

## MISSION 1 — NINETEEN

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** the Blake house; Marlon's Tavern and the alley behind it · **When:** Monday September 8 – Sunday September 14 · **Length:** ~35 min

### The box

Monday, late afternoon. Ellis comes home from Roy's with grease to the elbows. On the kitchen table, next to the bills pile, there's a carton from Southern Star Records addressed to ELLIS BLAKE, COLD BRANCH RD, HOLLOW RIDGE GA. The delivery slip is signed *W. Blake*, in the square railroad hand from the back of the coupon book.

Wayne is in his chair in the front room with the television on low.

**WAYNE:** Came for you.

**ELLIS:** I see that.

**WAYNE:** Man in a brown truck. Wanted me to sign.

**ELLIS:** You signed.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

Ellis carries it down the hall, past Grace's closed door, and opens it on his bed: ten copies of *Borrowed Stone*, shrink-wrapped, in shredded newspaper.

The cover is a black-and-white photograph: the 1962 Jazzmaster, no strap, leaning on an unpolished block of gray granite in a yard full of them, the fog not burned off yet. Vance's art man took it at seven in the morning in the yard of Hollow Ridge Monument & Vault, across from the cemetery gate, because Ellis said that was where it should be and wouldn't say why.

On the back, under the songs and the credits and a column of thanks that ends with Tater, in Ellis's handwriting, because he wrote it on the paste-up at the last minute and Vance let it go:

> *for G.*

Then a prompt: Ellis can take one copy back down the hall. If the player does, he sets it on the kitchen table, on the bills pile, on the coupon book, and goes to bed. In the morning it's gone. Nobody mentions it. (In Chapter IX it's behind the seat of Wayne's truck, still in its shrink-wrap.)

> **Replay layer.** The monument yard is where Wayne picked out Grace's stone in April 1973 and signed for forty-eight payments. "For G." is on the back of every copy of *Borrowed Stone* ever pressed.

### Sunday

Sunday, September 14, 1975. Ellis's nineteenth birthday. The HUD says SUNDAY and nothing else, and nobody in the Blake house mentions it. There's a biscuit under a paper towel on the stove, which is either for Ellis or for Tater, and has always been ambiguous.

Overnight someone has added a line to the town-limit sign on US 19 in white house paint, not very well:

> HOLLOW RIDGE · POP. 2,100
> *HOME OF THE BLAKES*

(Dean and Tully, at 3 a.m. For the rest of the game the county paints it out and somebody paints it back.)

Dean called last night about a rehearsal at Marlon's, closed or not. ("We're professionals now, Blake. It's in the contract.") At two o'clock Ellis goes in the back door with his guitar.

### Surprise

Dark inside. Then the lights, and about thirty people shout it: the band, Tully, Marlon, Roy and Evelyn, Wesley Tate, Patty Holloway, Opal Hensley at her corner of the bar on a Sunday, and a banner across the stage, HAPPY 19TH BLAKE, in Dean's writing, the letters getting smaller toward the end because he ran out of banner.

Ellis turns around and walks back out the door. The player can't stop him.

### Negotiation

The gravel lot. Dean comes out after him at a run.

**DEAN:** Where are you going?

**ELLIS:** Home.

**DEAN:** It's your birthday.

**ELLIS:** I told you I didn't want one.

**DEAN:** Wesley Tate made a *shirt*, Blake.

That stops him. The player can push hard or give in. Every version ends in a deal:

**ELLIS:** One hour.

**DEAN:** Three.

**ELLIS:** One.

**DEAN:** Two, and you have to eat cake.

**ELLIS:** One hour, I eat cake, and I don't make a speech.

**DEAN:** Deal.

They shake. Dean doesn't let go.

**DEAN:** Happy birthday.

**ELLIS:** Let go of my hand.

### The party

One hour of free movement inside Marlon's, with a clock on the wall. Everyone has something.

- **The cake**: Piggly Wiggly white sheet cake, HAPPY BIRTHDAY ELIS. Donna Kay Carson (Sisk that was, Ellis's high-school girlfriend, now on the register) took the order. *"She knows how to spell it."* / *"Allegedly."* Ellis eats a piece, as agreed, while Marlon gives away beer, which the county allows on a Sunday if nobody pays for it.
- **Wesley Tate**, twelve, in a white T-shirt with THE BLAKES ironed on in black felt letters, the second E drawn in marker because the Western Auto ran out of E's. If the player talks to him, Ellis says it's the best shirt he's ever seen, and means it, and Wesley goes red to the ears.
- **Patty Holloway**, sixteen, at the *Fireball* machine. Ellis can show her the kickback trick on the left outlane; she beats him on the third ball, and he demands a rematch with complete seriousness. (Patty is the age Grace would be.)
- **Cal**: a twenty-foot cable he built himself, white tape on each end: ELLIS · 20 FT · DO NOT LEND TO DEAN.
- **Tully**: "I'm here. That's the present."
- **Deputy Lyle Pruitt** comes in the front door in uniform at the forty-minute mark. The room goes quiet. He looks at the free beer, and at Marlon. "Happy birthday, Ellis," he says, takes a piece of cake on a napkin, and leaves.

### Riley

In the back hallway, where it's quieter, Riley hands him a package in brown paper: a notebook bound in dark green leather, the pages sewn, from a stationer on the Row, nothing like the Western Auto memo books he buys three for a dollar. It smells like a shoe store.

**ELLIS:** Thank you. It's too nice.

**RILEY:** It's paper, Ellis.

**ELLIS:** I'll mess it up.

**RILEY:** That's what it's for.

There's a place on the cover where initials could have been stamped. They weren't. He doesn't open it. He puts it inside his jacket, against his chest, and it's too big for the pocket and he leaves it there anyway.

> **Setup.** Ellis doesn't write in the green notebook for nine months. In Chapter IX he starts, and the poems in it are addressed to "M." Riley finds it after he's gone.

### The speech

At fifty-nine minutes Dean climbs on a chair and clinks a beer bottle with a pen.

**DEAN:** Speech!

**ELLIS:** We had a deal.

**DEAN:** The deal expires in one minute, at which point you're a free man and I'm a liar.

The room chants. The player can refuse, and Ellis walks out the back door to applause, or stand him on the plywood stage where he played every Thursday for six months:

**ELLIS:** Thank y'all for coming to this thing I didn't want.

Laughter.

**ELLIS:** Donna Kay can't spell.

Donna Kay throws a napkin at him. He stands there a second longer than he means to, looking at the chalkboard by the door, at Wesley's shirt, at Riley.

**ELLIS:** Okay.

He gets down.

### The alley

Nine o'clock. Ellis goes out back with a last piece of cake on a napkin, and Clara is on the crate by the rail line, the same crate, with her feet up on a cable spool.

**CLARA:** You walked out of your own party.

**ELLIS:** I walked back in.

She holds out her hand.

**CLARA:** Give it here.

He gives her the cake. She holds it on her knee on its napkin. (She never eats anything.)

**CLARA:** Nineteen.

**ELLIS:** Yep.

**CLARA:** You look the same.

**ELLIS:** When's yours?

She looks at him.

**CLARA:** My what?

**ELLIS:** Birthday. You never said.

**CLARA:** I don't have one.

**ELLIS:** Everybody has one.

**CLARA:** Pick one, then.

He thinks about it. The player can't choose for him. He says it as if it's the first month that came into his head:

**ELLIS:** November.

**CLARA:** November's fine.

A freight comes through on the main line thirty feet away, and they stop talking and watch it the way they always do, and when it's gone the napkin on her knee is empty and the cake is on the crate beside her where Ellis must have set it down.

He drives home the long way, with the green notebook inside his jacket.

> **Replay layer.** Grace Clara Blake was born November 2, 1958. In Chapter IX Ellis writes "New Skin": *I gave you a birthday every year you didn't get one.*

## MISSION 2 — RELEASE DAY

**Playable:** Dean → Riley → Cal → Ellis · **Where:** Tenth Street Records, Laurel City · **When:** Monday September 15, 10 a.m. – 6 p.m. · **Length:** ~25 min

### The plan

Dean's idea: go to Tenth Street on release day and watch somebody buy it. Cal came to make sure Dean didn't do anything insane. Roy gave Ellis the day: "It's your record, go on." Mitch has twenty-five copies in the NEW bin by the register and a card in the window: LOCAL · HEARD 'EM FIRST.

**MITCH:** You people can't stand in my store all day.

**DEAN:** We'll take shifts.

Four two-hour shifts, the switch passing between them at the door. Nobody buys the record for a very long time.

### Dean (10:00 – 12:00)

Dean re-files *Borrowed Stone* in front of *Fleetwood Mac*, then on top of the Eagles in the window. Mitch moves it back each time. The player can talk to every customer, and it goes nowhere. Two girls from the university want to know if he's anybody. ("I'm the drummer." / "Of what?" / "That's a great question.") A man flips past *Borrowed Stone* without stopping.

### Riley (12:00 – 2:00)

Riley in the classical bins, where nobody under forty goes, holding Bach chorale preludes played on a church organ in Leipzig. The player can buy it ($3.49, for her mother) or put it back. A woman reads the back of *Borrowed Stone* all the way through and puts it down. Riley watches her with her heart going.

**RILEY** *(to Mitch)*: She read the whole back.

**MITCH:** She liked the picture.

### Cal (2:00 – 4:00)

Cal fixes the listening-station headphones on the counter with a pocketknife and electrical tape while Mitch watches with his arms folded.

**MITCH:** That's been broke three years.

**CAL:** Your cord's shot at the strain relief.

**MITCH:** I don't know what that means.

**CAL:** It means it's been broke three years.

He notes the stock in the ledger without being asked (*9/15/75 — Tenth St., 25 on hand at open*) and looks at the NEW bin every time the door opens.

### Ellis (4:00 – 6:00)

The light's going orange on Tenth Street. The player is Ellis, waiting for a stranger to want something he made.

At 5:40 a woman in a white nurse's uniform under a raincoat goes straight to the NEW bin, takes *Borrowed Stone* without looking at anything else, and carries it to the register. Ellis stops breathing. So does the band, who have all come back and are hiding behind the soul section, badly.

**MITCH:** Four ninety-eight.

She pays. Dean can't stand it. He comes out from behind the bin.

**DEAN:** Excuse me, ma'am. Can I ask why you bought that one?

**NURSE:** My son asked for it.

**DEAN:** Where'd he hear it?

**NURSE:** College station. He's in the hospital with his leg. He broke it on a motorbike, he's fine, he's driving me up the wall. He had me write it down.

She shows them the scrap of paper in her pocketbook: *BLAKES — BORROWED STONE — ALBUM NOT THE 45*. Then she looks at the four of them, and at the cover in the bag.

**NURSE:** Oh. You're them.

**ELLIS:** Allegedly.

**NURSE:** Would you sign it for him? He'll die.

Ellis signs it with Cal's Parker Jotter; the player chooses the words (default: *To Marcus · Stay off the motorbike · Ellis Blake*). Dean adds a drawing of a drum, Riley signs small in the corner, Cal his full name.

**NURSE:** Is it any good?

**DEAN:** It's the best record ever made.

**ELLIS:** Some of it.

She laughs and leaves. The bell over the door rings. Nobody says anything for a while.

**CAL:** Twenty-four on hand.

### G

In the van, Dean is reading the back cover for the fortieth time.

**DEAN:** "For G." Who's G?

**ELLIS:** Georgia.

**DEAN:** You dedicated the record to the state of Georgia.

**ELLIS:** It's a good state.

**DEAN:** That's the most sentimental thing you've ever done.

**ELLIS:** Don't tell anybody.

Riley, in the passenger seat, doesn't turn around.

> **Knowledge.** All four of them raised a glass to Grace at Marlon's in May (Chapter V). Dean knows exactly who G is. He asked so Ellis could say "Georgia," and he let him have it.

## MISSION 3 — REGISTRATION

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** Tannersville College; the Riley house, Linwood · **When:** Friday September 19; Sunday September 21 · **Length:** ~30 min

### The form

Friday, the last day of late registration. Riley's Datsun in the college lot. On the passenger seat: a registration form, a dog-eared course catalogue, and Eddie Farris's mimeographed itinerary, fifteen fall dates, most of them Thursday through Sunday, three in other states.

The player fills out the form with her: choosing courses, checking times, writing in section numbers. Twelve to eighteen hours.

- PHIL 412 · Mind and Self II · Landry · TTh 10:00
- MUS 302 · Counterpoint · MWF 9:00
- GER 201 · Intermediate German · MWF 11:00 (for reading, she tells herself)
- ASTR 110 · The Solar System · Tu 7:00 p.m. (the constellations; she's always wanted to)

Counterpoint is the one she wants. She has written every harmony the band sings by ear, and she'd like to know, once, why the ones that work work. She presses harder on that section number than on the others.

Whatever the player picks, it doesn't fit Eddie's itinerary. Thursday nights in Macon. A Friday in Columbia. The week of November 10 in Virginia.

She fills it out anyway, all of it, in pen, and signs it: *Margaret A. Riley.*

### The gym

Tables by alphabet under hand-lettered signs, lines snaking between the bleachers, a student worker with a bullhorn who doesn't need one. A girl from her hall says, "Aren't you supposed to be famous?" Hannah, across the gym in a line of education majors, waves. Riley waves. They don't cross the gym.

P through S. At the front, a woman with reading glasses on a chain holds out her hand for the form.

There's one prompt.

Riley folds the form in half, then in half again, and puts it in the back pocket of her jeans.

**WOMAN:** Honey?

**RILEY:** Sorry. I'm in the wrong line.

She walks out of the gym into the September sun.

> **Design note — why there's no choice.** Riley decided on the drive over. The player filled out the form to feel what she was leaving. It stays in her things, folded in quarters; in the epilogue it's in a shoebox with the notebooks.

### Landry (optional)

On the way to the lot she passes the philosophy building. Landry's door is open. He's on the phone, feet on the desk, pipe unlit, and holds up one finger: *one minute.* If she waits:

**LANDRY:** Miss Riley.

**RILEY:** I'm not registering.

**LANDRY:** I noticed. There's a boy from Macon who's been in my office twice a day wanting your seat.

**RILEY:** Give it to him.

**LANDRY:** I did. On Monday.

**RILEY:** Then why'd you—

**LANDRY:** I wanted to see if you'd come tell me.

She doesn't know what to say.

**LANDRY:** Send me the record.

She mails him a copy the following week.

### Sunday dinner

Sunday, Linwood. The Riley house, a brick ranch with a carport and a dogwood; the leaf in the table because Tommy is home on a pass from Norfolk; Tom carving a roast and making a pun about it the player can hear coming a long way off.

The player chooses when she tells them. The longer she waits, the more ordinary dinner comes first (Tommy's Navy stories, an argument about Ford), and the harder it is. It comes out the same.

**RILEY:** I didn't register this semester.

Tom puts his fork down. Tommy looks at his plate.

**TOM:** For how long?

**RILEY:** I don't know.

**TOM:** Is it the band?

**RILEY:** It's the band.

Tom looks at Joan. Joan is buttering a roll. She keeps buttering it.

**TOM:** Well.

The player can let the silence go, or have Riley fill it. If she defends it, Tom says, "I didn't say anything." If she apologizes: "Don't apologize, Maggie, just explain." If she jokes, Tommy laughs, and Tom almost does.

Then Tom picks the fork back up.

**TOM:** Your mother made pie.

### The piano

After dinner, Joan washing, Riley drying. Joan turns off the water, dries her hands and goes into the living room, and the player hears the upright: a Bach chorale, slowly. Riley goes to the doorway with the dish towel. Joan plays to the end without looking up.

**JOAN:** I had a place at Oberlin. In 1949.

Riley doesn't move.

**RILEY:** You never told me that.

**JOAN:** I auditioned in the spring. They wrote in May. Your father asked me in June.

**RILEY:** And you didn't go.

**JOAN:** I thought I'd go the next year.

She plays a chord, and doesn't resolve it.

**RILEY:** Why didn't you?

**JOAN:** Tommy.

That's the whole answer. Joan resolves the chord.

**JOAN:** I'm not telling you anything, Margaret. I'm telling you what I did.

The player can have Riley sit beside her on the bench. If she does, Joan slides the hymnal over and plays the chorale again, and Riley sings the alto line, and they don't talk about it. If not, she stands in the doorway until it ends and goes back to the dishes.

On the way out, Riley gives her mother the Leipzig record, if the player bought it.

**JOAN:** Where did you get this?

**RILEY:** A store on Tenth Street.

**JOAN:** What were you doing on Tenth Street?

**RILEY:** Watching somebody buy our record.

**JOAN:** Did they?

**RILEY:** Eventually.

> **Knowledge.** Riley has never heard about Oberlin before tonight. She wrote "Sunday Clothes," about her mother's Saturday mornings alone at the church organ, without knowing it.

## MISSION 4 — OCCASIONALLY ASTONISHING

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** the van, US 19 and off-map to Chattanooga · **When:** Saturday October 11 · **Length:** ~20 min

### The reviews

The van, westbound for a college gym in Chattanooga. Riley in the passenger seat with a manila folder of clippings she's been collecting for three weeks and hasn't admitted to anybody.

**DEAN:** What's that?

**RILEY:** Nothing.

**DEAN:** It's reviews. She's got reviews.

**RILEY:** I've got *some* reviews.

**DEAN:** Read them.

**CAL:** Don't read them.

**DEAN:** Read them in voices.

She does. The player picks the clipping and the voice.

- **The *Tannersville Courier*, Saturday entertainment page.** Riley reads it as a Baptist deacon. *"Local musicians of evident sincerity who would be well advised to turn down. The Row has seen this kind of thing before."*
- **The *Laurel City Herald*.** A whole column, warm. Riley reads it straight, because it's good, and her voice goes a little careful. *"…and 'Sunday Clothes,' in which Ellis Blake imagines a church organist playing Bach to an empty sanctuary, is the album's tenderest moment…"* She stops.
**DEAN:** Keep going.
**RILEY:** "Ellis Blake imagines."
**ELLIS** *(from the back)*: I didn't write that.
**RILEY:** I know you didn't write it.
**ELLIS:** I'll call them.
**RILEY:** It's fine.
She tucks her hair behind her ear.
- **Billboard, "Recommended LPs," three lines.** Riley reads it as a newscaster. *"Regional quartet's debut is occasionally astonishing and frequently loud. Southeastern dealers should stock."*
**DEAN:** "Occasionally astonishing."
**CAL:** That's good. From them, that's good.
**DEAN:** Occasionally. Occasionally!
**ELLIS:** It's more than they say about most people.
- **The *Red and Black***, a college rave clearly written at 3 a.m. Riley reads it as Dean, and Dean is delighted.

"Occasionally astonishing" enters the band's language at once, for anything. ("How's the coffee?" / "Occasionally astonishing.")

### Correction

A week later, in a short coda, the *Herald* prints a correction in six-point type:

> *A review Sept. 28 misattributed the song "Sunday Clothes" on the Blakes' album "Borrowed Stone." It was written by Margaret Riley.*

Cal wrote the letter and told no one. Dean finds the correction and reads it aloud in his newscaster voice. Riley looks at Cal in the rearview mirror. He's watching the road.

**RILEY:** Margaret.

**CAL:** That's what's on the copyright.

### The vote

At a Stuckey's past the state line, Eddie Farris is on the pay phone, because he's been chasing them for an hour. The player holds the receiver as Riley.

**EDDIE** *(on the phone)*: The Civic Auditorium. November first. Sixteen hundred seats. Laurel City.

A noise from Dean that makes a trucker turn around.

**EDDIE:** And I want to take Theo Marchand off the Lantern and put him on the road with you. Front of house. You need your own. I need a vote.

No band decisions alone. Riley relays it.

**DEAN:** Yes. Obviously. He made us sound like Zeppelin at the Lantern.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

Riley looks at Cal.

**CAL:** I'll abstain.

**DEAN:** Why? You love that guy's mixes. You said the Lantern tape was the best we ever sounded.

**CAL:** Conflict.

**DEAN:** With what?

**CAL:** Scheduling.

Dean looks confused. Ellis doesn't look at anything. Riley casts the last vote. There's one option.

**RILEY** *(into the phone)*: Three yes, one abstain.

**EDDIE:** What's the abstain?

**RILEY:** Scheduling.

She hangs up. She doesn't look at Cal. Cal pays for the gas.

> **Knowledge.** Riley knows about Cal and Theo (Chapter VI, the Blind Tiger). Ellis and Dean don't, yet. Cal abstained from hiring the man he's in love with because voting would be dishonest.

### The list

That night in the motel, Riley opens her notebook to the inside cover, where she keeps a list of words she likes (*palimpsest; ramshackle; susurrus; eaves; jubilate; Tuesday*), and adds, in pen: *occasionally astonishing*. The list goes back to 1972.

## MISSION 5 — LAST SHIFT

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** Hollow Ridge Auto & Service · **When:** Saturday October 18, 7:00 a.m. – 1:00 p.m. · **Length:** ~25 min

### Why today

Eddie's fall dates don't leave room for a garage. Ellis told Roy two weeks ago. Roy said "All right," and they've been polite to each other ever since in a way neither of them can stand. Saturday is the last half-day: ordinary jobs, the diagnostic systems from Chapter I, and Ellis is faster at all of it now.

### The jobs

- A pulpwood truck with a slipping clutch: twelve minutes, where Chapter I took half an hour. A flat on a school bus driver's Pontiac.
- **Mrs. Pardue's Rambler**: "the goose again, but smaller." The same heat shield, the same hose clamp, loose again. She's in the office doorway in a cardigan.
  **MRS. PARDUE:** I bought your record.
  **ELLIS:** Where?
  **MRS. PARDUE:** The Western Auto. They had two.
  **ELLIS:** Who bought the other one?
  **MRS. PARDUE:** I couldn't say. *(Then:)* Are you writing, Ellis?
  In Chapter I he lied to her. Now: "Yes ma'am" gets "Good." "Some" gets "Some is plenty." Silence gets a long look and the same *hm* as Chapter I.

> **Replay layer.** If the player didn't leave a record on the kitchen table in Mission 1, Wayne bought the other Western Auto copy, and Mrs. Pardue knows it. Either way Wayne has one.

### The pumps

Around eleven a green Ford with a stock rack pulls up to the pumps: Floyd Tolliver, seventy, a face like a walnut. The Tolliver farm is on Tolliver Road. Ellis hasn't seen him up close in two years.

Clara is on the bench by the drink box with her boots crossed.

### Danny

**ROY:** You want your check now or you want me to mail it.

**ELLIS:** Now's fine.

A week's pay, plus fifty dollars that isn't.

**ELLIS:** This is wrong.

**ROY:** I can add.

**ELLIS:** Roy.

**ROY:** Go on.

Ellis doesn't know what to do next, so he looks at the shelf. Roy follows his eyes.

**ROY:** Danny worked that bay. Summer of sixty-six.

Ellis doesn't say anything.

**ROY:** He couldn't tell a carburetor from a coffee pot. Wanted to be a radio announcer. Talked all day. Talked to the cars.

**ROY:** You don't talk to the cars.

**ELLIS:** Sometimes.

**ROY:** Not out loud.

Ellis starts to take off the gray Dickies work shirt with the oval patch that says ELLIS in red script.

**ROY:** Keep the shirt.

**ELLIS:** It's the shop's.

**ROY:** It's got your name on it. What am I gonna do with it, hire another Ellis?

Ellis keeps the shirt.

At the door:

**ROY:** Ellis.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**ROY:** Don't become an asshole.

**ELLIS:** I'll try.

**ROY:** Don't try. Just don't.

### Clean hands

At home the Lava soap gets the grease out of his knuckles, and it doesn't come back. From here on, one of his idle animations is looking at his hands, the way you'd look at a borrowed pair of gloves. The gray shirt goes in his closet until Chapter X.

## MISSION 6 — 11:47 P.M.

**Playable:** Cal → relay (Eddie, a stranger, Dean) → Ellis · **Where:** off Tenth Street, Laurel City → the SR 9 Spur → a bedroom past the reservoir → the Starlite → the Blake house · **When:** Tuesday October 21, 11:40 p.m. – 12:40 a.m. · **Length:** ~25 min

### Theo's kitchen

Cal has been spending Tuesdays in Laurel City since September. The band believes this involves a man who sells vacuum tubes.

Theo's apartment off Tenth Street, the fire escape and the plants. Game 6 of the World Series is on the little black-and-white television with the sound off (Theo can't stand the announcers), and the kitchen radio is on WLRC, because Theo listens to other people's mixes the way other people read the paper. Cal has Theo's reel-to-reel in pieces on the kitchen table. Theo is drying a skillet.

On the television, in silence, a man in a Red Sox uniform slides into home and is called out. Theo doesn't look.

**THEO:** Who's winning?

**CAL:** It's tied.

**THEO:** Is that good?

**CAL:** It's the ninth inning of the sixth game of the World Series.

**THEO:** I'll take that as a yes.

The player can keep working on the Teac, or watch the game, or talk to Theo.

Then the song on WLRC ends, and the voice that comes on is one the player knows.

**MARTIN KELLER** *(on the radio; twenty-three now, a little deeper, still earnest)*: Ninety-seven-one, WLRC, it's eleven forty-six, I'm Martin Keller, and if you're watching the ballgame you're going to miss this. The first chord of "Still Here."

Cal stops with the soldering iron in the air.

Theo sets the skillet down and turns the radio up.

There's no prompt that matters. Cal listens to himself on a real radio station in a real city, and the camera holds on his face and doesn't cut.

At the end of the first chorus he takes a matchbook off the table and writes *11:47* on it with his Parker Jotter.

**CAL:** Bass is buried.

**THEO:** It is not.

**CAL:** It's a little buried.

**THEO:** Your father said that. About the college station.

**CAL:** He was right then too.

Theo laughs and puts his hand on the back of Cal's neck, and leaves it there.

### The relay

The song carries the camera out of Theo's kitchen window: the radio relay from Chapter III, on a fifty-thousand-watt signal. Each stop lasts a section of the song; the player controls each person lightly, and the song plays across all of them without a break.

- **First verse, end:** a Checker cab downtown, the driver drumming on the wheel. "Who is this?" / "Don't know. Turn it up?"
- **Second verse:** **Eddie Farris** in his Buick on the SR 9 Spur. He pulls onto the shoulder with his flashers on, and at the chorus he hits the wheel with both palms, twice, and laughs out loud in an empty car.
- **Bridge:** a bedroom in a subdivision past the reservoir. A girl of sixteen in a flannel nightgown holds a Panasonic cassette recorder up to a clock radio, holding her breath so she won't be on the tape. Her name is on a spiral notebook on the bed: *SHERRY TOLAN*.

**LYNETTE:** Get down.

**DEAN:** It's us.

**LYNETTE:** I know it's you. Get down off my seat.

He gets down. He's crying a little and doesn't care who sees. The pulpwood drivers look at him, then at the radio, then at each other.

**DRIVER:** That's them boys from Hollow Ridge.

**OTHER DRIVER:** One of 'em's a girl.

### Martin

The song ends. The switch goes into the WLRC control room: the red ON AIR light, Martin Keller at the board in a cardigan, and all six request lines blinking.

**MARTIN** *(on air)*: That was the Blakes. Hollow Ridge, Georgia. "Still Here." Phones are... okay. Hang on.

**MARTIN** *(on air)*: I'm going to play it again.

He does. It's 11:51.

### The Blake house

From Martin's board the switch travels down a phone line: Dean at the Starlite pay phone, dialing with the receiver jammed between his ear and shoulder, a stack of dimes on the shelf.

The phone rings in the hall of the Blake house.

The player is Ellis, lying on his bed with a transistor radio on his chest, AM only, tuned to the ballgame. So is the television in the front room, where Wayne is in his chair. They are listening to the same game in separate rooms, both turned up, the way they do Georgia football on Saturdays.

The phone rings four times. Wayne doesn't get up. Ellis does.

**ELLIS:** Hello.

**DEAN** *(screaming)*: TURN ON NINETY-SEVEN.

**ELLIS:** It's the tenth inning.

**DEAN:** THEY'RE PLAYING IT. MARTIN'S PLAYING IT. HE PLAYED IT ONCE AND NOW HE'S PLAYING IT AGAIN, BLAKE, HE'S PLAYING IT *AGAIN*.

Ellis stands in the hall with the receiver.

**ELLIS:** I don't have FM.

**DEAN:** WHAT?

**ELLIS:** Nothing in the house gets FM.

**DEAN:** THEN GO FIND SOME.

Dean hangs up (he's out of dimes).

### The truck

Ellis stands in the hall for a second, then goes out the kitchen door into the yard in his socks. Tater gets up off the porch and follows him.

Wayne's F-100 in the dark. Unlocked; it always is. Ellis gets in the driver's side and turns the key to accessory. The dash lights come up.

Under the dash, screwed to the bottom of the cracked panel with two sheet-metal screws, is a small aftermarket FM converter, the kind they sell at the Western Auto for nineteen dollars. It's tuned to **88.9**.

Ellis looks at it.

88.9 is WTCR, the ten-watt college station in Tannersville. The one you can only hear from the top of Stony Knob.

Ellis doesn't say anything. He turns the dial to 97.1.

The last chorus of the second play. Through one bad speaker in his father's truck, in the dark, with the dog sitting in the gravel by the door looking up at him.

*And I'm still here. And you're still here.*

The song ends. Martin Keller says "That's twice. Don't tell my program director," and goes into a commercial for a waterbed store.

Then the prompt. Ellis can leave the converter on 97.1, or turn it back to 88.9.

- If he leaves it on 97.1, the next morning Wayne gets in the truck and the converter is on a different station, and he knows someone's been in his truck, and the player never sees his face when he figures it out.
- If he turns it back to 88.9, carefully, to the exact line on the dial, nobody ever knows.

Either way, Ellis gets out, and pats his leg for Tater, and goes back inside.

> **Replay layer.** The converter was installed in November 1974, the week of the WTCR broadcast in Chapter III. Wayne has left it on 88.9 for eleven months even though WTCR has been off the air every summer and he can't hear it from the yard anyway. Tracked: on the drive to Sylva in Chapter IX, if Ellis left it on 97.1, Wayne looks at it and says, "Somebody moved my radio last fall." If not, it's still on 88.9, and Wayne turns it on as they pass the fire tower.

### Extra innings

The front room. Wayne in his chair. The television: extra innings, the tenth, Fenway Park at night.

Ellis stands in the doorway.

**WAYNE:** Who was that on the phone?

**ELLIS:** Dean.

**WAYNE:** It's midnight.

**ELLIS:** They played it on the radio. In Laurel City. WLRC.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

A pitch. A foul ball.

**WAYNE:** Which one?

**ELLIS:** "Still Here."

Wayne doesn't say anything. (He heard that song once, in a funeral home in Tannersville, and asked who it was about, and went white.)

**WAYNE:** Twice?

**ELLIS:** Twice.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

Ellis doesn't go back to his room. The player can make him, but the prompt that pulses is the couch. He sits on the end nearest the door. His father doesn't look at him.

The ballgame plays out in real time on the television. There's a catch in right field in the eleventh that makes Wayne say a word Ellis has never heard him say. Tater comes in and lies down on Ellis's feet. Nobody speaks. The player can leave at any time. If they stay until 12:34 a.m., they see it:

The twelfth inning. A catcher hits a high fly down the left-field line toward the foul pole, and stands at home plate waving his arms at the ball to stay fair, and Wayne Blake comes halfway out of his chair doing the same thing with both hands.

It's fair. It hits the pole.

Wayne slaps the arm of the chair and says "*Hah!*" out loud, one syllable, like a much younger man.

Ellis laughs. Wayne looks at him. For a second, two men who've just watched the same thing and liked it.

Then Wayne sits back.

**WAYNE:** Well. Go to bed.

Ellis goes to bed.

> **Design note.** The night the Blakes' song breaks in Laurel City is the night Ellis finds out his father has been listening to him from a mountaintop, and the two of them spend it watching a ballgame and not talking about either thing. (The game is real: Game 6 of the 1975 World Series, Fenway Park, Tuesday October 21, ended by Carlton Fisk's home run at 12:34 a.m. Eastern.)

### The next morning

From today "Still Here" is in WLRC's rotation, on every FM set in range of Laurel City: taxis, apartment windows on Tenth Street, the van. The program director comes in at nine to sixty-one phone messages. Southern Star rushes out a single.

## MISSION 7 — WHERE'S ELLIS?

**Playable:** Cal → Ellis → Cal · **Where:** the Civic Auditorium; Tenth Street; the Spur; the downtown rail yard; Engineers Park · **When:** Saturday November 1, 3:00 – 5:30 p.m. · **Length:** ~25 min

### The building

The Laurel City Civic Auditorium: a 1958 municipal hall, sixteen hundred seats, a union stage crew, and an echo off the back wall Theo has been fighting since noon. The marquee says THE BLAKES · TONIGHT · SOLD OUT. The player is Cal, walking the building with a clipboard.

- **Tully**, crew chief now, at the loading dock with his hands in his pockets, because the union won't let him touch anything, watching strangers carry the Ampeg in, badly. "You want me to say something?" / "No. I want to hit somebody, but I'm not going to."

### Dex

**DEX:** Dex Lundgren. *Rave.* Harold Vance sent me your record with a note that said "Please." Nobody says please to me. I flew down from Detroit. Is this the bass player?

**CAL:** Cal Mercer.

**DEX:** You look like a bass player. That's not an insult, the bass players are the only honest men in rock and roll. You want a vitamin?

**CAL:** No.

**DEX:** Correct answer. Where's the singer?

Every option is a lie, because Cal doesn't know: **"Getting strings."** / **"Parking."** / **"He'll be here."** Whichever Cal says ends up in the magazine.

**CAL** *(to Dean)*: Keep him busy.

**DEAN:** Who?

**CAL:** *Rave.*

**DEAN:** How?

**CAL:** Talk.

**DEAN:** Oh, I can do that.

> **Consequence.** Dean spends ninety minutes with Dex and a film canister. The article's first sentence comes from this afternoon: Dean, high and full of love, telling a stranger with a tape recorder that Ellis Blake is a genius.

**CLARA:** They'll sing it.

**ELLIS:** Reckon so.

**CLARA:** Martin said. People call every night and ask for it.

**ELLIS:** So they'll sing it.

**CLARA:** You stole those off me.

## MISSION 8 — SIXTEEN HUNDRED

**Playable:** all four (rotating during the performance) · **Where:** the Civic Auditorium · **When:** Saturday November 1, 8:30 p.m. – 1:00 a.m. · **Length:** ~45 min

### Before

The dressing room. 8:25. The union stage manager bangs on the door: "Five minutes." Through the wall, sixteen hundred people make a sound none of them has ever heard from the inside: a low pressure, like weather coming in.

Dean is jiggling one knee so hard the makeup mirror rattles. Riley is sitting cross-legged on the counter with her glasses on, reading the set list over and over. Cal has his bass on already. Ellis is by the door.

Cal's set list, on the back of a union time sheet:

> LOW WATER
> ICE MACHINE
> LINWOOD
> BORROWED STONE
> PARIETAL HOURS
> NO NAME
> SUNDAY CLOTHES
> TOMORROW PROBLEM
> STONY KNOB
> STILL HERE
> ——
> SHAPE NOTE

**DEAN:** Kick drum.

In the dark behind the curtain, each of them touches the Vistalite's kick drum. Dex Lundgren is standing in the wings with his notebook out, watching this.

**DEX:** What is that? Is that spiritual?

**DEAN:** It's a drum.

**DEX:** What does it mean?

**DEAN:** It means we touched the drum.

Dex writes this down with visible happiness.

### The room

The crowd model at the Civic is slow and enormous. Sixteen hundred people in seats with armrests answer late, like thunder after lightning: the player does something, and the room answers a beat and a half later, front rows first, balcony last. The monitors are bad, so Ellis can barely hear Riley, and the back wall throws everything back a quarter-second late. The band has to play to each other harder than they ever have, facing inward, the hollow square, or they come apart.

The performance rotates through all four, a song or two each, with switches on the band's own musical cues (Cal's headstock, Dean's sticks, Riley's nod):

- **Dean** opens "Low Water" alone on the floor tom. With *read the room* the crowd is a huge dim field, bright in the first ten rows where the college kids have pushed forward, dark under the balcony. The task is to find the warmth and play to it.
- **Riley** on "Linwood" and "Sunday Clothes." On "Sunday Clothes," Ellis sings harmony under her, because it's her song. The player chooses her phrasing. If the player looks out, there are three girls in the fourth row who know every word, and one of them is crying, and it isn't about Ellis.
- **Cal** on "Borrowed Stone" and "Parietal Hours." The player's job is mostly *lock*, holding Dean back from rushing into the echo, and Cal is watching Ellis more than he usually does.
- **Dean** on "Tomorrow Problem." He throws his sticks up on the last chorus and the whole front section shouts the title back at him (*THAT'S A TOMORROW PROBLEM*), and the balcony, a beat and a half late, shouts it too. No crowd has sung Dean's song before.
- **Ellis** on "No Name" and "Stony Knob," and then on the one they've all been waiting for.

### "This one's for Clara"

Between "Stony Knob" and "Still Here," the stage goes dark except for a single light on Ellis. He's supposed to count it in.

He steps to the mic. The player gets no prompt here. The line is fixed, because of the grandstand.

**ELLIS:** This one's for Clara.

That's all. He counts it in.

One look at each of the others as the first chord rings, through Ellis's *attention*:
- **Riley** is looking at the stage-left wing, where it's dark. Then she looks at her hands.
- **Cal** is looking at Ellis.
- **Dean** is grinning at him, because Dean assumes Clara is a girl in Hollow Ridge, and he's delighted.

And in the stage-left wing, past the fly rail, in the dark, a young woman in a jean jacket is standing with her arms folded, watching.

### The second verse

First verse. Ellis sings it. By the second line, the front rows are singing it with him.

*You sat on a crate in the alley in the cold…*

By the end of the verse, it's the whole floor. The player can feel it through the controller: a low vibration under the music that isn't the bass.

Then the chorus, and the balcony's in.

Before the second verse, Ellis gets the *space* verb: step back from the mic and don't sing. (If the player chose Cal's first answer in the grandstand, the prompt pulses. If not, it's there, quietly.) If the player takes it, Ellis steps back.

And sixteen hundred people, in a municipal auditorium in Laurel City, sing the second verse of "Still Here" without him:

*Everybody in this town can see my business.*
*Nobody here knows you.*

Behind the kit, off-mic, Dean says it to Cal, and means it:

**DEAN:** Are they doing that for us?

**CAL:** Yes.

**DEAN:** All of them?

**CAL:** Keep time.

If the player, as Ellis, uses *attention* to look at the stage-left wing during the second verse, Clara is watching the crowd sing her words, and her face is very hard to read. Under sixteen hundred voices, Ellis can hear her humming the harmony, the high part, the part Riley sings, and she's singing it better.

If the player doesn't take the *space* verb, Ellis sings the whole song and the crowd sings along underneath him, and it's still enormous, and he never finds out what it sounds like without him. Both are complete.

The last chorus. Riley drifts back to the stage-left corner of the riser, where she always ends up. The player brings the song home: Ellis turns all the way around to face Dean, the band's oldest signal, and Dean brings the song down, and it ends.

Sixteen hundred people stand up. It arrives at the stage a beat and a half late, like everything in this room.

Ellis doesn't turn back around to face them for a few seconds. He stands facing the drum riser. Dean, behind the Vistalite, is the only one who can see his face.

> **Replay layer.** Sixteen hundred strangers sang "Nobody here knows you" to a young man none of them knew, who was looking at someone none of them could see.

### Encore

"Shape Note." Nine minutes of drone. The crowd, who came for "Still Here," stands through it at first out of respect, then sits down, then goes very quiet, and by minute six a lot of them are listening in a way they didn't know they could. Theo, at the front-of-house desk, has taken his hands off the faders.

The player can be any of the four and switch freely on the band cues. There's nothing to win.

### The stage door

Midnight. A hundred people in the alley behind the Civic under a sodium light, with records and programs and pens. The band comes out one at a time, and each of them has a different stage door.

- **Dean** signs everything, including a man's bald head.
- **Riley** signs records. A boy of about seventeen asks if she's Ellis's girlfriend. The player chooses her answer (a joke, a question back, "I'm the one who plays the twelve-string," or nothing). Whatever she says, he nods and asks her to sign it "to Doug."
- **Cal** comes out carrying his bass. A girl walks up to him, hands him her pocketbook, says, "Would you hold this," and goes to get Ellis's autograph. Cal holds it. She comes back, takes it, says "Thanks," and doesn't ask for his. Tully, beside him, says, "I'd have asked," and Cal laughs, really laughs.
- **Ellis** comes out last.

The crowd moves toward him and the player feels it: the camera tightens, the sound closes in. People say his name. People say *Ellis* the way you'd say the name of someone you know, and none of them know him.

A girl of sixteen pushes to the front with a copy of *Borrowed Stone*: *SHERRY TOLAN*, the girl with the cassette recorder.

**SHERRY:** I taped it off the radio. Both times.

**ELLIS:** Both times?

**SHERRY:** The second one's better. He turned it up.

Ellis signs her record. Then she asks it.

**SHERRY:** Who's Clara?

Behind her, right behind her shoulder, in the sodium light, Clara is standing in the crowd with her hands in her jacket pockets.

Every option is small: *"Old friend."* / *"Just somebody."* / say nothing. If the player says nothing, Sherry waits, and Clara says, "Tell her," and the prompt comes back with only one option in it.

**ELLIS:** Old friend.

**CLARA:** Old?

Ellis almost laughs. Sherry thinks he's laughing at her question and goes red, and he says "No, it's a good question," and she goes away happy.

**CLARA:** Quit grinning.

Dex Lundgren, leaning on the wall beside the stage door with his notebook, writes down *Old friend.*

### The interviews

The dressing-room hallway, 12:30 a.m. Dex interviews them one at a time on a folding chair with the Sony running. Each interview uses the standard dialogue grammar and is tracked. Nothing on screen says what will be done with the answers.

**Dean** (already interviewed all afternoon; this is a victory lap). Dex asks what the Blakes' sound is. The options: *"Space death."* / *"Like a train falling off a mountain."* / *"Loud."* Dex asks where Ellis was at soundcheck. Dean covers for him (every option is a cover). Dex asks what Ellis is like. There's only one option, because Dean is high and happy and loves his friend:

**DEAN:** Blake's a genius, man. I'm not kidding. He's the real thing. The rest of us are just trying to keep up.

**Riley.** Dex asks what it's like to be the girl in the band. The options: *"I'm the one who can read music."* / *"One of us isn't a boy."* (the band's running joke) / talk about the arrangements (a long answer about voice-leading, her mother, and how the twelve-string drone works under Ellis's glide) / silence. Dex listens to all of it with real interest. He asks one follow-up about her mother.

**Cal.** Dex asks whose band it is. The options: *"It's ours."* / *"Nobody's. It's a band."* / *"Ask the bank."* If the player picks another answer, Dex pushes ("Come on. It's Ellis's band. Everybody knows it's Ellis's band.") and the prompt comes back, and this time Cal says it:

**CAL:** It's ours.

**Ellis.** The last interview. Dex turns the Sony toward him. Ellis has the Kevin letter in one pocket and a lot of adrenaline in the rest of him and nothing to say to a man from Detroit. It's built almost entirely on the silence mechanic: the player can hold the button through any question, and Dex waits, and asks another. Questions: *Where do the songs come from?* ("Hollow Ridge.") *What's Hollow Ridge like?* ("Small.") *Why'd you quit the garage?* ("I didn't quit. I left.") *Is it true you walked out of your own birthday party?* ("That's a matter for the courts.") *Who's Clara?* (Dex asks, then answers himself, "I know, I know. Old friend.") *What do you want?* This last question, the player can answer with silence, or with one line: *"To go home."* / *"Sleep."* / *"I don't know."*

Dex turns off the Sony.

**DEX:** You're the worst interview I've done in two years.

**ELLIS:** Thank you.

**DEX:** No. It's great. It's going to be great.

> **Design note — the interview system.** Nothing is misquoted. Every quote is real; the selection is Dex's, from the answers that fit the story he already wanted to tell.

### The money

1:00 a.m. Cal counts the settlement on the makeup counter in stacks, facing the same way. His ledger page:

> *11/1/75 — Civic Aud., L.C. — 1,580 pd @ $4.50 = $7,110 gross. Band guarantee — $2,400.00. Eddie 15% — 360.00. Crew (T.V. 60, T.M. 90) — 150.00. Fund 20% — 378.00. Shares (4) @ 378.00.*

Cal hands each of them $378 in cash.

At Roy's, Ellis made about seventy dollars a week. This is five weeks in one night. He folds it into his shirt pocket, and then takes it out and looks at it again.

Dean has already spent his, in his head, twice.

## MISSION 10 — BELLE GROVE

### The study door

Richard at his desk in a cardigan. Carol on the leather sofa. Patty listening from the stairs. On the green blotter, squared to its edges, two things. **PATTY:** You forgot these.

**DEAN:** Thanks.

**PATTY:** You're an idiot.

**DEAN:** I know.

**PATTY:** No, you don't.

## MISSION 11 — LEDGER

**Playable:** Dean → Cal · **Where:** Cal's room over the dry cleaner, Tannersville · **When:** Wednesday November 12, 3:50 – 5:30 a.m. · **Length:** ~15 min

### Four in the morning

One room and a kitchenette over the dry cleaner, everything labeled, a shelf of eleven clocks ticking slightly out of step. Cal asleep with his glasses folded on the nightstand. Dean on Tully's Army cot, wide awake at 3:50, knee bouncing, for the obvious reason.

The ledger is open under the lamp where Cal fell asleep over it. Since the Civic the money has gotten complicated (crew pay, per diems, a royalty statement that says they're unrecouped by $11,340), and something's off by $270. Cal has spent three nights not finding it.

### The puzzle

A real, small puzzle: two pages of entries and a reconciliation that doesn't match the bank statement. If the player hesitates, Dean says it out loud to nobody:

**DEAN:** Two-seventy. Divides by nine.

His father's trick from the dinner table when he was eleven: if an error divides evenly by nine, somebody transposed two digits. The player finds the pair: *10/24 — Macon, gross $1,630.00*, and on the deposit slip, *$1,360.00*.

Then Dean keeps going, faster than the player could: Eddie reimbursed twice for the same $14.60 gas receipt; the per diems rebuilt on a clean page; in the margin, how many records they'd have to sell before Southern Star pays a royalty. (The number is large.) Each correction the way his father does it: one line through, the right figure above, *D.H.*

Then he turns back through the book, and finds his own name, again and again: *Dean — owes fund $20.00. Dean — owes fund $15.00. Dean — adv. against share $30.00.* He adds them up without meaning to. $340.

He has eleven dollars.

He writes under the last one, in pen: *D.H. — to be paid in full.* He underlines it.

### Coffee

Stutter. Cal wakes to the smell of coffee at 5:10. Dean is at the table with two mugs.

**DEAN:** Morning, Dad.

Cal reads the ledger, turns the page and turns it back: the corrections in a neater hand than his, the initials, the Macon transposition circled.

**CAL:** You found the two-seventy.

**DEAN:** Divides by nine.

**CAL:** What?

**DEAN:** If the mistake divides by nine it's a transposition. Everybody knows that.

**CAL:** I didn't know that.

**DEAN:** Richard knows that.

Cal looks at him. Dean is grinding his teeth, his pupils big in the lamplight. Cal sees it, and the player sees Cal see it.

**CAL:** Were you up all night?

**DEAN:** I'm always up all night.

**CAL:** Where'd you learn this?

**DEAN:** At the dinner table. For eight years. Pretending not to listen.

He drinks his coffee.

**DEAN:** Don't tell anybody.

**CAL:** Why?

**DEAN:** Because then they'll expect it.

Cal thinks about that longer than it deserves.

**CAL:** Okay.

**DEAN:** Promise.

**CAL:** I said okay.

### Sundays

Cal goes to shave. Dean, from the table, through the bathroom door:

**DEAN:** Where do you go on Sundays?

Cal's razor stops.

**DEAN:** You're gone every Sunday. Tully says you go to Laurel City.

**CAL:** I visit somebody.

**DEAN:** A girl?

Cal shaves.

**CAL:** Somebody.

**DEAN** *(delighted)*: Dad's got a girlfriend.

Cal doesn't answer, and the player, as Cal, can't make him.

> **Replay layer.** Cal doesn't lie; he withholds. He also didn't correct Dean. In Chapter VIII, after Ellis says the worst thing he ever says, Dean will bring Cal a coffee and say, "For the record, I figured you were just boring."

> **Object.** The ledger now has Dean's initials in it eleven times, and one line in his hand: *to be paid in full.* It's never paid (the epilogue).

## MISSION 12 — ROOM 614

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** the Monroe Hotel, Richmond, Virginia (off-map) · **When:** Saturday November 15, 1:40 – 2:30 a.m.; Sunday morning · **Length:** ~20 min

### The sixth floor

The Richmond run: Charlotte Thursday, Raleigh Friday, Richmond Saturday. Tomorrow's Norfolk, where Riley's brother is stationed and has promised to bring half his ship.

The Monroe is a 1920s hotel going quietly to seed: a dry fountain in the lobby, a sixth floor of dark carpet with a single ice machine at the end under a window onto a brick wall. Eddie got four rooms for the price of three because the manager's daughter likes "Still Here." Riley's in 608. Ellis and Dean in 610. Cal and Tully in 612. Theo in 614.

After the show, after the diner, after Dean's fallen asleep in his clothes with the television on, the player is Ellis, in the hallway, at 1:40 a.m., in socks, walking toward 608.

The line of light under Riley's door is on. She's waiting up. This is what they do on the road.

### The radiator

Clara is sitting on the radiator at the end of the hall, with her boots up on the ice machine, blocking nothing, sitting in his way anyway.

**CLARA:** Where you headed in your sock feet?

**ELLIS** *(quietly)*: Bed.

**CLARA:** That's not your room.

**ELLIS:** Go to sleep.

**CLARA:** I don't sleep. Lord, you combed your hair. For bed.

He keeps walking. She says it to his back, just loud enough:

**CLARA:** She doesn't know you.

He stops.

**ELLIS:** She knows me.

**CLARA:** She knows what you told her in a field. That's not the same thing.

**ELLIS:** That's what knowing somebody is.

**CLARA:** Is it.

The player can walk on (the prompt to Riley's door is there, glowing) or turn around. If they walk on, Ellis stands at 608 with his knuckles an inch from the wood, and Clara says, from the end of the hall, "Tell her about the car, then," and his hand drops, and he turns around. Every path turns him around.

He walks back down to the ice machine. He's angry now, the quiet kind.

**ELLIS:** What do you want?

**CLARA:** I want you to stop pretending they know you. Sixteen hundred people sang my words and not one of them knows who they were singing to.

**ELLIS:** So?

**CLARA:** So they don't know you.

A pause. She looks at the door with the light under it.

**CLARA:** Neither does Riley.

**ELLIS** *(too loud)*: She knows me better than you do.

**CLARA:** Hush. Nobody knows you better than me.

**ELLIS:** You're not even—

He stops. He doesn't know how the sentence ends. He's never known.

**CLARA:** Not even what?

He doesn't answer.

### The girl with the bucket

A sound behind him. The player turns the camera.

At the other end of the hallway, by the stairwell door, there's a girl of about seventeen in a Blakes T-shirt from tonight's merch table with an empty ice bucket in her hands. She's not a guest. She came up the stairs because the manager's daughter told her which floor. She's been standing there long enough.

She's looking at Ellis. At the end of the hallway where he's been talking. At the radiator, which has no one on it.

**GIRL:** Sorry. I was just...

She sets the bucket down on the carpet very carefully, as if it might go off, and goes through the stairwell door, fast. Her footsteps go down six flights.

The bucket stays in the middle of the hall.

Clara is still on the radiator.

> **Consequence.** The girl is Denise Allard. In Chapter VIII *Rave*'s letters page prints her account of Ellis Blake in a hotel hallway at two in the morning having a fight with nobody, under the headline SPACE DEATH, INDEED. It's the first public crack, and it's a joke.

### 614

A door opens behind him, farther down the hall. 614.

Cal comes out in his socks, carrying his shoes. Theo is in the doorway behind him in an undershirt. Theo says something too low to hear, and puts his hand on the side of Cal's face, just for a second, and Cal laughs, quietly. Theo closes the door.

Cal turns and sees Ellis at the end of the hall by the ice machine.

Neither of them moves. Clara slides off the radiator and goes round the corner past the ice machine while Cal and Ellis look at each other.

The player, as Ellis, gets nothing. No prompt. Ellis just stands there with his hands at his sides, and so does Cal, with his shoes in his hand.

**CAL:** Ice machine working?

**ELLIS:** Somebody left a bucket.

Cal nods. He goes into 612 and closes the door.

Ellis stands in the hallway. He looks at the empty radiator. He looks at 608. The light under Riley's door is still on.

He goes back to 610 and lies down next to the television and doesn't sleep. The player can watch the clock on the nightstand. At 3:10, if the player walks him back out into the hall, the light under 608 has gone out.

### Breakfast

Sunday morning, the coffee shop off the lobby, Tully reading the sports. Cal is alone in a booth with eggs he isn't eating, braced; the player can see it in his shoulders.

Ellis comes in and sits down across from him.

Nobody says anything. A waitress pours Ellis coffee. No timer on the silence. Cal won't break it.

Then:

**ELLIS:** He seems all right.

Cal looks at him.

**CAL:** He is.

**ELLIS:** Can I have the ketchup.

Cal passes the ketchup.

Riley comes in a minute later with wet hair and sits down next to Cal. She looks at Ellis.

**RILEY:** You didn't come by.

**ELLIS:** I fell asleep.

She looks at him a moment longer than she needs to. Then she takes his toast.

Riley doesn't know that Ellis knows, and she knows Ellis lied about falling asleep, and doesn't know why. Nobody knows about the girl with the bucket except Ellis and, soon, Richmond.

## MISSION 13 — WHO THE HELL IS ELLIS BLAKE?

**Playable:** relay (Dean → Riley → Cal → Ellis) · **Where:** a newsstand on the Row; Cutler Street; Vale Music; the Ridge Pharmacy and Main Street, Hollow Ridge · **When:** Tuesday November 25 · **Length:** ~25 min

The January 1976 issue of *Rave* goes on sale on Tuesday, November 25, 1975. The cover is somebody much more famous. Down the left side, in yellow type:

> **WHO THE HELL IS ELLIS BLAKE?**
> **Dex Lundgren in the Georgia Hills**

Six pages, with photographs from the Civic, all of Ellis; in all but one the band has been cropped out. Each of the four finds it in their own place, and the player reads the passages that concern them, built from the answers chosen in Mission 8. Nothing is misquoted. Everything is selected.

### Dean

A newsstand on the Row. Dean, on foot now, buys six copies with most of his remaining money and reads it on the sidewalk, and for one page he's the happiest man in Tannersville. It opens with him:

> *"Blake's a genius, man," says Dean Holloway, the Blakes' drummer, a curly-headed Tannersville rich kid with a chipped tooth, a Hawaiian shirt in November and a film canister of what he calls vitamins. "I'm not kidding. He's the real thing. The rest of us are just trying to keep up."*
> *He's right about the first part.*

Dean laughs out loud. Every Dean quote is in it, exactly as said. Then the page turns to a full-page photograph of Ellis alone at the Civic, backlit, head down, over a pull-quote in large italic type:

> *"The rest of us are just trying to keep up."*

Dean stops laughing. He reads it twice. He said exactly that. He keeps five copies and gives the sixth to a man at the bus stop.

### Riley

Cutler Street. Riley at her kitchen table with the copy Dean left in her mailbox (*YOU'RE IN IT*), Gerald on the windowsill, tea going cold.

> *And then there's Riley (just Riley), the band's lone woman, a college girl in wire-rims with a twelve-string and a comeback for everything. "One of us isn't a boy," she told me, when I asked what it was like to be the girl in the band.*

That's if the player had her give the joke answer. If she talked about voice-leading and her mother and the drone for two minutes, it reads instead:

> *…a college girl whose mother plays the organ in a Presbyterian church, which may explain the choirboy harmonies she lays down under Blake's voice.*

Two minutes about counterpoint became a clause about her mother. If she said *I'm the one who can read music*, it's there word for word, followed by: *Blake can't, which tells you something about what matters.* "Sunday Clothes" is mentioned once: *the one Riley sings.*

She reads it all the way through, including the part about Grace. Then she gets the scissors and the scrapbook of reviews from under the bed and pastes in all six pages. If the player makes her stop, she puts the scissors down, and picks them up again five seconds later.

### Cal

Vale Music, lunch break, the magazine flat on the glass case. Mr. Vale reads over his shoulder, which he's never done in four years.

> *"It's ours," says bassist Cal Mercer, with the patience of a man who has said it before. He's wrong, and he knows it. This is Ellis Blake's band the way the weather is God's.*

Cal's pen taps twice on the glass, the thing he does before he speaks. He doesn't speak.

**VALE:** Well, now.

**CAL:** What?

Mr. Vale goes back to the repair bench. After a minute, without turning around:

**VALE:** Eleven years in a territory band. Papers only ever wrote down the man in front. I couldn't tell you his name now. I could tell you the bass player's.

Cal goes into the back room and fixes a radio nobody asked him to fix.

### Ellis

Hollow Ridge, 4 p.m. Ellis walks into the Ridge Pharmacy for a pack of Winstons and the soda counter goes quiet. On the wire rack by the register, where the Ridge Pharmacy has never carried *Rave* in its life, three copies are left out of ten.

**MR. CANTRELL:** You're in it.

**ELLIS:** So I hear.

He buys a copy and reads it in the Valiant with the engine running for the heater. The part the whole town already read:

> *Blake doesn't talk about his family. He doesn't have to. In Hollow Ridge, everybody talks about it for him. His mother left when he was nine. His younger sister, Grace, was killed in the spring of 1973 on a mountain road outside town, in a car her brother was driving. She was fourteen. Three people told me this in one afternoon: the barber, a woman at the pharmacy counter, and a man buying seed at the feed store, and all three told it kindly, the way you'd tell a stranger which road to take.*
>
> *Who is Clara? "Old friend," he says, and he says it the way men say the names of women who are married or dead. "This one's for Clara," he told sixteen hundred people in Laurel City, and sixteen hundred people sang it back to her, whoever she is.*
>
> *I asked him what he wants. He didn't answer for so long I thought the tape had run out.*

(Then whichever the player chose: *"To go home," he said finally. Then he lit a cigarette and looked at me like I'd stolen something.* Or *"Sleep."* Or *"I don't know," which is the most honest thing anyone has said to me this year.* Or, for silence: *He never did answer. I think that was the answer.*)

The last line:

> *The Blakes' debut album is called Borrowed Stone. The cover is a guitar leaning on a blank headstone. Nobody in the band will tell you what it means, and in Hollow Ridge, nobody has to.*

Clara is in the passenger seat, reading along. She points at her name, a finger's width above the paper.

**CLARA:** I'm in a magazine.

Ellis doesn't say anything.

**CLARA:** "Whoever she is."

She says it pleased, like a joke. Then she looks at him. He's looking at the pharmacy window, where three people at the soda counter are pretending not to look back.

**CLARA:** Quit looking at them. Drive.

No waypoint, only Clara, with her boots on the dash: "Your daddy's on the section till six. Take that home and turn it face down." Main Street is open:

- **Marlon's.** A copy behind the bar, face down. "He came in here. On the Monday. I told him we were closed." / "You weren't closed." / "I was for him."

> **Design note.** Nobody betrayed Ellis. The town knew, the way small towns know, and a man from Detroit only had to ask three times.

In Dayton, Ohio, a woman named Lorraine Hubbard reads it on Friday in a grocery-store checkout line (Mission 19).

## MISSION 14 — THE EXTRA CHAIR

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** the Riley house, Linwood, Laurel City · **When:** Thanksgiving, Thursday November 27, noon – midnight · **Length:** ~35 min

### Why he's here

Riley invited Ellis in October. He said maybe. On Tuesday night, after the article, Joan called Riley at Cutler Street and said, "Bring him," and hung up, which is not like Joan.

Wayne works Thanksgiving; the railroad doesn't stop, and the Blakes haven't done Thanksgiving since 1972 anyway. Ellis told Riley, "We don't really do it," the way you'd say you don't really do golf.

So Ellis is at the Rileys' for the long weekend. He'll sleep in Tommy's room. (Tommy's on his ship.) Tom has already made two jokes about the hallway floorboards and which ones squeak.

### The table

The dining room in Linwood. The leaf in the table. The good china with the gold rim that has been rubbed half off by forty years of washing. The player, as Riley, helps set it and knows exactly where everything goes.

Seven places. At the table: Tom, Joan, Riley, Ellis; Joan's sister, Aunt Frances, a widow and a talker; old Mr. Halvorsen from next door, eighty-one, invited every year since his wife died. And at the corner by the sideboard, a seventh place: a plate, a napkin, a glass, silverware, and an empty chair.

**ELLIS:** Who's that for?

**RILEY:** Nobody. Whoever.

**JOAN** *(bringing the rolls)*: My mother always set a place for whoever might come. You never know who's going to be on the road on a holiday.

**ELLIS:** Does anybody ever come?

**JOAN:** Mr. Halvorsen did, once.

**MR. HALVORSEN:** Nineteen sixty-eight. I've been coming ever since. They've never gotten rid of me.

### Dinner

A long, warm, playable dinner. The player, as Riley, can join conversations or rescue people from them.

- **Tom** tries a pun on Ellis at the first possible moment: *"Ellis, do you know why the turkey joined the band? Because it had the drumsticks."* Ellis, deadpan: *"That's terrible, Mr. Riley."* Tom is thrilled. Nobody in his family has groaned properly in years. They get along from that moment.
- **Aunt Frances** asks Ellis whether he's "the one in the magazine," and Riley kicks her under the table, and Aunt Frances says "*Ow*, Margaret," and the subject changes.
- **Mr. Halvorsen** tells Ellis about riding the train through Hollow Ridge in 1922, when the depot had a lunchroom and you could get a whole chicken for a quarter. Ellis is genuinely interested. He asks about the lunchroom. Riley watches him be good with an old man.
- **Joan** gives Ellis the drumstick without asking. She refills his iced tea before it's empty. She never mentions the magazine. The player, as Riley, notices every one of these things. Joan hasn't said a word about school since September either. Riley has had her answer ready all day, three versions of it, in the order she'd use them. Nobody asks.

Then:

**JOAN:** Is your mother...?

**ELLIS:** She's in Ohio.

**JOAN:** Oh.

A small pause.

**JOAN:** And your father's working today?

**ELLIS:** Yes ma'am. Railroad doesn't stop.

**JOAN:** No. It doesn't.

Riley flinches at *She's in Ohio*. It's Wayne's sentence, the only one he ever gave his children about their mother, and Ellis says it in his father's exact rhythm.

### The chair

The camera is Riley's all through dinner, which means the chair at the corner by the sideboard is empty all through dinner.

But Ellis keeps glancing at it. Once when the turkey comes in. Once when Aunt Frances says something about young people these days. Once, during grace (Tom's, short, with a pun in it), when everybody's head is bowed, the player can have Riley open her eyes and look, and Ellis's eyes are open, and he's looking at the empty chair, and there's the corner of a smile on his face, like somebody at a table who has just seen a friend do something rude and funny.

Nobody else at the table notices a thing.

> **Replay layer.** From Ellis's side, Clara is in the seventh chair, in her jacket, with her boots up on the rung of the chair and a roll she isn't eating. Joan's mother set a place for whoever might come. Somebody came.

### The article in the hall

After dinner, Riley goes to the hall closet for Mr. Halvorsen's coat and finds her father's copy of *Rave* on the hall table under the mail. Tom has read it. In pencil, in the margin next to *the band's lone woman*, in the careful block capitals of a man who has graded chemistry lab reports for twenty-six years, he's written:

> HARMONY IS NOT DECORATION

and underlined it twice.

Riley stands in the hall with it. The player can have her laugh, or not. Either way she puts it back exactly where it was.

### The piano

The dishes. Joan washes; Riley dries; Mr. Halvorsen asleep in Tom's recliner in front of the football; Tom and Aunt Frances arguing about the Bicentennial.

Ellis drifts into the living room. The upright piano. On the music rack, under the Presbyterian hymnal and a book of Bach chorales, an oblong maroon songbook with the same spine as one on a shelf in the Blake house: *The Sacred Harp.*

Ellis picks it up. He turns pages. He doesn't have to look for anything; he knows where things are in it.

Joan comes in from the kitchen, drying her hands.

**JOAN:** Do you know those?

**ELLIS:** Some. Most of them, I reckon.

**JOAN:** I went to a singing once. Nineteen sixty-eight, in Alabama. A church in a field. I bought the book at the table and I've never been able to make them work.

**ELLIS:** They don't work on piano.

**JOAN:** Then how do they work?

He thinks about it.

**ELLIS:** Loud.

Joan laughs, surprised.

**ELLIS:** Everybody in a square, facing in. My grandmama's church had a pine floor, and when the basses came in you could feel it through your shoes. You sing the shapes first. Then the words.

**JOAN:** Show me.

The player, as Riley, is in the kitchen doorway with the dish towel. She's been here before, in September, watching her mother at this piano. The camera holds on her.

Ellis opens the book to a page he doesn't need to look at. He sings the tenor line, which in this music is the melody, first in the shape-note syllables his grandmother taught him (*fa sol la fa sol la mi fa*), plain and loud, not performing, the way you'd sing in a field.

Joan leans over the book, and finds the treble line with her finger, and reads it, and on the second time through she sings it with him. She's a trained church musician and she reads fast, and she's never sung like this in her life: open fifths, full voice, nobody to please.

Riley watches her mother light up.

The player can have Riley join them. There's an alto line in the book. If she comes in, it's three parts in a Linwood living room, and on the next time through Ellis switches to the words:

*Brethren, we have met to worship*
*and adore the Lord our God…*

If she doesn't join, she stands in the doorway and watches, and that's complete too.

At the end, Mr. Halvorsen has woken up and is applauding from the recliner. Tom is in the other doorway holding a gravy boat. Joan has her hand flat on the open book, and she's a little out of breath, and she says, to nobody in particular:

**JOAN:** Well.

It's the same *Well* she said on the phone in June when Riley declined Edinburgh. It means something completely different.

> **Design note.** A boy with no mother and a woman with a daughter who's leaving the life she chose for her, singing a hymn from a book neither of their churches uses, in a room where there's an empty chair. Nobody says anything that could be quoted.

### Tommy's room

Midnight. Riley in her childhood bedroom (a Joni Mitchell poster, the constellations on the ceiling in glow-in-the-dark stars her father put up when she was ten). Ellis across the hall in Tommy's room. The house asleep. On the inside cover of her notebook, under *occasionally astonishing*, she writes one more word: *whoever*.

The player can stay in bed, or cross the hall. The floorboards Tom warned about are real; the player has to step around them, and if they don't, one of them creaks and they freeze and wait to hear if anyone stirs.

If she crosses: Tommy's room, Navy pennants, a model of the *Enterprise*, the carrier. Ellis is awake, sitting up in a single bed with his back against the wall. She sits on the end of it. They talk in whispers.

**RILEY:** Was she at dinner?

A pause.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**RILEY:** Where?

**ELLIS:** The chair.

**RILEY:** The extra one.

**ELLIS:** Your mama set a place.

Riley puts her hand over her mouth so the laugh won't wake anybody. Then it isn't a laugh anymore, and she keeps her hand there.

**RILEY:** Did she behave?

**ELLIS:** She put her feet on the rung.

**RILEY:** Mama would've hated that.

**ELLIS:** She knew.

A long quiet. Somewhere downstairs a clock.

**RILEY:** We said we'd talk to somebody.

**ELLIS:** You said.

**RILEY:** Ellis.

**ELLIS:** She sat in a chair, Riley. She didn't do anything.

The player chooses what Riley does with that: push (*"Define 'anything.'"*), let it go, or say the true thing (*"I'm not scared of her. I'm scared of what happens to you."*). Whichever the player picks, Ellis's answer is the same, very tired:

**ELLIS:** After New York.

**RILEY:** After New York what?

**ELLIS:** After New York I'll talk to somebody.

She knows it's a promise he doesn't intend to keep. She takes it anyway, because he has never put this in the future tense before.

She goes back across the hall. She misses a floorboard. It creaks. From Tom and Joan's room, very faintly, Tom's voice: *"Goodnight, Maggie."*

> **Tracked.** "After New York" is the second promise in the game Ellis makes knowing he might not keep it (the first was "Promise," to Clara, in Chapter IV). Riley brings it up in Chapter VIII.

## MISSION 15 — HOLD THE LIGHT

**Playable:** Ellis → Riley · **Where:** US 19 in the Laurel Gap; the Blake house · **When:** Sunday November 30, 9:40 – 11:55 p.m. · **Length:** ~15 min

### Rain

The drive back from Linwood. Riley's Datsun, Riley driving, Ellis in the passenger seat, the leftovers Joan packed in a grocery sack at his feet. It started raining at the county line and it's coming down hard now on the two-lane through the Laurel Gap, the wipers not keeping up.

The player is Ellis, and there's nothing to do but be a passenger: his right hand on the door handle, his left forearm, which he's rubbing without knowing it (it aches before rain). Every set of oncoming headlights, the camera tightens a little.

Riley notices. She slows down from fifty to forty without saying anything. He notices her notice. Neither of them mentions it.

### The dip

A low place in the road a few miles east of the Starlite where the ditch floods. A pulpwood truck comes the other way fast and throws a sheet of water across the Datsun's hood, and the engine coughs, and catches, and coughs, and dies.

Riley coasts it onto the shoulder. Flashers on. Rain on the roof like gravel.

**RILEY:** Well, shit.

**ELLIS:** Pop the hood.

**RILEY:** It's pouring.

**ELLIS:** It's wet. That's the whole problem. Pop the hood.

He gets out into it.

### The engine

The diagnostic system from Roy's garage, in the rain, at night, by flashlight: listen (Riley cranks it; it turns over fine and won't catch), smell (no gasoline; not flooded), look.

**ELLIS:** Get the flashlight out of the glovebox. Come here.

She comes around with the flashlight and her father's peacoat over her head.

The player finds it: water inside the distributor cap, which on this Datsun sits low and forward where the splash hits it. Ellis unclips the cap. He needs something dry. The player can use his shirt tail (soaked), Joan's dish towel from the leftovers sack (the right answer), or the peacoat lining (Riley will object).

**ELLIS:** Hold the light.

She holds it. It wanders, because she's watching him instead of the engine.

**ELLIS:** Down. Left. There. Hold it still.

He's drying the inside of the cap, the rotor, the plug wires one at a time, working fast with his whole body hunched over the engine bay to keep the rain off it, the way you'd shield a match.

**ELLIS:** I love you, hold it still.

He doesn't look up. He doesn't hear himself. He keeps working.

The flashlight beam doesn't move a quarter of an inch for the rest of the job.

### High beams

Around the bend, a pickup comes the other way with its high beams on.

The whole engine bay goes white. Ellis's hands stop. He straightens up, too fast, and his wrist hits the radiator hose, and he turns toward the light with the distributor cap in his hand and doesn't move.

The pickup goes by in a hiss of spray. Its taillights go around the bend.

Ellis is still standing there.

She puts her hand flat on his back, through the wet shirt.

**RILEY:** Hey. Ellis.

He comes back. He looks at the cap in his hand like he's not sure how it got there.

**ELLIS:** I'm fine.

He bends back over the engine.

> **Setup.** A pickup with high beams on a two-lane road at night in the rain is what came around Tolliver Bend on April 12, 1973. Ellis doesn't remember that yet. His body does. In Chapter X a light hits his eyes on a stage.

### Switch

Stutter. The camera slides off Ellis and settles on Riley, and the player is holding the flashlight.

From here: the rain, the hood, the back of Ellis's wet shirt, his hands working in the circle of light that she's keeping perfectly still. The player, as Riley, can't move the light (if they try, she doesn't). She's smiling in the rain and trying not to.

The player gets the dialogue grammar:

- **"What did you say?"** Ellis: "Hold it still." / Riley: "Before that." / Ellis, not looking up, after a second: "I don't know. Hold it still." His ears have gone red. He knows.
- **"Ellis."** He says "What," and she says "Nothing," and he says "Then hold it still."
- **Silence.** She holds the light and doesn't say anything, and neither does he.

The distributor cap goes back on. He clips it.

**ELLIS:** Try it.

She gets in and turns the key. It catches on the second try.

### The porch

They drive the rest of the way to Hollow Ridge with the heater on high and the windows fogging and Joan's dish towel (if that's what he used) steaming on the dashboard. Nobody says anything about anything. Riley drives forty. The rain quits before the Hollow Ridge line.

Cold Branch Road. The Blake house. The one bulb over the porch is on. Wayne's truck is in the yard.

Ellis gets out and leans back down into the open door.

**ELLIS:** Call me when you get home.

**RILEY:** It's an hour.

**ELLIS:** Call me anyway.

She drives off. Behind the screen door, in the dark front room, Wayne says it to nobody in particular:

**WAYNE:** It's gonna rain.

The sky over Cold Branch Road is clear to the ridge. Ellis stands on the porch until her taillights are gone.

### 11:52

The Blake hall. Ellis, in dry clothes, sitting on the floor next to the telephone table with his back against the wall, not pretending to be doing anything else. Tater asleep across his legs.

The phone rings at 11:52 and he has it before the first ring's over.

**RILEY** *(on the phone)*: I'm home.

**ELLIS:** Okay.

A pause on the line. The player, as Ellis, can say something or not. The options are all small: *"Good."* / *"Was it raining there?"* / silence.

Then Riley. This choice belongs to her, and the switch goes to her end of the call: Cutler Street, the kitchen, the wall phone, her hair still wet. On the table, her mother's Martin with a capo on the second fret, and the page she's been working on since Chattanooga: a new ending for "Sunday Clothes," where the band drops out and her voice holds the last line alone. She wants to try it in New York. The options:

- **"I love you too."**
- **"Goodnight, Ellis."**
- **Silence**, until he says goodnight.

Whichever she chooses, the switch goes back to the hall, where Ellis hangs up and sits there a while longer.

Down the hall, Wayne's bedroom door has been open a crack. It closes, quietly.

> **Tracked.** Whether Riley said it back. It isn't mentioned again until the epilogue, in one of the notebook poems addressed to "M.," which differs by one line depending on what she said.

## MISSION 16 — LOUD

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** the Blake kitchen; the Lantern, Laurel City · **When:** Wednesday December 3 (kitchen); Friday December 5 (show) · **Length:** ~25 min

### A ticket

Wednesday. Eddie has mailed four guest tickets for Friday's Lantern show, the homecoming before New York. The player can leave one on the kitchen table, on the coupon book (*Coupon 33 of 48*). If they do, it's gone in the morning. If they don't, Wayne comes anyway: Roy buys two at the box office and hands one to Wayne at the depot without explanation.

### The Lantern

Friday. The Lantern, eight hundred sold, their room, smaller than it used to feel, and better. Theo is back at his old desk, mixing his own band.

Clara is in the stage-left wing for the first three songs, sitting on an amp case, kicking her heels against it. Between songs she tells him to stand up straight and to tune his high E, which has gone flat.

During "Ice Machine," with *attention*, the player can look out past the lights to the back of the room, by Theo's desk.

Wayne Blake is standing there, in his work khakis and his hat, with eight hundred kids giving him three feet of room without knowing why. Theo, without looking up from the board, holds out two cotton balls. Wayne puts one in his left ear and holds the other in his hand for the rest of the night.

When Ellis looks back at the stage-left wing, Clara is gone.

### "Borrowed Stone"

"Borrowed Stone" is fourth. With Wayne in the room, the player gets a choice the band will never understand: Ellis can lift his headstock toward Cal, the *change coming* signal, and call something else. Cal will follow, and not know why. Or Ellis can play it:

*There's a book on the table with the pages torn out,*
*one for every month since the rain.*
*Daddy pays on a stone like you'd pay on a truck.*
*Nineteen more and it's ours.*

At the back of the room, Wayne is looking at the floor. When the song ends he's still looking at it.

(There are fifteen coupons left, not nineteen. The song was written in August. It's already out of date.)

### Loud

The loading dock, 12:40 a.m. Wayne is leaning on the F-100's fender with a Camel. Ellis comes out with a towel around his neck.

**ELLIS:** Well?

**WAYNE:** Loud.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

A long pause. Wayne smokes.

**WAYNE:** I liked it.

The player gets no prompt for a second, and then one, and it's only silence.

**WAYNE:** That drummer.

**ELLIS:** Dean.

**WAYNE:** Your sister would've hated him.

Ellis almost laughs.

**ELLIS:** She'd have married him.

**WAYNE:** That's what I mean.

Now Ellis does laugh. Wayne almost does. He looks down the alley.

**WAYNE:** Read your magazine.

**ELLIS:** It's not my magazine.

**WAYNE:** That fellow ever comes to Hollow Ridge again, I'll put him in the creek.

He says it flat, without heat.

### The girl

Wayne steps on the cigarette. He doesn't get in the truck.

**WAYNE:** That girl.

**ELLIS:** What girl.

**WAYNE:** In the song. The one on the radio.

A pause.

**WAYNE:** She still around?

The player chooses Ellis's answer:

- **"Sometimes."** Wayne nods slowly, as though that's the answer he expected, and the one he was afraid of.
- **"Why?"** Wayne says, "No reason," and it's so obviously a lie that Ellis stares at him.
- **"No."** Wayne looks at him for a long moment and says, "Hm." He doesn't believe it.
- **Silence.** Wayne waits, and then nods as if Ellis had said *sometimes*.

Then Wayne reaches out and fixes his son's collar, where the jacket's folded under from the towel. It takes a second.

**WAYNE:** You eat anything?

**ELLIS:** Not yet. Fixing to.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

He gets in the truck and drives off down the alley.

Later, in the van, Ellis reaches in his jacket pocket for his cigarettes and finds a twenty-dollar bill folded in quarters that wasn't there before. He made $378 in one night in November. Wayne makes about $280 a week. Ellis puts it back. He doesn't spend it. (It's still on him in Chapter X.)

> **Design note.** Wayne leaves money where it can be found: the heater, the half a tire, the Engineers tickets. Tonight he does it with his hands, disguised as fixing a collar.

## MISSION 17 — TWENTY-ONE DATES

**Playable:** Cal · **Where:** the back room at Vale Music, Tannersville · **When:** Saturday December 6, 2:00 p.m. · **Length:** ~20 min

### The meeting

The back room at Vale Music, where they rehearsed in the winter: the repair bench, the space heater, Mr. Vale out front with a customer. Eddie called the meeting and has a folder. Tully's in the doorway.

**EDDIE:** It's done. Sawtooth. Twenty-one dates, January fourteenth through February fifteenth. Detroit, Cleveland, Toledo, Chicago, Milwaukee, the whole winter. Arenas, eight to fifteen thousand. You open. Thirty-five minutes a night. And New York next Friday, which you know about.

Dean stands up.

**DEAN:** *Arenas.*

**RILEY:** Wait. What's done?

**EDDIE:** The deal. It's signed.

**CAL:** Who signed it?

Eddie looks at Ellis.

**EDDIE:** Ellis said yes Tuesday. I called the house. They needed an answer by Wednesday or it went to somebody from Chicago.

Everyone looks at Ellis.

### Tuesday

**RILEY:** When did you say yes?

**ELLIS:** Tuesday.

**RILEY:** It's Saturday.

**ELLIS:** I know what day it is.

**CAL:** We have a rule.

He doesn't have to say which one. It's the one he made, in the van, in May: no band decisions alone.

**ELLIS:** He needed an answer. You were all out. I called Riley. I called Dean at your place. I called you.

**CAL:** Be specific.

A pause.

**ELLIS:** You weren't home. Tuesday.

Cal looks at him. Ellis looks back. The player, as Cal, understands exactly what Ellis could say next, and that he isn't saying it.

The player gets a prompt to press the point anyway (*"You could have waited."*). If they do, Ellis says, "Till when?" and Cal has no answer that doesn't end in Laurel City. If they don't, Cal lets it go. Either way:

**CAL:** Fine. Tuesday.

**DEAN:** Who cares? It's *arenas.* Richard's going to have to read about it in the paper.

### The list

**EDDIE:** Thirty-five minutes. Seven songs, eight if they're short. Ellis already gave me a list.

He reads it off a page in his folder.

**EDDIE:** "No Name." "Low Water." "Borrowed Stone." "Stony Knob." "Still Here." "Ice Machine." "Shape Note," cut down.

A silence in which everyone does the arithmetic of what isn't on it.

**DEAN:** Where's "Tomorrow Problem"?

**ELLIS:** It's a joke.

**DEAN:** It's the one they sing.

**ELLIS:** They sing it because it's a joke.

**RILEY:** Where's "Sunday Clothes"?

**ELLIS:** Thirty-five minutes, Riley.

**RILEY:** "Shape Note" is nine.

**ELLIS:** We'll cut it to six.

**RILEY:** You'd rather play six minutes of drone to fifteen thousand people waiting for Sawtooth than my song.

**ELLIS:** They're not waiting for us. They don't know who we are. We've got thirty-five minutes to make them remember. The real ones do that.

**RILEY:** The real ones.

**ELLIS:** You know what I mean.

**RILEY:** Say what you mean.

The player, as Cal, can offer a practical fix (*"We can fit eight if we cut the drone to four."*). Nobody hears it.

**ELLIS:** Mine cost something.

The space heater ticks.

Riley goes very still. Dean looks at the floor. Tully, in the doorway, looks at Ellis and then out at the store.

### The sentence

Now the player gets Cal's choice, and it's a timing choice: speak now, or hold.

If the player holds, Ellis keeps going, because nobody stopped him, and it gets worse:

**ELLIS:** You want to play a song about curfew in a dormitory, fine. Dean wants to yell about his homework, fine. Somebody wrote six pages about what my songs are about, and it wasn't curfew.

And then Cal speaks anyway.

If the player speaks, Cal says it before Ellis can say the rest. Either way it's the same sentence, said quietly, the way Cal says everything:

**CAL:** Pain doesn't automatically make everything you do important.

Nobody moves.

**ELLIS:** You don't know anything about it.

And here the player, as Cal, gets the one prompt that could end the argument: *Tell him what Marlon told you.*

The player can press it. Cal opens his mouth.

**CAL:** I know more than you—

He stops. The prompt dims and goes out. He promised Marlon. The player can press it again and nothing happens.

**CAL:** No. I don't.

Ellis picks up his jacket and walks out through the store. The bell on Vale's front door rings behind him. Mr. Vale, at the counter with a customer, doesn't turn around.

> **Design note.** Cal is right and cruel at once, and he's holding back the one fact that would prove Ellis wrong in a different way, because he gave his word to a man at a bar at two in the morning.

### After

The back room. The heater ticking. Eddie with his folder.

**EDDIE:** So. Is it a yes?

Nobody answers.

**EDDIE:** I'm going to take that as a yes.

**RILEY:** It's a yes.

Everyone looks at her.

**RILEY:** It's a yes. We're going. But he doesn't get to write the set list.

**CAL:** I'll write it.

**RILEY:** I know you will.

She leaves too, the back way, into the alley.

Dean sits down on an amp box next to Cal. Tully goes out front and asks Mr. Vale about a guitar he doesn't want, to give them the room.

**DEAN** *(quietly)*: He's right about one thing. "Tomorrow Problem" is a joke.

**CAL:** It's a good joke.

Dean thinks about that.

**DEAN:** It's a really good joke.

> **The van promise, first breach.** In May the four of them made four promises in the van. Tonight two of them broke: *no band decisions alone* (Cal's, broken by Ellis on Tuesday) and *no treating each other like employees* (Riley's, broken by Ellis this afternoon, with a set list). The other two break in Chapter VIII.

## MISSION 18 — FOUR ROOMS

**Playable:** relay, Ellis → Cal → Riley → Dean → Ellis · **Where:** Hollow Ridge; east Tannersville; Cutler Street; the Starlite · **When:** Saturday December 6, 11:40 p.m. – 2:10 a.m. · **Length:** ~15 min

### The grammar

Tonight the four of them are in four places across the map, not speaking, and the switch has nothing to carry it but the railroad: one line, the Laurel City yards to east Tannersville, with freights running it all night. In each room a whistle sounds, and on the whistle the camera lets go of one person and finds the next, forty or ninety miles away, where another whistle is sounding on the same line.

> **Design note.** Wayne Blake maintains a stretch of that track.

### Ellis

Hollow Ridge, 11:40 p.m. Wayne asleep. The green notebook on the dresser, still blank. Ellis is at the desk with Patty's violet envelope, which Dean gave him on the Richmond run; the fold has gone soft. The player can read it.

> *Dear Ellis,*
> *Dean doesn't know I'm writing this. If he's reading it over your shoulder, hi Dean, you're an idiot.*
> *I listen to "No Name" a lot. The one about the kitchen light. I know it isn't about my brother. But since he moved out Mama leaves the porch light on all night, every night, and Daddy turns it off when he goes up and she comes back down and turns it on again, and neither of them says anything.*
> *I just wanted to ask: in the song, do they come back? You never say.*
> *Patty Holloway*
> *P.S. I beat you at pinball fair and square.*

Then the player writes the answer with him, line by line, like the songwriting system but plainer. The central choice is whether to tell her the truth about the song.

If he tells her:

> *It's about my mother. She left when I was nine. She didn't come back. I wrote it when I was sixteen and I don't know if the person in the song comes back either. I'm sorry that's not a better answer.*
> *Your brother's different. He's got nowhere else to eat on Sundays.*

If he doesn't:

> *I don't know. I never finished figuring that out.*
> *Your brother will come back. He's got nowhere else to eat on Sundays.*

Either way it ends:

> *Tell your mama to leave the light on.*
> *You did not beat me fair and square. The left flipper sticks.*
> *Ellis*

Clara is on the floor with her back against the bed, reading it upside down.

**CLARA** *(in his voice)*: "Thirty-five minutes, Riley."

He keeps writing.

**CLARA:** Bet you don't even need them.

He stops. He looks at her.

**ELLIS:** Yes I do.

Clara starts humming, low, under nothing. Then she gets up off the floor.

**CLARA:** Eat something.

She goes out into the dark hall, still humming. Below the town, a freight blows for the Depot Street crossing.

> **Setup.** "Yes I do" is Ellis choosing the band over Clara, out loud. He says it annoyed.

### Cal

The whistle carries the camera forty-five miles east, to the lit window of Mercer Radio & TV across from the east Tannersville yard.

Midnight. Cal at his father's bench, because Dean's asleep on the cot at his place and he couldn't be in that room. Walt came down in his bathrobe when he saw the light and has been handing Cal tools for forty minutes before Cal asks for them. The player works on a Philco with a bad filter capacitor. The player can try to make Cal say something; the options are all things Cal would never say out loud (*"I told Ellis his pain doesn't make him important. In front of all of them."*), and if one is chosen, Cal opens his mouth, and closes it, and asks for the long-nose pliers instead. Walt hands them over.

**WALT:** Your mother read the magazine.

**CAL:** Yeah.

**WALT:** She was mad.

**CAL:** At who?

**WALT:** The man that wrote it.

A pause. Walt solders a joint.

**WALT:** You want to stay here tonight? Your room's made up.

**CAL:** Yeah.

**WALT:** She'll make pancakes.

**CAL:** I know.

The Philco comes on under their hands, dial light first. Across the street a yard engine blows two shorts.

### Riley

The whistle carries it across town to the trestle below the college, and up to a second-floor kitchen on Cutler Street. 1:00 a.m. Riley at the table with her mother's Martin and a spiral notebook, Gerald on the windowsill.

She's writing a song, and it's about neither Ellis nor her mother. The first line on the page is the phrase from the inside cover of her notebook: *Occasionally astonishing.* The player works on it with her, in open D, moving words around. It's a song about being the thing people notice only sometimes, and it's funny, and it's angry in a way she can't be out loud yet.

> **Setup.** "Occasionally Astonishing" is the first song on Riley's first solo record, in 1979.

At some point the player can have her dial the Blake house. Three rings. A sleep-thick voice: "Hm?" It's Wayne. Riley doesn't say anything. "Hello?" twice. She hangs up, and writes the second line, while the freight's horn goes on and on across the trestle.

### Dean

**DEAN:** It's a rhythm.

**LYNETTE:** Go home.

## MISSION 19 — NORTH

**Playable:** all four (rotating) · **Where:** the van, I-81 through Virginia (off-map); New York City (off-map): a hotel on lower Lexington Avenue, a steakhouse in midtown, a showcase club on Bleecker Street, a bar on the Bowery, a secondhand store on St. Marks Place · **When:** Tuesday December 9 – Saturday December 13 · **Length:** ~60 min

### The receipt

Tuesday, 3:30 p.m., the alley behind Vale Music, the van and Tully's truck loaded. They haven't all been in one place since Saturday, and nobody's mentioned Saturday. The player is Cal, at the wheel. Ellis climbs in last and, before he goes to the wheel well, puts the folded Western Auto receipt on the dashboard in front of Cal.

Cal unfolds it, then takes his own list out of his shirt pocket and lays them side by side on the dash. The same seven songs. Cal's is in a different order, and cuts "Shape Note" to five minutes, not four.

Cal folds Ellis's receipt and puts it in the ledger. He puts his own back in his pocket.

**CAL:** Five minutes.

**ELLIS:** Four.

**CAL:** Five.

**ELLIS:** Whatever Cal says.

**DEAN** *(from the back)*: Exactly.

Dean has seen *TOMORROW PROBLEM* on the dash from his bench. He starts drumming on the wall, and Riley, in the passenger seat, who has seen *SUNDAY CLOTHES*, opens her crossword and says to it:

**RILEY:** Occasionally astonishing.

Cal pulls out of the alley.

### Night driving

North off the edge of the map, then Virginia: the long valley between the ridges, I-81, dark by six.

A truck stop near Wytheville at 11 p.m. Cal has driven eight hours. He's falling asleep at the pumps.

**ELLIS:** I'll drive.

Cal looks at him. The player, as Cal, has the choice, and Cal knows what happened the last time Ellis drove someone at night.

- **Give him the keys.**
- **"I'm fine."** Ellis waits. Cal nearly falls asleep standing up. Riley says, "Cal. Let him." The prompt comes back with one option in it.

Cal gives him the keys.

The switch goes to Ellis inside, paying for gas. A trucker in a quilted vest sees the Georgia plates and the look on Ellis's face and slides something across the counter in a paper napkin.

**TRUCKER:** Four hundred miles?

**ELLIS:** About.

**TRUCKER:** Stay awake, stay alive.

Two small white tablets, scored with a cross.

If Ellis takes one, the drive is sharp and bright and loud inside his head, his thoughts a little too fast. If he leaves them ("I'm all right." / "Suit yourself."), the drive has a drowsiness system: the lane lines swim, and the player has to catch it. Neither changes the story. Tracked.

### Keep going

I-81 at night in December. Dry, cold, clear. Stars over the ridges. The heater that only works on the passenger side. The van at exactly fifty-five miles an hour, both of Ellis's hands on the wheel.

Behind him: Dean asleep on his bench with his mouth open. Cal asleep sitting up on the amplifiers in back, arms folded. Riley asleep in the passenger seat against the window with her crossword on her chest. Tully's taillights a quarter-mile ahead.

On the engine cover between the front seats, Clara is sitting with her boots up on the dashboard.

She's quiet for a long time. The player drives. About eight real minutes, no cut.

**CLARA:** You're driving.

**ELLIS** *(barely audible)*: Yeah.

**CLARA:** At night.

**ELLIS:** It's not raining.

**CLARA:** You've got all of them in here.

He looks in the mirror at Dean's open mouth. At Riley.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** Eyes on the road.

He looks back at the road. So does she.

**CLARA:** Keep going.

> **Design note.** Clara never says "go on." When she says "keep going," it's always in a car.

Near Staunton, a little after two, she tells him it's Wednesday now and to quit yawning. At 3:12 a.m., somewhere past Harrisonburg, the player can tune the radio (the van's FM converter, the one Vale installed). A station out of Washington, faint, a late-night DJ with a soft voice:

**DJ** *(on the radio)*: …a band out of Georgia I don't know one thing about, except that my phone won't stop ringing when I play it. So here it is again.

"Still Here," through one speaker in the dash, four hundred miles from home, in a state none of them has ever set foot in.

Ellis reaches over and turns it down, so it won't wake them.

### The skyline

Dawn on the New Jersey Turnpike: refineries, gas flares, marsh, gulls, a gray sky, and then, all at once, off to the right across the flats, Manhattan.

Dean wakes up and screams, and everybody wakes up.

**RILEY:** Oh my God.

**CAL:** Is that it?

**DEAN:** *That's it!*

Ellis, driving, doesn't look for a long time, because he's driving. Then he does.

The Lincoln Tunnel. Tile walls, fumes, the long curve. Then light, and they're in it.

### The city

New York is an off-map mission space, a compact slice of lower Manhattan and Midtown, loud and cold, playable on foot as any of the four during free time on Wednesday and Thursday:

- **The hotel.** The Albemarle, lower Lexington Avenue, cheap, radiators that bang all night. The desk clerk reads GEORGIA on Ellis's registration card and hums the banjo part from *Deliverance*. Dean hums the guitar part back, perfectly, and the clerk laughs and gives them a better room.
- **Riley** at a newsstand on Astor Place that has every magazine in the world. She buys *Ms.* and turns *Rave* around to face the wall. Then she walks up Fifth Avenue to a church where an organist is practicing on a weekday afternoon and sits in a back pew for all of it, Bach to an empty nave, so she can tell her mother.
- **Ellis** walking. Clara with him, delighted, in the crowds on Fourteenth Street. *"Nobody looks at anybody here,"* she says. *"I like it. Button your coat."* She points him at strangers (*"Look at that one, with the cake box on her head"*), and the Observe cue comes up where she's pointing; when he turns the wrong way, *"Not that way. Hotel's behind you."* The notebook fills with strangers.

### Lunch

Thursday, a Midtown steakhouse with old waiters. Harold Vance flew up, and so did a man from Monarch Records, the New York major Southern Star wants for national distribution.

**Gil Tarver**, thirty-five, a good suit, a better haircut, and he's done his homework. He calls Riley "Margaret" because that's what's on the copyright, and then "Riley" the moment she corrects him, and never slips again. He asks Cal real questions about publishing and listens to the answers. He tells Ellis he's played "Borrowed Stone" for his mother. He's very, very good.

After, at the coat check, the player is Cal, waiting for his borrowed overcoat, and hears Gil around the corner with Vance, low:

**GIL:** The franchise is the singer, Harold. The rest is packaging. Good packaging. But packaging.

Cal takes his coat. Gil comes around the corner and sees him, and his smile doesn't change at all.

**GIL:** Cal! Great to meet you. Great questions today.

Cal tells nobody.

> **Setup.** Gil Tarver and the key-man clause, Chapter VIII. "The franchise" is what he'll call Ellis to his face by February.

### The showcase

Friday, December 12. A 400-seat room on Bleecker Street, and three hundred people who are paid to decide things: Monarch's staff, radio people, agents, critics, a man from a TV show. Everybody has a drink. Nobody has a reason to like anybody.

The Room runs cold. With Dean's *read the room* it's a flat gray field, people talking through the first song loud enough to hear from the stage. The things that won the Civic don't register.

Dean's field shows three warm points: a table of waitresses by the kitchen door; Dex Lundgren at the bar with his sunglasses on; and a woman alone at a two-top near the back with a notebook who is watching Riley and nobody else.

> **Character introduced.** **Nina Sorensen**, twenty-nine, critic at a New York weekly. She'll write the first serious piece about Riley as a musician in Chapter VIII.

The trick, unprompted, is to stop playing to the room. If the player uses *attention* to face the band instead of the crowd, the others follow into the hollow square, playing to each other as if it were two in the morning at Dalton Sound, and the gray field begins, slowly, to warm from the back where Nina's sitting.

The set is the one on the receipt and on Cal's list:

- **"No Name,"** into **"Low Water."** The room keeps talking.
- **"Sunday Clothes."** Riley's lead. This is where it turns, if the player has the band facing inward: Nina Sorensen puts her pen down; the talking at the front tables stops, then the bar. The player, as Riley, controls her phrasing and harmony choices, and the song, in this room, is entirely hers. If the player wants it, she takes the new ending from Cutler Street: the band drops out, and the room holds still for her last line.
- **"Still Here."** Before it, the player, as Ellis, can say "This one's for Clara," or not. (If he does, Dex, at the bar, grins into his drink.)
- **"Tomorrow Problem."** The waitresses by the kitchen door know it from the Washington station and shout the tomorrow part, and the bartender joins them, and a man from Monarch looks around and then shouts it too, embarrassed.
- **"Stony Knob."**
- **"Shape Note,"** five minutes, because Cal said.

At the end, Gil Tarver, at the back by the door, says one word to Harold Vance that the player can see and can't hear: *Sold.*

### The Bowery

After midnight, Dean takes them to "the place Dex told me about," a long narrow bar on the Bowery with a stage the size of Marlon's.

A band is playing: four boys in leather jackets and torn jeans, songs ninety seconds long, three chords, as loud and fast as they can, no solos, no drone, no space. The Blakes stand in the back with their coats on.

The player can be anyone:

- **Ellis** goes to the front and stands there for the whole set without moving. It's the first music he's heard in a year that isn't reaching for anything.
- **Cal:** "They're playing three chords." / **Ellis** (if nearby): "They're playing them like they mean it."
- **Riley** likes it more than she expected and less than Ellis does.

At the bar afterward, a skinny kid in a leather jacket with a torn T-shirt under it looks at Riley's peasant blouse and Ellis's brown corduroy jacket.

**KID:** Nice. Hippies.

**ELLIS:** You play in that band?

**KID:** Bass.

**CAL:** You're rushing.

**KID:** What?

**CAL:** On the second song. You're rushing the changes.

A pause.

**KID:** Yeah. I know.

They end up buying each other beers until two.

### The jacket

Saturday morning, New York December cold with a wind off the river, and the brown corduroy jacket Ellis has worn since Chapter I isn't enough. A secondhand store on St. Marks Place, down four steps. The player can try on anything. A sheepskin makes Riley laugh so hard she has to sit down on the steps.

And a black leather jacket, secondhand, a little big in the shoulders, soft at the elbows, smelling faintly of someone else's cigarettes. Eighteen dollars.

It fits.

**RILEY:** Well, look at you.

**ELLIS:** Don't.

**RILEY:** You look like one of those kids last night.

**ELLIS:** I look warm.

**RILEY:** Fair.

He pays out of his Civic money. Before the corduroy goes into a paper sack he goes through its pockets: Winstons, a carpenter's pencil, and Wayne's twenty, still folded in quarters. He moves it to the inside pocket of the leather.

### The mail

Saturday, noon. The coffee shop off the Albemarle lobby. Eddie comes in with a rubber-banded stack of fan mail Southern Star forwarded to the hotel: forty-odd letters, most of them from girls, some from boys who want to know how to get "that sound," one from a prisoner in Alabama.

He drops it on the table in front of Ellis.

**EDDIE:** Your public.

Ellis flips through them with one hand, eating toast with the other. Clara is across the booth from him with her boots on the seat. *"Eat your eggs,"* she says. He eats the toast.

Then he stops.

A plain white envelope. Blue ink. A looping, careful hand the player hasn't seen before. (They will again, in Chapter IX, on two birthday cards in a cigar box.)

> *Ellis Blake*
> *c/o Southern Star Records*
> *Laurel City, Georgia*

A small printed return-address label: *L. HUBBARD · 118 MAYFAIR DR · DAYTON, OHIO 45405.* Postmark: DAYTON OH, NOV 29 1975.

When Ellis looks up from it, the other side of the booth is empty. Clara hasn't gone out a door or around a corner, the way she usually leaves. She isn't in the booth anymore. Her help goes with her: until the Bowery, nobody tells the player where to look or which way to walk.

The player gets three options:

- **Open it.** Ellis turns it over. He gets his thumb under the flap. He stops. The prompt dims. He puts it in the inside pocket of the leather jacket.
- **Throw it away.** He drops it in the trash can by the register, gets as far as the door, goes back, and takes it out. There's a coffee ring on one corner. He wipes it on his jeans and puts it in the inside pocket of the leather jacket.
- **Put it in his pocket.** He puts it in the inside pocket of the leather jacket.

Nobody at the table notices anything. Dean is reading a letter from a girl in Tampa out loud in a Southern-belle voice. Eddie is doing the check.

> **Knowledge.** Lorraine Mathis Blake Hubbard read the *Rave* article on Friday, November 28, in a grocery checkout line in Dayton. She read in a magazine what she'd heard at the back of a church. She wrote him that weekend. Ellis never opens the letter. At his funeral, Wayne gives it back to her unopened, and she takes it. What it says is never shown.

### The photograph

Saturday, 3:40 p.m. The Bowery. The brick wall with the torn posters. Snow starting, the dry kind.

The cold open plays again, all the way through this time, and now the player knows why he's in a leather jacket, and what's in its inside pocket.

**DENNY OSTROW:** Closer. You're a band, not a bus stop.

**DENNY:** Don't smile.

Dean smiles.

**DENNY:** The drummer can smile.

The only input is where Ellis looks. The player can move his gaze freely while Denny shoots a whole roll, and the frame that becomes famous is the one where the player held Ellis's gaze longest.

- **The lens.** Ellis looking straight into the camera, his face unreadable. Known, later, as *the stare.*
- **Across the street.** Ellis looking past the photographer, off the left edge of the frame, at something none of the other three are looking at. Known, later, as *the look.* In the 1996 documentary, fans argue about what he was looking at.
- **Riley.** Ellis looking sideways at Riley, who's looking at the camera.
- **Up.** Ellis with his face tilted up into the falling snow, eyes half closed. Known, later, as *the snow picture.*

Dean smiles in every version.

**DENNY:** Got it.

**DEAN:** Can we go inside now?

**CAL:** There's a coffee shop on the corner.

**RILEY:** I can't feel my face.

**ELLIS:** Your face is fine.

They go. The player walks with them. Behind them the wall with the torn posters, and the newsstand across the street, and the snow.

## Chapter VII — design summary

### What happened
The record came out, and for a while nobody bought it, and then a nurse did. A DJ played a song twice while a catcher waved a ball fair. Sixteen hundred people sang a private conversation back to the person who made it up. A magazine printed Ellis's sister's death as his biography. Wayne came to a rock show, said "loud," and fixed a collar. A letter from Dayton went into the pocket of a jacket that was about to become famous.

### The reveals, and what's held back
- **Revealed (optional):** GRACE CLARA BLAKE and CLARA TATE BLAKE, on the east slope.
- **Revealed to the world:** Ellis's sister died in a car he was driving; his mother left; "Old friend." The facts, and none of the truth.
- **Revealed to the player:** Wayne has had an FM converter tuned to the college station for a year. Ellis's body reacts to high beams in the rain. Clara vanishes when Lorraine's handwriting appears.
- **Held back:** Clara's face; what happened in the car; the other truck; Grace's last words; what Lorraine wrote.
