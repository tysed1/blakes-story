# CHAPTER III — SIGNAL

> **Tannersville · Hollow Ridge · Laurel City · the South Fork · Monday, November 4 – Friday, November 29, 1974**
> Main-story runtime: ~5 hours · Playable: Cal Mercer, Riley, Ellis Blake, Dean Holloway

## Chapter thesis

Until now The Blakes have existed only where they were standing. This is the chapter where their music leaves the room. Once something leaves you, you don't control what it becomes.

The chapter's motif is radio, a voice with no visible speaker that you accept as real because you understand the mechanism. The game never says so.

## World state at open

- The Blakes have played Marlon's twice. A college station manager has asked for a tape.
- November: bare trees on the ridges, the first hard frost, election day (Georgia picks a governor on the 5th), Ali knocked out Foreman on the 30th of last month and every barroom argument in Hollow Ridge is now about something else.
- The band owns one used PA, owes Vale thirty dollars on it, and has one cassette of a rehearsal nobody can listen to.

## COLD OPEN

Black. Radios warming up: a shelf of old tube sets at Mercer Radio & TV coming on one after another before the shop opens, each hum rising into a different station (a preacher, the farm report, a Top 40 jingle, static). Somebody switches them off, one by one, until only the hum is left. Then a button clicks down on a cassette recorder.

**ELLIS** *(on tape):* Is it going?

**CAL** *(on tape):* It was.

**ELLIS:** What do you mean was?

**CAL:** You asked after we stopped it.

**DEAN** *(distant, on tape):* This is going great.

Riley laughs. A guitar headstock scrapes something metal.

**DEAN:** Ow—

The tape stops. Silence. Click. Whirr of rewinding. Click.

Then music: not polished, not professional, The Blakes in Marlon's on a Sunday, recorded on one cassette machine sitting on the bar. Cymbals like breaking glass. Ellis's voice somewhere behind them. Riley barely there. The bass sounds like it's coming from the next county.

Fade in. **Vale Music, Tannersville, Monday morning.** Cal is alone behind the counter with the Panasonic recorder on the glass. The tape reaches Ellis's second verse. Cal frowns. Rewinds ten seconds. Listens. Rewinds. Again.

Mr. Vale comes down the back stairs with his coffee and stops.

**VALE:** You planning on wearing that tape out before anybody hears it?

**CAL:** The tempo moves.

Vale listens a moment.

**VALE:** Sounds fine to me.

**CAL:** It moves.

**VALE:** People move too.

Cal looks at him. Vale keeps walking toward the front door with his keys.

**VALE:** Store opens in five.

The player gains control.

## MISSION 1 — TAPE

**Playable:** Cal · **Where:** Vale Music · **When:** Monday Nov 4, 8:55 a.m. – noon · **Length:** ~20 min

Cal's first objective is mundane: open the store. Lights. Unlock the cases. Count the register. Plug in a demonstration amp and play the four notes Cal always plays to check it. Move a shipment of Ernie Ball strings from the back. Sell a set of harmonica reeds to a man who wants to talk about Sonny Terry.

But the tape keeps playing from the workshop, because Cal left it running on the bench. The Blakes are physically absent and their music is here. That's new, and Cal keeps drifting back toward the bench. He will not admit he likes hearing them.

### The recording problem

The tape isn't usable. The player, as Cal, can diagnose why the way he'd diagnose an amplifier: the recorder was on the bar, three feet from Dean's crash cymbal; Ellis moved off his microphone halfway through the second song; Riley's voice was pointed at the wall; the bass was coming out of an amp aimed at the floor. The room was louder than the band.

And yet, underneath it, something's there.

**VALE** *(from the counter, not looking up from the newspaper):* You know what your problem is?

**CAL:** Several things.

**VALE:** You're listening for what isn't there.

**CAL:** That's generally how you fix something.

**VALE:** Music isn't an alternator.

**CAL:** Everything has parts.

**VALE:** People don't.

Cal looks at him.

**VALE:** That's why they're harder.

Cal doesn't answer. It shouldn't feel like prophecy. It's an older man who knows Cal, talking over the sports page.

### Riley

The bell over the door. Riley, with books and her Martin and a paper cup from the campus snack bar.

**RILEY:** Please tell me that isn't our tape.

**CAL:** Unfortunately.

From the workshop, Dean's recorded voice:

**DEAN** *(on tape):* We're professionals.

Then something crashes.

Riley closes her eyes.

**RILEY:** Destroy it.

**CAL:** I've considered fire.

**RILEY:** Good.

She puts a card on the counter: WTCR, *10 WATTS OF FREEDOM*.

**RILEY:** Martin says we can use the production room. Monday night, after the station signs off at midnight, if I'm there the whole time and we're out by three. He'll run the machines.

**CAL:** What machines?

**RILEY:** Two Ampex reel-to-reels, a board, four microphones that work and two that don't. A room with egg cartons on the walls.

It isn't a studio. To The Blakes, it might as well be.

**CAL:** Who did you have to kill?

**RILEY:** Nobody.

**CAL:** Then this worries me more.

### Switch

Riley leaves with the cassette in her bag (to play for Martin so he knows what he's in for). Cal watches from the doorway. The camera follows Riley across River Street. A city bus pulls between them and fills the frame. Stutter. The bus pulls away. Riley is still there, walking toward campus. Cal isn't.

Control transfers.

## MISSION 2 — BORROWED TIME

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** Tannersville College; WTCR · **When:** Tuesday Nov 5 → Monday Nov 11 · **Length:** ~45 min

Riley has a problem. She has promised a studio she doesn't completely have. Martin Keller agreed to let her record something only if it doesn't interfere with station operations, only if Riley is physically there the entire time, and only until three. Dean has already interpreted this as *We own a recording studio.*

### Election Day

Tuesday, November 5. The campus is papered with flyers for the governor's race and for the women's caucus (the ERA is going to the Georgia Senate in January, and the caucus is organizing a bus to the capitol). Riley has classes, a paper overdue, an office-hours appointment she already moved once, and a phone call from home. The band has started eating hours that belonged to other things, a few at a time.

### Landry's office

Third floor of the philosophy building. Books on every surface. A pipe in an ashtray shaped like Kentucky. Professor Landry reads the first page of her paper with his glasses on his forehead.

**LANDRY:** This isn't your best.

**RILEY:** I know.

He looks up.

**LANDRY:** That's usually the part students argue about.

**RILEY:** Would it help?

**LANDRY:** Not particularly.

She sits. He keeps reading.

**LANDRY:** You're distracted.

**RILEY:** Busy.

**LANDRY:** Those aren't the same thing.

**RILEY:** They're cousins.

He almost smiles. He sets the paper down.

**LANDRY:** Your work is good enough that I notice when you're not doing it.

Riley takes that seriously. He isn't the professor who says *your rock band will ruin your life.* That would be too easy. He's someone showing her another future worth keeping.

**LANDRY:** Edinburgh wants a writing sample by the fifteenth of January. Don't send this one.

**RILEY:** No sir.

### Home

The hall phone again. Her mother.

**JOAN:** Your father says you haven't sent the transcript request.

**RILEY:** I will.

**JOAN:** When?

Riley looks at the pocket calendar in her hand. Monday the 11th, circled, *WTCR 12–3*.

**RILEY:** This week.

**JOAN:** Margaret.

Disappointed, which is worse than angry.

**RILEY:** I said I'll do it.

Her mother softens.

**JOAN:** I know you will.

Then, before they hang up, Riley says it, for no reason she could name.

**RILEY:** I'm in a band.

A pause on the line.

**JOAN:** A what?

**RILEY:** A band. We play in Hollow Ridge. At a tavern.

**JOAN:** Do they have an organ?

**RILEY:** Mama.

**JOAN:** I'm only asking.

**RILEY:** I play guitar. And sing.

**JOAN:** Harmony?

**RILEY:** Mostly.

A longer pause. When Joan speaks again her voice is different, careful, interested and a little afraid.

**JOAN:** Well. Is it good?

**RILEY:** I think so.

**JOAN:** Who's got the melody?

The line hums. Riley doesn't have an answer ready, and her mother doesn't ask twice.

### WTCR — Monday night

The basement of the student union, 11:50 p.m. Fluorescent corridors. Posters for the fall concert series. Stacks of records on every flat surface with handwritten rotation cards. An ashtray on top of the transmitter. The on-air booth behind glass, and next to it a second, smaller room with egg crates stapled to the walls and a sign on the door in marker: **PRODUCTION — DO NOT TOUCH ANYTHING — THIS MEANS YOU, KEVIN.**

Martin Keller (twenty-two, beard, cardigan, the seriousness of a man who has chosen radio as a vocation in a world that thinks it's a hobby) signs the station off at midnight: the anthem on a scratchy 45, the transmitter switched to standby. Then he turns to the four of them and Tully, who has carried in the drums and is now standing against the wall like furniture.

**MARTIN:** Nobody touches anything unless I tell you.

**DEAN:** Absolutely.

Dean touches a microphone.

**MARTIN:** You.

Dean takes his hand back.

**DEAN:** Understood.

Dean immediately loves Martin.

### The session

This is one of the chapter's strongest gameplay sections, because recording is not "perform the song successfully." Recording is different. Everything's exposed. There's no crowd noise to hide in and no crowd energy to lift you. Cal thrives on it. Dean hates it. Ellis gets self-conscious. Riley becomes fascinated.

The room is tiny. The drums go in a corner with a mattress behind them (Tully brought the mattress; nobody asks where from). Martin places four microphones with a care that makes Cal like him.

**Take one: "No Name."** Ellis comes in a bar early. Stop.

**Take two.** Dean overplays the second verse like he's trying to knock the egg crates off the walls.

**CAL:** You don't need to fill every empty space.

**DEAN:** Empty space is depressing.

**CAL:** That's why Ellis writes lyrics.

**ELLIS:** Fuck both of you.

Riley laughs.

**Take three.** Beautiful, until Riley's harmony goes flat on the long note in the last chorus. She raises her hand before the song's even over.

**RILEY:** Again.

**ELLIS:** That was good.

**RILEY:** No.

Ellis looks at Cal.

**ELLIS:** Lord, it's catching.

### Playable Riley — layering harmony

After the band takes, Martin shows Riley something: with two tape machines he can play the first take back while recording it onto the second machine with a new voice added. Sound-on-sound. It's primitive and lossy and every generation adds hiss, and to Riley it's a revelation.

The mechanic: Riley records a harmony over the band take, then plays it back and records a second harmony over *that*. The player chooses each line (above, below, unison) by listening. She hears herself layered with herself, and then herself with Ellis, and suddenly she can build a vocal arrangement that two voices couldn't sing live.

She looks through the glass at Ellis.

**RILEY:** Again.

**MARTIN** *(talkback):* That one was clean.

**RILEY:** I know. I have an idea.

They run it again. She puts a third voice under the chorus, a low one, almost a hum. It changes the whole chorus.

Ellis hears the playback. He looks at her.

**ELLIS:** Where'd that come from?

**RILEY:** I don't know.

**ELLIS:** Do it again.

She smiles. "Again" is becoming theirs.

### Playable Cal — "I will"

Cal listens to a playback. On one transition, his bass is a hair late. The player, as Cal, can hear it; the other three can't.

**CAL:** I want to redo the bass on the second turnaround.

**DEAN:** Nobody will hear that.

**CAL:** I will.

That's his father's line, near enough, and he doesn't know it.

**DEAN:** You planning to be sitting by every radio in Georgia?

**CAL:** Ideally.

**DEAN:** Psychotic.

Cal punches in the bass (the player plays two bars into a live tape, with no second chance on Martin's budget of reel) and it's right.

### Playable Dean — the take

Dean has been restraining himself for an hour and a half. It's 1:40 in the morning. Finally Martin, exhausted, over the talkback:

**MARTIN:** Do one however you want.

**DEAN:** Really?

**MARTIN:** So you'll stop asking.

Dean grins. The player controls the full take, "Low Water," with no restraint required.

Dean goes enormous. But something surprising happens: he's incredible, inventive and musical as well as loud, answering Ellis, leaving holes for Riley, crashing back in a half-beat late on purpose so the chorus lands like a wave. The Room system rewards it: the band follows him instead of the other way around.

Cal, through the glass, expected a disaster. He gets brilliance. After the take there's silence.

**DEAN** *(talkback):* Too much?

**CAL:** ...No.

Dean's smile changes. Cal rarely gives him that.

### Playable Ellis — the booth

Last, Ellis's vocal on "No Name," alone in the production room with headphones on while the others watch through the glass. This is harder for him than singing in front of sixty people. The room is dead. Everyone is looking at him.

**MARTIN** *(talkback):* Whenever you're ready.

**ELLIS:** That's reassuring.

He starts. Stops.

**ELLIS:** Can you turn the lights down?

Martin looks at Riley. She shrugs. He dims what he can. The production room goes dark except for the red REC light and the glow from the control room.

Ellis tries again. Better.

Then, during a quiet line in the second verse, Ellis looks through the glass into the control room.

Clara is standing there beside Riley, just behind her shoulder, arms folded, the control-room light on her face. No one reacts to her. The framing makes it look natural: the control room is crowded; Tully's by the door; Dean's leaning on the console.

Clara nods, once.

Ellis finishes the take. It's the best vocal of the night.

> **Blocking note.** Clara is on the far side of glass from Ellis, in a dim room full of people facing the other way. A barrier literally separates her from everyone who could check. Nobody in the control room is facing her.

### Playback

2:40 a.m. All four of them sit on the floor of the control room while Martin rewinds. He presses play.

Tape hiss. Then their song, as an object: something that exists without them.

Dean's face goes completely serious for once. Riley closes her eyes. Cal listens with his head tilted, hearing everything wrong with it and not saying so. Ellis stares at the reel turning. His own voice is coming from somewhere outside his body. It bothers him and fascinates him at once.

**MARTIN:** Not bad.

**DEAN:** *Not bad?*

**MARTIN:** You want me to say you're Zeppelin?

**DEAN:** Eventually.

**MARTIN:** Not tonight.

### The first tape

Martin dubs the two songs onto a fresh reel for Wednesday's local-music show and makes a cassette copy for the band. Cal takes the cassette. Dean reaches for it. Cal pulls it back.

**DEAN:** Why?

**CAL:** Because I'd like us to still own it tomorrow.

### The corridor

Almost three. Riley locks up behind them. Everyone's exhausted and wired at once.

**DEAN:** We need a van.

**CAL:** We need money.

**DEAN:** Same conversation.

**RILEY:** We also need sleep.

**DEAN:** No we don't.

**CAL:** You especially do.

Dean's expression changes slightly. He knows what Cal means. He doesn't engage.

## MISSION 3 — DEAD AIR

**Playable:** Ellis → Riley → Dean → Cal → Ellis · **Where:** Roy's garage → the Blake house → Stony Knob → the Blake house · **When:** Wednesday Nov 13, 1:00 – 11:45 p.m. · **Length:** ~40 min

The Blakes are supposed to be on WTCR's *Local Motion* tonight. Martin said "around ten-forty, maybe." Ellis pretends not to care. He cares enormously.

### The garage

A normal afternoon of work: a brake job, two oil changes, a tractor tire. Roy's bench radio is tuned, as always, to the Laurel City AM country station. Ellis keeps checking the Pepsi clock. Roy notices.

**ROY:** Something happening?

**ELLIS:** No.

Ten minutes later he checks it again.

**ROY:** Must be a busy nothing.

**ELLIS:** College station's playing us.

Roy stops, wrench in hand.

**ROY:** Us.

**ELLIS:** The band.

**ROY:** Oh.

He turns the wrench.

**ROY:** What time?

**ELLIS:** Ten-forty. Maybe.

**ROY:** We're listening.

**ELLIS:** You can't get that station here. It's ten watts. It barely gets across the river.

**ROY:** Then we'll go somewhere that does.

Ellis looks at him. Roy keeps working. No speech.

### Wayne

Ellis goes home at six to eat. Wayne is at the kitchen table with the *Banner* and a plate of pork chops he cooked for both of them, which is new, and which neither of them mentions.

Ellis almost tells him. Doesn't. Eats half a pork chop. Then:

**ELLIS:** We're on the radio tonight.

**WAYNE:** Who?

**ELLIS:** The band.

Wayne looks up.

**WAYNE:** What station?

Ellis wasn't expecting the question.

**ELLIS:** The college station. In Tannersville.

**WAYNE:** That thing reach here?

**ELLIS:** Reckon not.

**WAYNE:** What time?

**ELLIS:** Late. Ten-forty, maybe.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

That's all. Ellis waits for the mockery. It doesn't come. Somehow that's more unsettling.

### The mountain

At ten Roy picks Ellis up at the garage in his pickup. On the seat between them sits a portable transistor radio in a red leather case, a Panasonic with an AM/FM switch and a telescoping antenna.

**ROY:** Evelyn's kitchen radio. She wants it back by morning.

Up Cold Branch Road past the Blake house (the kitchen light is on; Wayne's truck is in the yard), past the falls, switchbacks climbing into the North highlands. The player drives. Ellis holds the radio in his lap with the antenna up and turns the dial.

This is a tuning mini-game that also teaches something true: FM travels in straight lines, and Tannersville is on the other side of the Tanner Valley ridge. At the bottom of the mountain there's only static. As the truck climbs, the band fills with other things: a Laurel City country station; a preacher; the farm report rerun; a Top 40 station playing "Kung Fu Fighting"; classical music from the Tannersville public station; static. The WTCR frequency (88.9) is dead air until the truck clears the last switchback and pulls into the gravel at the top of **Stony Knob**, where the whole Tanner Valley is spread out to the east and, forty miles off, the orange glow of Tannersville sits on the clouds.

Ellis turns the dial a hair.

A young man's voice, faint, then clear.

**MARTIN** *(radio):* —so that was the Tannersville Folk Collective, and if you want to know what the Tannersville Folk Collective sounds like after four beers, come to the Exit on Friday. You're listening to *Local Motion* on WTCR, ten watts of freedom—

Ellis sits up straighter. Roy looks at him and doesn't say anything.

### The DJ

**MARTIN** *(radio):* Got something here I've been wanting to play you. These folks have been playing out Hollow Ridge way, at Marlon's Tavern, if you know where that is, and if you don't, don't worry, neither did I. They call themselves The Blakes—

**ELLIS:** We do not.

**ROY:** Apparently you do.

**MARTIN:** —and they came in and recorded right here at WTCR Monday night, at an hour I don't recommend. This one's called "No Name," or at least that's what they wrote on the box—

**ELLIS:** I'm fixing to kill Dean.

Then the song starts.

### The first broadcast

No montage yet. Stay with Ellis.

He hears his guitar. His voice. Riley's harmony, three of her. Cal. Dean. Coming out of a cheap speaker in a red leather case in a truck on a mountain. Somewhere else, strangers are hearing exactly this.

It is almost impossible for him to understand.

Roy nods his head slightly to the beat, looking straight ahead through the windshield at the valley.

**ROY:** That's you?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**ROY:** Huh.

**ELLIS:** What?

**ROY:** You're pretty good.

**ELLIS:** You sound surprised.

**ROY:** I am.

Ellis laughs, and turns to look out the side window, and he's smiling.

### The relay

The song continues uninterrupted. The camera moves toward the radio's speaker grille. The music thins into static for a heartbeat, the camera passes through it, and the sound comes back out of a different speaker in a different room. Stutter. This is the chapter's signature use of the switch: the song carries the camera.

**Riley's dorm.** Riley's clock radio on the floor. Riley, Hannah and five girls from the hall sitting in a circle around it. When Riley's harmony enters, everybody cheers. Riley tells them all to shut up because she can't hear. The player, as Riley, can turn it up. That's all she wants to do.

Static. Stutter.

**Dean's car.** The Chevelle at a red light on River Street, windows down in November, the radio all the way up. Dean shouting at the driver of the station wagon beside him:

**DEAN:** *THAT'S ME!*

The driver, a man in a hat, looks straight ahead.

**DEAN:** *ASSHOLE!*

He laughs. The light changes. The player can floor it or not; Dean floors it either way.

Static. Stutter.

**Mercer Radio & TV.** After hours. One lamp over the bench. Walt Mercer has tuned a customer's walnut console radio to 88.9 because Cal asked him to, and the two of them are standing side by side in front of it like it's a fireplace.

**WALT:** That you?

**CAL:** Yeah.

Walt listens with his head down, the way he listens to a set that isn't working.

**WALT:** Bass is buried.

Cal laughs out loud. Walt looks at him, then back at the radio. He reaches over and turns it up.

Static. Stutter.

### Back on the mountain

The last chorus. The song ends. Martin comes back on.

**MARTIN** *(radio):* The Blakes. From Hollow Ridge. Keep an ear out. I've got a feeling about these folks.

Ellis stares at the radio.

**ROY:** Well.

**ELLIS:** Well what?

**ROY:** Guess you better write another one.

> **Observe** (available at the guardrail): *Nov 13. Forty miles off there's a boy with a beard reading my name off a box, and it comes all this way in the dark and hits the mountain and stops right here. This is the only place it lands.*

### Coming down

Roy drives them down. Just below the summit, at the fire-tower spur, where the valley is still in view, there's a pickup parked facing the valley with its engine off and its dome light on. Roy's headlights sweep across it as they come around the bend.

It's Wayne's F-100. Wayne is sitting behind the wheel. There's a radio on the dash.

He doesn't look at them. Or he turns his face toward the valley just as the headlights reach him. It's hard to say.

Roy doesn't slow down. He doesn't say anything. Ellis doesn't say anything. They pass. The player can turn the camera and watch the dome light get smaller through the back window until the bend takes it.

### Home

Roy drops Ellis at the garage. Ellis drives the Valiant home. Wayne's truck is already in the yard, the hood ticking as it cools. Wayne must have come down the fast way.

Wayne is at the kitchen table with coffee, as though he's been there for hours.

**WAYNE:** Heard you were on the radio.

**ELLIS:** How?

**WAYNE:** Pruitt told me.

**ELLIS:** Since when does Pruitt tell you anything?

**WAYNE:** Said he heard it in the cruiser.

**ELLIS:** Pruitt listens to the college station.

**WAYNE:** Apparently.

Ellis looks at him. Wayne looks at his coffee.

**WAYNE:** Said he couldn't hear half the words.

**ELLIS:** The recording's rough.

**WAYNE:** Said it was a strange song.

**ELLIS:** Okay.

Ellis starts down the hall.

**WAYNE:** Said the guitar sounded good.

Ellis stops. He doesn't turn around.

**ELLIS:** Thanks.

**WAYNE:** I didn't say it. Pruitt said it.

A beat. Then Wayne ruins it, because he can't stand to leave it there.

**WAYNE:** Still need rent Friday.

Ellis's shoulders drop.

**ELLIS:** Yes sir.

He walks away. Wayne sits at the table and regrets it.

> **Replay layer.** Wayne drove up the mountain in the dark to hear his son on a ten-watt station and invented a deputy so he wouldn't have to admit it. Ellis knows. Roy knows. Nobody will ever say a word.

### Clara

Ellis's room. Clara is sitting on the floor by his clock radio, which is on and tuned to static.

**CLARA:** I heard you.

He smiles.

**ELLIS:** Everybody heard me, apparently.

**CLARA:** Not everybody.

**ELLIS:** Felt like it.

She looks at him.

**CLARA:** You liked it.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** That's okay.

**ELLIS:** Didn't say it wasn't.

**CLARA:** Didn't have to.

He reaches past her and switches the radio off.

> **Replay layer.** In Chapter IX the player will hear a cassette of a thirteen-year-old girl doing a pretend radio show from her bedroom, *"WGRC, Grace Radio,"* introducing her brother as the in-studio guest. *"Not everybody."*

## MISSION 4 — OUT OF TOWN

**Playable:** Dean → Cal · **Where:** Vale Music → Tannersville → Tanner Valley → Hollow Ridge → Laurel Gap → Laurel City · **When:** Friday Nov 15 (the booking) → Saturday Nov 16, 2:00 – 6:30 p.m. · **Length:** ~25 min

### The booking

The radio play produces the band's first chance outside the immediate area. It's small and plausible. No record executive heard them. Martin knows the booker at **the Blind Tiger**, a rock club on Laurel City's Tenth Street, and the Blind Tiger's opening act for Saturday (a Southern boogie band called Cottonmouth) just broke up in a parking lot. Forty-five minutes. A hundred and fifty dollars. And an audience that doesn't know them.

Dean found out first because Dean finds out everything first. He bursts into Vale's on Friday afternoon while Cal is restringing a banjo.

**DEAN:** Laurel City.

**CAL:** What about it?

**DEAN:** We're playing there. Tomorrow.

**CAL:** Who said?

**DEAN:** Me.

**RILEY** *(from the back, where she's buying strings):* That isn't how bookings work.

Dean slaps a piece of paper on the counter: a Blind Tiger contract form, typed, signed by a booker named Artie Szabo, with "THE BLAKES" typed in the blank.

**DEAN:** Then explain this.

Cal reads it. It's real.

**ELLIS** *(on the phone, when Cal calls Roy's):* How much?

Cal checks. Everyone looks at him.

**CAL:** Enough.

**DEAN:** Road trip.

**RILEY:** It's eighty miles.

**DEAN:** Road. Trip.

### The van

They can't fit four people and the PA and a drum kit into their cars safely. Mr. Vale has a delivery van, a 1969 Chevrolet step van with VALE MUSIC — TANNERSVILLE — INSTRUMENTS · REPAIR · RENTAL painted on the side. He agrees to lend it under conditions.

Vale looks at Dean.

**VALE:** You're not driving.

**DEAN:** What did I do?

Everyone looks at him.

**DEAN:** Recently.

Cal gets the keys. Dean is offended beyond reason for twenty minutes.

### The road (playable Dean, passenger; then Cal, driving)

The player begins as Dean in the passenger seat (a chance to experience the van from Dean's side: he messes with the radio, puts his feet on the dash, opens a bag of Funyuns, drums on the door). At the Hollow Ridge city limit sign (POP. 2,100) they stop for Ellis, who's standing by the road with Loretta's case and a paper sack of boiled peanuts. Stutter at Ellis getting in the back. Control goes to **Cal** at the wheel.

West on US 19 through the **Laurel Gap corridor**: the road follows a river gorge down out of the mountains, past the Starlite truck stop, a fireworks stand closed for the season, a ruined gristmill, the long lakes at the edge of Laurel City, the first four-lane road any of them has driven on in months, and then the city: new towers downtown, an expressway half built with its concrete ramps ending in the air, billboards, traffic, a skyline that looks like a promise somebody's still making.

The player can do all the van things: change the radio (Laurel City gets real FM rock; the van has an under-dash FM converter Vale installed so he could hear the Tannersville jazz station on deliveries), pass snacks, argue about directions (Ellis has drawn a map on a napkin; it is wrong; his maps are always wrong), stop for gas.

**The name argument:**

**DEAN:** The name's growing on me.

**CAL:** So does mold.

**RILEY:** Strong position.

**ELLIS:** We're changing it.

**DEAN:** Too late. The radio said it.

**ELLIS:** The radio can say something else.

**DEAN:** Brand recognition.

**CAL:** You took one business class.

**DEAN:** And apparently learned more than you.

**The set-list argument:**

Riley wants "Low Water" in the set. Cal wants only the tightest covers because this audience doesn't know them. Ellis agrees with Riley. Dean wants all originals because Dean has no fear.

**CAL:** They didn't pay to hear songs they don't know.

**ELLIS:** They didn't pay to hear *us*.

**CAL:** That's my point.

**ELLIS:** So what are we protecting?

That stops Cal for a second.

**RILEY:** Two originals.

**CAL:** One.

**DEAN:** Four.

**CAL:** Nobody asked you.

**DEAN:** Three.

Eventually: two. Democracy, barely.

### The Gulf station

At a gas station at the city's edge, the attendant (young, sideburns, a Laurel City accent that's flatter and faster than theirs) pumps the gas and hears Ellis ask for the restroom key.

**ATTENDANT:** Where y'all from?

**ELLIS:** Hollow Ridge.

**ATTENDANT:** Hollow Ridge. Up in the hills. Y'all bring your banjo?

He hums four notes of "Dueling Banjos" and grins at the others like they're in on it.

**ELLIS:** Only on Sundays.

Dean laughs because it's funny. Ellis doesn't. He takes the key and walks around the building. Riley watches him go.

> **Period note.** *Deliverance* (1972) was filmed in the north Georgia mountains. By 1974 every mountain kid who went to a city heard the banjo joke. Ellis will hear it again. It's never the same size twice.

### Laurel City

The city should feel substantially bigger than anything the band has played. Brick warehouses along the rail yards, a downtown grid, Tenth Street with its head shops and record stores and hippies who are mostly past it now, runaways, a Hare Krishna on the corner, neon. People who don't know them.

Ellis looks out the window. It's everything he wanted: somewhere nobody knows him. For tonight.

## MISSION 5 — STRANGERS

**Playable:** rotating · **Where:** The Blind Tiger, Tenth Street, Laurel City · **When:** Saturday Nov 16, 6:30 – 11:45 p.m. · **Length:** ~35 min

### The venue

The Blind Tiger is rougher than Marlon's: a long, low room in a former tire shop, black walls, a real stage two feet high with lights on a pipe, a house PA, a backstage room with a couch nobody should sit on, and posters for bands that played here and never played anywhere else. Their name is written on the chalk sign out front in small letters under the headliner:

**SATURDAY — STARFIRE
w/ THE BLAKES 8:00**

Dean takes a Polaroid of it. Ellis pretends not to care. Riley touches the chalk letters with a fingertip. Cal goes straight to the stage to check the house connections.

> **Ritual, established.** Dean takes an SX-70 photograph of every venue's sign from now on and keeps a matchbook from every venue in his trap case. By 1976 there will be hundreds of each.

### The headliner

Starfire, the headliner, is a Laurel City band that's been playing the Southeast for five years and has a single on a small label. They're not villains. They're more established and a little bored. Their guitarist, **Skeeter**, looks the four of them over in the backstage room.

**SKEETER:** Y'all from Tannersville?

**ELLIS:** Hollow Ridge.

**SKEETER:** Never heard of it.

**ELLIS:** That's most people.

Skeeter sees Riley setting down her guitar case and assumes she's somebody's girlfriend.

**SKEETER:** Your girl need a backstage pass too?

**RILEY:** I play.

**SKEETER:** Right.

Riley smiles, not upset. Later, onstage, she'll make him feel stupid without ever addressing him.

### Soundcheck

Real equipment exposes weaknesses. Dean is too loud in a room that's built to be loud. Ellis doesn't understand where to stand relative to the monitor and keeps turning away from it. Riley's vocal mic squeals with feedback every time she steps back. Cal adapts fastest: he talks to the house engineer in the engineer's own language within two minutes.

**HOUSE ENGINEER** *(to Ellis, over the talkback):* Bass player knows what he's doing.

**ELLIS:** Don't tell him.

**CAL** *(from across the stage):* I can hear you.

### The show

The room is half full when they start. Almost nobody cares. This is the chapter's lesson: Marlon's audiences have affection; Laurel City has indifference, and indifference is harder than hostility.

**Song one:** polite. No disaster. No magic. Ellis looks uncomfortable. Dean's trying too hard. Cal notices. Riley stays composed.

**Song two:** a cover people recognize. Better. The crowd loosens. A couple of people come closer to the stage.

**"No Name" — early.** Cal wanted to save it for the end. Ellis calls it third, off the set list, off-mic.

Cal looks over: *what are you doing?*

Ellis looks at him. It's a new use of an old signal.

**ELLIS:** Stay with me.

Cal sighs, and plays.

The player switches through all four. This is their first performance where they have to *win* people. Riley's harmony (the three-voice part she built at WTCR, which she now sings as one line, the lowest one) brings people forward. Cal's structure keeps Dean's nervous tempo steady. Dean's energy becomes useful when Cal gives it something to push against. And Ellis stops trying to impress anyone and just sings, and it works.

### The woman at the bar

A woman in her late twenties, sitting alone at the bar in a hairdresser's smock under her coat, starts listening during "No Name." Not in a flirtatious way. She's hearing the words. When the song ends she doesn't clap much. She just doesn't leave.

That tells Ellis more than applause would. Someone who owed him nothing chose to stay. The player sees her in every Room glance for the rest of the set: same stool, coat still on.

### "Low Water"

The last song. Riley takes the opening alone, a verse on the Martin with just her voice. Skeeter, backstage in the wings, looks up from his beer.

He realizes she actually plays. Cal, watching Skeeter from across the stage, notices, and smiles slightly. Riley never sees it.

The song builds. Dean comes in on the second verse like the door being kicked. They finish.

Applause. Not huge, but stronger than what they walked in to. A few strangers yell:

**CROWD:** One more!

Dean is already counting it off. Artie Szabo, the booker, shakes his head at the side of the stage (Starfire has to be on by nine-fifteen). Dean looks like he's going to argue. Cal pulls him offstage by the back of his jacket.

They walked in unknown and walked off remembered by maybe forty people.

### After

The backstage room. Dean is ecstatic.

**DEAN:** Did you hear that?

**CAL:** Forty people.

**DEAN:** Forty people who didn't know us two hours ago.

That actually stops Cal.

**RILEY:** Mark the date.

**CAL:** Don't get used to it.

Ellis is quiet. Riley notices.

**RILEY:** You okay?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

He looks at the stage door.

**ELLIS:** Nobody knew me.

Riley doesn't understand why that matters.

### The money

Artie pays them a hundred and fifty dollars in twenties and a ten, the most they've made at once. Not rich. Real.

They divide it on the backstage couch nobody should sit on. Dean wants to spend it celebrating. Cal wants a share for the band fund. Riley suggests they keep enough for food. Ellis wants to send some home: he's thinking about Friday's rent. Nobody judges anyone's answer. Cal proposes twenty percent to the fund and the rest in four shares, and writes it in the green ledger:

> *11/16/74 — Blind Tiger, Laurel City — $150.00. Band fund $30.00. Shares $30.00 x4. PA balance paid (Vale). Ellis owes band $20 (by agreement) — PAID.*

Ellis paid it back tonight. Cal writes PAID and underlines it once. Neither of them mentions it.

The band has become an economic entity.

### The invitation

Starfire's bass player, a heavyset man of about thirty-five named **Lonnie**, stops by the couch on his way to the stage.

**LONNIE:** Y'all going back up toward the mountains tonight?

**CAL:** Yes.

**LONNIE:** There's a thing at a farm in the South Fork. On your way, more or less. Some folks from the art school, some pickers. It's all right. I'll be there after.

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** Yes.

**RILEY:** Maybe.

**ELLIS:** That's usually a yes from musicians.

Riley looks at him. He remembered. She smiles.

**CAL:** I hate all of you.

## MISSION 6 — THE FARMHOUSE

**Playable:** Dean → Cal → Riley → Ellis · **Where:** a farmhouse in the South Fork highlands · **When:** Sunday Nov 17, 12:30 – 3:50 a.m. · **Length:** ~50 min

This is the game's first large look at 1970s counterculture as it actually was in the rural South in 1974: not a costume party.

An old two-story farmhouse at the end of a gravel road in the South Fork, rented by four art students and a potter, with a kiln in the barn. Cars parked in the field. A bonfire in a ring of fieldstones. Records going inside (the Band, then Little Feat, then someone's Coltrane, then back to the Band). Live acoustic music on the porch: a fiddle and two guitars. Beer in a cattle trough full of creek water. Marijuana. People from the colleges, from bands, from a commune up in the North highlands that's mostly broken up; barefoot people and people in pressed jeans; a veteran with a prosthetic hand who built the porch; rich kids pretending not to be rich and poor kids pretending not to care; people talking about politics, Watergate, Carter (who hasn't announced; someone swears he will), Castaneda, astrology, the price of land, a potter's co-op, the draft, God.

The four of them split up naturally. There is no menu. The game follows whoever matters.

### Dean

Dean walks into the party like he was born there. Within five minutes: a drink in one hand, a cigarette, a line in a bedroom upstairs with a sculptor named Pete, someone who knows him from somewhere, then three people who know him. Then he's behind a makeshift drum kit on the porch (two buckets, a washboard, a cardboard box and a real snare somebody brought) playing with the fiddler, and the porch is dancing.

No downward spiral. This is Dean functioning exactly the way he wants to: too alive, too awake. The player should enjoy it.

### Switch — Cal

Dean passes Cal on the porch steps going for another beer. Stutter. Cal.

Cal is deeply uncomfortable with aimless socializing. He wanders into the kitchen and within ten minutes is on his knees fixing the farmhouse's broken turntable with a butter knife and a borrowed flashlight (a belt slipped off its pulley; a tonearm wire has come loose).

A woman (a weaver, late twenties, with clay under her nails) watches him from the counter.

**WEAVER:** Do you always fix things at parties?

**CAL:** Only when they're broken.

**WEAVER:** Everything here's broken.

**CAL:** Then I'll be busy.

She laughs.

### Cal and Lonnie

When the turntable works, the whole kitchen cheers, and Lonnie, Starfire's bassist, who arrived an hour ago, sits down at the table with Cal and two beers. He treats Cal as a musician first, which almost nobody outside the band has. They talk shop: tone, technique, whether flatwound strings are for cowards, the Ampeg B-15, what it's like to tour the Southeast in a Ford Econoline for five years. Broken vans. Bad contracts. A manager who took thirty percent. A drummer who went to prison. Bands that destroyed themselves over tiny resentments.

**LONNIE:** You all friends?

**CAL:** Depends when you ask.

**LONNIE:** Good answer.

He drinks.

**LONNIE:** Friends make terrible bands.

**CAL:** Why?

**LONNIE:** Our drummer did two years in Reidsville over a Buick. I still owe him forty dollars.

He goes to find the bathroom.

### Switch — Riley

Cal crosses the room toward the back door. Through the window, Riley at the bonfire. Stutter. Riley.

She's comfortable here, more than Cal. She speaks this language: consciousness, perception, William James, the doors of perception. People at the fire are discussing whether LSD is a sacrament or a symptom. Somebody has a kettle on a grate over the coals.

In the kettle: a tea made from mushrooms somebody picked out of the cow pasture across the road in September and dried in a coffee can (the can is on the log by the fire). *Psilocybe cubensis*: brown caps, blue-bruising stems; in season they come up out of cow manure after a rain. People in the South Fork have picked them since the sixties. A potter explains, cheerfully, how to tell them from the ones that'll kill you.

Riley knows what it is. Ellis comes out of the dark and sits beside her on a log.

### The psychedelic conversation

This must be handled with care. Riley doesn't push. She doesn't say *you need to try this.* Someone offers a cup. Ellis looks at Riley.

**ELLIS:** You done this?

**RILEY:** Yes.

**ELLIS:** What's it like?

She thinks.

**RILEY:** Depends.

**ELLIS:** Helpful.

**RILEY:** Sometimes beautiful.

A pause.

**RILEY:** Sometimes not.

**ELLIS:** You gonna do it?

**RILEY:** Probably.

Ellis looks at the cup in the stranger's hand. Riley watches him.

**RILEY:** You don't have to.

**ELLIS:** I know.

That line matters. It's his choice, not Riley's doing. He takes the cup.

> **Design note — choice.** The player cannot decline here in the main story. The scene is written so that Ellis's choice is visibly his own: Riley's "You don't have to" is said as he's reaching, and the camera stays on his hands. Years later, Riley will torment herself with this scene. The player will have watched exactly what she did and didn't do.

### Playable Riley — early experience

For a while, nothing. They talk. Music. Fire. Dean's porch band in the distance. Cal somewhere in the house.

Then perception shifts, and the game must not turn this into a neon kaleidoscope. It's subtle:
- The bonfire leaves a faint trail when the camera moves quickly.
- Music gains depth: the fiddle on the porch and the record in the house become separate, placed in space.
- Tree branches against the sky seem rhythmically connected, as though they're moving to the fiddle.
- Distances feel slightly wrong: the barn looks near and then far.
- Faces are more expressive than usual.

Riley is relaxed. Ellis is fascinated.

**ELLIS:** Everything's breathing.

**RILEY:** Yeah.

**ELLIS:** You say that like it's normal.

**RILEY:** Tonight it is.

### The family-history line

This happens almost by accident. They're farther from the fire now, sitting in the grass at the edge of the field where it's darker. The conversation has turned personal the effortless way these conversations do.

Riley tells him about her great-aunt Louise in Macon, who believed Arthur Godfrey sent her personal messages through the radio and wrote him weekly for eleven years, and got two signed photographs back, which only made it worse. Ellis laughs.

**ELLIS:** My daddy's uncle got sent to Milledgeville.

**RILEY:** The hospital?

**ELLIS:** Where they send you.

**RILEY:** For what?

**ELLIS** *(shrugging)*: Hearing people, I think. Nobody says.

**RILEY:** Did you know him?

**ELLIS:** No. Family quit talking about him before I was born.

A beat. Ellis looks down at his hands. The conversation moves to something else (Riley asks if he can see the Pleiades; she points them out; he can't find them; she guides his arm).

That's it. No music cue. No visual emphasis. No journal entry. A new player may not remember the line an hour later.

> **Design note.** Milledgeville (Central State Hospital) is a place-name every Georgian of the period knew as a threat. The line is deliberately forgettable in the moment. It's the first of four family-history clues (III, VI, VIII, IX).

### Switch — Ellis alone

Riley gets up to find water. She walks toward the farmhouse. The camera stays with Ellis. Stutter. Control becomes Ellis. He's alone. Apparently.

### Ellis's walk

This should be beautiful, and a little dangerous emotionally, without becoming horror.

The player can wander: the field in starlight; the tree line; a creek where the water sounds enormous; the old barn with the kiln still warm inside; the gravel road, white under the moon. Sound behaves oddly: the porch fiddle is distant, then close, then distant again as the wind shifts. Ellis touches tree bark and laughs. He looks at his hands for a long time.

For once he isn't examining his pain. He's astonished to exist.

> **Observe** (available, and different on mushrooms: the lines come out stranger and more specific): *Nov 17. The creek is saying the same word over and over and it isn't a word I know but I know what it means.*

### Clara

Ellis turns. Clara is standing in the field, much farther away than she ever stands, fifty yards out in the frosted grass, in her jean jacket.

She smiles.

**CLARA:** Hi.

Ellis laughs. He isn't frightened.

**ELLIS:** What are you doing out here?

**CLARA:** Same as you.

**ELLIS:** How'd you get out here?

**CLARA:** Same way you did.

**ELLIS:** I rode in a van.

**CLARA:** Well, then.

He laughs again; he's too high to follow it and it doesn't matter.

She walks toward him, and she's more vivid than usual: not sinister, just more there, as though someone turned her up. They walk together along the tree line.

**ELLIS:** You ever think everything already happened?

**CLARA:** I think you're fixing to walk in the creek.

He looks down. He was.

**CLARA:** You're high.

**ELLIS:** Extremely.

They laugh. They walk. The fiddle comes and goes. Then Ellis, looking at the ground:

**ELLIS:** I wish you'd come around more.

Clara looks at him. There's something sad in it, very small.

**CLARA:** You sure?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** Okay.

> **Design note — "You sure?"** This is one of the most important exchanges in the chapter. Nothing supernatural happens. Ellis has said out loud that Clara makes him feel safe and that he wants more of that. His mind will provide it. As stress, substances and sleep loss accumulate, she will come around more. Players will remember "You sure?"

### Riley finds him

Riley comes back across the field with two cups of water.

**RILEY:** There you are.

Ellis turns.

**ELLIS:** I wasn't lost.

**RILEY:** You were standing in a field talking to yourself.

A tiny silence. The first direct crack.

Ellis looks at Clara, who's standing a few feet to his left, looking back at him. Riley follows Ellis's eyes, and her gaze crosses the place where Clara stands and keeps going to the barn, the tree line, the dark. From Ellis's camera it's ambiguous: did she look right at her? Did she look past? It's dark. They're both high.

**ELLIS:** Song.

**RILEY:** What?

**ELLIS:** I was working out a song.

Riley looks at him. He's high. She's high. It makes enough sense.

**RILEY:** Oh.

She sits down in the grass beside him and hands him the water. Clara stays where she is, a few feet off. Riley can't see her. The player gets no confirmation either way. Riley starts talking about the stars again, and Ellis's attention goes naturally to her, and the camera goes with it.

When the camera later drifts back to where Clara was standing: empty grass, the frost on it unbroken.

> **Design note — fairness.** The player must not solve it here. People talk to themselves when they're high. Ellis is a songwriter. Clara has always come and gone on her own. A player may assume she walked off as Riley came up. Some perceptive players will start to wonder. That's good. Most shouldn't know yet.

### Ellis and Riley in the field

They lie on their backs in the frosted grass and look up.

**RILEY:** How are you doing?

**ELLIS:** Great.

**RILEY:** Actually?

He thinks about it.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

He means it. That's why what comes later is tragic: his first psychedelic experience is beautiful. It makes him feel connected. It makes music feel enormous. It makes Clara vivid. Why wouldn't he want it again?

### The fire, 3:30 a.m.

Dean is still going. Cal is done.

**CAL:** We're leaving.

**DEAN:** No.

**CAL:** It's three-thirty.

**DEAN:** Exactly.

**CAL:** That doesn't mean anything.

**DEAN:** It means it's early.

Riley and Ellis come back from the field. Cal looks at Ellis in the firelight. At his pupils. Then at Riley.

**CAL:** Seriously?

Riley immediately hears the judgment in it.

**RILEY:** Don't.

**CAL:** We're driving.

**RILEY:** *You're* driving.

**CAL:** I know.

**ELLIS:** I'm fine.

**CAL:** Everybody here's fine, apparently.

Ellis's mood turns.

**ELLIS:** What's your problem?

**CAL:** Nothing.

**ELLIS:** Then quit talking like there is one.

**CAL:** I have three people I need to get home alive.

**DEAN:** Aw. That's sweet.

**CAL:** Get in the van.

**DEAN:** Yes, *Dad.*

Cal turns around.

**CAL:** Fuck you, Dean.

It's the first time Cal has actually lost his temper. The fire crackles. Somebody on the porch stops playing.

Dean grins. It hurt.

### The walk to the van

Cal goes ahead to start the van and get the heater running. Riley steers Dean across the yard by the back of his jacket. Ellis comes last, alone, across the frosted field toward the headlights.

Clara falls in beside him.

**CLARA:** You had fun.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** Good.

**ELLIS:** Cal hates me.

**CLARA:** No he doesn't.

**ELLIS:** He might could, after tonight.

**CLARA:** He worries loud.

Ellis smiles. Then:

**ELLIS:** Riley couldn't see you.

Clara looks at him. This is the closest the game has come.

A long pause. Then she looks ahead to the van, where Riley is climbing into the passenger seat.

**CLARA:** She's never met me.

Ellis accepts this. Why wouldn't he? Neither does the player necessarily notice that it isn't an answer.

At the fence Clara stops with her hands in her jacket pockets. She doesn't get in. Ellis climbs into the back with Dean and the equipment, and Cal pulls out.

## MISSION 7 — NIGHT ROAD

**Playable:** Cal → Ellis · **Where:** the South Fork road → Cold Branch Road, Hollow Ridge · **When:** Sunday Nov 17, 3:50 – 7:20 a.m. · **Length:** ~30 min

Cal drives Vale's van. Riley in the passenger seat. Dean and Ellis in the back among the equipment. It isn't a chase. There's no action. But the gameplay is tense, because the dynamics are changing and because the road is bad.

Fog in the low places of the South Fork. The van's headlights on high make it worse; on low the road disappears twenty feet out. The road has no shoulder and no center line for miles. Everyone except Cal is intoxicated. Dean talks too much. Ellis is quiet. Riley watches Ellis in the mirror. Cal's hands are at ten and two.

> **Design note.** The route home from the farmhouse runs north up the South Fork road, which at one point passes the mouth of Tolliver Road, dark and unmarked, on the left. Cal doesn't know to notice it. Nothing happens. It's there.

### Dean and Cal

**DEAN:** You know what your problem is?

**CAL:** Apparently everybody does tonight.

**DEAN:** You think if you're responsible enough, nothing bad happens.

Cal watches the road.

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** Yes you do.

**CAL:** I think being irresponsible makes bad things more likely.

**DEAN:** That's the same thing.

**CAL:** It isn't.

**DEAN:** Close enough.

From the back, quietly:

**ELLIS:** He's right.

Cal glances in the mirror.

**CAL:** Which one of us?

**ELLIS:** Both.

That ends it.

### Riley and Cal

After Dean falls asleep against a PA column (it takes about four minutes, the way it does when the cocaine runs out and the beer doesn't):

**CAL:** You give Ellis whatever he took?

**RILEY:** He chose.

**CAL:** I didn't ask that.

**RILEY:** Then ask what you mean.

**CAL:** You know him better than me.

**RILEY:** Barely.

**CAL:** That's the problem.

Riley bristles.

**RILEY:** He's not a child.

**CAL:** Didn't say he was.

**RILEY:** You're acting like I did something to him.

**CAL:** I said I don't know him.

A pause. The fog opens and closes in the headlights.

**CAL:** Neither do you.

That line stays with Riley, because it's true, and because she'll spend a long time convincing herself it isn't.

### Switch — the back of the van

The camera moves toward the rearview mirror. In it: Ellis, awake, looking out the back windows at the fog. Stutter. Control transfers to Ellis.

Dean is asleep on the other side, his mouth open. Ellis watches the fog close over the road behind them.

### Drop-off

The van climbs out of the South Fork as the sky grays. Cold Branch Road. The Blake house. Ellis gets out first; the others are going on to Tannersville.

**RILEY** *(through the window):* Drink water.

**ELLIS:** Yes, Mom.

**RILEY:** Don't call me that.

**DEAN** *(from the back, half asleep, with no idea what's happening):* Cal's mom.

Everybody laughs except Cal. Cal drives away.

### Wayne on the porch

Sunrise. Frost on the yard. Wayne is sitting on the porch in his coat with a coffee cup that's gone cold. He's been awake a while. Again.

Ellis walks up the gravel with Loretta's case. Wayne looks at him and immediately knows.

**WAYNE:** What are you on?

**ELLIS:** Good morning.

**WAYNE:** What did you take?

**ELLIS:** Coffee.

**WAYNE:** Don't you lie to me.

The mushrooms' calm evaporates.

**ELLIS:** Why do you care?

**WAYNE:** Because I live here.

Ellis laughs. Wrong answer.

**WAYNE:** Look at me.

**ELLIS:** No.

Wayne comes down the steps and takes hold of his shoulder. Ellis tears away.

**ELLIS:** Don't touch me.

**WAYNE:** What did you take?

The player can hold silence here, and for two seconds it holds. Then it doesn't. Ellis reaches for the thing between them and uses it.

> **Design note.** Everywhere else in the game, holding keeps Ellis quiet. Here the hold gives out after two seconds and the line comes anyway. It's the Wayne inheritance, made playable: the player knows the sentence is coming and can't stop it.

**ELLIS:** Why? You scared I'm gonna wreck something?

Wayne freezes.

Ellis regrets it before it's finished. He's angry enough not to stop.

Wayne's face changes.

**WAYNE:** Get in the house.

**ELLIS:** Fuck you.

**WAYNE:** *Get in the house.*

**ELLIS:** You don't get to care now.

**WAYNE:** I have cared every goddamn day of your life.

**ELLIS:** Could've fooled me.

**WAYNE:** I already buried one child.

Silence.

**WAYNE:** I'm not burying another one.

Ellis stares at him.

**ELLIS:** You think that's what I'm doing?

**WAYNE:** I don't know what you're doing.

**ELLIS:** You don't ask. You tell.

Neither of them has anywhere to go. Ellis walks past him into the house. Wayne doesn't follow. Tater, who has watched this from the porch, gets up and goes to stand by Wayne's leg.

### Clara, after

Ellis's room. He closes the door.

Clara is sitting on the floor under the window.

**ELLIS:** Don't.

She says nothing.

**ELLIS:** I mean it.

Still nothing.

**ELLIS:** Say something.

**CLARA:** What do you want me to say?

He starts crying. Quietly. Almost angrily.

**ELLIS:** I don't know.

Clara moves over and sits beside him against the wall. She doesn't touch him. He leans toward her. The framing makes it look as though he rests his head against her shoulder: over his shoulder, his hair against her jacket, the horse patch in soft focus at the edge of the frame.

In fact Ellis is sitting alone against a wall with his head tipped against the window frame. The game doesn't show that.

## MISSION 8 — SOMETHING WRONG

**Playable:** Cal · **Where:** Vale Music · **When:** Tuesday Nov 19, 4:30 – 7:00 p.m. · **Length:** ~20 min

Cal is working. The bell. Ellis, alone, unscheduled, in coveralls, with the WTCR cassette in his hand.

**CAL:** What's wrong?

**ELLIS:** This is a social call.

**CAL:** You've never made a social call.

**ELLIS:** Maybe I'm growing.

Cal waits. Ellis puts the cassette on the glass.

**ELLIS:** I want to redo the vocal.

**CAL:** Why?

**ELLIS:** I can do it better.

**CAL:** It's already been on the radio.

**ELLIS:** So?

**CAL:** So nobody complained.

**ELLIS:** I did.

Cal hears himself in Ellis. That's interesting to him.

### Work

They use Vale's back room: not recording, just arrangement work. Cal on bass, Ellis on Loretta. No Dean, no Riley. The dynamic is completely different: sharper, more argumentative, and productive. This is a playable duet section: the player (as Cal) can respond to Ellis's phrases in ways that make them tighter or looser, and Ellis's AI reacts.

Cal tells Ellis a line in a new song is dishonest.

**ELLIS:** How can a line be dishonest?

**CAL:** Because you're pretending you don't care.

**ELLIS:** Maybe I don't.

**CAL:** Then why'd you write a song about it?

Ellis stares at him. Cal looks down at his fingerboard.

**CAL:** Play the bridge again.

### The slip

They stop. Ellis tunes. Almost idly:

**ELLIS:** Clara likes the old version better.

Cal keeps tuning.

**CAL:** Who?

**ELLIS:** Clara.

Cal looks up.

**CAL:** Who's Clara?

Ellis looks almost surprised.

**ELLIS:** You've met her.

**CAL:** Have I?

**ELLIS:** At Marlon's.

Cal thinks. He's good with faces.

**CAL:** No.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CAL:** Don't think so.

Ellis shrugs.

**ELLIS:** Whatever.

Cal goes back to his bass. Ellis starts the bridge again.

Tiny. Do not score it. Do not let Cal stare suspiciously. Nothing happens. On replay, it's enormous.

> **Cal's rule.** Cal doesn't think *my singer is seeing a person.* That would be ridiculous. He thinks *who's Clara?* And because Cal remembers anomalies the way he remembers a buzz, he'll think it again.

## MISSION 9 — DEMAND

**Playable:** Riley → Dean → Cal → Ellis · **Where:** WTCR → the campus → Vale Music → Marlon's · **When:** Wednesday Nov 20 – Friday Nov 22 · **Length:** ~15 min

Riley stops by WTCR between classes. Martin hands her an envelope with the station's address on it in round handwriting, forwarded from the student mail room.

**MARTIN:** For y'all.

**RILEY:** What is it?

**MARTIN:** Somebody wrote the station.

She opens it on the stairs. A sheet of pink notepaper from a beauty-supply company.

> *To WTCR. I saw a band called the Blakes at the Blind Tiger in Laurel City on Nov 16. The drummer said you played them on your station. I can't get your station. I would like to buy their record, the one about the kitchen light. I don't have a record player but my sister does. Where can I get it? Thank you, Carla Vickery.*

A Laurel City address. Riley stares at it. There is no record. She laughs. Then she stops. Someone, somewhere, wants one.

### The relay

This is a short handoff sequence, the letter passing hand to hand across the map, each switch triggered by the paper changing hands.

Riley carries it across the quad and sees Dean coming out of the student union. Stutter. Dean has it.

**DEAN:** We're making a record.

**RILEY:** One person wrote a letter.

**DEAN:** *Demand.*

Dean carries it into Vale's. Cal is at the counter. Stutter. Cal reads it.

**CAL:** One person.

**DEAN:** Demand.

**CAL:** Stop saying that.

Cal drives it to Marlon's for Friday's rehearsal and Ellis walks in with Loretta. Stutter. Ellis. Dean hands him the letter.

Ellis reads it slowly. Then again.

*The one about the kitchen light.*

He looks up.

**ELLIS:** She wants it?

**RILEY:** Apparently.

He looks at the three of them. Something changes. Until this moment the band has been an experience. Now it might become a thing, something that exists when they aren't in the room.

> **Replay layer.** Carla Vickery is the woman in the hairdresser's smock who stayed. She asked for the song he wrote about his mother leaving, the one he was driving to play the night Grace died.

## MISSION 10 — THANKSGIVING

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** the Starlite · **When:** Thursday Nov 28, 4:00 – 7:45 p.m. · **Length:** ~20 min

> The HUD shows **THURSDAY**.

Thanksgiving. The Blake house doesn't cook. (Before 1973 the Tollivers had them out every year. Now Ada Tolliver asks, every year, and Wayne says they have plans.)

The plan is supper at the Starlite, the Thanksgiving plate: $1.95, turkey and dressing, cranberry from the can with the ridges still on it, green beans, a roll, pie extra.

Wayne drives, because it's his idea, though it isn't his idea; it's what they do. They sit in a booth across from each other. The diner is half full: truckers far from home, an old man alone, a family with a baby, a couple who aren't speaking. A television on a bracket over the counter is showing the late game, the Cowboys and the Redskins from Dallas.

Lynette brings the plates. She recognizes Ellis.

**LYNETTE:** You're with the drummer.

**ELLIS:** Allegedly.

**LYNETTE:** He was in here Tuesday at four in the morning and left a two-dollar tip on a sixty-cent coffee.

**ELLIS:** That's Dean.

**LYNETTE:** Tell him I'm not impressed.

**ELLIS:** He'll love that.

She goes. Wayne looks at Ellis.

**WAYNE:** Drummer.

**ELLIS:** Dean.

**WAYNE:** Rich kid.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

They eat. They don't talk. The player can do small things: pass the salt, look out the window at the trucks, watch the game.

Then something happens on the television. Dallas's starting quarterback is knocked out of the game, and a rookie nobody has ever heard of comes in, a skinny backup the announcers keep calling "the Mad Bomber," and Dallas is losing, and then they're not losing by as much, and then the whole diner is watching. The trucker at the counter stands up. The old man turns around on his stool. Wayne puts his fork down.

And for about four minutes, Wayne and Ellis Blake are a father and son at a ballgame. Wayne slaps the table when the rookie throws the long one at the end. Ellis laughs out loud. Wayne says *"Would you look at that"* to nobody and Ellis says *"Kid's never thrown a pass in his life"* and Wayne says *"Well, he has now,"* and they both laugh.

Then the game is over and the diner goes back to being a diner. They finish their pie. Wayne pays. They drive home in the same truck without talking, and it's a different kind of not talking than usual.

> **Design note.** This is one of the chapter's two quietest scenes and one of its best. It's a real game (Thanksgiving Day, 1974). The player should feel how little it takes, and how rarely it comes.

## MISSION 11 — SIGNAL

**Playable:** rotating through a performance · **Where:** Marlon's Tavern · **When:** Friday Nov 29, 8:30 p.m. – 1:30 a.m. · **Length:** ~30 min

Another Friday at Marlon's, the day after Thanksgiving. Fuller again: people home for the holiday, college kids who drove up from Tannersville, a few faces from the Blind Tiger who made the drive. The performance isn't the point. The letter is.

Before they start, Dean tapes Carla Vickery's pink letter to the resonant head of his kick drum, facing the crowd, with electrical tape at the corners.

**CAL:** That's going to get destroyed.

**DEAN:** Then we'll remember it.

**RILEY:** Why not put it somewhere safe?

**DEAN:** Because then nobody sees it.

Ellis looks at the letter. Then at the room. Everyone touches the kick drum. Riley touches it right on the letter.

**CAL:** Ready?

**DEAN:** Born ready.

**CAL:** No you weren't.

**DEAN:** Technically true.

**RILEY:** Can we play?

Ellis looks at the stage and says, without any defense in it:

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

### The show

Gameplay rotates between all four. They're better. More confident. And new dynamics are visible in the Room: Dean pushes too hard sometimes and Cal corrects him (the player may do this as Cal, or watch the AI do it); Riley is writing more (she has a new song, "Linwood," that she sings lead on, a little too fast, and the room likes it anyway); Ellis is getting comfortable being the center, and the player can feel his attention verb loosen: he looks at the crowd more.

During "Low Water," Ellis looks toward the back of the room. Clara is there, leaning on the wall by the cigarette machine, watching.

She used to show up mostly when Ellis was alone. Now she's come into the band's space, in the middle of a show, and nobody else sees her.

Ellis smiles toward her.

Riley, at her mic, catches Ellis smiling at apparently nobody over the heads of the crowd. She assumes he's looking at someone behind her. She keeps playing.

### After

Everyone's gone. Marlon is putting chairs up and wiping the bar and pretending not to listen. The four of them sit on the edge of the little stage with their legs hanging off. Dean is exhausted for once. Riley is barefoot. Cal is counting the money. Ellis has the letter; it came through the show with a boot print in one corner.

Nobody talks for a while.

Then:

**DEAN:** Record.

**CAL:** We can't afford a record.

**DEAN:** Demo.

**RILEY:** We have one.

**CAL:** A real demo.

Ellis looks at him. Cal is the last person anyone expected to say it.

**CAL:** Three songs. Done right. In a real room.

Dean slowly smiles.

**RILEY:** Where?

**CAL:** I'll figure it out.

**DEAN:** Oh, *now* we're ambitious?

**CAL:** One person wants to buy it.

Dean points at him, triumphant.

**DEAN:** *Demand.*

**CAL:** I hate you.

They laugh. Ellis looks down at the letter. Then:

**ELLIS:** Let's do it.

Everyone goes quiet.

**DEAN:** Do what?

**ELLIS:** Make something.

**CAL:** Three songs?

**ELLIS:** Three good songs.

**RILEY:** One great one.

**DEAN:** And then we're rich.

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** Famous.

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** Internationally feared.

**RILEY:** Closer.

Ellis laughs.

### The walk out

Marlon turns off the lights behind them. The four of them walk out the back toward the lot. The camera goes with Ellis, a few steps behind the others: Dean and Cal arguing about whose car to put the snare in; Riley talking over both.

Clara is walking beside him. Only Ellis turns toward her.

**CLARA:** You're doing it.

**ELLIS:** Doing what?

**CLARA:** Leaving.

Ellis looks at the others. They aren't leaving Hollow Ridge tonight. Clara isn't talking about geography.

He smiles.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** Don't forget me.

He looks at her. A strange line.

**ELLIS:** How could I?

She smiles.

**RILEY** *(from the cars):* Ellis!

**ELLIS:** Coming!

He jogs toward them. At the cars he looks back over his shoulder. From his eyes: Clara alone in the middle of the gravel lot.

Then a car's headlights (somebody leaving from the front of Marlon's) swing across the lot and into his eyes, and he turns his face away from them. When he looks again, the lot is empty.

### Signal

Cut to black.

Radio static. Martin Keller's voice, faint:

**MARTIN:** —that was The Blakes—

Static. A different radio, a different room: the sound of a kitchen, a spoon against a pot, and the song coming out of a speaker somewhere. We never see the listener. Static. Another radio: a car, a window down, wind. Static. Another: a hospital break room, a vending machine hum. The song keeps coming back out of the noise in rooms we never see, carried by strangers.

Then Ellis's voice, clear, from the recording:

**ELLIS** *(radio):* *...a light in the kitchen that stays on all night—*

Static. Silence.

**CHAPTER III COMPLETE**
**SIGNAL**

## Chapter III — design summary

### What happened on the surface
A college station recording. A 10-watt broadcast caught from a mountaintop. A show in a city that didn't care until it did. A farmhouse and mushrooms. A letter from a stranger. A decision to make something real.

### What happened underneath
**Ellis** has received evidence: a radio carried his voice when he wasn't there; a stranger wants his song; people in another city stayed. The world outside his head is beginning to confirm that what's inside it has value. That should be healthy. It also quietly teaches him that the strange things inside him might be the things everyone else was missing, and that will make it harder, later, for him to question his own perceptions.

**Radio** is the motif: a voice without a visible body that you accept because you understand the mechanism. Clara is also a voice whose speaker no one else sees, and Ellis accepts her because he thinks he understands the mechanism: she's Clara. The chapter never says this.

**Riley** believes altered states can be meaningful, and Ellis's first experience seems to prove her right. It's beautiful. Clara gets more vivid, but Clara already existed, and that distinction is essential. Years from now Riley will ask herself *was that when it changed?* The game will never give her the comfort of a yes.

**Cal** heard the name "Clara" once, and filed it. He noticed Riley and Ellis on the mushrooms and said the true, hard thing: *neither do you* (know him). He lost his temper once, at Dean, over the word "Dad."

**Dean** is still fun. His drug use hasn't been shown through suffering yet. He parties, plays brilliantly, talks to strangers, stays up. Life rewards all of it. That's exactly why it'll be dangerous.

**Wayne** drove up a mountain in the dark to hear his son and lied about it, and the same week, on his porch at sunrise, said the thing the chapter has been circling: *I already buried one child.*

### The Grace ladder after Chapter III
Wayne confirms he buried a child. Ellis weaponized "wreck." The player assembles: a car, a death, a son who survived, a father who can't forgive it. Nobody has said Ellis was driving.
