# CHAPTER II — SECOND VERSE

> **Tannersville · Hollow Ridge · Saturday, October 19 – Saturday, November 2, 1974**
> Main-story runtime: ~5 hours · Playable: Riley, Dean Holloway, Ellis Blake, Cal Mercer

## Chapter thesis

Finding each other was an accident. Staying together requires a decision. Each of the four makes it for a different reason. None of them says so out loud.

## World state at open

- Nobody outside one Friday crowd at Marlon's knows The Blakes exist. The chalkboard by Marlon's door says FRIDAY — THE BLAKES in Marlon's capitals over a gray smear.
- Riley, Cal and Dean live in Tannersville; Ellis is forty minutes west in Hollow Ridge. The Tanner Valley corridor becomes a road the band uses.
- Tully Voss, a stranger from Chapter I, has started showing up.
- Money: still scarce. The band has no equipment of its own except instruments.

## MISSION 1 — CIRCULATION

**Playable:** Riley · **Where:** Tannersville College, Gorman Library · **When:** Saturday Oct 19, 8:00 – 11:30 a.m. · **Length:** ~20 min

### Open

Black. The sound of a rubber date stamp: *thunk*, a pause, *thunk*.

**LIBRARIAN** *(o.s.):* Miss Riley.

Nothing.

**LIBRARIAN:** *Riley.*

Cut in. The circulation desk of the college library, 8:40 on a Saturday morning, sun in the tall windows, nobody in the building but a boy asleep on a stack of *Congressional Records*. Riley is behind the desk in yesterday's clothes under a different sweater. She's holding the date stamp over a card and hasn't stamped it. Mrs. Voorhees, the head of circulation (sixties, a cardigan held shut with a clip), is looking at her over the reserve cart.

**MRS. VOORHEES:** The Fielding goes back on reserve, dear. Not in your pocket.

Riley looks down. She has been writing on the back of an overdue notice. Chords: *Em — G — D —*, and under them, in her careful hand, a line from last night: *a light in the kitchen that stays on all night.*

She covers it with her hand.

**RILEY:** Sorry.

**MRS. VOORHEES:** You were somewhere else.

**RILEY:** Hollow Ridge.

Even Mrs. Voorhees almost smiles.

### The morning after

Chapter II begins with consequences, small ones. Riley has a work-study shift she took to cover the difference her scholarship doesn't, and The Blakes have already gotten into it, by about an inch. That's how this chapter works: inches, not miles.

The player works the desk (check-ins, the reserve shelf, re-shelving a cart in the stacks by call number). It's quiet, pleasant, a little tedious. As she shelves, Riley hums without noticing. Nobody notices until Hannah comes in with two coffees and a paper bag of doughnuts from the Krispy Kreme on River Street.

**HANNAH:** So?

**RILEY:** So what?

**HANNAH:** Your mountain men.

**RILEY:** They're not mountain men.

**HANNAH:** You met them in a bar next to a railroad track.

**RILEY:** One of them's from Belle Grove.

**HANNAH:** Dean Holloway doesn't count as a man. He counts as weather.

**RILEY:** Fair.

**HANNAH:** Was the cute one good?

**RILEY:** Dean?

Hannah stares at her. Riley breaks immediately.

**RILEY:** I'm kidding.

**HANNAH:** Jesus.

They laugh. Hannah takes a doughnut and nods at the overdue notice.

**HANNAH:** What's that?

**RILEY:** An overdue notice.

**HANNAH:** You've been humming it since we got home.

**RILEY:** Have not.

Hannah hums the melody of "No Name," the chorus, perfectly, with her mouth full.

**HANNAH:** Have too.

Riley looks irritated. Then she realizes: she remembers the song, all of it, the chords and her harmony and the place where Cal's bass went somewhere she didn't expect.

### The telephone

The desk phone rings. Mrs. Voorhees answers, listens, and holds it out to Riley at arm's length, as if it might be contagious.

**MRS. VOORHEES:** For you. It's a young man. He's very loud.

**RILEY:** Gorman Library, circulation.

**DEAN** *(on the phone):* Eleven tomorrow.

Riley holds the receiver away from her ear.

**RILEY:** Who is this?

**DEAN:** Dean.

**RILEY:** How did you get this number?

**DEAN:** Your roommate said you'd be at the library. The library's in the phone book.

**RILEY:** That's disturbing.

**DEAN:** Marlon's. Eleven tomorrow.

**RILEY:** For what?

A silence. Dean sounds genuinely confused.

**DEAN:** Rehearsal.

**RILEY:** We have a rehearsal?

**DEAN:** We do now.

**RILEY:** Did anybody else agree to this?

**DEAN:** I'm working on it.

Click.

Riley stares at the receiver. Hannah is watching her over her coffee.

**HANNAH:** Are you smiling?

Riley stops.

**RILEY:** No.

### Dean on a bench

At 11:30 Riley clocks out and crosses the quad toward the student union. On a bench under an oak, someone is asleep under the *Tannersville Banner*. One boot on. One sock. His satin jacket is folded under his head.

Riley stops and looks at Dean Holloway, asleep. The player is tempted to wake him; there's even a prompt. She decides against it (the prompt fades if the player waits) and walks on.

The camera stays on Riley, then catches Dean in the background. Stutter. Focus shift. Riley walks out of frame.

Dean's eyes open. He sits up. The newspaper slides off. He looks down.

**DEAN:** Where the hell's my shoe?

Control transfers.

## MISSION 2 — FOUR CALLS

**Playable:** Dean · **Where:** Tannersville College → Vale Music → Hollow Ridge Auto & Service · **When:** Saturday Oct 19, 11:35 a.m. – 2:00 p.m. · **Length:** ~30 min

Dean checks his watch. Realizes he's late for something. Tries to remember what. Remembers: nothing.

He grins. Good day.

### The shoe

Objective one is literally retrieving his boot. Somebody (the player can find out who by asking around the quad; it was two guys from his old fraternity pledge class who find him hilarious) has put it on the head of the bronze statue of **Colonel Nathaniel Tanner**, the city's founder, in front of the administration building.

Dean climbs the plinth. It's a small, silly traversal puzzle. Halfway up, a campus security officer arrives: the same one Dean almost ran over last Friday.

**SECURITY:** Holloway.

Dean freezes, one hand on the Colonel's sword.

**DEAN:** Morning.

**SECURITY:** Why is your shoe on the Colonel?

Dean looks up at the bronze founder.

**DEAN:** Political statement.

**SECURITY:** Get it down.

**DEAN:** That's exactly what the establishment wants.

The officer puts his thumbs in his belt and waits.

**DEAN:** Getting it down.

### Dean's objective

Nobody has granted Dean any authority. This doesn't concern him. He has decided that the four of them are rehearsing tomorrow and that he will now inform them. Riley: done (by phone). Three to go. He drives.

### Vale Music — Cal

Cal is behind the counter restringing a Gibson for a customer. The bell over the door rings. He doesn't look up.

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** I didn't say anything.

**CAL:** Save us both the time.

**DEAN:** Eleven tomorrow.

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** That's when we're rehearsing.

**CAL:** Who's "we"?

**DEAN:** The Blakes.

**CAL:** Don't call us that.

**DEAN:** See? *Us.*

Cal looks at him. Damn. Dean caught him.

**CAL:** Two hours.

**DEAN:** Three.

**CAL:** Two.

**DEAN:** Two and a half.

**CAL:** Ten to noon.

**DEAN:** That's two.

**CAL:** Good.

Dean grins, already backing toward the door.

**DEAN:** See you at eleven.

**CAL:** Ten.

**DEAN** *(out the door):* Eleven!

**CAL** *(to the closing door):* *Ten.*

Mr. Vale, from the repair room: *"Who was that?"* Cal: *"Holloway."* Vale: *"The pig one?"*

### West through the valley

Dean drives to Hollow Ridge in the Chevelle: the player's first westbound run of the Tanner Valley corridor, and their first as Dean. It is a different road at Dean's speed. The switchbacks come fast. The Church of God marquee flashes past (REVIVAL OCT 20–27 — BRING A FRIEND). The Chevelle's radio gets the Tannersville rock station until the ridge eats it, then static, then a preacher, then Dean turns it off and drums on the steering wheel.

The world changes around him: campus roads become county highway; the commercial strip becomes a closed apple stand and a feed store; the hills close in. The player can feel Dean's discomfort as the valley narrows. He doesn't like places he can't see out of.

### Roy's garage — Ellis

Dean parks directly in front of a service bay with Bad Company still playing. Ellis is under a Pontiac on a creeper. Only his boots are visible.

**DEAN:** Blake!

**ELLIS** *(from under the car):* Jesus Christ.

Dean crouches down and looks at him sideways. Ellis, upside down, looks back.

**DEAN:** Eleven tomorrow.

**ELLIS:** How did you find me?

**DEAN:** Asked the guy at the gas station on the way in. He said, "Ellis? Roy's." Everybody in this town knows where everybody is.

**ELLIS:** That's the whole problem with it.

**DEAN:** We're rehearsing.

**ELLIS:** We're what?

Roy comes out of the office wiping his hands.

**ROY:** Friend of yours?

**ELLIS:** Never seen him before.

**DEAN:** Drummer.

**ROY:** That explains it.

Dean looks offended.

### The fourth call

**DEAN:** Can I use your phone?

**ROY:** Is it long distance?

**DEAN:** It's in town.

Roy points at the office. Dean calls Marlon's (the number's on a matchbook he took Friday; he keeps every matchbook). The player hears one side of it.

**DEAN:** Mr. Pettit! Dean Holloway. The drummer. From— yes. Yes, the one who invited— Right. Listen. Sunday morning, you're closed, the room's empty, it's a shame, is what I'm saying, for a room like that to— We'd clean up. Afterward. Completely. ...Yes sir. ...Eleven. ...*Ten.* Ten's fine. Thank you. You won't regret— Hello?

He hangs up.

**DEAN:** He's thrilled.

### Switch

Dean wanders off toward the Coke machine. Ellis slides out from under the Pontiac on the creeper. The camera follows Ellis sitting up, wiping his hands. Stutter. Dean becomes background.

Control transfers.

## MISSION 3 — HALF A TIRE

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** Hollow Ridge Auto & Service → the Blake house · **When:** Saturday Oct 19, 2:00 – 7:30 p.m. · **Length:** ~30 min

Roy hands Ellis a rag.

**ROY:** Your circus bothering my customers?

**ELLIS:** Apparently I'm in a band.

**ROY:** You weren't in one yesterday?

**ELLIS:** Not to my knowledge.

**ROY:** Good to keep track.

### Ellis and Dean

Dean has a Coke. He sits on an oil drum and drinks it while Ellis works, which Roy tolerates the way he tolerates rain.

**DEAN:** Marlon's letting us use the place before he opens tomorrow.

**ELLIS:** Marlon agreed to that?

**DEAN:** Eventually.

**ELLIS:** What'd you promise him?

**DEAN:** Nothing important.

**ELLIS:** How unimportant?

**DEAN:** We clean afterward.

Ellis stares.

**DEAN:** *You* clean afterward.

Ellis throws the rag at him. Dean catches it and laughs.

### The tire

Roy tells Ellis something came for him. Leaning against the air compressor is a wheel off the Valiant's spare with a used Firestone mounted on it: good tread, clean whitewall, a grease-pencil mark on the sidewall that says BLAKE.

Ellis knows immediately.

**ROY:** Carson brought it by this morning. Wayne was in there last night.

**ELLIS:** He pay?

**ROY:** Half.

**ELLIS:** Why?

Roy looks at him as though the answer is obvious.

**ROY:** 'Cause he's your dad.

**ELLIS:** Could've fooled me.

Roy hears the bitterness. He doesn't pry.

**ROY:** You owe Carson four dollars. I'll put it on for nothing, but I'm not doing it today.

### Ellis's money

It's Saturday. Ellis has what's left from Friday's pay, Marlon's forty, and now four dollars owed to Carson. The player gets the money screen in the notebook's back page again. Rent to Wayne isn't on it yet.

Dean watches Ellis count bills on the workbench, twice.

**DEAN:** Need anything?

Ellis, immediately:

**ELLIS:** No.

**DEAN:** Didn't say money.

Ellis looks up. Dean means it. For a second there's something under the noise.

**ELLIS:** I'm good.

Dean accepts it. He doesn't make a show of accepting it. He doesn't embarrass him.

### Dean leaves

**DEAN:** Eleven.

**ELLIS:** Cal said ten.

Dean stops.

**DEAN:** Traitor.

He goes. The Chevelle leaves a little rubber on Roy's apron, and Roy looks at the marks with the patience of a man who has been looking at teenagers' tire marks for forty years.

### Roy and Ellis

**ROY:** He's gonna get you arrested.

**ELLIS:** Probably.

**ROY:** You sound excited.

**ELLIS:** Allegedly.

Roy smiles. Goes back to the Pontiac. Then, from under the hood:

**ROY:** You going?

**ELLIS:** Reckon I might.

**ROY:** Go on.

Ellis looks at him. Roy doesn't come out from under the hood.

**ROY:** You're eighteen once.

**ELLIS:** You telling me to make bad decisions?

**ROY:** I'm telling you you're gonna anyway.

> **Band language — "Go on."** Roy says it constantly: go on and get it, go on home, go on. So do Marlon and Wayne. It's how mountain people say *it's all right, do it.* The game never lingers on it.

### Home

Late afternoon. Wayne is in the side yard mending a section of the dog-lot fence that Tater has never once tested. Ellis carries the wheel up from the Valiant and drops it by the porch steps.

**ELLIS:** I didn't ask you to buy that.

Wayne doesn't look up from the wire.

**WAYNE:** You're welcome.

**ELLIS:** I'll pay you back.

**WAYNE:** Didn't ask.

**ELLIS:** I don't want—

Wayne turns.

**WAYNE:** It's four dollars, Ellis.

Silence. Wayne catches himself and goes back to the fence.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**WAYNE:** When you putting it on?

**ELLIS:** Tomorrow.

**WAYNE:** Before you go anywhere.

**ELLIS:** I got something tomorrow.

Wayne's hands stop on the wire.

**WAYNE:** What.

**ELLIS:** Band practice.

**WAYNE:** Jesus Christ.

**ELLIS:** What?

**WAYNE:** Band. Practice.

**ELLIS:** That's what it's called.

**WAYNE:** You gonna throw that job away for this?

**ELLIS:** I practiced once.

**WAYNE:** You don't do anything once.

That lands strangely. Ellis doesn't know why.

**ELLIS:** What's that supposed to mean?

**WAYNE:** You get something in your head and everybody else gets to live with it.

Ellis's face goes flat. Wayne sees it and looks back at the fence.

**WAYNE:** Put the tire on.

**ELLIS:** Fuck the tire.

He starts inside. Wayne, without turning around:

**WAYNE:** That attitude's gonna get real expensive when you're paying rent.

Ellis stops on the steps.

**ELLIS:** You charging me now?

**WAYNE:** You're eighteen.

Ellis laughs, bitterly.

**ELLIS:** Interesting timing.

**WAYNE:** Twenty a week. Starting Friday.

**ELLIS:** You know what I make.

**WAYNE:** Then you'd better keep making it.

Wayne twists the wire off with a pair of pliers. Ellis goes inside. The band has just acquired another cost.

> **Replay layer.** "You get something in your head and everybody else gets to live with it." On April 12, 1973, Ellis had a gig in his head, and his sister was in the car.

### Clara on the porch

Dusk. Ellis changes the tire himself in the gravel, angry, with a flashlight in his teeth. This is a short hands-on sequence: jack, lug nuts in a star pattern, the old wheel off, the new wheel on. The lug wrench slips on a rusted nut and skins his knuckles.

**ELLIS:** *Fuck!*

**CLARA** *(o.s.):* Good technique.

She's on the porch steps with her boots on the step below and her elbows on her knees. Tater is asleep on the other end of the porch, facing the yard.

**ELLIS:** You want to do it?

**CLARA:** Not particularly.

**ELLIS:** Then support silently.

She watches. He gets the nut loose.

**CLARA:** He bought it.

**ELLIS:** Half.

**CLARA:** Still.

**ELLIS:** Don't defend him.

**CLARA:** I'm not.

He moves to the next lug.

**CLARA:** Half's more than he usually buys.

**ELLIS:** He bought it for the car.

She doesn't answer. Somewhere down the valley a freight blows for the Depot Street crossing.

**CLARA:** You playing tomorrow?

**ELLIS:** I don't know.

**CLARA:** Yes you do.

He looks over at her. She's smiling.

**ELLIS:** You're annoying.

**CLARA:** I've had practice.

> **Replay layer.** A perfectly normal line. Much later, horrifying in a very different way.

If the player snugs the new lugs around the circle instead of across it, Clara says, without getting up: *"Crisscross. You'll warp the drum."*

He finishes. He throws the bald tire in the Valiant's trunk to take to Carson's. When he looks back at the porch, she's still there. When the porch light comes on (Wayne, inside, flipping the switch without being asked) she's gone, the way people go when you look away.

## MISSION 4 — RULES

**Playable:** Ellis → Cal → Riley → Dean · **Where:** Marlon's Tavern, closed · **When:** Sunday Oct 20, 9:50 a.m. – 12:30 p.m. · **Length:** ~45 min

Sunday morning at Marlon's. Closed. Sunlight through dirty front windows makes the place look like a different building: chairs upside down on the tables, the floor still sticky, the jukebox dark, the pool table under a cover. No smoke, no crowd: four young people trying to find out whether Friday meant anything.

Across the street the Methodist bell is ringing for Sunday school, and continues ringing, faintly, for the first ten minutes.

### Early

Cal is already there. Obviously. The HUD clock reads 9:58. Marlon let him in on his way to church and left the key under a flowerpot, with instructions about the flowerpot.

Ellis comes in at 10:02 with Loretta and a paper cup of coffee from the Gulf station.

Cal looks at the clock over the bar.

**CAL:** Good.

**ELLIS:** What?

**CAL:** Two minutes. That's within tolerance.

**ELLIS:** Was that approval?

**CAL:** Don't get excited.

### Homework

Cal has written a set list and a rehearsal plan on a sheet of yellow legal paper, numbered, with times.

**ELLIS:** You made homework.

**CAL:** I made a list.

**ELLIS:** Looks like homework.

**CAL:** This is why some people graduate.

Ellis looks at him. Cal realizes he doesn't actually know anything about Ellis's education. Ellis lets him sit in the discomfort for a full three seconds.

**ELLIS:** I graduated.

**CAL:** Congratulations.

**ELLIS:** Asshole.

### Riley

10:14. Riley, with coffee, a canvas bag of books and her Martin in a soft case. Cal looks at his watch.

**RILEY:** Don't.

**CAL:** I didn't say anything.

**RILEY:** Your face did.

**ELLIS:** That's just his face.

Riley laughs.

**CAL:** Great. There's two of you now.

### Dean

10:43. Nothing.

10:51. Still nothing.

**CAL:** I'm leaving at noon whether he's here or not.

**ELLIS:** He'll come.

**CAL:** Based on what?

**ELLIS:** Nothing.

**RILEY:** Solid argument.

At 10:57 the front door opens and Dean comes in carrying a cardboard tray of four coffees and a sack of sausage biscuits.

**DEAN:** Good morning, musicians.

**CAL:** You're fifty-seven minutes late.

**DEAN:** And yet—

He hands Cal a coffee.

**DEAN:** —I brought peace.

**CAL** *(taking it):* You're still late.

**DEAN:** But hydrated.

Behind him, the back door opens without a knock. Tully Voss comes in carrying a folded drum rug under one arm.

**TULLY:** Heard y'all were practicing.

**CAL:** It's our first practice.

**TULLY:** Heard anyway.

He puts the rug down where the drums should go and goes back out to Dean's car for the drums. Cal watches him go.

**CAL:** Who is he?

**DEAN:** Tully.

**CAL:** Who's Tully?

**DEAN:** He's Tully.

Nobody ever hires Tully. He simply keeps carrying things until he's the crew.

### Rules

Cal decides they need rules. Dean decides they don't.

**CAL:** Rule one. Show up.

**DEAN:** I showed up.

**CAL:** At the time agreed.

**DEAN:** I don't remember agreeing.

**CAL:** Because you hung up before I finished speaking.

**RILEY:** He's got you there.

**DEAN** *(pointing at her):* No alliances.

**CAL:** Rule two. Know the songs before the gig.

**ELLIS:** Reasonable.

**DEAN:** Boring.

**RILEY:** I have a rule.

Everybody looks at her.

**RILEY:** I'm not dropping out of school because you three can't use a calendar.

**DEAN:** Nobody asked you to.

**RILEY:** I'm getting ahead of you.

**CAL:** That's smart.

**DEAN:** That's why she's in college.

**ELLIS:** What's my rule?

**CAL:** Don't rewrite songs halfway through them.

**ELLIS:** That's not my rule.

**CAL:** It is now.

**ELLIS:** I'll take it under advisement.

**DEAN:** There's our frontman.

**ELLIS:** I'm not the frontman.

All three of them look at him.

**ELLIS:** What?

**RILEY:** You sing most of them.

**ELLIS:** So?

**CAL:** That's generally how it works.

Ellis isn't comfortable with the word. Nobody else notices.

> **Band rules, established** (Cal writes them on the bottom of his list, and the list is taped inside the lid of Dean's trap case for the rest of the game): 1. Show up. 2. Know the songs. 3. Riley isn't dropping out because we can't use a calendar. 4. Ellis doesn't rewrite songs halfway through them. *(Rule 4 is broken by the next afternoon.)*

### Rehearsal (Ellis)

This should feel like discovering chemistry, and it's worse than Friday.

Friday had adrenaline. Sunday has daylight, and everything is exposed. Dean rushes. Ellis changes the arrangement of a cover in the middle of it because he's bored. Riley's harmony on the second cover collides with Ellis's melody on a note neither of them expected. Cal stops the song every sixteen bars.

The player, as Ellis, experiences this as friction in the Room: the band's AI follows him slowly and imperfectly, and every time Ellis reaches, somebody drops.

After the fifth stop:

**ELLIS:** Can we finish one fucking song before you autopsy it?

**CAL:** Finishing it wrong doesn't make it right.

**ELLIS:** It's music.

**CAL:** I know. That's why I stop it.

That tells Ellis something: Cal is pedantic because he cares about music more than he'll ever say.

### Playable Cal — "Kick with me"

Cal walks across the stage to stand beside the kit. Camera catches. Control transfers.

From Cal's side, rehearsal becomes almost analytical: the player hears small timing problems more clearly, the space between Dean's kick drum and Cal's note a few milliseconds wide, then wider.

**CAL:** Kick with me.

**DEAN:** I am.

**CAL:** No. You're playing *at* me.

**DEAN:** What's the difference?

Cal plays four bars alone: a simple figure where every bass note lands with a kick drum and the spaces between are exactly the same size. The player plays it (Cal's **lock** verb, now with Dean as AI). Dean listens. Tries it. Misses. Tries it. And then the bass and the drums lock, and the player feels it through the controller and hears it as a single instrument.

Dean's face changes.

**DEAN:** Oh.

**CAL:** Yeah.

**DEAN:** Do that again.

Dean has asked for instruction without a joke attached. A friendship starts underneath the antagonism.

### Playable Riley — the second verse

They come back to "No Name." Ellis plays the second verse the way he rewrote it Friday night, the melody climbing on the third line now. It's better. It's still stuck on the fourth.

Camera finds Riley. Stutter. Switch.

She hears the problem Cal named Friday night, and she hears that Ellis's fix solved half of it. The other half is under the melody: the chords haven't moved with it.

**CAL:** It's better. It still sits there at the end.

**RILEY:** Don't shorten it.

**CAL:** Nothing happens in the last two bars.

**RILEY:** Because the chord doesn't go anywhere. He took the melody up and left the floor where it was.

She plays it: the same progression, then on the last two bars a borrowed chord, a C where there's been a G, the one that makes the ear lean forward. The player chooses it from Riley's voicing options; the right one isn't marked, but it's audibly right.

Ellis hears it immediately.

**ELLIS:** Again.

She does. Ellis moves his melody to meet it. Cal adds movement under both. Dean comes in softly on the snare. The second verse opens up. The song suddenly has somewhere to go.

Nobody says anything for a second.

**ELLIS** *(to Cal):* Still hate it?

**CAL:** Less.

**ELLIS:** Less is up from Friday.

**DEAN:** I said the same thing Friday.

**CAL:** I'm leaving.

He doesn't leave.

### Home

A practical problem, discovered by playing: Marlon's stage has no monitors. From the front, Ellis can't hear the others well enough to end a song together. Twice they end in three different places.

**CAL:** When you want to end it, turn around. Face Dean.

**ELLIS:** Why Dean?

**CAL:** Because he's the one who has to stop. If you're facing him, we all know. We'll land it.

**DEAN:** Like coming home.

**CAL:** Sure.

**DEAN:** I like being home.

**RILEY:** You live with your parents.

**DEAN:** And I *like* it.

They try it. The player, as Ellis, turns all the way around to face the kit, and the band lands the song on the same beat. It feels good in the hands. The player will do this dozens of times across the game.

> **Band language, established.** Turning to face Dean is now **home**: bring it in, end the song. It's the input the player uses to end songs for the rest of the game, including the last one.

### "Low Water" (all four)

While Cal and Ellis argue about the bridge of a cover, Dean starts tapping on the edge of a table with his fingers: not a song, just hands, a loping thing with a hitch in it.

Cal, without looking, answers it on the bass: three notes, a slide, a rest where the hitch is.

Riley looks up. Adds a chord. Then another that shouldn't follow it and does.

**ELLIS:** Keep doing that.

Nobody planned this. The player rotates across all four as it forms: Dean holding the hitch; Cal finding a figure that walks down; Riley making the chords ring open; Ellis, with no words, singing nonsense syllables over the top. Then a line appears. Then another:

> *When the creek comes up you can't get across,*
> *so you sit on the hood and you wait.*

It's the low-water bridge on the South Fork, the one that floods every spring. Riley answers the line with a harmony that's almost a second melody. Cal changes his figure. Dean drops out completely for a bar and comes back like a door opening.

Ellis laughs in the middle of a line, because he's having fun, purely. No audience, no money, no Wayne. For about three minutes Ellis is just eighteen years old making noise with his friends.

That needs to matter, and it can only matter if it's allowed to run. The player can let this jam go on as long as they like, and the game will hold it.

When they finally stop:

**DEAN:** What's that called?

**ELLIS:** Low water.

**RILEY:** Like the bridge?

**ELLIS:** You know the bridge?

**RILEY:** Hannah drove us over it at three in the morning and I screamed.

**ELLIS:** Everybody screams at that bridge.

### Clara in the booth

They break. Ellis goes to the bar for a glass of water from the soda gun. He glances at one of the booths along the wall.

Clara is sitting in it with her feet up on the opposite seat, the way she sits everywhere. She looks like she's been listening.

**CLARA:** That one's better.

**ELLIS:** Than mine?

**CLARA:** Yep.

**ELLIS:** Traitor.

Across the room, Riley, who can hear only Ellis:

**RILEY:** What?

Ellis turns toward her.

**ELLIS:** Me and the soda gun are having words.

When he looks back at the booth, Clara is still there, still with her boots up. No disappearance, no sting. The player assumes Riley simply couldn't hear a quiet conversation across a bar, or that Ellis was muttering. Nothing is emphasized.

> **Blocking note.** Riley is at the stage, thirty feet away, facing the kit. The booth is in her peripheral vision at best. Nothing here strains belief.

### After

Noon. Cal packs his bass. Tully is already rolling the drum rug.

**DEAN:** Next Sunday.

**CAL:** Ten.

**DEAN:** Eleven.

**CAL:** Ten.

**RILEY:** Ten-thirty.

**ELLIS:** Democracy.

**CAL:** Fine.

**DEAN:** See? Band.

Nobody contradicts him this time.

### The back steps (Ellis and Cal)

Everyone's leaving. Ellis is sitting on the back steps with a cigarette, looking at the rail line, while Cal loads his car in the alley. Cal comes and stands beside him because the car is loaded and he has nowhere else to be.

**ELLIS:** You ever relax?

**CAL:** I'm relaxed now.

Ellis looks at Cal's posture: straight, hands in his jacket pockets, weight on both feet.

**ELLIS:** You look like you're waiting on a bus.

**CAL:** What does relaxed look like?

Ellis thinks about it.

**ELLIS:** Not you.

Cal almost smiles.

**CAL:** You rewrote the second verse Friday night.

**ELLIS:** Couldn't sleep.

**CAL:** It's better.

**ELLIS:** I know it's better.

**CAL:** I'm saying it anyway.

**ELLIS:** Why?

Cal thinks about how to answer.

**CAL:** Because when it's worse I'll say that too. I want you to know I don't only say worse.

Ellis looks up at him. It's the most Cal has said at once.

**ELLIS:** Okay.

**CAL:** Okay.

Cal goes to his car. Ellis sits there with the cigarette. A freight comes through. He counts the cars without meaning to.

## MISSION 5 — THURSDAY

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** the Blake house · **When:** Thursday Oct 24, 7:40 – 9:15 p.m. · **Length:** ~15 min (the player decides)

> The HUD shows **THURSDAY**.

This is the shortest mission in the chapter and it has no objective marker. The player starts in the Valiant in the gravel of the Blake yard, engine off, at 7:40 on a Thursday evening.

For six months Ellis has spent Thursday nights at Marlon's. Since Friday, the chalkboard says the band plays Fridays. There's no reason to go to Marlon's tonight. There's nowhere else in Hollow Ridge to be.

Clara is in the passenger seat with her boots on the dash.

**CLARA:** It's Thursday. Rent's Friday.

She looks at the lamp in the front window.

**CLARA:** Starlite's open. You haven't eaten.

The player can drive away (anywhere, with Clara along; the mission quietly ends and the chapter continues). If the player goes inside (Clara stays in the car, and she's gone when he comes back out):

**WAYNE:** Not playing tonight?

**ELLIS:** Band's Fridays now.

**WAYNE:** Hm.

The player's only prompts are **Sit** (the other chair, the one with the afghan on the back), **Watch**, and **Leave**.

**WAYNE:** Night.

**ELLIS:** Night.

Wayne goes down the hall. Ellis sits in the dark for a while. Then he goes out and fills Tater's bowl, which is already full.

## MISSION 6 — PROFESSIONAL MUSICIANS

**Playable:** Cal → Ellis → Riley → Dean · **Where:** Vale Music → Tannersville Motor Sales · **When:** Saturday Oct 26, 9:00 a.m. – 4:30 p.m. · **Length:** ~40 min

### The problem

Friday's second rehearsal (offscreen) established what everybody already knew: Marlon's house amplifier can carry one voice, badly. With two singers and Dean, they need a PA. Cal has found one through Vale's: a used Shure Vocal Master, two columns and a head, $325.

At Vale's counter, the four of them look at the price tag.

**DEAN:** My dad could—

**ELLIS** *(immediately):* No.

Dean looks at him.

**DEAN:** I was going to say my dad knows somebody.

**ELLIS:** Oh.

**DEAN:** But good to know where that land mine is.

Ellis looks embarrassed. Cal redirects before it can go anywhere.

**CAL:** We earn it.

**RILEY:** How?

Cal puts a flyer on the counter. Tannersville Motor Sales, the big used-car lot on the Route 60 strip, is having an AUTUMN SAVINGS SPECTACULAR: balloons, free coffee, WKTN's morning man broadcasting live, and LIVE MUSIC 10–4. Pay: $80.

Dean reads it.

**DEAN:** Absolutely not.

**CAL:** Eighty dollars.

**DEAN:** We're artists.

**RILEY:** You were asleep on a bench a week ago with one shoe.

**DEAN:** Artists sleep on benches.

### Tannersville Motor Sales

This mission should be hilarious. It's the first "gig" that isn't Marlon's, it's in broad daylight on a car lot, and nobody here wants what the band does.

Balloons tied to antennas. Pennant strings snapping in the wind. Salesmen in plaid sport coats. A man in a mascot costume (a sort of tiger, maybe) waving at traffic. The WKTN van with the morning man, Buddy Flowers, broadcasting live between country songs. Coffee in Styrofoam. Hot dogs. Families looking at station wagons. And The Blakes, set up on a flatbed trailer next to a row of used cars, under a banner someone painted this morning:

**LIVE MUSIC — THE BLAKES**

Ellis stares at it.

**ELLIS:** This is humiliating.

**CAL:** It's eighty dollars.

**ELLIS:** We're next to a Plymouth.

**CAL:** A very nice Plymouth.

**DEAN:** I love this.

**RILEY:** Of course you do.

### The performance

This is a performance mission tuned for comedy. The room model is different: the "crowd" is people walking past with other things on their minds. Children, grandparents, a man test-driving a Pinto who keeps honking. Nobody wants dark originals at eleven in the morning. The set list is Cal's: lighter covers everybody knows, a little country, a Chuck Berry, the Carpenters (Riley, deadpan, and it kills).

Ellis hates it. Dean treats it like Madison Square Garden. He plays to the children. He hits a cymbal every time a salesman shakes a customer's hand, which the salesmen start doing on purpose. Riley adapts easily. Cal performs flawlessly and without expression, which is its own kind of comedy.

Eventually Ellis loosens up. A little boy of five or six in a John Deere cap stands directly in front of him through three songs, staring at the guitar. Between songs Ellis crouches down to him.

**ELLIS:** You play?

The boy shakes his head.

**ELLIS:** Good. Still time to save yourself.

The boy smiles. Ellis digs a pick out of his pocket (a tortoiseshell Fender medium) and gives it to him. The boy runs to show his mother.

> **Observe** (available): *Oct 26. A kid in a ball cap watched my hands for twenty minutes like they were going to tell him something. They didn't know anything either.*

### Car-lot chaos

Halfway through the second set, the wind catches the inflatable gorilla on the roof of the sales office (it isn't a tiger; the tiger is the mascot; there's also a gorilla), tears it loose, and rolls it across the lot between the cars. A salesman chases it. The mascot chases the salesman. The gorilla bounces off a Buick.

Dean keeps playing. Riley starts laughing in the middle of a harmony and can't stop. Ellis loses the lyric because he's laughing. The morning man is narrating it live on WKTN.

Cal keeps playing perfectly.

> **Design note.** Not every mission should be sad. Players need stories from this world that feel like their own memories. The gorilla will be mentioned in Chapter VII and in the 1996 interviews.

### Break — Riley and Ellis

Between sets Riley sits under a Cinzano umbrella somebody brought for the coffee table, with a textbook open. Ellis sits down next to her with two hot dogs and gives her one.

**ELLIS:** You're doing homework here?

**RILEY:** Some of us have futures.

**ELLIS:** Wow.

Riley looks up, realizing the joke landed harder than she meant.

**RILEY:** I didn't—

Ellis smiles.

**ELLIS:** I'm kidding.

She studies him. She isn't sure he is.

**ELLIS:** What's the book?

She tells him: William James, *The Varieties of Religious Experience*, for a seminar; a Harvard psychologist in 1901 collecting accounts of people who saw things, heard things, felt God; James arguing you can't dismiss an experience just because you can explain its cause.

He listens seriously. He doesn't mock college. He asks questions, and they're good ones. *"So if a man says he saw his dead mother at the foot of the bed, James says what?" / "James says, what did it do for him?" / "That's a strange question." / "That's the whole book."*

Riley realizes Ellis is a lot smarter than his circumstances make people assume. She tests it.

**RILEY:** You ever think about college?

Ellis laughs. College is so far away it's absurd.

**ELLIS:** With what money?

**RILEY:** There are scholarships.

**ELLIS:** For what? Being poor?

**RILEY:** Sometimes, actually.

**ELLIS:** That's depressing.

Riley laughs. Buddy Flowers announces the band is back on in five minutes, and a salesman is fixing to make somebody a deal they won't believe. The conversation gets interrupted before it can turn into something.

> **Replay layer.** "So if a man says he saw his dead mother at the foot of the bed—" Ellis asked that question. Neither of them notices he did.

### Behind the lot — Dean and Cal

Dean has disappeared. Cal, sent to find him, walks behind the service building.

Dean is leaning on the fender of a trade-in Oldsmobile doing a line off the back of a *Chilton's* repair manual he found on the seat. Discreetly, but not discreetly enough.

Cal stops.

**DEAN:** You want some?

**CAL:** No.

**DEAN:** Then we're good.

**CAL:** We're playing.

**DEAN:** Exactly.

**CAL:** You need that to play a car lot?

Dean laughs.

**DEAN:** Need's a strong word.

**CAL:** Then you won't miss it.

Dean looks at him. First real friction.

**DEAN:** You always like this?

**CAL:** Like what?

**DEAN:** Old.

**CAL:** You asked.

**DEAN:** Actually, I offered.

Cal turns to go. Dean, to his back:

**DEAN:** You're not my father, Mercer.

**CAL:** Thank God.

Dean's smile goes.

**DEAN:** Then stop acting like him.

That lands. Cal stops, and doesn't say anything, and walks back around the building.

Dean stands there a moment. Then he closes the manual and puts it back on the seat of the Oldsmobile exactly where it was.

### The money

They finish. The sales manager (a man named Stroud in a white belt) tries to pay sixty because they "took a lot of breaks."

Cal steps forward before anyone else can speak. Ellis catches Riley's eye: *here we go.*

**CAL:** The flyer says eighty.

**STROUD:** The flyer says ten to four. You took three breaks.

**CAL:** Your gorilla took one.

Stroud looks at the gorilla, deflated on the roof of a Buick. He pays eighty.

Back at Vale's that evening, they pool it on the counter: the $80, Cal's $35 from the week's session work, Riley's $20, and Dean, who throws in sixty like it's lint. Ellis looks at the pile. His rent to Wayne is due Friday: twenty dollars.

Cal notices. Says nothing. He takes twenty back off the pile and folds it into Ellis's shirt pocket without looking at him.

**CAL:** We're twenty short on the down payment anyway.

**ELLIS:** I can—

**CAL:** Next gig.

**ELLIS:** I'll pay it.

**CAL:** Didn't say you wouldn't.

No pity, and it earns Cal another piece of Ellis's trust.

Mr. Vale lets them take the Vocal Master with the balance owed after Friday. He looks over his glasses at the four of them.

**VALE:** Who's responsible for it?

Everyone points at Cal.

**VALE:** Good.

**DEAN:** I feel attacked.

> **Ledger begins.** Cal buys a green clothbound accounts book at the Western Auto on the way home and writes the first entry: *10/26/74 — Motor Sales — $80.00. PA down payment. Ellis: owes band $20 (by agreement).* Every entry after this one is in the same hand.

## MISSION 7 — THE LONG WAY HOME

**Playable:** Riley → Ellis · **Where:** Tannersville College · **When:** Tuesday Oct 29, 10:30 a.m. – 4:30 p.m. · **Length:** ~40 min

### Ellis on campus

Roy sends Ellis to the college maintenance shop again with two rebuilt alternators, and Ellis arrives an hour early for no reason he'd admit. He's carrying a folded sheet of notebook paper.

Riley comes out of a classroom building at 10:50 and finds him on the steps with his elbows on his knees, looking completely out of place among the students: coveralls, boots, oil on his hands, a cigarette cupped backward in his palm.

**RILEY:** Are you lost?

**ELLIS:** Extremely.

He hands her the paper.

**ELLIS:** Changed the bridge on "Low Water."

Riley reads it. Looks up.

**RILEY:** It's better.

**ELLIS:** Cal's gonna be unbearable.

**RILEY:** Don't tell him.

**ELLIS:** Finally, some education I can use.

### The lecture

**RILEY:** I've got Landry at eleven. Big lecture. Nobody checks.

**ELLIS:** I've got alternators.

**RILEY:** Are they going to get less rebuilt?

He goes. They sit in the back row of a sloped lecture hall with eighty students in it. The player, as Riley, watches Ellis look at the room: the chalkboard, the professor's pipe on the lectern, a girl knitting in the second row, the tall windows.

Professor Landry lectures on memory. He tells them about an experiment from 1932, Bartlett's "War of the Ghosts": English students were given a Native American folk tale to read and then asked to write it down from memory, again and again over weeks and months. Every time they retold it, the story changed. Details that didn't make sense to them disappeared. Things they expected crept in. The canoes turned into boats. The ghosts dropped out. The story became theirs.

**LANDRY:** Memory isn't a recording.

A student asks if that means memories get less accurate over time.

**LANDRY:** Read Bartlett for Thursday.

Riley writes it down.

The player can switch the camera's attention to Ellis in the seat beside her. He's writing too. The Observe prompt is available on him, and if the player takes it (the camera briefly shifts to Ellis's hands; this is a rare case of an Observe line collected from outside Ellis's own control) it shows what he wrote:

> *Oct 29. A story passed hand to hand till the canoes turned into boats. Like the depot after the fire. They put the clock back on the wrong side and now that's where it goes.*

Neither of them says anything about it after.

> **Replay layer.** Ellis's memory of the crash, that he was speeding, was built by a concussed sixteen-year-old out of what was lying around: his father's version, said a hundred times. He hears this lecture in Chapter II and it means nothing to him yet.

### Ellis on campus

Riley has another class at three. She takes Ellis around instead of sending him back. Until today he's only been on campus as a delivery.

She shows him **WTCR**: the tiny booth, the wall of records, Martin Keller cueing up a Neil Young side while he eats a sandwich. Martin nods at Riley and looks at Ellis's coveralls with the polite suspicion of a man who has been asked to fix the station's transmitter by the wrong people before.

She shows him the **practice rooms** in the music building: little cells with an upright piano and a window, a girl playing Chopin badly in one and a boy playing it well in another.

She shows him the **student gallery** (an exhibition of photographs of Appalachian people taken by an art professor from New Jersey), and Ellis looks at a picture of a man in overalls on a porch for a while and says, finally, *"I know that porch,"* and doesn't say anything else. Riley notices.

He sees a world where people are allowed to spend their time thinking about music and pictures and ideas. He's fascinated and a little resentful.

**ELLIS:** People get credit for this?

**RILEY:** Some of it.

**ELLIS:** I'd have been incredible at school if they let me do whatever I wanted.

**RILEY:** That's not usually how school works.

**ELLIS:** That's exactly my problem with it.

### Hannah

On the walk across the quad they run into Hannah with an armful of posterboard for her education methods class. She knows exactly who Ellis is.

**HANNAH:** The cute one.

Riley elbows her.

**ELLIS:** Which one's the ugly one?

**HANNAH:** Bass player.

**ELLIS:** Correct.

**RILEY:** You're both awful.

It puts Ellis at ease. For an afternoon he's part of Riley's world rather than Riley coming into his.

### The quad

Late afternoon. Students on the grass. Somebody's portable radio playing WTCR. Riley and Ellis sit away from the others under the same oak where the player first saw her, two weeks ago.

Riley lights a joint and offers it.

**ELLIS:** That weed?

**RILEY:** No. Tobacco shaped exactly like weed.

**ELLIS:** Smartass.

He takes it. He's been near marijuana before (it's 1974 and he's eighteen), but he's never smoked it with somebody he wanted to impress. He coughs.

Riley tries not to laugh. Fails.

**ELLIS:** Fuck you.

**RILEY:** Very rock and roll.

### The world slows

No psychedelic spectacle. The world gets a little slower. Sounds separate (the radio, a frisbee, somebody laughing across the quad). Colors stay colors. The main thing that changes is Ellis: his shoulders come down. That alone is strange enough to feel altered. The controls show it: Ellis's walk, if the player tries to stand, is looser; the camera drifts slightly more with the right stick.

They listen to the radio. Riley lies back in the grass. Ellis stays sitting up.

**ELLIS:** You always do this?

**RILEY:** Do what?

**ELLIS:** Nothing.

**RILEY** *(smiling):* Sometimes.

**ELLIS:** I'm bad at nothing.

**RILEY:** I noticed.

He looks over. She isn't making fun of him.

### What do you want?

**RILEY:** What do you actually want?

**ELLIS:** Right now?

**RILEY:** Life.

**ELLIS:** That's a stupid question.

**RILEY:** Probably.

A long silence. The player can fill it (a prompt offers Ellis a deflection) or hold (silence is always an option). If the player holds:

**ELLIS:** I don't know.

**RILEY:** Everybody knows something.

**ELLIS:** Fine.

He looks across campus, past the bell tower, west.

**ELLIS:** I don't want to die where I was born.

Riley studies him.

**RILEY:** That's different.

**ELLIS:** From what?

**RILEY:** "I want to be famous."

**ELLIS:** I don't care about famous.

He believes it. He's eighteen.

**RILEY:** What about music?

**ELLIS:** Music's how I'm leaving.

**RILEY:** And if it doesn't work?

He thinks about it.

**ELLIS:** Then I'll leave badly.

Riley laughs. He isn't entirely joking.

### Grace

Eventually, lying in the grass with her eyes closed, Riley asks the kind of question you ask on a quad in the afternoon.

**RILEY:** Brothers? Sisters?

Ellis goes very still. Not dramatically. Just enough.

**ELLIS:** Had a sister.

Riley opens her eyes. She heard the tense.

**RILEY:** I'm sorry.

He looks away.

**ELLIS:** Grace.

Riley waits.

**ELLIS:** Her name was Grace.

**RILEY:** How old?

**ELLIS:** Young.

Riley doesn't push. After a while:

**RILEY:** I'm sorry.

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

Then, almost under his breath:

**ELLIS:** You didn't do anything.

Riley looks at him. He realizes how that sounded.

**ELLIS:** I mean— you don't have to keep saying sorry.

**RILEY:** Okay.

And she doesn't. That is one reason he'll trust her.

> **Replay layer.** On a first playthrough, this is grief. On a second, "You didn't do anything" is a boy answering a sentence from the other side of an argument he can't remember having.

### Music

Riley gets her Martin from under the tree where she left it this morning. She plays softly. Ellis takes it after a while and plays "Low Water" on one guitar, slow, and she sings the harmony. No mission objective. No audience. Just the two of them, not quite looking at each other.

This is probably the first truly intimate Ellis and Riley scene, and nothing physical happens in it at all. That restraint is what will make the rest of it believable.

### Switch — the camera stays

At a quarter to three Riley gets up for her class. Ellis says he'll find his way back to the truck.

She walks away across the quad. The camera follows her, as it did two weeks ago, and the player expects the story to go with her.

Then, behind her, the camera catches on Ellis still sitting under the tree. Stutter. It turns back toward him. Control returns to Ellis.

This is a variation of the grammar. The story chooses not to follow Riley, because something else is about to happen.

### Clara in the grass

Ellis sits alone. Students pass. He picks at the grass.

When he glances to his left, Clara is lying in the grass where Riley was lying a minute ago, hands behind her head, eyes on the oak leaves.

**CLARA:** She's nice.

**ELLIS:** Don't.

**CLARA:** Didn't say anything.

**ELLIS:** You were about to.

**CLARA:** I like her.

**ELLIS:** Fantastic.

**CLARA:** She likes you.

**ELLIS:** You're high.

**CLARA:** *You're* high.

He laughs.

The player reads this as Clara teasing him. Something subtle has happened: Clara appeared immediately after Ellis smoked. She's no more vivid than usual; she's just there.

It's one thread among several. Clara existed long before this afternoon, and the game has already shown it.

## MISSION 8 — SECOND FRIDAY

**Playable:** rotating · **Where:** Marlon's Tavern · **When:** Friday Nov 1, 7:30 p.m. – 12:30 a.m. · **Length:** ~35 min

### The sign

The chalkboard says FRIDAY — THE BLAKES. Under it, taped to the front window, Marlon has hand-lettered a sign on the back of a Pabst display card:

**FRIDAY — THE BLAKES — $1 AT THE DOOR**

Ellis stops on the sidewalk when he sees it. It's real now, a name painted where strangers can read it.

Riley stands beside him.

**RILEY:** Still hate it?

**ELLIS:** Yes.

Dean walks past between them carrying a floor tom.

**DEAN:** You're welcome.

**CAL** *(behind him with the PA column):* Nobody thanked you.

### The room

More people than two weeks ago. Word traveled, the way it does in a town with one bar. Maybe sixty. Railroad men and their wives, the pool regulars, a table of Tannersville College kids (Hannah among them, and Russell, and Deb), a few people Ellis doesn't recognize at all.

That matters. Last time they played because the booked band vanished. Tonight, some people came to hear them.

Ellis notices. His breathing changes. The player can feel it in the controls: Ellis's attention verb is stiffer, his phrasing tighter.

**RILEY:** You okay?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

He's more nervous than he was two weeks ago, because now there's something to lose.

Outside the front window, an eleven-year-old face is pressed against the glass under the sign: Wesley Tate, too young to come in, watching. Ellis sees him and lifts one hand off the guitar neck. Wesley lifts his.

### Backstage

In the storeroom, Dean is bright-eyed and fast, jiggling one knee, talking over everybody. Cal looks at him once, the way you check a gauge.

**CAL:** Tempo.

**DEAN:** I know.

That's all. It isn't resolved; it doesn't need saying twice. Before they go out, Dean puts his hand flat on the head of the house kick drum.

**DEAN:** Everybody.

**CAL:** What.

**DEAN:** Hand. On the drum.

**CAL:** Why?

**DEAN:** Because we're a band.

**RILEY:** That's the stupidest thing I've ever heard.

She puts her hand on it. Ellis does. Cal looks at the three hands on the kick drum like it's a union meeting he wasn't told about. He puts his on it.

**DEAN:** Okay. Now we're a band.

> **Ritual, established.** Before every show for the rest of the game, all four touch the kick drum. Everybody mocks it. Nobody skips it.

### The performance

The set is noticeably better than two weeks ago. Songs have arrangements now: intros, endings, a place in each song where Riley's voice comes in. Cal and Dean lock harder. Riley knows where Ellis is going a beat before he goes there. Ellis looks at the others more than at his hands. And the player uses **home** to end songs for the first time in front of a crowd, and the band lands together, and it feels like a small trick they've pulled on the room.

### Dean's problem, in music

Halfway through the third cover, Dean starts to push. The tempo creeps. The player, as Cal, sees it before hearing it (Dean's knee going faster than his hands).

Cal looks back at him. No reaction.

The player's choice, as Cal: **lock** slightly *behind* Dean, dragging the groove back without stopping the song. If the player does it well, Riley feels it and adjusts. Ellis follows. Dean realizes. He looks at Cal. Cal holds his gaze. Dean backs off.

No dialogue. The band's first argument about Dean's drug use happens entirely inside a song, and the player plays it.

### The new song

Then "Low Water," the song that belongs to all four of them. It's rough and still unfinished, but it has something "No Name" doesn't. "No Name" sounds like Ellis with a band. "Low Water" sounds like The Blakes.

Riley takes the second verse on lead vocal and Ellis harmonizes under her, which the player hasn't heard before. Dean drops out almost completely before the last chorus. Cal plays the melodic bass line Ellis hated at rehearsal and now doesn't. Then everything comes back in at once.

Within the scale of a sixty-person bar, it's enormous. The room erupts. Dean stands up behind the kit. Riley laughs into the mic. Cal actually smiles and doesn't hide it fast enough. Ellis looks at all three of them.

### First fan

After the set, a boy of about eighteen with a Hollow Ridge High letter jacket and a paper napkin comes up to Ellis at the bar.

**BOY:** Can you sign this?

**ELLIS:** Why?

The boy thinks he's joking.

**BOY:** Because you played.

**ELLIS:** So did they.

**BOY:** Yeah, but—

Ellis takes the napkin. Looks at the pen. He has never signed an autograph. He writes *Ellis Blake* in a hand that looks like somebody filling out a parts order.

He looks at it. It looks stupid to him. He hands it back.

**BOY:** Thanks.

The boy runs off to show his friends.

**CLARA** *(beside him):* Looks stupid.

She's leaning on the end of the bar by the pickled eggs.

**ELLIS:** That's what I thought.

Junior, Marlon's nephew who works the bar on Fridays, passes behind Ellis with a tray of empties and hears him.

**JUNIOR:** What?

**ELLIS:** No comment.

Junior keeps going.

If the player has Ellis buy a round with Friday's pay, Clara, from beside the pickled eggs: *"That's rent. Put it back."* It's rent day.

### The radio man

Martin Keller, the WTCR station manager, came with the college table. He approaches Riley and Cal at the stage while they're packing.

**MARTIN:** You guys ever record anything?

Dean appears out of nowhere.

**DEAN:** Yes.

**CAL:** No.

Dean looks at Cal.

**MARTIN** *(laughing):* We do local music on Wednesday nights. Ten to midnight. Bring me a tape and I'll play it.

**DEAN:** Done.

**CAL:** We don't have a tape.

**DEAN:** We will.

**RILEY:** We don't have anything to record on.

**DEAN:** We know people.

**CAL:** You know people.

**DEAN:** Exactly.

Martin gives Riley a WTCR card with the station's address and the words *10 WATTS OF FREEDOM* printed on it, and leaves. A small opportunity. Nothing huge. The world past Marlon's has asked for more.

## MISSION 9 — FOUR IN THE MORNING

**Playable:** rotating (the player moves the camera between them; control is conversational) · **Where:** the Starlite, Laurel Gap truck stop, US 19 · **When:** Saturday Nov 2, 1:30 – 4:00 a.m. · **Length:** ~25 min

Nobody wants to go home. Dean suggests another party. Riley says absolutely not. Cal wants food. Ellis doesn't care. They end up, all four, at the Starlite, the twenty-four-hour diner at the Laurel Gap truck stop on US 19, because it's the only thing open between here and anywhere.

Nothing to perform and nothing to win: just people.

### The Starlite

Red vinyl booths patched with red tape. A counter with pie under plastic domes. A jukebox at every booth with a coin slot and flip cards. Truckers at the counter in feed caps. Pulpwood trucks idling outside under the sodium lights. The night waitress, **Lynette Crowe**, twenty-two, pencil in her hair, a paperback romance face-down by the register, brings four cups and the pot.

**LYNETTE:** Coffee?

**DEAN:** Whatever keeps me up.

**LYNETTE:** Everything keeps you up, honey. I can see it from here.

Dean looks at her like he's been struck by lightning. She's already walking away.

When she comes back, Ellis asks for another coffee before he's touched the first. She sets it beside the first. He drinks both.

Dean orders enough food for six people: two breakfasts, a hamburger steak, a side of hash browns scattered and smothered, pecan pie, a milkshake.

**CAL:** You have money for that?

**DEAN:** Technically.

**CAL:** Be specific.

Riley has kicked off her shoes under the table. Ellis is exhausted and happy, and he doesn't recognize the second feeling right away.

### Dreams

The conversation turns, the way four-in-the-morning conversations do, toward what happens if this works.

**DEAN:** When.

**CAL:** If.

**DEAN:** When.

**RILEY:** Fine. When we're the biggest band on earth, what are you doing?

**DEAN** *(immediately):* Everything.

**CAL:** Useful.

**DEAN:** Everything. Every city. Every country. Never go home. Never go to sleep.

**RILEY:** You already don't sleep.

**DEAN:** Practice.

They look at Cal. Cal drinks his coffee.

**CAL:** Pay my rent.

**DEAN:** That's the saddest dream I've ever heard.

**CAL:** Own a house, then.

**DEAN:** Better.

**CAL:** Maybe a studio.

That surprises everybody.

**RILEY:** Really?

Cal shrugs.

**CAL:** Somewhere people can make things right. Where the room's right and the gear works and nobody's in a hurry.

They look at Riley. She thinks about it seriously, turning her cup.

**RILEY:** I want to make one record that's still good when I'm forty.

**DEAN:** One?

**RILEY:** One great one.

**DEAN:** Low ambition.

**RILEY:** Quality control.

Then all three look at Ellis.

**ELLIS:** What?

**DEAN:** Your turn.

**ELLIS:** I don't know.

**CAL:** Bullshit.

Ellis looks out the window at US 19 going west into the dark, a single pair of taillights on it.

**ELLIS:** Somewhere with a better diner.

Dean smiles. That, he understands completely.

### The photograph

Dean produces, from inside his satin jacket, a Polaroid SX-70, the folding one his father gave him for his twentieth birthday.

**DEAN:** First official band photograph.

**CAL:** We look terrible.

**DEAN:** Authenticity.

**RILEY:** That's not what authenticity means.

**DEAN:** Everybody keeps telling me what words mean today.

He waves Lynette over and hands her the camera with a quick lesson she doesn't need. The four of them squeeze onto one side of the booth: Dean on the end half off the seat, Riley against Ellis, Cal at the window side looking like a hostage. Ellis tries not to smile. Riley elbows him.

Flash.

The camera ejects the print with its little motor whir. Dean puts it on the table between the ketchup and the napkins, and they watch it develop, which takes a few minutes: gray-green, then shapes, then four faces coming up out of the chemistry.

Dean reaches across for the ketchup and gets a thumbprint of it on the corner of the white border.

**CAL:** You idiot.

**DEAN:** Adds history.

> **Object — the Polaroid.** This photograph will outlast everything else these four make. For now it has ketchup on it.

### The check

Lynette puts the check down. Dean pats his pockets, then his jacket, then his pockets again.

**DEAN:** I'm four short.

**CAL:** Of course you are.

Ellis puts four ones on the table.

**ELLIS:** You owe me four dollars.

**DEAN:** That's a tomorrow problem.

Cal writes it down.

> **Running joke — the four dollars.** Ellis will bring this up for two years. Dean will never pay it. It's on a list in Ellis's handwriting at the end of the game.

### A smaller conversation

Dean goes to the counter to flirt with Lynette, who tolerates it while refilling ketchup bottles. Riley goes to the restroom. Cal and Ellis are left in the booth with the photograph.

Cal looks at it.

**CAL:** You know he actually thinks we're going to be famous.

**ELLIS:** Dean thinks he's going to be president.

**CAL:** He probably thinks that's beneath him.

Ellis laughs. Then Cal, still looking at the picture:

**CAL:** You don't?

**ELLIS:** Don't what?

**CAL:** Think this could go somewhere.

Ellis considers it seriously.

**ELLIS:** Reckon tonight was good.

Cal nods.

**ELLIS:** That's enough.

Cal studies him. Cal is perhaps the first person to notice that Ellis has an almost superstitious unwillingness to believe good things will continue. He doesn't understand why yet. He files it, the way he files a buzz.

### The lot

They finally leave around a quarter to four. The truck stop lot is loud with idling diesels. Dean is still energetic.

**RILEY:** How are you awake?

**DEAN:** Healthy living.

Cal stares at him.

**DEAN:** Don't.

Cal doesn't. For now.

Dean holds the Polaroid out to Ellis.

**DEAN:** You keep it.

**ELLIS:** Why me?

**DEAN:** Because Blake is our corporate headquarters.

**ELLIS:** That sentence doesn't mean anything.

**DEAN:** Put it somewhere safe.

**ELLIS:** Why me?

**DEAN:** Because Cal will file it.

**CAL:** Correct.

**DEAN:** Riley will turn it into art.

**RILEY:** Also correct.

**DEAN:** And I'll lose it.

**ELLIS:** At least you know yourself.

Dean hands it to him.

**DEAN:** Don't lose us.

Everyone laughs. Ellis looks down at the photograph in his hand: four faces, and Dean's ketchup thumbprint on the corner of the border.

## MISSION 10 — THE HALLWAY

**Playable:** Ellis · **Where:** US 19 → the Blake house · **When:** Saturday Nov 2, 4:00 – 4:45 a.m. · **Length:** ~15 min

Ellis drives home alone. **No Clara.** For once, the drive belongs entirely to the living.

The radio plays quietly: an all-night country station out of Laurel City, a trucker's request line. The Polaroid rides on the passenger seat. Ellis glances at it at stoplights (there's one) and at the crossing. He smiles.

> **Notebook.** If the player opens number 44 during the drive (parked, at the crossing), the entry on the page after the band list, written earlier in the week, is a single line: *Nov 2.* Nothing else on the page.

### The door

The house is dark. Ellis lets himself in quietly and starts down the hallway.

Then he stops.

Grace's door is open. Three inches. It's always closed. There's warm lamplight inside.

He goes to it. We don't go in.

Through the gap, Ellis sees Wayne sitting on the edge of a bed made up with a quilt, in a room he's never let anyone see. On the wall behind him, the corner of a poster. On the windowsill, the silhouettes of small figurines. Wayne is holding one of them in both hands (a little plastic horse, like the one on Ellis's dresser) and turning it over and over.

He's only sitting there, sober and quiet: an older man alone in a child's bedroom at a quarter past four in the morning.

Here the player sees Wayne when Ellis isn't the target of his grief. His cruelty is no less wrong. It is much more understandable.

Wayne looks up. Sees Ellis in the gap. They look at each other.

Wayne's face closes like a door.

**WAYNE:** What.

Ellis almost says something. He doesn't.

**WAYNE:** Then go to bed.

Ellis walks away. Behind him, Grace's door closes. *Click.*

Wayne's footsteps; his own door. Ellis waits in the hall until the snoring starts.

> **Replay layer.** November 2 is Grace's birthday. She would have been sixteen today. Ellis knew; it's the only thing on a page in his notebook. The band played the best show of their short lives the night before her birthday, and her brother came home happy, and her father was sitting in her room.

### The frame

Ellis's room. He tucks the Polaroid into the frame of the mirror over the dresser, next to the plastic horse. He looks at it. Four people smiling badly.

**CLARA** *(o.s.):* You look happy.

He doesn't turn around. The camera finds Clara sitting on his bed. She doesn't appear; from Ellis's side of it, she's simply been there.

**ELLIS:** Don't ruin it.

**CLARA:** Wasn't fixing to.

He keeps looking at the photograph. She gets up and comes to stand beside him at the dresser to look at it too.

> **Blocking note.** The camera never takes the mirror glass while she's at the dresser. It holds the Polaroid in the frame and their two faces from the side (Rule 12).

**CLARA:** Dean looks insane.

**ELLIS:** Dean is insane.

**CLARA:** Riley's pretty.

**ELLIS:** Go away.

She laughs.

**CLARA:** Cal didn't smile.

**ELLIS:** He did.

**CLARA:** Barely.

**ELLIS:** Counts.

She studies Ellis in the photograph.

**CLARA:** You did.

**ELLIS:** Did what?

**CLARA:** Smile.

He looks. He hadn't noticed. And he is smiling, in the picture: not the crooked half-grin he uses to get out of things, a real one.

**ELLIS:** Huh.

**CLARA:** Keep that.

He assumes she means the photograph. The player assumes she means the photograph.

### Bed

The lamp is on the nightstand by her. If the player asks, *"Get the lamp?"*, she doesn't move: *"You've got hands."* Either way he turns it off himself. Dark. After a while:

**ELLIS:** Clara?

**CLARA** *(in the dark):* Yeah?

**ELLIS:** You think it'll last?

Silence. The player expects her to reassure him. She doesn't.

**CLARA:** Do you want it to?

**ELLIS:** Yeah.

**CLARA:** Then tomorrow, act like it.

He thinks about it.

**ELLIS:** Yes ma'am.

**CLARA:** Goodnight, Ellis.

**ELLIS:** Night.

### Morning

Sunlight. Ellis asleep in his clothes. The bed on the far side, where Clara was sitting, is flat and made.

The camera moves slowly across the room: the Polaroid in the mirror frame, the plastic horse, Loretta in her case, notebook 44 open on the card table, Friday's pay envelope with twenty dollars folded separately and a note on it in carpenter's pencil, *RENT*, then the photograph again. Four young faces.

Under it, faintly, audio from Sunday's rehearsal: Dean counting in; Riley laughing; Cal saying *"Too fast"*; Ellis saying something nobody can quite hear; and then all four instruments hitting together.

Cut to black.

## Chapter II — design summary

### What changed
Chapter I made The Blakes by accident. Chapter II makes each of them choose it, for different reasons.
- **Ellis** discovers something foreign to him: belonging without debt. (Cal's twenty dollars. Dean's "Didn't say money.")
- **Riley** discovers the band wakes up a part of her that college doesn't reach, and that she's already writing differently because of it.
- **Cal** discovers musicians who frustrate him precisely because they might be worth taking seriously.
- **Dean** finds what he's been looking for without a name for it: people who keep saying yes. That will make him dangerous to Ellis later. Yes will save Ellis first.

### Ellis and Riley
Not a romance yet, and the restraint is the point. They're attracted to each other, and more than that they're interested in each other's minds. Riley sees that Ellis is much smarter than people assume. Ellis sees that Riley is more complicated than "college hippie." They already make each other's music better. When romance comes, the player should think *finally*, not *of course*.

### Ellis and Cal
Cal has criticized Ellis twice; Ellis has told Cal off twice; Ellis has used every useful criticism. He doesn't obey Cal. He listens. There's an enormous difference. Cal also protected Ellis's pride over twenty dollars without saying so, and Ellis noticed, and neither of them will ever mention it.

### Dean and Cal
The first real fracture: Cal sees irresponsibility; Dean hears control. And Dean has found the sentence that reaches Cal ("Then stop acting like him"), which he'll use again when he shouldn't.

### Wayne
More complicated without being softer. He bought half a tire because he's terrified of the road, and then used the road as a weapon. He charges rent because money's tight and because he thinks Ellis needs discipline, and because watching Ellis chase music reminds him Ellis has a future Grace didn't get. He sits in her room on her birthday. He watched *The Waltons* with his son and handed him a Coke.
