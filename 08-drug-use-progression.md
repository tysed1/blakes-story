# Drug Use Progression

**Deliverable 10.** Who uses what, when, in what context, how often, who knows, what it costs, and how much say the player has, chapter by chapter, for Ellis, Dean, Riley and Cal, with a shorter table for everyone else. It closes with the day Ellis died.

**Sources.** Compiled from the finished chapters (`chapters/`, committed V6/V7 text through commit 2431ed7), which are canon. `01-story-bible.md` §6 (character "Drugs" fields) and §12 rule 8 give the intent; where they differ from the chapters, see *Discrepancies noticed*.

**Citations.** Roman numeral = chapter; M = mission; CO = cold open; "VIII Snow Day" = the unnumbered interlude between VIII M8 and M9; Epi = epilogue.

---

## 1. Design principles

**The rule.** Bible §12, rule 8: "Drugs are neither glamorized nor moralized. They are fun, useful, beautiful, frightening and dangerous at different times for different people, and the game shows all of it."

How the chapters apply it:

1. **Honest about the pleasure first.** Dean's first use is shown as 1974 saw it: "For Dean cocaine is glamorous, adult, social and useful… The game offers no stat bonus and no glowing screen edges. What changes is the sound… The player should understand why he likes it. If the game is going to be honest about what happens later, it has to be honest about this first." (I M4). The first mushroom trip is allowed to be beautiful (III M6), and so is the start of Riley's acid trip: "It starts beautifully, and it's played straight" (VIII M11).
2. **No stat effects, no horror effects.** Drug states are rendered in sound, pace and camera (sharper sound, a quicker Dean; trails and depth on mushrooms; the medicated camera "a beat late"), never as gameplay bonuses (I M4, III M6, VIII M18).
3. **The function changes before the substance does.** Dean moves from fun (I–IV) to working fuel (IV–VI) to regulating feelings: "Recreation has become regulation. The drug is now for feelings." (V M6). By VII, "it isn't a choice Dean is making" (VII M10).
4. **Choice tracks the character's real agency.** Where the character is choosing, the player may be given the choice (the white crosses, VII M19) or the choice is written to be visibly the character's own (Ellis's mushrooms, III M6, VI M9). Where the character has stopped choosing, the player is not asked (Rusty's vial, VIII M3; Dean's Starlite bathroom, VII M10). When the game makes the player perform the act, the prompt names it plainly: *Take it.*, *Palm it.*, *Pour it out.*, *Flush.*, *Put it back.*, *Flush it.* (VIII M17, IX M7, IX M8, X M3, Epi M4; IX M7 design note).
5. **Clara is not a drug effect.** She appears sober, in daylight, after a full night's sleep (IV M6, IV M9), and "Clara came before the mushrooms" (VI M12). The drugs change how vivid she is (III M6, VI M9), never whether she exists.
6. **The ending is kept clean.** "What matters is that what happens tonight doesn't happen because of a drug." (X M3). The acid is put back, then flushed by Dean (Epi M4); the coroner finds him clean (Epi M4).

## Discrepancies noticed

1. **"Earlier chapters let the player choose whether Dean uses at a party" (VII M10).** No chapter from I to VI offers that choice. Dean's use in I M4, II M6, III M6, IV M4, IV M8, V M1 and V M3 is shown without a prompt, and V M6 says outright, "The player has no choice here." The first Dean drug choice is the film canister in VII M10 itself.
2. **Ellis's "truck-stop amphetamines" (bible §6.1).** The bible lists "Truck-stop amphetamines to drive at night (VII)" as part of his history. In VII M19 the white crosses are optional; if the player leaves them he never takes any.
3. **Dean's supplier and the Quaaludes (bible §6.7).** The bible names "a Tannersville College supplier" and "Quaaludes at parties." Neither appears in the chapters.
4. **"Seven weeks tomorrow."** Dean's last use is a bump at about 2 a.m. on Sat Jul 10 (IX M8); "I'm six days" on Fri Jul 16 and "seven days sober" on Sat Jul 17 (IX M9) count from Jul 10. By that count he is seven weeks sober on Sat Aug 28 itself, but X M3, M6 and M8 say "seven weeks tomorrow" on Aug 28 (and X's opening says "nearly seven weeks" on Aug 26).
5. **Dex in 1996.** Bible §8 has Dex "sober and older" in 1996; the epilogue's 1996 frame (Epi M6) no longer says he's sober.
6. **Wayne's Early Times.** Bible §6.4 gives Wayne "Early Times, two or three a night." No chapter shows him drinking.

*Resolved in V7.*
1. The VII M10 note no longer claims an earlier choice.
2. The bible marks the truck-stop pills as the player's choice. The trucker's white crosses are restored to VII's tracked choices.
3. The supplier and the Quaaludes are marked as background.
4. Dean's sobriety date is July 10, so X now says "Seven weeks today."
5. Dex is "Sober." again in 1996.
6. The bible notes that Wayne's Early Times is off screen.
