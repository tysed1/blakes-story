# THE BLAKES — Character Arc Maps

*Deliverable 5. One map per principal, then the supporting cast. Each map gives the arc in one line, the want/need/wound/lie, the chapter-by-chapter movement, the turning points, the last thing, and the lines that carry it. Mission references are to the chapter files (e.g., VII M8 = Chapter VII, Mission 8).*

---

## ELLIS WAYNE BLAKE (Sept 14, 1956 – Aug 28, 1976)

**Arc in one line.** A boy who believes he isn't allowed a future builds one with three other people, is turned into a myth by a country, remembers his sister telling him to go, turns toward the living, and doesn't get the time.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | to leave (I–II); to be heard (III–V); to be understood (VI–VIII); to know what happened (IX); to go home (X) |
| **Need** | to stop paying for the crash with his future; to let Grace go without leaving her |
| **Wound** | the crash; the sentence he said in the car; the mother who left |
| **Lie he believes** | that his suffering must be made useful to be survivable; that forgiveness has to come from Grace; that leaving a place abandons the people in it |
| **Truth he reaches** | he was driving at forty-five; a truck crossed the line; she told him to go on |

| Ch | Where he starts | What moves him | Where he ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Thursdays alone at Marlon's; Clara in the alley; Wayne's blame | an accident on a Friday: three strangers follow him | a name on a chalkboard; "Yeah. You got to keep yours." |
| II | half in, half out | rehearsals, "home" defined, the Polaroid, "Grace" said aloud once | he chooses the band; Grace's sixteenth birthday in the hallway |
| III | the band is his first real thing | the radio carries him; the farmhouse; Wayne's dome light | strangers want the record; "Don't forget me" |
| IV | ambition arrives | the 45, the van, the kiss, the Lantern; "Grace died in a car wreck. I was there." | "Take me with you." / "Promise." |
| V | velocity | strangers sing his words; the band learns about Grace; "El" | the midpoint: he's hugging air under a streetlight |
| VI | making the album | the Jazzmaster; Raymond; "I see her"; the description that turns Wayne white | his hand on the glass, alone |
| VII | fame | release; 11:47 on a couch with his father; sixteen hundred singing; *Rave* prints his sister; "Mine cost something" | a letter from his mother in a jacket that becomes a poster |
| VIII | the loop | the key-man clause; the page stolen; the switch fails on Clara; the cruelest sentence (to Cal); the photographs; Tolliver Bend; "I was driving" | the medication; the pen doesn't move |
| IX | medicated calm | the report; Hollis; the argument remembered; stopping the pills; Grace's room; the 1972 afternoon; the closed door | "Dad — Sunday?" |
| X | at his best and least safe | the tab put back; "Sunday's fine"; Roy's "Go on"; the open verse; the memory in pieces; home, and the song lands | turns toward the living; steps toward Riley; the light; the gap; 9:52 p.m. |
| Ep. | — | the world explains him; the people who knew him refuse to | an ordinary Saturday in 1974; GO HOME |

**Turning points.** The Friday turn (I M8). "I was there" (IV). The pull-back (V M16). The glass (VI M18). "Mine cost something" (VII M17). "At least mine isn't somebody I keep in a hotel room" (VIII M7). "I made you." (VIII M14). "When I go, I'm not coming back for you." (IX M6, remembered). *Palm it* (IX M7). *Close the door* (IX M14). *Put it back* (X M3). *HOME* (X M8).

**Voice across the game.** Mountain speech used lightly (*reckon, fixing to, Hm*), mock-formality (*Allegedly. That's a matter for the courts.*), sudden specific images when he forgets to guard them. Funniest at 3 a.m. and when things are going badly. On medication (VIII–IX), fluent and polite and wrong; the images and the silences go. Off it (IX–X), fast, funny, a little too fast.

**Last lines.** To Wayne: "Your biscuits are terrible." To Clara: "What are you?" To the crowd: "That's not— I'm not him." To the band: "Stay with me." In the coda, to his father at a kitchen table in 1974: "Hm."

## RILEY (Margaret Anne Riley, b. 1955)

**Arc in one line.** A philosophy student who believes altered perception reveals truth watches perception take someone she loves, stops using the thing she called a way of seeing, refuses to be his nurse or his witness, and becomes the person who decides what the world gets to keep of him, and then makes her own records.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | to make one record still good at forty; to be taken seriously as a musician and a mind |
| **Need** | to choose a life that's hers, not her mother's and not Ellis's |
| **Wound** | the fear of becoming her mother (music kept in a church loft for other people's God) |
| **Lie** | that altered perception reveals truth; that understanding someone gives you a say in what happens to them |
| **Truth** | "It doesn't show you anything. It just turns everything up." She can't make him choose, and she won't stand in a kitchen and watch him not. |

| Ch | Beat |
|---|---|
| I–II | The Martin D-18; the dorm; harmony found in a bar; rules; "Professor." |
| III | The WTCR session; the farmhouse (she's there when he takes the tea); Joan: "Who's got the melody?" |
| IV | Room 12 ("Riley does things"); the kiss; Ellis tells her about Grace. |
| V | "Saturday Bach" (she writes "Sunday Clothes" in a back pew); the missed exam; "Can I meet her?" |
| VI | Edinburgh declined; arranging "Sunday Clothes" and arguing Cal out of every verse; the Pasture (she holds out the jar; then the switch, and she can't see Clara); "I'm scared. There's a difference."; her hand on the glass. |
| VII | The registration form, filled in and folded; Oberlin, 1949; her song credited to Ellis; "Margaret"; the girl in the band (*Rave*); HARMONY IS NOT DECORATION; the seventh chair; "I love you, hold it still"; "Occasionally Astonishing" begun. |
| VIII | "Sing it right": she gives her song to his voice, by her own consent; her harmony (or silence) under it; Nina's piece; the trip that loops her own hand; she stops; she drives to Wayne; "Stay there"; "Name one more thing it has to be after." |
| IX | Swims every morning; her mother leads "Holy Manna" in the Tabernacle; counts the pills; "I can't watch you choose her"; leaves the house, keeps the band; plays him her song on a dock. |
| X | A bridge she can't finish on the walk through the hayfield; Nina asks to interview her, alone, about her songs; talks to Kit ("Define 'girl.'"); "Define 'want.'" / "That's two things."; votes yes; stands at the riser's stage-left corner with her hand out, and he steps toward her. |
| Ep. | "Maps" at her mother's piano; pallbearer ("I'm carrying him." / "She carries."); Hannah's card; the green notebook: *M. —*; the train line she won't publish; the book with the player's title; hands in the registration form; *Occasionally Astonishing* (1979); 1996: his maps were always wrong. |

**Turning points.** The folded form (VII M3). *Sing it right* (VIII M2). The stairwell on Greene Street (VIII M11). "I can't watch you choose her" (IX M10). *Hand it over* (Ep. M5).

**Protected from.** Becoming the girlfriend who exists to fix the troubled genius. She never fixes him. Her own story (the song, the mother, the form, the record) runs in every chapter.

**Last lines.** To Ellis on the dock: "I always do." To the grave: nothing; her hand on the clay. In 1996: "He drew maps that were wrong and he was sure about every one of them."

## CAL MERCER (b. 1953)

**Arc in one line.** The man who believes every problem has a cause and a fix finds the one person he can't fix, counts, provokes, is punished, comes back on his own terms, forgives with one word, and learns the difference between noticing and saving too late to use it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | a studio ("the room where good work happens"); the band to last; Theo, openly |
| **Need** | to act on what he notices; to let himself be seen |
| **Wound** | his grandfather at the bench; being blamed; being seen before he chooses |
| **Lie** | that watching closely enough keeps people safe |
| **Truth** | "I counted more." |

| Ch | Beat |
|---|---|
| I–II | The damaged amp; the ledger begins; "Be specific"; defines "home." |
| III–IV | "Who's Clara?"; the Lantern; Theo's matchbook; *Clara?* in the ledger margin, struck through. |
| V | "I'm your friend" (bleeding, Saigon night); "Can I meet her?" |
| VI | "Mine's real."; the contract; Marlon tells him about the Thursday; he promises not to say; his call to Wayne, and Wayne hangs up. |
| VII | Abstains ("Scheduling"); writes the correction ("Margaret"); searches Laurel City and finds Ellis at Engineers Park; "It's ours"; is ignored at a stage door; Dean fixes his books; Ellis sees him with Theo ("He seems all right"); says the truest hard thing in the chapter and keeps his promise to Marlon; overhears "the franchise." |
| VIII | SIGNED UNDER PROTEST; the ER; counts to eleven; "She isn't real, Ellis"; Kenosha; Theo's hand on his arm under the truck-stop lights; fixes a radio twice; "Theo's mine. That's real. You don't get to use it." |
| IX | Theo at the lodge as himself; the grandfather story on the lake; carries Ellis through the lost verse on the bass; three nights of Le Guin in one voice; teaches Ellis to solder; "I've known since August"; "I counted more." |
| X | "Is she here?" / "Yeah." / "Okay."; the vote; "What day is it?"; holds the flashlight in the gap. |
| Ep. | The list on the dresser; the empty shares column; tells Marlon he kept it; cries once, in the van under the tarp; Mercer Sound; "He wasn't so bad." |

**Turning points.** *Clara?* struck through (IV). The promise to Marlon (VI M16). "Pain doesn't make everything you do important" / "I know more than you—" (VII M17). "She isn't real, Ellis" (VIII M7). "Theo's mine" (VIII M8). The bass high up the neck (IX M9).

**Last lines.** To Ellis: "Okay." (X M1). To Marlon: "I never told him it was you." In 1996: "He wasn't so bad."

## DEAN HOLLOWAY (b. 1954)

**Arc in one line.** The man who says yes to everything learns to say stop, in time for himself and not for Ellis, and keeps the Polaroid.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | never to be forty and ordinary; everything at once; the band enormous |
| **Need** | to stop, and to let himself be good at something that isn't fun |
| **Wound** | his father's disappointment; boredom, which he thinks of as death |
| **Lie** | that momentum is life and stopping is dying |
| **Truth** | "Stop." (on the record) |

| Ch | Beat |
|---|---|
| I–II | The getaway car ("Who are you?" / "Drive."); names the first song by accident; the kick-drum ritual; the Polaroid; the $4. |
| III–VI | Fun, then functional, then medicinal; Lynette; Bobby's crayon drawings; the Vistalite; his best takes at 2 a.m.; "I don't ever want this to end." |
| VII | Blake's a genius (the first sentence of the myth); cut off at Belle Grove; Patty's envelope; Tully ("Drive." "Good enough."); Lynette's pie; Cal's cot; *divides by nine*; *to be paid in full*; "Dad's got a girlfriend"; Bobby sings "Tomorrow Problem" in the bathtub. |
| VIII | The Lincoln (breaks his own clause); arenas; Rusty's vial; the best show of the tour; an irregular heartbeat in Akron; "Keep him alive till Tuesday"; the Corvette; the drum kit that says BOBBY; "Leave the drums"; the porch light he can't walk up to; the coffee: "For the record, I figured you were just boring." |

## WAYNE BLAKE (b. 1929)

**Arc in one line.** The man who needs it to be his son's fault hands his son the report that says it wasn't, drives nine hundred miles because his son asked him to come, holds his hand in an ambulance, and becomes the only Blake left, feeding the dog twice.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | his daughter back; his son safe; not to be the last Blake; to be forgiven without asking |
| **Need** | to let the crash be nobody's |
| **Wound** | Raymond; Lorraine; Grace; every person who left |
| **Lie** | that blame would make grief bearable; that hardness keeps a boy safe |
| **Truth** | "I couldn't have it be nobody's." |

**How he loves, chapter by chapter** (always sideways until IX): the wax-paper lunch and the tire (I–II); the dome light on Stony Knob and "Pruitt told me" (III); "You want me to tell her?" (IV); the heater, the star, the tickets, the ballgame, forty miles an hour home (V); "Wanted to see it" and "You tell me" (VI); signs for the box, the converter on 88.9, the twelfth inning on one couch, "Loud" / "I liked it," the collar and the twenty (VII); the box on the table, "Son," five hours with the photographs, a blanket for Riley, eggs, "I'll drive you," two hours in a parking deck, the pie (VIII); the report and his own knife, "Yes," Sylva, two apologies to the mountains, the bass line in the gallery, the coffee can, "Go on in," "You want me there?" (IX); nine hundred miles behind a bus, a Coke on a bumper, the collar again, "Sunday's fine. I'll make biscuits," running a quarter mile through a crowd, the ambulance (X); biscuits on a Sunday, "She carries," the envelope handed back, a closed door on a documentary crew, the extra chair and a plate left washed in the drainer before the coffee, the dog, both stones paid from the rent, Opening Day, "He could sing" (Epilogue).

**How he wounds.** Instantly, exactly, with the sentence that will hurt most (I, III). The game shows Ellis inheriting it (VIII M7) and the player recognizes the face.

**Would never say, and tries to in the ambulance if the player chooses:** "It wasn't your—" (he stops). "You done good up there" (for *I'm proud of you*). "Son." (for *I'm scared*, and for itself). Only "Son." comes out whole.

**Last line.** "He could sing." (Opening Day, 1977.) The world's last look at him is "Hm." at a door in 1996.

## CLARA

**Arc in one line.** The part of Ellis that couldn't leave his sister in the car, given a body: a friend, then a need, then a jealousy, then a question she can't answer, then a fourteen-year-old who couldn't be Grace, then the one who points him home.

| Ch | Clara |
|---|---|
| I–II | Friend and confidant, mostly at night, alone. "Second one was better." "Liar" (1). |
| III | Farther, on mushrooms, in a field. "I wish you'd come around more." |
| IV | Daylight; "Take me with you." / "Promise." |
| V | Possessive; flickers younger once; "Liar" (2); "El"; can say where they met, not when; the midpoint: she isn't there. |
| VI | Brushes off the question of her reality ("You can see me." / "It's the one you're getting. Eat something."); absent from Riley's view. |
| VII | "November." "You made it prettier." "It was a conversation." "Old?" The first cruel thing: "They don't know you. Neither does Riley." The seventh chair. "Keep going." Vanishes at Lorraine's handwriting. Never in the photograph. |
| VIII | "He dresses like a sofa." "Liar" (3, the last tease). The switch tries to become her and fails, twice. Louder with no bass. "He's taking me." Gone from the doorway when the photographs come out. Grace in the passenger seat, older at each glance; "Daddy'll kill us both." "I made you." / "Does that make me less real?" "You asked me to." / "Right here." Then the medication, and silence. |
| IX | The empty chair. "Clara?" to the lake. Back on the fire tower: "Ask me something I know." "Who are you?" / "Who are you?" "That one's mine." "Now everybody's got me." Fourteen in the hallway: "I couldn't be her." / "She'd have put a frog in your bed." Into Grace's room. The door. |
| X | Back, easier, not pretending. "Quit writing on yourself." "Eat something." *Come on, come on* in the rain. "It's a good room." Asked what she is: "Late, is what. Push your hair out of your eyes." "Come on. They're waiting on you." On the lip of the stage for the last song. Her hand over her mouth at the last line. Looks at him and says nothing. |
| Ep. | Absent. Nobody remarks on it. |

**How she helps.** She is the game's only guidance (bible §11.8): what to look at, the high part before Riley sings it, the way, the day, the rent, who's lying. Always right about the world; always steering him away from the wound (Tolliver Road, doctors, the Blake plot, telling Riley, going home). Absent on the medication, back at the fire tower, and silent at HOME.

**What she never does.** Affects the world. Speaks to anyone but Ellis. Appears in a photograph or on film. Gets a reaction from Tater. Goes to the cemetery. Stays long with Wayne. Knows anything he doesn't. Says *go on*.

## GRACE CLARA BLAKE (Nov 2, 1958 – Apr 12, 1973)

Never met alive except in memory. Loud, bossy, braver with Wayne than Ellis ever was; a horse girl without a horse; alto in the youth choir, the high part on the Louvin records; WGRC; green felt-tip; named the dog, the guitar, and the song "later."

**Where the player meets her.** The snapshot on the mare (I). The hallway on her sixteenth birthday (II). A hand on a dashboard in the rain (V). The passenger seat on Tolliver Road, aging (VIII). The Impala, the argument, the look (IX M6). WGRC #1, #4, #7, #9, #11, #14 (IX M13). A whole Saturday in July 1972 (IX M13). The car after the white: "Stop saying sorry." "Liar." "It's okay." "Go on." (X M8). The unlabeled tape: "Don't get weird." (post-credits).

**How she differs from Clara.** Sillier, meaner in a fourteen-year-old's way, less wise. She teases instead of consoling. She wants to ride a horse and name a song and make her brother hang their father's underwear. She never forgives anybody gracefully. Clara was kinder because Ellis needed her to be.
