# Relationship Arc Matrix

*Deliverable 6. A great ensemble is a web, not spokes around a protagonist. Every pair below has a history that exists whether or not Ellis is in the room. V7: every Late and Last entry checked against the final chapters; chapter references are to the scene where the beat happens.*

Fields: **Start** (starting assumption) · **Friction** · **Bond** (bonding event) · **Joke** (private joke) · **Betrayal** (first betrayal) · **Disagreement** (deepest) · **Admires** (secretly) · **Misreads** · **Late** (late-game state) · **Last** (final memory, the thing the survivor keeps)

---

## The band

### Ellis ↔ Riley
- **Start:** She's a college girl slumming at Marlon's. / He's a mountain kid with one good song.
- **Friction:** "Thinking about taking it?" "Seeing if it'd be worth the felony." Friction as flirtation, with class underneath (her "Some of us have futures," II).
- **Bond:** The harmony on "No Name" (I): she sings the part Grace used to sing, and he says "Again." Then the quad (II), and Grace's name.
- **Joke:** "That's usually a yes from musicians." / "Maybe." Also "Again." Also his maps, which are always wrong.
- **Betrayal:** His first, twice over. In VII he takes the Sawtooth dates without asking ("Mine cost something"). In VIII he kisses a stranger in a Chicago hotel bar to make her stop talking about Clara, and Dex prints it. Hers: she drives to Cold Branch Road alone and asks his father about him (VIII). Then his worst: palming the pills and lying about it all summer (IX).
- **Disagreement:** Whether altered perception shows you anything true. She starts out believing it does. By the Greene Street trip (VIII), where she loops her own hand on the studio glass, she stops.
- **Admires:** He admires that she chose her own name and her own life. She admires that he *sees*, specifically, the thing nobody else noticed.
- **Misreads:** She thinks she has special access to him; she has the access he permits. He thinks she'd leave if she knew about the car. She leaves over the pills (IX, "I can't watch you choose her"), not the car.
- **Late:** Not together, and not apart. On the dock (IX M15): "Play me your song." She plays "Occasionally Astonishing." "It's good." "I know." Then "Stand by Dean." / "I always do." / "I know. Do it anyway."
- **Last:** For him: the song lands, and she's at the riser's stage-left corner with her hand out, and he laughs and steps toward her. For her: the green notebook, a first page that says *M. —* and nothing else, a line about a train she'll never publish, and a song called "Maps."

### Ellis ↔ Cal
- **Start:** "Friendly." / "Working." Each thinks the other is exactly what he looks like.
- **Friction:** Criticism. "Your second verse needs work." "Nobody asked you." "You played it in front of me."
- **Bond:** Cal follows Ellis's wrong chord in the first set (I), and Ellis sees it. Cal rearranges the money so Ellis can pay later (II), and Ellis sees that too.
- **Joke:** "Think in E minor." / "That's just his face." / "It was a turn." Also "Be specific."
- **Betrayal:** Ellis breaks Cal's van clause first, booking the Sawtooth dates alone (VII). The real one is in VIII: Cal says "She isn't real, Ellis," and Ellis answers with "At least mine isn't somebody I keep in a hotel room," in front of Dean and Tully.
- **Disagreement:** Structure against chaos, and whether pain makes art important ("Pain doesn't make everything you do important," VII).
- **Admires:** Cal admires Ellis's nerve. Ellis admires Cal's certainty and, secretly, Cal's father.
- **Misreads:** Cal thinks Ellis wants to be excused. Ellis thinks Cal wants to be in charge.
- **Late:** Cal comes back on his own terms ("Theo's mine. That's real. You don't get to use it," VIII) and refuses the apology in that room. The real apology happens at Walt's bench (IX M12), where Ellis is bad at soldering and Cal is the expert: "I counted more." At the Tabernacle (IX), Cal plays the lost verse high on the bass until Ellis finds it.
- **Last:** For Cal: holding the flashlight in the gap. Then the list on the dresser, the ledger's empty shares column, the van ceiling where he cries once, and "He wasn't so bad."

### Ellis ↔ Dean
- **Start:** "Who are you?" "Drummer." "For what?" "Whatever this is."
- **Friction:** Class. Dean has a pool; Ellis has a bald tire. At the Holloway Christmas (V), Dean's father is kind to Ellis and Dean hates it.
- **Bond:** "Need anything?" / "No." / "Didn't say money." (II). Dean knows when not to make something serious. The hood of the van in the rain, and "being forty" (V).
- **Joke:** The four dollars. "That's a tomorrow problem." "Demand." Peanut rankings (fourth place is a crime).
- **Betrayal:** Dean's, out of love. He spends an afternoon high with Dex Lundgren (VII), and "Blake's a genius, man" opens the *Rave* piece and becomes the myth. Then he breaks his own clause for a Lincoln with ambitions (VIII).
- **Disagreement:** Whether you should ever stop.
- **Admires:** Dean admires that Ellis is real in a way nobody in Belle Grove is. Ellis admires that Dean is happy, and that his father is a good man.
- **Misreads:** Dean thinks Ellis's darkness is authenticity. Ellis thinks Dean's lightness is shallowness.
- **Late:** Reversed. Seven weeks sober, Dean stands up in the green-room trailer and says "Stop" (X M6). He votes yes anyway: "But I said it. I want that on the record."
- **Last:** For Dean: the Polaroid, left on his nightstand at Knob House and buttoned back into his pocket in the trailer: "Keep it." / "I'll lose it." / "You won't." Then "I owe you four dollars" in the gap. Then four dollars under a rock, stolen by Tuesday. A tab of acid he flushes twice so nobody can ever say it. "He was funny. Nobody writes that."

### Riley ↔ Cal
- **Start:** She's a hobbyist. / He's a scold.
- **Friction:** The farmhouse (III): "You give Ellis whatever he took?" / "He chose." / "Neither do you [know him]."
- **Bond:** The studio (III–IV): both hear the same wrong note. She sees him with Theo (VI) and says nothing to anyone.
- **Joke:** Lists against crosswords. "Both can be true." / "No."
- **Betrayal:** None between them. It's the band's one alliance that never breaks.
- **Disagreement:** Whether you can protect someone from himself.
- **Admires:** He admires her ear. She admires his steadiness, and that he's at peace with himself.
- **Misreads:** He thinks she's naive. She thinks he's unfeeling.
- **Late:** Cal makes sure the single's label copy says *(M. Riley)* on every pressing (VIII). They're the two who fight the label's *Last Light* (Ep.).
- **Last:** At the graveside he says "Riley," and she gets up, and they go down the hill (Ep. M2). In 1996, the album with the last verse left open: "We left it open. It's his."

### Riley ↔ Dean
- **Start:** "Oh Christ." / "Riley!" / "Why do you know my name?"
- **Friction:** He mocks school; she mocks him ("You are literally the worst advertisement for higher education I've ever seen").
- **Bond:** The parties. She wants experiences that make her think; he wants experiences that stop him thinking. At twenty they look identical from outside.
- **Joke:** "Professor." Also the five-dollar bet on the kiss. Also her pancakes, which are always burnt.
- **Betrayal:** None, which is the point: the one uncomplicated friendship in the band.
- **Disagreement:** The ERA, loudly, in the van.
- **Admires:** He admires her brain. She admires that he's never once been embarrassed.
- **Misreads:** She thinks his using is the same as hers. It isn't.
- **Late:** The morning after the ditch (IX M8), she makes pancakes. They're burnt. He eats four.
- **Last:** A paper sack from his mother's pantry with *RILEY* painted on it in his drum-head letters, left on the step in Linwood without ringing the bell (Ep. M4–M5). He never tells her what else was in the bag.

### Cal ↔ Dean
- **Start:** "No." / "You don't even know what I want." / "Money." / (door opens)
- **Friction:** Time, tempo and cocaine. "Then stop acting like him."
- **Bond:** "Kick with me. You're playing at me." (II) The rhythm section locks, and Dean says "Do that again" without a joke.
- **Joke:** "Dad." / "Eleven!" / "TEN." / the ledger.
- **Betrayal:** The punch behind the van (V). Cal throws it; they both know Dean earned it; neither apologizes.
- **Disagreement:** Whether responsibility protects you.
- **Admires:** Dean admires that Cal is exactly who he is. Cal admires that Dean can make any room love him.
- **Misreads:** Dean hears his father in Cal. Cal thinks Dean doesn't know he's in trouble; Dean knows exactly.
- **Late:** Knob House, three nights (IX M8). Cal in a chair by the bed reading *The Dispossessed* aloud in the voice he uses for wiring diagrams, until Theo does a line of Shevek in a Cajun accent and Dean laughs until he cries.
- **Last:** Two lines in Cal's ledger: *D.H.: "Stop." (On the record.)* and *D.H. owes E.B. $4.00 (Starlite)*, which Cal never marks paid.

## The Blake family

### Ellis ↔ Wayne
- **Start:** Two men in a house with a closed door.
- **Friction:** Every conversation: "cars after the fact"; "You got to keep yours"; "I already buried one child"; "She know about Grace?"
- **Bond:** Wordless and practical: the wax-paper lunch (I), half a tire (II), a mountain in the dark with the dome light on (III), a car heater (V), forty miles an hour home from the ballpark (V), Game 6 on one couch (VII), peanuts on a tailgate in Sylva (IX), two Cokes on a truck bumper in Harrisburg (X).
- **Joke:** "Hm." Georgia football on the radio in separate rooms. Tater, fed twice by two men who don't know it.
- **Betrayal:** Wayne knew about the other truck the day after the crash and said there wasn't one. "I couldn't have it be nobody's." (IX M4)
- **Disagreement:** What Grace would want.
- **Admires:** Wayne admires Ellis's work (the studio, VI; the FM converter on 88.9, VII). Ellis admires that Wayne kept going to work every single day.
- **Misreads:** Each thinks the other blames him. Both are right, and both are wrong.
- **Late:** "You want me there?" / "Yeah." (IX M15). Then the trailer doorway at Glen Arbor (X M6): "Sunday's fine. I'll make biscuits."
- **Last:** For Wayne: the ambulance on Route 14, where the player chooses what he means and he can't finish it: "It wasn't your—" "You done good up there." "Son." Then biscuits at a Virginia counter on Sunday, the dog fed twice, the coupon book stamped PAID IN FULL, the stone, and the door he closes on a documentary crew in 1996.

### Ellis ↔ Clara
- **Start:** Old friends. She's "from before."
- **Friction:** Gentle teasing, then possessiveness as others take her place (the harmony, the audience).
- **Bond:** Every night he couldn't be alone. "You're allowed to have something good."
- **Joke:** "Liar." "Second one was better."
- **Betrayal:** "Don't tell them." She keeps him from help to keep herself.
- **Disagreement:** Whether he's allowed to leave.
- **Admires:** He admires everything about her, because he made her out of what he loved.
- **Misreads:** He thinks she's someone he knows. She is.
- **Late:** In the hallway (IX M14), at fourteen: "I couldn't be her." He closes Grace's door.
- **Last:** On the walk to the stage (X M7) he asks "What are you?" and she doesn't answer. "Late, is what. Push your hair out of your eyes." Then, not *go on*: "Come on. They're waiting on you." She isn't in the coda.

### Ellis ↔ Grace
- **Start:** Brother and sister, two years apart, their mother gone.
- **Friction:** She's in his room, on his records, in his cigarettes, in his car.
- **Bond:** Harmony on the Louvin Brothers in the front room; WGRC; "You're my brother. Unfortunately." (July 1972, IX M13)
- **Joke:** "Grace Made Me Late." The Turnip. Loretta. "Don't get weird."
- **Betrayal:** "When I go, I'm not coming back for you."
- **Disagreement:** Leaving.
- **Admires:** She admired that he was going to get out. He admired that she stood up to their father.
- **Misreads:** He thinks she died hating him. She died teasing him.
- **Last:** "Liar." "It's okay." "Go on." And a song she was going to name *later*.

## Across the web

### Wayne ↔ Riley
- **Start:** "That the college girl?" / "Her name's Riley." / "Fine. Riley."
- **Bond:** The kitchen on Cold Branch Road (VIII): she drives up alone to ask him about his son. He tells her about Raymond, Milledgeville and Grace singing harmony, things he has never told Ellis. He's known since August that something was wrong. "Does she have a name?" "Clara." "That was my mama's name." Then: "There's a box."
- **Late:** At the church steps (Ep. M2), when the funeral director says it's customary for the gentlemen, Riley says "I'm carrying him," and Wayne backs her: "She carries."
- **Last:** Wayne drives the memo books to Linwood himself. "He'd want you to have them." / "You don't know that." / "No. But I want you to." (Ep. M5.) At Thanksgiving he sits in the chair by the sideboard; she looks at him across the table and doesn't look away. He's gone before the coffee.

### Wayne ↔ Cal
- **Start:** Wayne thinks the bass player is the one with sense. Cal thinks Wayne is the reason for most of it.
- **Bond:** At the Harrisburg motel (X M1), Cal watches the two Blakes on the F-100's bumper and goes inside so they can have it. In the Glen Arbor trailer (X M6) he steps back from the doorway for the same reason.
- **Last:** Wayne calls Vale Music and asks for Cal: "There's band things in his room. Guitars. I don't know what's what." Then "Come Saturday." (Ep. M3.) Cal inventories the room while Wayne sits on the porch.

### Wayne ↔ Theo
- **Last:** September 11, 1976, the porch on Cold Branch Road. Theo sees Cal's face at the list, puts the boxes down, and goes out to sit with Wayne and the dog so Cal can be alone. Neither man says anything. It's the right thing, and both of them know it.

### Wayne ↔ Dean
- **Start:** Mutual incomprehension.
- **Last:** Wayne asks Dean to carry Ellis's canvas duffel home on the bus (Ep. M1). Dean takes it, doesn't give it to Riley for a week, and finds the tab. Wayne never knows what his son decided, and never needs to: Dean made sure there's nothing to know.

### Ellis ↔ Roy
- **Start:** Boss and a boy he's watching out for.
- **Bond:** The ridge and the radio (III). "Guess you better write another one."
- **Joke:** "Allegedly." "You'd get more for the truck." The two transmissions.
- **Late:** The gray work shirt with ELLIS on it, off the peg (VII), which ends up on a poster. Danny, mentioned once (VII).
- **Last:** At the foot of the stage stairs (X M7): "Go on. Get up there." For Roy, the drive home with his cap over his face, not asleep at first.

### Ellis ↔ Marlon
- **Start:** A bar owner who charges him for beers.
- **Secret:** Marlon invited him to play on April 12, 1973. Marlon told Cal (VI); Cal never told Ellis who told him (Ep. M3: "He said okay").
- **Last:** The chalkboard: THURSDAY — THE BLAKES (NEW YORK), never erased. In 1996 it's framed behind the bar, and Marlon doesn't give an interview.

### Ellis ↔ Patty Holloway
- **Start:** A fifteen-year-old in a Christmas crowd who isn't impressed.
- **Bond:** He talks to her like a coworker, gentler than Dean has ever seen him with anyone. She sends him a violet envelope by way of Dean (VII) about "No Name"; he writes back.
- **Last:** His reply, in a dime-store frame over her desk in Belle Grove (Ep. M4).

### Ellis ↔ Tully
- **Bond:** Two men who don't talk about what happened to them, carrying amplifiers.
- **Late:** "That's enough." (VIII, Eleven Times), when Cal and Ellis have gone past what can be taken back.
- **Last:** Tully is the first one down into the gap.

### Ellis ↔ Dex Lundgren
- **Start:** Dex is funny and smart and reads poetry, and Ellis likes him.
- **Betrayal:** The *Rave* piece (VII). A page torn out of memo book 68 on the bus (VIII). The Chicago kiss in print.
- **Late:** In the press tent (X M4), "She's in the room with my sister" goes into his notebook. It becomes his book's most-quoted line, and nobody who quotes it knows what it means.
- **Last:** At the cemetery fence, in a black suit, no notebook: "I'm not writing about today." / "Good." / "I'm writing about everything else." In 1996: "I sold a lot of magazines."

### Cal ↔ Walt Mercer
- **Start:** "Gig tomorrow?" / "Supposed to." / "Pays?" / "Then it's work."
- **Bond:** Talking while facing an engine or a chassis, never each other.
- **Late:** After the Tabernacle (IX), Walt shakes his son's hand, which he has never done, and then Theo's. At the bench (IX M12), he lowers his newspaper an inch when Ellis laughs, and raises it again.
- **Last:** By 1996 Walt's shop is MERCER SOUND, with the Philco on the shelf over the console.

### Cal ↔ Theo
- **Start:** The Lantern (IV). "You ever want work in Laurel City, call."
- **Bond:** Sunday mornings; a Teac only Cal can fix.
- **Late:** Knob House (IX), where Theo is there as himself and Dean carries the fiddle-leaf fig. At Glen Arbor (X), from the mix tower eighty yards out, Theo pulls the guitar channel down to nothing, then stands with both hands on his head.
- **Last:** The porch light left on in Tannersville the night Cal cries in the van (Ep. M3). In 1996: "Theo, my partner," once, and the documentary doesn't make a point of it.

### Riley ↔ Joan Riley
- **Start:** "Maggie." A mother who says "You're very lucky" and means everything by it.
- **Bond:** Voice-leading at the piano. "Every voice has somewhere it wants to go."
- **Friction:** Oberlin, 1949, which Riley learns about at Thanksgiving (VII).
- **Late:** She hears her daughter's song on the radio in a boy's voice: "He sings it too fast." (VIII) At the Tabernacle (IX), she leads "Holy Manna."
- **Last:** The alto row of the square at the graveside (Ep. M2). Then a phone call to Wayne Blake about an extra place at the table.

### Riley ↔ Hannah
- **Start:** Roommates who argue about efficiency.
- **Drift:** The band takes Riley. "Don't get famous," in a parking lot (VI). A wave across the registration gym (VII).
- **Last:** A drugstore card from Macon with a cardinal on it: *Call me when you want to. Not before.* Riley calls in December. "There you are." (Ep. M5)

### Riley ↔ Landry
- **Start:** "Would you like to join us?"
- **Bond:** He never tells her to quit. "Do you want me to tell you not to go?" / "No." / "Good. I wasn't going to."
- **Late:** After the Tabernacle: "I hated most of it. The part with your mother I didn't hate." (IX)
- **Last:** Three typed lines on department letterhead: *There is a chair.* One raised finger through an open office door in January (Ep. M5).

### Dean ↔ Richard Holloway
- **Start:** "At some point you've got to decide what you're doing with your life."
- **Bond:** The contract (VI): watching his father be brilliant on his behalf.
- **Betrayal:** Cutting him off (VII). From Dean's side: "The invoice."
- **Late:** By the buses after the Tabernacle (IX): "Dinner's at six." / "I know." / "Tomorrow." / "I'll be there."
- **Last:** Sunday dinners. Richard asks about the injunction technically, and says "Good. That's good," and passes the rolls, which is how he grieves for somebody else's son (Ep. M4).

### Dean ↔ Patty Holloway
- **Bond:** The bottom step, the cigar box of matchbooks (VII). "You're an idiot." / "I know." / "No, you don't."
- **Last:** A punch in the arm, hard, and then a hug, harder (IX).

### Dean ↔ Lynette
- **Start:** "Coffee?" "Whatever keeps me up." "Everything keeps you up, honey."
- **Late:** On the phone to Cal from the ER in Akron: "Keep him alive till Tuesday. I'll yell at him Tuesday." (VIII) Bobby's fifth birthday and a drum kit: "Leave the drums." (VIII)
- **Last:** "You want to come in?" She hasn't asked since February (Ep. M4). Bobby yells *DEAN* and hits the crash cymbal.

### Dean ↔ Tully
- **Bond:** Saigon night (V): Dean follows him out to the curb of a Savannah motel lot and sits beside him for an hour, and says nothing, the only time in the game Dean is silent.

### Riley ↔ Nina Sorensen
- **Bond:** The first critic to write about Riley as a musician (VIII, four columns in *Metro*). The Greene Street party, and the stairwell after, where Nina sits two steps below her and says "Okay" and nothing else, and walks her down four flights to a cab.
- **Last:** At the back of the church, standing, because Riley asked her to come. Her column: "He was nineteen."

### Tully ↔ Kit Adair
- **Bond:** At Glen Arbor (X) he dries her sun-gun's connector with the film crew's hair dryer, and he's the one who shows her where to stand in the wing.
- **Last:** After the gap (X M9), with Ellis's blood on his knees, he sits down beside her on an equipment case outside the film truck and takes the light out of her lap so she doesn't have to hold it. He doesn't say anything. The game never names her again.

### Wayne ↔ Lorraine
- **Last:** At the cemetery gate, an envelope handed back unopened. The game never tells the player what he says.

### Wayne ↔ Roy
- **Start:** Old friends who stopped talking about anything that mattered in 1968.
- **Late:** Two old men and a dog in an F-100, nine hundred miles north at exactly fifty-five, to watch a boy play (X). "He's riding with us, then."
- **Last:** Opening Day, 1977. "He could sing." / "He could." Then, in the parking lot: "Go on. I'll catch up."

### The band ↔ Eddie Farris
- **Start:** "You got thirty minutes. Don't tune forever."
- **Late:** He rents Knob House for them for less than the Vistalite cost (IX). In the trailer, with a label man on the phone who'd kill him: "Your call." (X M6)
- **Last:** In a pew at the funeral with everyone else.

### The band ↔ Frank Dalton
- **Late:** He stops all sessions at Knob House for three days without being asked, and goes fishing, badly (IX).
- **Last:** His Knob House reel labeled *LAST V. OPEN*, which the band releases in 1996 with no vocal on it. And, in the credits, his voice on the talkback: "Rolling."
