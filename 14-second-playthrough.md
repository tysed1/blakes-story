# THE BLAKES — Second-Playthrough Notes

*Deliverable 16 (phase 15, the replay audit, in its player-facing form). The game is built to be played twice. The first time, it's a band story with a friend in a jean jacket. The second time, every scene has a second scene inside it. This document lists what changes, chapter by chapter, and states the rules that keep the second playthrough fair.*

## The fairness rules

1. **Every revelation is prepared.** Nothing the player learns in VIII–X depends on information the game hid from the player's eyes. It depends on things the player saw and read as something else.
2. **Clara's rules are never broken by accident** (bible §11.3, rules 1–13). On replay a player can check any Clara scene and find that no one else ever addressed her, touched her, reacted to her, or saw her.
3. **Objective shots are framed, not faked.** Wide shots before the midpoint are blocked (shadow, distance, occlusion) so her absence isn't noticed. They never show her.
4. **Nothing is retconned.** A second-time player will find that nothing in I–VI is contradicted by VII–X. Where a line reads differently, it's because it always meant two things.

## What a second-time player sees

### Chapter I
- **"Second one was better."** His sister was in the car the night of his first show. She'd have been in the room.
- **"You keep playing in public. That's basically asking."** The real line, which the song "Still Here" will later make prettier (VII M7).
- **The 1964 portrait**, face down: Lorraine's gap teeth. The face Clara has.
- **Clara says "your daddy,"** a family friend's manners, not "Daddy," until VIII.
- **"I'm fine." / "Liar."** The first of three. Grace's last joke.
- **"Not that way"** at the Tolliver Road turnoff. She's keeping him off the road where it happened.
- **Opal Hensley** at the corner of the bar every Thursday. Her porch light was the only one on (IX M5).
- **The chalkboard**: THURSDAY — ELLIS BLAKE, erased. It will say THURSDAY again in X, and his name will still be there under it.
- **Tater** is fat because two men feed him (Ep. M7).
- **"Stay with me,"** the band signal, was the first thing he said to Grace in the car after the crash.

### Chapter II
- **Grace's sixteenth birthday, Nov 2.** Clara's birthday, when Ellis picks one in VII, is "November."
- **Wayne in Grace's room** through the crack in the door. He goes in on Nov 2 again in the epilogue.
- **The two coffees at the Starlite.** Habit, or company.
- **Rent, $20 every Friday.** Every dollar is in a coffee can (IX M13).
- **"Home"** is defined because Marlon's has no monitors. It's how every song ends, and it's how his last one ends.

### Chapter III
- **The dome light at the pull-off.** Wayne, with an FM converter he bought at the Western Auto, on 88.9 for a year (VII M6).
- **"I already buried one child."**
- **The farmhouse tea.** Riley's hand. She'll watch it on a loop in a SoHo bathroom (VIII M11).
- **Clara in the field, far off.** "I wish you'd come around more." / "You sure?" The first time she asks him to stay.

### Chapter IV
- **"El caught it, I held it. G."** on the crawdad snapshot. Only Grace called him El.
- **"Ohio."** His mother isn't dead. She left.
- **"Grace died in a car wreck. I was there."** He doesn't say *I was driving* until VIII.
- ***Clara?*** in Cal's ledger margin, struck through. Cal finds it again after.
- **"Take me with you." / "Promise."** He does. To the end.

### Chapter V
- **"Liar" (2).**
- **"El."** Clara says it once and doesn't know why.
- **"Out back of Marlon's." / "When?"** She can't say when because it was the first anniversary.
- **The pull-back** (the embrace as a held input). On replay, the player knows what they're holding.
- **April 12:** a pie nobody eats; a ballgame; forty miles an hour home in the rain. Wayne driving slow.
- **"Nobody here knows you."** Sixteen hundred people will sing it to him in VII; it was always about both of them.

### Chapter VI
- **"You tell me."** Wayne heard his dead daughter's jacket described in a funeral home.
- **"Before Clara."**
- **Raymond** "heard people." Wayne drove him to Milledgeville singing every verse.
- **Marlon tells Cal.** Every Cal scene afterward is a man keeping a promise.
- **The glass.** Riley's hand beside his.

### Chapter VII
- **"November."**
- **"You made it prettier."** His memory is rebuilding things: the alley, and the crash.
- **"This one's for Clara."** He gave her the song back in public.
- **The seventh chair at Thanksgiving.** Clara in it, boots on the rung. Wayne will sit in it in November 1976.
- **The FM converter's dial.** Whatever the player did, IX and VIII remember.
- **High beams on US 19.** The flinch, in the rain, with a distributor cap. The body remembering.
- **Clara vanishes at Lorraine's handwriting.** Part of her face is the face of the woman who wrote it.
- **The photograph.** She's across the street, never in it.
- **The leather jacket's inside pocket.** The letter goes to New York in it, and back to Dayton in Wayne's hand.

### Chapter VIII
- **The switch tries to become Clara and fails.** On replay, the player feels it coming.
- **"Daddy'll kill us both."** What Ellis said to Grace when she asked to come.
- **"You asked me to." / "Right here."** *Stay with me.*
- **The two photographs.** The player checks the face in the doorway.
- **"Name one more thing it has to be after."**
- **The pie, eaten.** Medicated. Gains and losses.

### Chapter IX
- **The report**: *We were arguing and I looked at her.* The player's *Look at her* input in the memory.
- **Hollis**: "I couldn't make it out." The player knows now it was *It's okay* and *Go on*.
- **"Ask me something I know."** Clara was never going to forgive him. She can't know anything he doesn't.
- **"Who are you?" / "Who are you?"** And on WGRC #9, the answer: *You're my brother. Unfortunately.*
- **"Later."** Grace was going to name the song.
- **The closed door.** He's letting her stop pretending.
- **"Stand by Dean."** He's asking Riley to be where he'll turn.

### Chapter X
- **The tab, put back.** He dies clean.
- **"Sunday's fine."** The last thing they say face to face is about biscuits.
- **"Go on, get up there."** The second step, and the long hold.
- ***Come on, come on*** in the rain in the field. Never *go*. That was Grace's word, getting into the Impala.
- **The first flash of the sun-gun,** in "Shape Note," switched off as a technical problem. The flinch the player already knows from VII.
- **The open verse.** His *sorry* under the rain; her laugh and a word the rain takes (the player knows the word); *Stay with me*, clear; *It's okay. Go on.* under it.
- **The turn.** The song lands first. Riley is ahead of him and to his left, where the light comes from; the swerve takes him away from her.
- **How long the player held *home*.** It's in the footage, rounded, and Nina says it in 1996.

### Epilogue
- **Biscuits at a Virginia counter on a Sunday.**
- **"She carries."**
- **Tater getting thin.**
- **Clara's absence from the coda**, which the second-time player notices immediately.
- **"Hi, El."** The post-credits tape. The only time the player hears Grace say it.

## What the second playthrough changes mechanically

- **New Game Plus** (optional): the notebook carries over. Observe lines from the first run appear dimmed in the margins, so the player can see what they noticed last time and what they didn't.
- **Replay flags.** A handful of lines change on replay only when the player has finished the game once:
  - In I M9, holding silence at Wayne's line lasts one beat longer.
  - In II, Clara's "your daddy" is followed by a half-second pause that wasn't there before.
  - In VII M1, when Ellis says "November," Clara's reply ("November's fine") is a fraction slower.
  - In X M7, Roy's "Go on" gets the second-step hold whether or not the player remembers why.
  These are timing changes only. No new information is added on replay. The player brings that.
- **The photograph** (VII M19) can be retaken on replay; the first playthrough's version remains the "canonical" one used in the player's save's 1996 documentary unless they choose otherwise.
