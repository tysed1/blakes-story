## 2. Period facts used, verified (research notes §§1–42)

- Georgia drinking age 18 (1974–76). Marijuana penalties. Gas about 55¢ in late 1974. Minimum wage $2.00 (1974), $2.10 (1975), $2.30 (1976).
- The WIN button (announced Oct 8, 1974). Ford's pardon (Sept 8, 1974). Saigon (Apr 30, 1975).
- Georgia was on **year-round emergency daylight time** from Jan 6 to Oct 27, 1974; sunrise was late in October (I M9 fixed accordingly).
- **The Georgia ERA vote**, Jan 28, 1975.
- **Carter**: announced Dec 12, 1974; won Iowa (Jan 19, 1976); nominated July 15, 1976.
- **Gear**: the Maestro Echoplex EP-3 (not a Space Echo); used Jazzmasters cheap and unfashionable; the Rickenbacker 360/12; the Ampeg B-15 and SVT; the Ludwig Vistalite; the Polaroid SX-70.
- **Radio**: 10-watt Class D college stations; FM is line of sight (hence Stony Knob); AM-only cars and under-dash FM converters.
- **Private-press 45s** from custom plants; royalty tricks (breakage, packaging, free goods, recoupment) and why publishing matters.
- ***The Waltons*** on Thursday nights. ***Deliverance*** jokes. **Sacred Harp** singings with dinner on the grounds. The **L&N-style** railroad in the mountains. **Funeral-home ambulances** in rural counties. **Pay phones and dimes.**
- **Psilocybin** fruits May–September (III fixed to dried mushrooms). **LSD** of the period was typically 50–100 µg (the game takes none in the end). **Cocaine** at $70–100 a gram in 1975.
- **Antipsychotics**, 1975–76: trifluoperazine (Stelazine) with benztropine (Cogentin) for stiffness; sedation, dry mouth, akathisia. Outpatient treatment was normal by 1976; Dr. Lusk's "You walked in here. You can walk out" is accurate to deinstitutionalization.

## 4. License taken, deliberately

- **WLRC carries part of the festival live** (X). Syndicated live-concert radio existed (*King Biscuit Flower Hour* was taped; some festivals had live feeds). A local station picking up a live feed is a plausible stretch that the story needs so Joan can hear it.
- **A 150,000-person festival in 1976.** By 1976 states had made mass gatherings hard, and Stompin' 76 (about 100,000) is the closest real analog. The game keeps the permit fight in the background.
- **Opening act on an arena tour gets thirty-five minutes and a tour bus from the label.** Common enough by 1976 with tour support.
- **The *Night Stage* silence being kept.** Live-to-tape shows kept mistakes. A producer choosing to keep a silence because it was electrifying is plausible and has precedent.
- **The notebooks leaked by a label publicist.** No specific precedent; plausible in a period without clear privacy norms for artists' materials.
- **A Sacred Harp class onstage with a rock band in 1976.** No precedent that we know of. Sacred Harp's revival among outsiders began in the 1970s; the class agreeing to "loud" is in character for rural singers who sing at full voice.

## 5. Things to check before shipping

- The Sacred Harp page numbers for "Wondrous Love" and "Holy Manna" in the 1971 Denson edition (the chapters avoid stating them).
- Whether Richfield Coliseum's 1976 capacity for concerts was about 18,000 (it's stated as "eighteen thousand seats").
- Whether a network news program on Sunday Aug 29, 1976 would have film from a festival fall the night before. Local affiliates in Syracuse or Elmira would; network is a stretch. The epilogue could make it "the news" without naming network.
- The Laurel City Double-A team as a real farm affiliate (kept fictional; don't name a parent club).
- Any real musician named on screen (Porter Wagoner, Conway Twitty, the Louvin Brothers, Jamerson, etc.): the game references them as history, never as characters.
