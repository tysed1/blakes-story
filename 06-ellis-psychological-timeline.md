# Ellis Blake: Psychological Timeline

**Deliverable 8.** A chronological reference for Ellis's state of mind and body from the crash (April 1973) to his death (August 28, 1976): sleep, substances, Clara, stressors, protective factors, what other people saw, what Ellis knows, medication, mood and functioning, creative output, and the trauma reflex. It ends with two sections on design intent: how the game avoids naming a diagnosis, and how the death is caused.

**Sources.** Compiled from the finished chapters (`chapters/`), which are canon. `01-story-bible.md` (§4, §5, §6.1, §6.3, §11.3, §12, §13) and `02-macrostructure.md` are used for backstory the chapters assume, and where they differ from the chapters the difference is listed under *Discrepancies noticed* at the end.

**Citations.** Roman numeral = chapter; M = mission; CO = cold open; "VIII Snow Day" = the unnumbered interlude between VIII M8 and M9; Epi = epilogue; Coda; Post-credits. "VII M15" means Chapter VII, Mission 15. "(bible)" marks a fact that is only in the bible.

**Vocabulary.** This document follows the game's own rule and uses no diagnostic label for Ellis. "Clara" means the young woman Ellis sees and hears. "The reflex" means his flinch and swerve away from a bright light coming from his left.

## 8. How the game avoids naming a diagnosis

The game gives the player evidence and withholds a label. The chapters do this consistently: no character in them uses a clinical term for Ellis. A search of the chapters finds no diagnostic word applied to him; the word "crazy" appears once, in his own mouth, as a fear ("Like I'm crazy," VI M10). The mechanisms:

1. **The family history is a story, not a verdict.** Raymond Tate heard and saw people, stood in Cold Branch for two hours, and was driven to Milledgeville by Wayne at nineteen. "Doctor called it something long. I didn't write it down." "Was he dangerous?" "No. Mostly scared." (VI M12). Chapter VI's story principle: "The game still doesn't diagnose Ellis. Family history is a clue; the mushrooms, trauma, sleep and grief are all factors; Clara came before the mushrooms. Nobody in the game has a name for it, and Wayne didn't write the doctor's down." (VI M12).
2. **Riley doesn't diagnose.** "No amateur diagnosis, no 'you inherited it,' no certainty" (VI M10). She asks three safety questions and says "I'm scared. There's a difference." (VI M10).
3. **The clinician declines.** Dr. Lusk: "I don't know yet… Grief can do this. A blow to the head can do it; you had one. Not sleeping can do it. So can other things, and some of those run in families. There are names for all of them. I'm not going to use any of them today… a name is hard to take back once somebody's heard it." Design note: "The game never names what Clara is, and Dr. Lusk is the reason it doesn't have to." (VIII M17).
4. **The drug is named; the condition isn't.** Trifluoperazine appears on the label (IX M10). Its class is never stated. The second drug is only "this other one" (VIII M17).
5. **Milledgeville is a fear, answered in 1976 terms.** "You walked in here. You can walk out. That's how this works now." (VIII M17).
6. **The factors are laid side by side.** Bible §6.3 (internal only, never shown to the player) lists grief, trauma, a family vulnerability, the concussion, sleep loss, and later drugs, fame and exhaustion as all present in the evidence, and says "The game never decides which." and "The characters don't know. The player doesn't need to."
7. **Clara predates the drugs.** She appears sober, in daylight, after a full night's sleep (IV M6, IV M9), so the player can't blame the mushrooms or the weed (IV M9 design note; VI M12).
8. **Clara's rules keep it out of horror and the supernatural.** She knows nothing Ellis couldn't know (rule 6; "Ask me something I know," IX M7). No horror grammar (rule 12; bible §12.6–7).
9. **The death thought is kept passive and private.** "If a train came through right now that'd be all right" is presented as a thought, not a plan (VIII M8; bible §6.1). Riley keeps it out of the book she publishes: "It was one bad night in Madison, and he wasn't trying to die." (Epi M5).
10. **Stopping the medication is presented as a choice made out of grief, not as a symptom.** The design note gives his reasons (the remembered sentence, and "Clara is the only Grace he has") and the prompt names the act plainly, *Palm it* (IX M7).
11. **Afterward, the world guesses and the people who knew him don't.** A talk station argues about drugs (Epi M1); Dex's book sells nine hundred thousand copies (Epi design summary). The people who knew him answer with ordinary things: "He was funny" (Dean), "He wasn't so bad" (Cal), "He drew maps" (Riley), "He was nineteen" (Nina) (Epi M6).

## 9. How the death is caused

### The chapter's own statement

> "He doesn't die because he was a poet, or because he was in pain, or because he stopped a medication, or because he took a drug (he didn't).
> He doesn't die because the player pressed *home*. The song ends first, and ends right.
