## 1. Continuity breaks

### P1

**P1-1. Riley's cup never happened (VIII M11 against VI M9 and III).** VIII M11's trip replays "one true thing (she handed him the cup)". What it shows: "the porch, the fireflies, the light-show wheel on the barn wall. Riley, eight months younger, in her peasant blouse, crossing the porch with the mug, holding it out to Ellis." The playable scene in VI M9 has a front room, an overhead projector with oil on the plaster, and "Someone passes a mason jar of mushroom tea". Riley hands him nothing and says only "It depends" and "You don't have to." (III is the same: "Someone offers a cup.") The bible (§6.5) wants her to have handed it. A player who played VI will see a different room, a different vessel and a handoff that never happened, in the only scene that dramatizes Riley's guilt. Fix both ends:
- VI M9: "Someone passes a mason jar of mushroom tea, cooled, from the June pasture. Riley has done this before and will tonight. Ellis looks at the jar." → "The mason jar of mushroom tea comes around the room, cooled, from the June pasture. Riley drinks and, because it's in her hand, holds it out to Ellis. Ellis looks at the jar."
- VI M10: "Riley thinks about the jar going around the front room, and what she told him about it." → "Riley thinks about the jar, and whose hand it came from, and what she told him about it."
- VIII M11: "a mug of tea somebody gave her. She looks down at her hands. It's the chipped enamel mug from the farmhouse in the South Fork in July." → "a jar of tea somebody gave her. She looks down at her hands. It's the jar from the farmhouse in the South Fork in July."
- VIII M11: "the porch, the fireflies, the light-show wheel on the barn wall. Riley, eight months younger, in her peasant blouse, crossing the porch with the mug, holding it out to Ellis." → "the front room, the fireflies past the screen door, the colored oil sliding on the plaster. Riley, eight months younger, in her peasant blouse, on the floor with the jar, holding it out to Ellis."
- VIII M11: "the porch, the mug, her hand, his hand taking it." → "the room, the jar, her hand, his hand taking it." Also "saying something about how it doesn't put things in your head." → "*You don't have to.*"; "(she handed him the cup)" → "(she handed him the jar)"; objects list "The chipped enamel mug." → "The jar, again."

**P1-2. Rule 13 isn't executed where it's named (VIII M13; also VII M19).** Rule 13 says she *vanishes on screen* when Lorraine appears, "her photograph in VIII". In M13 she stands in the doorway through five lines after the wedding photograph is down, then goes the ordinary way: "Then, between one look and the next, the doorway is empty." That's the everyday exit Rule 13 contrasts it with. VII M19 does the same with the letter: "When Ellis looks up from it, the other side of the booth is empty." The reveal needs her visible beside the photographs, so keep the look and make the vanish happen in frame:
- VIII M13: → "Then, while the player is looking straight at her, she isn't there. No cut, no turn of the head: the doorway is empty in the same frame."
- VII M19: → "Ellis looks up from it, straight at her, and in the same frame the other side of the booth is empty."

**P1-3. VIII M14 spends IX M14's reveal and writes the tabloid reading into the ledger.** IX M14's reveal image: "She's fourteen. Small, in the jean jacket that fits her, a child's size, her hair in a ponytail". VIII M14 has already used it, with dialogue: "A girl of fourteen, in a jean jacket that fits her, a child's size, with a horse sewn on the pocket, crooked. Muddy riding boots. A ponytail." The bible (§6.3, IX row) says "fourteen years old for the first time". Then VIII's clue ledger gives the truth as "| Grace in the passenger seat, older at each glance | — | Clara is Grace aging | **revealed** |". That is the "dead sister's ghost" reading, the one V M16 and VIII M13 call the tabloid version and IX refutes ("She was never Grace"). Fix:
- "A girl of fourteen, in a jean jacket that fits her, a child's size, with a horse sewn on the pocket, crooked." → "A girl of fourteen in a 4-H sweatshirt with the sleeves pushed up, hay on her jeans."
- "- Past the church, sixteen or seventeen. The jacket is bigger now, a grown-up jacket, the same one." → "- Past the church, sixteen or seventeen, and the jacket's on her now, the horse on the pocket, a grown-up size."
- Ledger truth → "What Ellis grew Grace into (never Grace; IX M14)".

## 5. Pacing and clarity

**The midpoint (V M16).** The pull-back works, and the **Hold** prompt is the best idea in my range: the player keeps a dead-air hug going with their own thumb. The recasting of her help lands only halfway. The booth's "She sings my part. / I sang it first." recasts the hum, but V never hums (the last hum was I M8, twenty hours earlier). V gives about six help lines in 6.5 hours, and none of them come in M16. The recast is really felt a chapter later, at VI M1's "It's half past nine." Two small fixes:
- In M16's celebration set, after "Marlon pours free drinks for the band.", add: "During 'No Name,' Clara, at the end of the bar, hums the high part under Riley's, and for one chorus the player can hear both."
- After the pull-back's "…and the player now knows exactly what that means.", add: "**CLARA:** It's Saturday now. Button your coat." That puts the first help after the truth inside the midpoint.

**VII after the 18% cut.** The seams are the P3s above (the ledger, "Tonight", what Dean and Cal couldn't know, Virginia, "Tomorrow Problem"). No setups went missing: the "Tuesday" alibi, the Leipzig record, the "Sunday Clothes" ending and Patty's letter all survive. The sag is at the front: M1–M5 run about 135 minutes before the song breaks, including 50 minutes of Riley back to back (M3, M4). Compress M2's Dean and Cal shifts into vignettes, keeping "Four ninety-eight". M19 runs 60 minutes, so add a checkpoint at the Lincoln Tunnel. One loose end: Norfolk's "half his ship" never pays off.

**VIII M4, the failed switch.** It matches bible §11.1 beat for beat, but three production gaps remain:
- Input state during the catch isn't specified. Keep it live: Ellis keeps walking, and only the camera catches.
- Camera coupling isn't specified. The normal grammar's camera "doesn't" keep walking. If it decouples here, the snap-back has to jump to a moved Ellis and reads as a bug. Keep the camera following, and make the six frames a focus pull inside the existing frame. §11.4 already makes that frame Ellis's eyes, so Rule 3 holds without a POV cut.
- "Six frames" means 100 ms at 60 fps and 200 ms at 30. Specify milliseconds.

**VIII M13–M17.** This is the strongest run in my range. M13's "This is the whole mission: looking" is exactly right (fix the vanish, P1-2). In M14, the aging passenger, "Daddy'll kill us both" and the scar "like a knuckle" all land (fix P1-3, the center line and "touches"). M15 finally pays off "It's gonna rain." M16's chair choice is quietly devastating. Lusk (M17) is the best-written professional in the game. One missed beat: her bias "away from doctors" gets only M6's line, and the doctor mission has no Clara at all, without comment. Consider one line at the clinic elevator (Wayne's in the truck): "Third floor. You don't have to." That means starting §11.8's no-help zone at the pill rather than at M17. The M18 medicated free roam risks aimlessness. Use the HUD's one allowed objective per day ("Riley's coming Saturday") so the quiet costs something without the player getting lost.

**Riley's segments.** Mostly yes, they are about her own want: V M1b, M2, M11 and M13; VI M5 and M13; VII M3, M4 and M18; VIII M2 and M11. Two are thin. In VIII M12 her want gets two sentences, then the scene is all Ellis (fix in §3 #9). In VIII M15 the third verse arrives, but the player never hears a word of it; give one line.

## 8. The single most important fix

**Make Riley's jar real (P1-1).** VIII M11 says the trip shows her "one true thing (she handed him the cup)", but the scene it replays (VI M9) shows a stranger passing a mason jar, in a different room, with a different light show and no handoff. This is the only place the game dramatizes Riley's guilt, and her quitting depends on it. Fix it at both ends: in VI M9, Riley holds the jar out to Ellis; in VIII M11, the replay shows that jar in that front room.
