# Critic A: Character, Dialogue and Emotional Red Team

*Scope: Ch I–VI as written; VII–X and the Epilogue as planned in `02-macrostructure.md`. Read against `01-story-bible.md`, `04-relationship-matrix.md`, and `00-process/WORKLOG.md`. Counts come from grep over `chapters/` and can be reproduced. Line numbers ("l.") refer to the chapter file.*

**Severity:** **P1** must fix · **P2** should fix · **P3** polish. Each finding gives the **Problem**, **Why it matters**, and the **Repair**.

## Headline verdict

1. **The Clara mystery is spoiled by Ch IV.** About eighteen on-screen tells land before the V midpoint. Six of them are in IV, including three people asking "Who're you talking to?" V then has a lyric that says "Nobody here can see you." The midpoint pull-back will confirm what players already suspect. It won't surprise them.
2. **The finale's callbacks aren't planted.** Clara never says "Liar" in I or V, and nobody says or writes "El" before Clara does. The player is never told that Ellis's mother *left*.
3. **Riley is written as Ellis's witness.** "Sunday Clothes" is the song VIII takes from her, and in I–VI it appears once, as a bullet in a montage.
4. **Voice.** The principals share one dry sitcom register ("Exactly." "Fair." "Strong recovery."). The texture the bible gives Ellis is missing, and the supporting adults (Landry above all) hand out aphorisms.
5. **The finale over-rhymes, and the LSD contradicts the game's own thesis.** The VIII outing can be earned, but only if it's staged as something Ellis inherited from Wayne, and it must not be defused by the "funniest scene."

## 1. Voice distinctness

**V1 [P1] One shared quip register.**
- **Problem:** Whole-line replies are spread across the cast:
  - "Exactly.": 7 speakers (Dean 7, Cal 4, Riley 3, Ellis 2, Vale, Hannah, Clara).
  - "Okay.": Riley 7, Ellis 6, Cal 4, Clara 3.
  - "Fair.": 4 speakers.
  - The deadpan appraisal has no owner: "Solid argument." (Riley II), "Strong position." (Riley III), "Strong recovery." (Riley IV), "Strong start." (Dean VI), "Excellent artistic process." and "Excellent philosophical analysis." (Cal VI). Ellis says "That's concerning." and Cal says "That's a concerning answer." in the same chapter (II).
  - The same joke gets two speakers: "Great. There's two of you now." (Cal II) and "Great. There's two of them." (Ellis III).
  - The knockout line "Neither do you" belongs to Cal (III) and then Riley (IV). "Neither do I" goes to Ellis (V) and Riley (VI).
- **Why it matters:** Strip the names and Ellis, Riley and Cal are interchangeable in roughly half their banter. The humor reads as one writer, and the player stops hearing people.
- **Repair:** Give each principal their own comic engine and ban cross-use:
  - **Ellis:** mock-formality ("That's a matter for the courts") and sudden specific images.
  - **Cal:** corrections and technical questions ("Be specific.").
  - **Dean:** nicknames, overstatement and bets.
  - **Riley:** argument form and the words on her list ("Define 'fine.'").
  - **Clara:** big-sister orders in mountain idiom.

  Then do one pass that leaves each stock reply ("Exactly." "Fair." "Strong X.") with a single owner.

**V2 [P1] Ellis's voice from the bible is absent.**
- **Problem:** Counts across the six chapters:
  - Absent: "reckon" 0, "might could" 0, "Hm" 0 (the bible's shared Wayne/Ellis syllable), "under advisement" or "matter for the courts" 0.
  - Barely there: "fixing to" 1, "Allegedly" 2.
  - What he gets instead: 18 uses of "fuck," and "Unfortunately." (which Riley and Cal also use).

  In VI his roughly 90 lines contain one joke ("Because you want ten."). The rest are "No." "What?" "I don't know." The bible says he is "funniest at 3 a.m. and when things are going badly."
- **Why it matters:** Ellis is the voice the player lives in for 58 hours, and he is the character least like his bible.
- **Repair:** Per chapter, give him at least three mountain markers, one mock-formal deflection, and one unguarded image *in dialogue* (not only in the notebook). Give him a "Hm" in the same scene as Wayne's in VI (Bloodline). Restore two pieces of gallows humor in the VI Pasture and Field scenes. That is where the bible's Ellis would joke.

**V3 [P2] Riley and Clara share one counselling voice.**
- **Problem:** Clara says "You're allowed to have something good." (I) and Riley says "You know you don't have to earn this, right?" (IV). Clara says "Two things can be true." (II) and Riley says "Nobody is." (IV). The IV note calls the convergence intentional.
- **Why it matters:** On the page this reads as the author reusing a line, not as two women filling one place.
- **Repair:** Converge their *function*, not their diction. Clara consoles by giving orders in idiom ("Hush. Eat something."). Riley consoles by arguing ("Name one thing you've earned. I'll wait.").

**V4 [P2] "Nothing." is the universal deflection.**
- **Problem:** 29 whole-line "Nothing." replies (I 1, II 7, III 3, IV 7, V 8, VI 3). Ellis remarks on it four times ("Everybody keeps saying that" twice in IV, once in V; "Everybody says nothing" in V).
- **Why it matters:** The script is noticing its own tic.
- **Repair:** Cut two-thirds. Replace per voice: Wayne "Hm."; Cal a technical question; Dean a joke; Ellis mock-formality; Riley a precise evasion.

**V5 [P2] Clara's idiom, and "Wayne."**
- **Problem:** The bible gives Clara "the same mountain accent as Ellis." On the page there is "Nah," "Well, then," and little else. She also says "What'd Wayne say?" (I M9). In 1974 north Georgia, a 21-year-old calling a 45-year-old man by his first name marks her as *not* a neighbor.
- **Repair:** She says "your daddy." It's period-true for a family friend, still hides her, and saves "Daddy" as the planned VIII slip.

**V6 [P2] Name-only lines.**
- **Problem:** 72 lines consist of just a name (I 10, II 10, III 4, IV 14, V 23, VI 11). Riley has 15 of them, 7 in VI alone ("Ellis?" and "Ellis." in M3, M9 and M18).
- **Why it matters:** It's used as an all-purpose "concern" beat, and it makes Riley a set of reaction shots.
- **Repair:** Replace at least half with physical beats (the bible's knuckle-to-glasses, hand on his arm) or a specific question.

## 2. Characters articulating themes

**T1 [P1] Landry dispenses an aphorism in every appearance.**
- **Problem:** Each scene ends on a maxim:
  - II M7 (l.1333): "Memory isn't a recording. It's a reconstruction… What you've been told. What you expect. What you can stand." Then: "accuracy and meaning aren't always the same thing."
  - IV M5: "See how much of it survives."
  - IV M8: "You're not allowed to neglect all but one and tell yourself it was inevitable."
  - V M11: "sooner or later they start charging each other rent." V M13: "Then own the cost."
- **Why it matters:** The II lecture is the mechanism of the crash truth. Delivered as a thesis, with "What you can stand" aimed straight at Ellis, it hands second-time players the answer, and Landry becomes the author's mouthpiece.
- **Repair:** Keep Bartlett (the canoes that turned into boats). Cut everything after "Memory isn't a recording," and let a student's question go unanswered ("Read Bartlett for Thursday."). Allow Landry one maxim in the whole game, "You're allowed more than one ambition" (it's in the matrix). Cut "neglect," "rent" and "survives." Let "own the cost" be Riley's own conclusion, reached later.

**T2 [P2] The rent metaphor has three owners.**
- **Problem:** Wayne charges literal rent (II). Landry's ambitions "charge each other rent" (V). Richard: "The record is the rent. The songs are the house." (VI M2).
- **Why it matters:** One metaphor in three unrelated mouths is the writer's voice.
- **Repair:** Keep Wayne's (it's literal) and Richard's (it's his trade). Cut Landry's.

**T3 [P2] Lonnie's prophecy (III M6).**
- **Problem:** "Friends make terrible bands… sooner or later the band asks you to pick." / "You'll find out." The direction says "Don't underline it," and the line underlines itself.
- **Repair:** Give him a specific, bitter story instead ("Our drummer did two years in Reidsville over a Buick. I still owe him forty dollars."). Cut "You'll find out."

**T4 [P2] Theo (VI M8).**
- **Problem:** "Everybody's got somebody they don't bring to the party." / "Maybe she's there for him." That's the Clara thesis, delivered by a man who has never met Ellis.
- **Repair:** Give him a funny, specific Louisiana memory ("My tante set a plate for Saint Jude every Sunday. Nobody moved it."). Keep "Mine's real." / "Well. Thank you."

**T5 [P2] Joan the oracle (III M2).**
- **Problem:** "Then be careful with it," followed by the narration "Riley doesn't know what that means. She will."
- **Repair:** Joan asks a musician's question ("Who's got the melody?") and the pause does the work. Cut "She will."

**T6 [P2] Richard (V M6).**
- **Problem:** "Sometimes growing up means finding out your life isn't only about what excites you." It's a speech.
- **Repair:** Keep the amortization-tables line and "I know." Richard is funnier and sadder when he stays technical.

**T7 [P3] Frank and Vale.** Frank's "There it is. / The reason you're here." and "That's the job now." Vale's "That's why they're harder." **Repair:** Cut each second sentence. Their jokes are their voice. Their benedictions aren't.

**T8 [pass] Roy and Marlon are the model.** Marlon's VI confession is plain and exact, and Roy's "Guess you better write another one" is the register every adult should hit. One nit (P3): Roy's "Because you're his." (II) is writerly. "'Cause he's your daddy" is truer.

**T9 [P1] The principals state the themes.**
- **Leaving:** Stony Knob (I); "I don't want to die where I was born… Music's how I'm leaving" (II M7); "I want to leave. / Everywhere." (II M9); Clara's "You're doing it. / Leaving." (III); "Yet." (IV); "You'll leave." (V).
- **Ownership:** Ellis names the album "Because none of it's ours forever," and the narration adds "Life. People. Places. Bodies. Time." (VI M17).
- **The tortured-poet thesis, in Wayne's mouth:** "I'm not burying another one because he thinks dying slow makes him interesting." (III M7)
- **Riley's wrong belief, as a slogan:** "It doesn't put things in your head. It changes how you see what's already there." (VI M9)
- **The game's replay frame:** "You ever think everything already happened? Like we're remembering it." (III M6)
- **Repair:**
  - Keep one leaving line (the quad). At the Starlite Ellis looks at the taillights and says "Somewhere with a better diner."
  - Album title: "It's a song on it." Cut the list.
  - Wayne stops at "I'm not burying another one."
  - Riley in VI: "It depends." That's her own callback to III.

## 4. Arc integrity

**Ellis [P2].**
- **Problem:** The arc moves (leave, then be heard, then be understood, then know himself). But the *leaving* note is spoken in every chapter from I to V, and his humor disappears in V–VI (V2). Nothing changes without a cause.
- **Repair:** After II, Ellis never says "leave." Show it instead (the LAUREL CITY 38 sign, the wall of tour dates) and let other people say it to him.

**Riley [P1]. Her interior life is not as full as Ellis's.**
- **Problem:**
  - **One beat, three chapters running:** she chooses the band over school in V (the exam), VI (Edinburgh) and VII as planned (registration).
  - **Her viewpoint serves Ellis:** six of her playable segments are about watching him (III M6, IV M10, V M7, VI M9, M10, M18).
  - **Her songs are missing:** "Sunday Clothes" is her song, and in VIII the label has Ellis re-sing it. In I–VI it appears once, as a montage bullet (VI l.1825). "Linwood" gets one sentence.
  - **Her family is off-screen:** Joan appears as three phone calls. Tom and Tommy are never seen. Linwood is never visited (she spends Christmas there off-screen, V M1). Joan's Saturday Bach, the image both her song and her fear rest on, is never shown.
  - **Her secret is spent early:** she says her mushroom guilt aloud in VI ("I shouldn't have given you anything"), using up the bible's "hides" before VIII's trip, which is meant to surface it.
- **Repair:**
  - Add a playable Riley mission in IV or V: the Saturday organ loft in Linwood during the Christmas break, Joan playing Bach to an empty church, Riley writing "Sunday Clothes" and choosing not to bring it to the band yet.
  - Merge Edinburgh into V's decision.
  - Spend VI M5 on arranging her song, including a fight with Cal about it.
  - Make her VI guilt silent: she bites her cheek (her bible tell) and doesn't say it.

**Cal [P2]. A full person trapped in one function.**
- **Problem:** Theo, Walt and the studio dream make him real. But every scene he has with Ellis is "notice Clara, file it": III M8, IV M6, M8 twice, M11, V M4, M7, VI M8. That's seven notices and no actions, and the narration says "Cal remembers anomalies" three times. By VI he knows everything: "He talks to someone… who isn't there," he heard the description, and Marlon told him "He was driving." The plan never has him use any of it.
- **Repair:** Collapse the notices to three that escalate (the name in III, the missed note at the Lantern in IV, "Can I meet her?" in V). Give him one *act* in VI: he calls Wayne after the Dalton playback and Wayne hangs up. That pulls the matrix's VIII phone call forward as a failed attempt.

**Dean [P2]. Moves well; two threads are unbuilt.**
- **Problem:**
  - His drug arc progresses cleanly: fun, then functional, then medicinal (V M6), then speed (VI).
  - **Lynette** has four lines in II and one in III, then nothing in IV–VI.
  - **Bobby** never appears.
  - His secret arithmetic has a single seed ("x is seven," I).
  - VI M17 has him "admitting to Frank… that he's exhausted," which spends his bible never-say ("I'm tired") early.
- **Repair:** Add two 4 a.m. Starlite beats (after the Holler House in IV, during Dalton in VI) with Lynette and a drawing by Bobby. Add two arithmetic flashes: he totals the chili-dog ledger in his head (IV M1) and corrects Eddie's fifteen percent (V). Frank sends Dean home before he admits anything.

**Wayne [P2]. The best arc in the game.**
- **Problem:** V stacks five thaws into one chapter (heater, paper star, tickets, ballgame and the drive home at forty, walking into Marlon's). Then VI M14 has him say "Because you're my son." By the bible he says "son" only when frightened, and he would never make a declaration like that.
- **Repair:** Move the walk into Marlon's to VI, or cut "Congratulations." In VI he says "Wanted to see it." and stops.

**Clara [P2]. Moves per the bible, but V–VI drain her.**
- **Problem:** Her 30 lines in VI contain no joke, and five of them are "Don't." (three more in V). The VI note says she must stay "warm, funny and lovable." She flickers younger three times in V (the Lantern wings, the Row window, Marlon's booth); the bible has one.
- **Repair:** Two genuine teases per Clara scene through VI. One flicker in V (keep the booth). Replace three of the "Don't"s with specific pleas in her idiom.

## 5. The Clara mystery

**C1 [P1] Too much, too early.**
- **Problem:** On-screen tells before the midpoint:
  - **I:** Pruitt looks past her.
  - **II:** Riley's "What?"; Junior's "What?"
  - **III:** Dean's "Psychotic," with Ellis glancing up (l.359). Milledgeville and "Hearing people," and Ellis immediately looks toward the tree line. Riley's "talking to yourself," with her gaze passing through Clara. Ellis's "Riley couldn't see you." (l.1451). Cal's "Who's Clara?… No."
  - **IV:** Marlon looks at "the empty space beside Ellis" (l.1559). Cal's "Who're you talking to?" (l.2051). A shot "From Cal's view: Ellis staring at an amplifier case" (l.2346). Wayne's "Who're you talking to?" with Clara three feet from him in daylight (l.2474–80). "I would've."
  - **V:** Ellis answers Clara and Dean says "I didn't say anything." "Do you hear that?" to the whole band. Cal's "Can I meet her?" The lyric "**Nobody here can see you.**" (l.1054).
- **Why it matters:** Players raised on *The Sixth Sense* will solve it at the Lantern, and V's pull-back becomes a confirmation rather than the floor dropping out.
- **Repair:**
  - Keep only Marlon's "Who're you talking to?" (the door hides her). At the EXIT sign Cal says "Blake. Now." Clara leaves the bedroom before Wayne comes in.
  - No shot from a third party's point of view before V. The camera stays on Ellis for the van mirror, and Marlon's eyeline doesn't land on her.
  - Change the lyric to "Nobody here *knows* you."
  - Cut "Ellis glances toward Dean" after "Psychotic." Move the Milledgeville line to a scene without Clara, such as Room 12 in IV.
  - Keep Clara out of vehicles with third parties before V (the IV van, the V engine cover). Nobody can fail to notice a fifth passenger in a van.
  - **Add social cover.** Roy: "That Clara girl find you? You said she might come by." Ellis orders two coffees at the Starlite and drinks both.

**C2 [P1] The finale's callbacks are unplanted.**
- **Problem:** The bible has Clara say "Liar" exactly three times (I, V, VIII) as a tease, so that X can turn it into Grace's last word. grep finds none in I–VI. Only Grace calls him "El," but nobody says or writes "El" before Clara's slip in V (l.2418), so the slip lands on nothing.
- **Repair:**
  - **I M9:** at "Did I?" / "For a little while," Ellis says he's fine and Clara says "Liar."
  - **V M16:** "Liar" on Depot Street, before "El."
  - **"El":** plant it in green felt-tip on the back of the crawdad snapshot (IV M9): "El caught it, I held it. G."

**C3 [P2] Rule breaches.**
- **Clara inside the house while Wayne is awake (the I–II rule):** broken in I M9 (Wayne awake in bed, l.2052) and II M10 (straight after Wayne leaves Grace's room). **Repair:** Amend the bible table to "never in a room with Wayne," or delay both appearances until his light is out.
- **Rule 3, objective shot:** III M11 has "The camera stays where it is… Clara is alone in the frame" after Ellis has jogged away (l.1931–35). A second-time player will call it a cheat. **Repair:** The camera goes with Ellis.
- **The spirit of rule 10:** Clara's "Go." under the EXIT sign (IV l.2043) is a syllable away from "Go on." **Repair:** "Get up there."
- **"Where did we meet?":** the bible says she can't answer. The chapter has "Tolliver Road" (l.2404), which also contradicts the canonical first meeting (the alley, April 1974). **Repair:** She says "Out back of Marlon's." Ellis: "When?" She can't answer. When it happened is where it cracks: the first anniversary of the crash.
- **Horror grammar** (bible rule 6; I's direction "no strange reflections"): the mirror in II M10, the shop-window flicker in V M9, the store glass in VI M3, and the Lantern approach where she's closer at each cut (IV M8). **Repair:** Cut the mirror and window framings. At the Lantern she stays in one place.

**C4 [P2] Too hidden.** Most replay layers are fair. **Repair:** VI never pays V M4's promise of the band's own 45 played back "only Riley." Show it.

**C5 [P2] Naming the diagnosis.**
- **Problem:** VI says "schizophrenia" twice (M10 and M12) and adds a detailed case history for Raymond. The bible says the game never decides what Clara is.
- **Why it matters:** It tilts the player toward heredity and away from the grief reading the ending depends on.
- **Repair:** Ellis has only "he heard people." Wayne: "Doctor called it something long. I didn't write it down."

**C6 [P3] The Pasture switch repeats V's news.** **Repair:** On Riley's side, include one line of Ellis's that only makes sense spoken to a child ("You don't have to sit up front.").
