*Cold read, V8: Chapters IX and X and the epilogue with the coda, read in full and in order; the X M8–M9 script checked against the chapter. Earlier chapters skimmed only to test setups. Quotes are exact strings for replacement.*

## 1. Continuity breaks

### P1 (breaks canon, rules or the finale)

**P1-1. The script puts an objective cut in front of the fall.** `scripts/X-M8-M9-the-last-light.md`, 8.8:
```
- `CAM (objective, 18 frames, from the stage-right wing):` Joel steps out onto the upstage-right deck for the reverse on the bow. Kit steps out with him, raises the sun-gun, and switches it on.
```
The chapter says "The camera stays in Ellis's eyes… and never cuts to a wide" and calls it "a light he doesn't see coming". Bible §11.4 allows three objective breaks; this is a fourth. After the "Shape Note" flare, showing the gun go on turns 2.5 seconds of accident into dread: the staged death the chapter refuses. The script wins on execution, so this ships unless cut. **Replace with:**
```
- `CAM (Ellis):` no cut. Joel and Kit step out behind his left shoulder, unseen. The player learns where the light came from only in Roll forty (9.2) and at the case (9.6).
```
In the chapter, after `and raises the sun-gun, and switches it on.` add ` None of this is on screen; the camera is in Ellis's eyes.`

**P1-2. The US 19 reflex runs the wrong way** (outside my range; the finale depends on it). Bible §13 and X M8 (twice) say his body swerves right, away from the light, "as on… US 19 in the rain". VII M15 plays it as a freeze facing the light: `He straightens up, too fast, and his wrist hits the radiator hose, and he turns toward the light with the distributor cap in his hand and doesn't move.` **Replace with:** `His body jerks right, away from it, too fast, and his wrist hits the radiator hose, and he stands there with the distributor cap in his hand and doesn't move.`

### P2 (visible contradiction)

- **X M2 vs IX M15, Riley's song.** In IX M15 she "plays him "Occasionally Astonishing," the whole thing", finished. X M2 then says: `She has a bridge in her head that won't finish, for a song nobody has heard but Ellis`. Only Ellis has heard "Occasionally Astonishing", so the two can't both hold. **Replace with:** `She has a bridge in her head that won't finish, for a song nobody has heard yet, not even Ellis`. Pay it off in Ep M5 (see §5H).
- **X design summary misquotes who said "Sunday's fine."** `The last things he did were laugh with his band, say *Sunday's fine* about biscuits with his father, finish a song, and step toward Riley.` Wayne says "Sunday's fine"; Ellis says the biscuits are terrible. **Replace with:** `The last things he did were laugh with his band, tell his father his biscuits were terrible, finish a song, and step toward Riley.` (The same misattribution is in `06-ellis-psychological-timeline.md`: `"Sunday's fine" to Wayne (X M6)`.)
- **IX design summary contradicts its own list.** `The chapter was built so that nothing good is reserved for being off the pills and nothing bad is blamed on being on them.` The very next sentences reserve the reach, the Room "thrilling again" and "Who Are You?" for being off them. **Replace with:** `The chapter was built so that neither side of the bottle gets all the good, and neither gets the blame.`
- **Knob House distances.** In IX M11, `Knob House is forty minutes up the mountain.` from Cold Branch Road, yet in IX M10 the drive from Knob House all the way to Linwood is `It's forty-five minutes.` Bible §3.1 puts Hollow Ridge 40–50 minutes from either city, and the overlook is a quarter mile from the lodge. **Replace with** `Knob House is twenty minutes up the mountain.` and `It's an hour and a quarter.`
- **The last song is mostly unwritten.** The script cites `the Music team's lyric sheet, "Who Are You?" v3`; no such sheet exists in the repo. Verses 2–3 need writing before a table read.

## 2. Rule breaks

- **Pulsing prompts are a hint layer (§11.8).** In X M3: `The default (the prompt pulses) is to put it on, under the black leather jacket from New York.` In Ep M1: `One option pulses, gently, not insisting:`. The epilogue is supposed to have "no help at all", and the HOME spec's "never pulses" only means something if nothing else pulses. → `The default is to put it on, under the black leather jacket from New York; the shirt is on top when he opens the bag.` and `One option is at the top of the list:`
- **HOME prompt copy could get built as hint text.** In X M8, `Turn all the way around to face Dean. Bring the song to its end.` sits under *HOME.* like UI copy. → `(The prompt is the word and nothing else. Pressing it turns him all the way around to face Dean and ends the song.)`
- **IX M14 staging is horror grammar (Rule 12, tone rule 6).** A dark hall and a child standing at the far end of it (`The hall light's off.` … `At the other end of the hall, by the front room, there's someone standing.`) is the most horror-coded image a hallway can offer. → `At the other end of the hall, in the spill from the kitchen light, somebody is sitting on the floor with her back against the wall and her boots out.` Change `standing in the hallway of the Blake house with her hands in the pockets` to `sitting in the hallway of the Blake house with her hands in the pockets`, and `She walks down the hall toward him.` to `She gets up and walks down the hall toward him.` The rule-keeping is otherwise clean: she perches, she speaks first and plainly, and she never gets closer between cuts.
- **"The only decision she doesn't help with" is true twice.** In IX M14, under *Close the door*, she's present and silent (its note even says she "doesn't steer him toward the door or away from it"). The X M8 spec says `and this is the one decision she leaves to them.` → `and this is the last decision she leaves to them; the first was the hallway door (IX M14).` This makes the hallway a rehearsal for HOME, which strengthens both.
- **The IX help table misses her most biased line.** Add `| M7 | "Don't do it again." | his body | **yes**: away from the pills |`
- **Everything else is clean.** Every owned phrase stays with its owner ("Hm." for Ellis only with Wayne; "Son" only in the ambulance). Clara never says "go", only "come on". Rule 8 is handled neatly: Wayne's presence on the trip is why she's gone from Harrisburg on.

## 5. Pacing and clarity (and the lead's questions)

**A. The medicated stretch (VIII M17 – IX M6).** The story doesn't say "pills kill art": the April 12 pie, "He's load-bearing", "New Skin" in one take, "That's him too", "Well. Good." and the lost verse (an art cost of *stopping*) balance it. The systems don't. On the pills Observe is muted, *reach* is gone and silence won't hold; off them it all returns, and the prose calls it "the thing that made them". The one medicated plus, "he finishes things", is only a design note. Fixes:
- Make it a verb. On the medication *home* always lands clean on the first press, the band never tires, and Frank keeps first takes. Say so in the Room.
- In X M8 "New Skin", give the pill-written song the crowd model's stillest response of the set.
- Put one medicated line (*Tater in the water to his chest, looking at a frog.*) in the authored fallback for Riley's book (Ep M5).
- IX M2 is 35 shapeless, unguided minutes. Put the session list on the HUD, make finishing "New Skin" the exit, and cap it at 25 minutes.

**B. The fire tower (IX M7).** It lands: the help flooding back is the best use of §11.8 in the range, and the bill arrives through other people's scenes, not a lecture.

**C. IX M14.** It lands once restaged (§2). "I was too nice." / "She'd have put a frog in your bed." is the right size, and Wayne's "Hm." in the doorway is the chapter's best ending.

**D. HOME and the fall.** The song lands first (chord, cut, silence, laugh), and the fall spec is exemplary: real time, no score, no bloom, seven frames, the crowd cheering at black. Beyond P1-1 and prose item 1: the script's `1.0 s of room silence` is too short. Set a floor of about 3 seconds from the chord cut to switch-on, laugh finished, so the song is felt as over. And "Holding off is *stay*. Pressing it is *go on*." must never reach the UI.

**E. Wayne in the ambulance (X M9).** The best scene in the range. The option labels (the bible's "Would never say" list) are a little schematic, but it works because every one fails except "Son." *Sing* is the one option that could turn beautiful: make it the script's hum (the chapter says "sings"), losing the line after a phrase and starting again.

**F. Does the epilogue have too many endings? Yes.** M6 (1996) plays as the game's ending: Wayne's "Hm.", the door, the LAST V. OPEN changes fading. Then M7 runs 20 minutes with six more closures (Grace's door, the washed dish, the second *Feed Tater*, "It's paid for.", the April 12 pie, "Wayne goes on."), then the coda, credits and post-credits.
- **Reorder.** Play M7 before M6, so the order runs 1976–77, then 1996, then the 1974 coda. Delete `The picture goes back to the game's own. Inside the house, twenty years earlier.` and cut from the documentary's closing door straight to the coda's morning window in the same house.
- **Cut the April 12 pie beat.** In 40 minutes the player feeds the dog four times: the biscuit on the rail, the second *Feed Tater*, "One for the dog", and the coda's crusts. The crusts are the one that has to stay fresh.
- **Cut "Wayne goes on."**
- **Keep the post-credits tape.** It's short, it's Grace, and it earns its place.

**G. The coda without Clara.** It works and is canon-safe: daylight, work and a house with Wayne awake are exactly where she never was in 1974. "Other way's quicker." at Tolliver Road shows the avoidance was always his. Don't touch it.

**H. Riley's late segments.** Chapter X is good (her bridge, Nina's "Not about him", Kit's "only girl", "Sunday Clothes" for Joan). The epilogue drifts back to him: M5 is his maps, his notebooks, his train line, his book, and her own want gets only the registration desk.
- In Ep M5, replace `It's about his maps: the napkins, the ballpoint on his arm, a pasture outside Macon at midnight with a cow looking in the windshield. The verse is done. The bridge won't come.` with `It's the one whose bridge wouldn't land in the hayfield in New York, and it isn't about him. The verse is done. The bridge won't come.` Let "Maps" begin after the notebooks.
- In 1996, after `On the shelf behind her, a small gray book: his notebook lines, with the title the player chose.` add `Beside it, spine out, four records with her own name on them.`

**I. Soft spot.** X M1–M2 (55 minutes before show day) is where a player sags. Dean's compound walk is a tour: cut about 10 minutes, keep Tully and Kit's tape whole.

## 8. The single most important fix

**Delete the 18-frame objective insert in the script at X 8.8, and lock the chapter's fall to Ellis's eyes (P1-1).** It decides whether the death is an accident the player lives through or a set piece the player watches coming. The chapter spec, the bible's three-break rule and the tone rule all say accident, and the script's execution authority means the insert ships unless it's cut.

Runner-up: reorder and trim the epilogue (§5F).
