# Critic D: Emotional, Replay and Prose-Quality Audit

*Scope: `01-story-bible.md` (§2, §5, §6, §11, §12, §13), `V3-character-audit.md` (voice sheet, binding), `V4-red-team.md`, and all eleven chapter files. VII–X and the epilogue were read line by line; I–VI in full for baselines and ladders. Counts are grep over the chapter files, hand-checked where a pattern over-matches. Criticism only; every repair is a proposal for the lead.*

**Priorities:** P1 = damages the ending or the central promise. P2 = a real flaw a careful player will feel. P3 = polish, continuity, accuracy.

---

## 0. Verdict

Chapters I–VI and most of VII are the best writing in the project: specific, funny, patient, built on mechanics that mean something (the switch, the held embrace at the midpoint, Riley's switch in the pasture). Three things go wrong after that. (1) **The narrator takes over.** In VII–E, "the player may notice/remember/realize," "the game lets/doesn't…," "as long as they want," superlative prize-giving and flash-forwards rise 5–20× over I–VI (§4). The document starts telling the player how to feel exactly where the story most needs to trust them. (2) **The voices flatten.** The V3 comic engines are close to absent from VIII, IX and X. (3) **The finale is over-engineered.** The HOME press is also the fall, the open verse is a transcript, Clara states the bible's thesis, and the last two hours hold about 35 callbacks. None of this needs new plot: it needs cuts, one re-timing and a voice pass.

### 1.3 Findings

**E-1 (P1). HOME is a kill switch.** *Problem:* in X M8 the press that ends every song is the frame he falls in, and the chapter telegraphs it from M2: "It's just a gap. Every outdoor stage has them."; Kit's "It'll light anybody."; "It's just a light."; M7's slot of light ("The game doesn't point at it."); the flinch in "Shape Note". The design note says "The player isn't being asked to kill him." The epilogue summary says "The last input in the game isn't *home*, which killed him." *Why:* withholding becomes "I won't kill him" rather than stay/go on, and the player's guilt becomes the ending's subject, against §12.9. Nina reading the hold "to the second" makes hesitation a public score, and a one-hour hold breaks the fixed 9:52 and the curfew. *Repair:* (a) cut the gap/light telegraphs; Tully's tape and the flinch are enough. (b) Let HOME succeed: he turns to Dean, the band lands the last chord, a second of right silence, Riley's hand. Then the roar, Joel steps out for the reverse on the bow, and the light hits him as he steps toward Riley. (c) Cap diegetic duration in the footage and in Nina's line ("almost three minutes").

**E-2 (P1). The open verse is a transcript.** *Problem:* six player-picked lines, each cutting to one line of 1973 dialogue in strict alternation and canonical order, with narration explaining each ("The player hears where they came from."; "The player hears where she got it."). The lyric 150,000 people hear paraphrases the transcript: "You told me stop saying sorry. / … / You said go on." *Why:* the game's own thesis is Bartlett (memory is reconstruction); here memory arrives as a clean recording, in the neatest structure in a game that prizes mess, and it is the weakest writing Ellis ever does. *Repair:* 3–4 lines; memory out of order and partly unheard (the laugh before "Liar"; "It's okay" and "Go on" arriving together, low, the way Hollis "couldn't make it out"); sung lines built from the player's Observe images ("like a hand over a mouth," "the rain hit it"); only the last line plain: *go on*. Cut every "The player has heard…".

**E-3 (P1). Clara speaks the bible.** *Problem:* X M7 "What are you?" / "I'm the part that couldn't leave her there." is bible §6.3 nearly verbatim, then: "It's the plainest thing she's ever said. The player has earned it, and so has he." *Why:* it explains the verse twenty minutes early; VIII M14 already did it by indirection ("You asked me to." / "When?" / "Right here."); Clara's voice is orders in idiom, not abstractions. *Repair:* keep "I didn't go anywhere. You did." Replace the thesis with planted evidence: "Somebody had to stay where she could see him." (Hollis, IX M5). Or rhyme IV's EXIT sign: "Tuck that in. You look like you got dressed in a ditch. Come on. They're waiting on you." Cut the "earned it" sentence.

**E-4 (P2). The ambulance menu is Wayne's never-say list.** *Problem:* X M9 offers "It wasn't your fault." / "I'm proud of you." / "I'm scared." / "Son." / Sing / Silence, and "the player can say everything on the list." *Why:* the first playable Wayne lands (the switch finally choosing him, the siren switched off, the ambulance slowing to the limit), but a checklist completes his arc too cleanly and breaks his voice when it matters most. *Repair:* the player picks the sentiment and Wayne says his version: "It wasn't your—" stops; "I'm proud of you" becomes "You done good up there"; "I'm scared" becomes "Son." Only "Son." comes out whole.

**E-5 (P1). The finale is over-rhymed.** *Problem:* M6–M9 (about 90 minutes of play) hold roughly 35 callbacks: the Polaroid, "That's just his face," the gorilla, the possum, the napkin autograph, the banjo, "Occasionally astonishing," four dollars twice, the ledger, "What day is it," "Stop," "Your call," the collar, the biscuits, Roy's shirt and the knock on the patch, "Go on," "Well/Well" twice, Tully's tape, the kick drum and Carla's letter, three lifted hands, Wesley's E minor, Marlon's mug, "Nobody here knows you," Joan's radio, Bobby's kit, the thrown stick, "New Skin," "Shape Note," the US 19 reflex, "Stay with me," "Liar," the flashlight (explained), "That's his daddy," "Wondrous Love," the yellow line. *Why:* each is fine alone; together they turn the death into a curtain call where every object takes a bow. *Repair:* cut a third: Roy's "Well/Well"; Wesley's chord **or** the hand to Wayne; Opal's lift and Marlon's mug; the flashlight paragraph; the "One of us isn't a boy" option for Kit; Earl's "Hm."; the Elmira stick "kept for twenty years".

**E-6 (P1). VIII breaks ordinary-first.** *Problem:* from M4 to M17, fourteen missions and about five hours, there is no purely ordinary mission; relief comes as seconds inside heavy scenes. *Why:* §12.1 asks for two ordinary scenes per grief scene; VIII runs about 1:6, and the fatigue lands on its three best scenes (M13, M14, M18). *Repair:* one ordinary mission after Cal returns (a snowed-in day off: a supper-club fish fry, Tully teaching Riley to bowl, Dean losing the bus at gin to Earl). Recast Elaine (§4.2 #14).

**E-7 (P2). The vote is rigged, and Dean retracts his one "Stop."** *Problem:* X M6: if Cal breaks the tie *no*, "Dean, of all people, says, 'Let him,' and changes his vote. Whatever the player does, the band plays." *Why:* a false choice at the emotional peak, and Dean takes back his hard-won word on the record. The bible's engine offers better: "Following… At the end it makes them late." *Repair:* a *no* stands; Ellis says "Then I'll play it by myself" and walks to the stairs; the band follows him.

**E-8 (P2). The fall puts Riley on the wrong side.** *Problem:* X M8: "Three yards to his right, at the riser's stage-left corner… Riley… He was turning toward her." Facing upstage from the stage-left lip, his right is the gap; the riser's stage-left corner is toward centre, his left-front (the crew's side). Bible §13 repeats it ("toward Riley's side"). *Repair:* Riley on his left, ahead, hand out; the swerve takes him away from her, which is the stronger image. Fix §13.

**E-9 (P2). Too many endings.** *Problem:* after X come five closes: M6's "Go on. I'll catch up." / "Wayne goes on." (perfect); the 1996 door; the open reel with Riley's "We left it open. It's his."; the coda's "Hm." / "Hm." / "Good."; the credits tape (ending on "Stay with me," "Allegedly"); the post-credits tape. The 1996 frame duplicates work done by the waitress's television, Dex at the fence and the M3 radio, and hands everyone a tidy life. *Repair:* move 1996 before M6 and compress it to about four minutes (Dean's "funny," Cal's "not so bad," Riley's maps, Dex's magazines, Nina's "nineteen," Wayne's door); cut the voice-over, the Joel/Kit interview and the bios. M6 then answers the closed door. End: "Wayne goes on." → coda → post-credits. Drop "Stay with me" from the credits tape.

**E-10 (P3). The "Liar" callbacks misremember their own scenes.** VIII M4: "sitting on his bed, and in December on the stairs"; X M8: "In Chapter I on his bed. In Chapter V on the stairs." I has her "on the floor under the window"; V has her "on the sill".

## 5. Thematic integrity

**T-1 (P2). Art and suffering: the finale tips it.** IX is honest ("New Skin" is finished on the pills). But X claims "the best set of their lives" off medication, and "New Skin" returns whole ("This time he has it… Every verse comes."), quietly reversing IX's lost verse. IX M3's "RILEY: That's not him. / Nobody argues." makes it band consensus that the medicated man isn't Ellis, and X M3's note says he goes on "entirely himself." Together these say stopping was right. *Repair:* in IX M3 Cal answers "He slept eight hours. That's him too."; cut "entirely himself" and "best set of their lives"; give X one visible crack the band covers.

**T-2 (P3). Dying completing him: mostly avoided.** The press-tent prophecy is defused well. The risk is structural (the verse "finished" at his death; the summary's "The list, answered"). E-2's fragment verse and the empty 1996 track fix it; cut the voice-over that explains the empty track.

**T-3 (P2). The drug question is over-insured.** There are four exonerations: the sink plus its note, Dean's flush, Nina's "He was clean," and Dean's 1996 "I know."
