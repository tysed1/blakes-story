# Critic D: Emotional, Replay and Prose-Quality Audit

*Scope: `01-story-bible.md` (§2, §5, §6, §11, §12, §13), `V3-character-audit.md` (voice sheet, binding), `V4-red-team.md`, and all eleven chapter files. VII–X and the epilogue were read line by line; I–VI were read in full for baselines and ladders. Counts come from grep over the chapter files and were hand-checked where a pattern over-matches. Criticism only; every repair is a proposal for the lead.*

**Priorities:** P1 = damages the ending or the game's central promise. P2 = a real flaw a careful player will feel. P3 = polish, continuity, accuracy.

---

## 0. The verdict in one paragraph

Chapters I–VI and most of VII are the best writing in the project: specific, funny, patient, and built on mechanics that carry meaning (the switch, the hold at the midpoint, Riley's switch in the pasture). Three things go wrong after that. (1) **The narrator takes over.** In VII–E, "the player may notice/remember/realize," "the game lets/doesn't…," "as long as they want," superlative prize-giving ("the funniest scene in the chapter") and flash-forwards rise by a factor of 5 to 20 over I–VI (table in §4). The design document starts telling the player how to feel at exactly the point where the story most needs to trust them. (2) **The voices flatten.** The V3 comic engines ("Exactly." / "Fair." / "Be specific." / Ellis's mountain markers and mock-formality) are close to absent from VIII, IX and X. (3) **The finale is over-engineered.** The HOME press is also the fall, the open verse is a transcript, Clara states the bible's thesis, and the last two hours hold about 35 callbacks. None of this needs new plot. It needs cuts, one re-timing, and a voice pass.

### 1.3 Findings

**E-1 (P1). HOME is a kill switch.**
*Problem.* X M8 "The turn": the press that ends every song is the frame in which he falls. The chapter telegraphs the gap and the light from M2 on: Dean looks into it ("It's just a gap. Every outdoor stage has them."); Kit's "Twelve volts and a thousand watts. It'll light anybody."; "Riley looks at it. It's just a light."; M7's slot of light under the stage ("The game doesn't point at it."); the flinch during "Shape Note". By the time *HOME* appears, the player knows exactly what pressing does. The design note says "The player isn't being asked to kill him." The epilogue's summary says "The last input in the game isn't *home*, which killed him." The documents contradict each other, and the second is how players will feel it.
*Why it matters.* Withholding becomes "I won't kill him" rather than "stay / go on". The player's guilt becomes the ending's subject, which pulls against §12 rule 9 (the death is not the meaning). The Nina line that reads the player's hold "to the second" turns hesitation into a public score, and a one-hour hold breaks the fixed 9:52 timeline and the eleven o'clock curfew.
*Repair.* (a) Cut "It's just a gap…", "It's just a light", "It'll light anybody" and "The game doesn't point at it". Keep Tully's tape and the flinch; they are enough. (b) Let HOME succeed: he turns, faces Dean, and the band lands the last chord together. One second of right silence, Riley's hand, Dean's cymbal choke. Then the roar comes up, Joel steps out for the reverse angle on the bow, and the light hits him as he takes a step toward Riley. The player chose home. The light chose the rest. (c) Compress diegetic time: the footage and Nina report a capped figure ("almost three minutes") whatever the real hold was.

**E-2 (P1). The open verse is a court transcript.**
*Problem.* Six player-picked lines, each cutting to one line of 1973 dialogue in strict alternation, in canonical order, with narration explaining each ("*Stay with me.* … The player hears where they came from."; "The player hears where she got it."). The default lyric 150,000 people hear is a paraphrase of the transcript: "You told me stop saying sorry. / I told you I wasn't going anywhere. / You laughed at me. You called me a liar. / You said it's okay. / You said go on."
*Why it matters.* The game's own thesis is Bartlett: memory is reconstruction. Here memory arrives as a clean recording. The metronome of sing/cut/sing/cut is the neatest structure in a game that prizes mess. And the only lyric the world ever hears Ellis improvise is the weakest writing he does in fifty hours.
*Repair.* Three or four lines, not six. Memory comes out of order and partly unheard: the laugh before the word "Liar"; "It's okay" and "Go on" arriving together, low, the way Hollis "couldn't make it out", understood only because Roy said "Go on" on the stairs. The sung lines are images drawn from the player's Observe lines (the bark "like a hand over a mouth," "the rain hit it"). Only the last line is plain: *go on*. Cut every sentence beginning "The player has heard…".

**E-3 (P1). "I'm the part that couldn't leave her there" is the bible, spoken.**
*Problem.* X M7: Ellis: "What are you?" Clara: "I'm the part that couldn't leave her there." This is bible §6.3 nearly verbatim ("She is the part of Ellis that couldn't leave Grace in the car"). It is followed by "It's the plainest thing she's ever said. The player has earned it, and so has he."
*Why it matters.* It explains the verse twenty minutes before the verse. VIII M14 already did this by indirection ("You asked me to." / "When?" / "Right here."). Clara, whose voice is orders in idiom, closes on an abstraction.
*Repair.* Keep "I didn't go anywhere. You did." Replace the thesis with evidence the player already holds: Clara: "Somebody had to stay where she could see him." (Hollis, IX M5: "You said you had to stay where she could see you.") Or cut the question altogether and have her rhyme IV's EXIT sign: "Tuck that in. You look like you got dressed in a ditch. … Come on. They're waiting on you." Cut the "earned it" sentence either way.

**E-4 (P2). The ambulance menu is Wayne's never-say list.**
*Problem.* X M9 offers "It wasn't your fault." / "I'm proud of you." / "I'm scared." / "Son." / Sing / Silence, and "the player can say everything on the list". These are the bible's "Would never say" items, served as a checklist.
*Why it matters.* The first playable Wayne lands. The switch finally choosing him, the siren switched off, the ambulance slowing to the speed limit are among the best things in the game. But a menu that lets the player complete Wayne's arc resolves him too cleanly, and it breaks the voice sheet at the one moment the voice matters most.
*Repair.* The player picks the sentiment and Wayne says his version. "It wasn't your—" stops. "I'm proud of you" comes out as "You done good up there." "I'm scared" comes out as "Son." Singing gives the bass line. Only "Son." is said whole.

**E-5 (P1). The finale is over-rhymed.**
*Problem.* M6–M9 (about 90 minutes of play) call back: the Polaroid, "That's just his face," the gorilla, the possum, the napkin autograph, the *Deliverance* banjo, "Occasionally astonishing," four dollars twice, the ledger, "What day is it," "Stop," the vote rule, "Your call," the collar, the biscuits, Roy's shirt with a knock on the patch, "Go on," "Well/Well" twice, Tully's tape, the kick drum with Carla's letter, the lifted hand, Wesley's E minor, Opal's lifted hand, Marlon's mug, "Nobody here knows you," Joan's kitchen radio, Bobby's kit, the thrown stick, "New Skin," "Shape Note," the US 19 reflex, "Stay with me," "Liar," the flashlight (Riley in VII and Hollis in 1973, explained), "That's his daddy," "Wondrous Love," the yellow line on a two-lane road. Roughly 35.
*Why it matters.* Each one is fine alone. Together they turn the death into a curtain call where every object takes a bow, and the chapter starts totaling a ledger instead of living an evening.
*Repair.* Cut about a third: Roy's "Well/Well" (keep Wayne's); Wesley's chord **or** the lifted hand to Wayne; Opal's lift and Marlon's mug at the bus; the flashlight paragraph ("The player may remember who held the light last time…"); the "One of us isn't a boy" option to Kit; Earl's "Hm."; the stick "a boy from Elmira … keeps for twenty years". Keep the Polaroid, the biscuits, "Stop," the four dollars in the gap, the kick drum.

**E-6 (P1). VIII breaks the ordinary-first rule.**
*Problem.* From M4 (The Bus) to M17 (Dr. Lusk), fourteen missions and about five hours run without one purely ordinary mission (food, money, weather, gear, nothing). Relief comes as seconds inside heavy scenes: "a Lincoln with ambitions," Dean's coffee, fifteen minutes of Bobby's party.
*Why it matters.* Bible §12.1 asks for two ordinary scenes per grief scene. VIII runs about one to six. Players will fatigue before the three best scenes in the chapter (M13–M14, M18).
*Repair.* Insert a short ordinary mission after Cal returns (between M8 and M9): a day off in a snowed-in Midwest town, a supper-club fish fry, Tully teaching Riley to bowl, Dean losing the bus at gin to Earl. And cut or recast Elaine (see §4, #14).

**E-7 (P2). The vote is rigged, and it costs Dean his one "Stop."**
*Problem.* X M6: if Cal breaks the tie *no*, "Dean, of all people, says, 'Let him,' and changes his vote. Whatever the player does, the band plays."
*Why it matters.* A false choice at the emotional peak, and it makes Dean retract his hard-won word on the record. The bible's engine offers a truer mechanism: "Following… At the end it makes them late."
*Repair.* Let a *no* stand. Ellis says, "Then I'll play it by myself," and walks to the stairs, and the band follows him, because following is what they do. The vote holds. Following is what fails.

**E-8 (P2). The fall geometry puts Riley on the wrong side.**
*Problem.* X M8 "The turn": "Three yards to his right, at the riser's stage-left corner… Riley… He was turning toward her." Facing upstage from the extreme stage-left lip, his right is the gap. The riser's stage-left corner is toward centre, which is his left-front, the same side as the crew's light. Bible §13 carries the same error ("toward Riley's side").
*Why it matters.* The image is wrong, and the true image is stronger: the reflex that swerves him away from the light also swerves him away from the living person he was turning toward.
*Repair.* Riley is on his left, ahead, with her hand out. He swerves right, away from her. Fix bible §13 to match.

**E-9 (P2). Too many endings.**
*Problem.* After X there are five closes: M6 "Go on. I'll catch up." / "Wayne goes on." (perfect); the 1996 door and "Wayne Blake declined to be interviewed"; the open reel with Riley's voice-over "We left it open. It's his."; the coda's "Hm." / "Hm." / "Good." with a fade on the kitchen light; the credits tape, which ends on "Stay with me" and "Allegedly"; and the post-credits WGRC "Don't get weird."
*Why it matters.* Each ending re-grieves. The 1996 frame's job ("what the world got") is already done by the waitress's television, Dex at the fence, and the radio in M3. Its bios hand everyone a tidy life: Dean married and sober 20 years, Bobby on the Row, Cal and Theo at 21 years, four Riley records, Dex sober.
*Repair.* Move the 1996 frame before M6 and compress it to about four minutes: Dean's "He was funny," Cal's "He wasn't so bad," Riley's maps, Dex's magazines, Nina's "He was nineteen," Wayne's door. Cut Riley's voice-over, the Joel/Kit interview, and the bios beyond one visual each. Then M6 plays as the answer to Wayne's closed door: the extra chair, the dog, the stone. "Wayne goes on." → coda → post-credits. Cut "Stay with me" from the credits tape.

**E-10 (P3). The "Liar" callbacks misremember the scenes they call back.** VIII M4 says Clara said it "sitting on his bed, and in December on the stairs"; X M8 says "In Chapter I on his bed. In Chapter V on the stairs." In I she is "on the floor under the window"; in V she is "on the sill". The finale's central callback should cite its own scenes correctly.

**V-1 (P1). The voices flatten in VIII–X.** *Problem:* the V3 rule ("Ellis gets at least three mountain markers, one mock-formal deflection and one unguarded image in dialogue per chapter") is met in I–VI and fails in VII–X; Ellis has zero "reckon/might could/fixing to" after VII M14. ## 6. Quality gate (phase 16): could this stand beside RDR2?

In I–VI and the first half of VII, yes. RDR2's story lives on long ordinary time and voices that stay themselves for sixty hours. This game matches the first and, through VI, the second, and its signature mechanic (the switch as a statement about perception) is something RDR2 doesn't have. In VIII–X it falls short on exactly RDR2's strengths: the voices thin out, and the finale explains itself where Arthur's ending trusts the player with one plain scene.

### The five strongest places

1. **The midpoint and the switch grammar** (V M16 held embrace and pull-back; VI M9 switch to Riley; VIII failed catches; X the camera that can't find anyone). Mechanic as meaning, at the level of the medium's best.
2. **Wayne's hidden love** (the dome light; 88.9 on the converter; Game 6's "*Hah!*"; 40 mph with both hands; the coffee can; Tater fed twice). The best sustained character thread in the project.
3. **VIII M13–M14** (two photographs and a doorway; the passenger who is older every time you look; the oak "like a knuckle"). The reveal is made by the player's eyes.
4. **IX M5–M6** (Hollis's porch, boiled peanuts ranked "Second.", then *Look at her* performed at 45 mph). The player's hands do the crash's one glance.
5. **The Sacred Harp line** (VII the living-room "Holy Manna" with Joan; IX the Tabernacle, Wayne's bass from the gallery, Joan leading; E the hollow square at the grave). Specific, earned and unlike any other game.

### The ten weakest, with the single most important repair each

1. **HOME = the fall** → let the song land, then the light (E-1).
2. **The open verse as transcript** → fragments and images, out of order; the last line plain (E-2).
3. **Clara's thesis line** → concrete evidence in her idiom, or cut (E-3).
4. **Narrator takeover in VII–E** → cut three in four "player may / game lets / as long as / superlative" sentences (P-1).
5. **Voice flattening in VIII–X** → restore the V3 comic engines and Ellis's markers (V-1).
6. **VIII's grief run and Elaine** → an ordinary day-off mission; recast Elaine's thesis line (E-6).
7. **The over-rhymed finale** → cut a third of the callbacks (E-5).
8. **Epilogue endings and the 1996 bios** → move 1996 before M6, compress it, cut the voice-over (E-9).
9. **Unpaid and unfair setups** → pay the glovebox, Lusk's letter, "El", the Polaroid transfer and the rain line; delete the rest (§2.4–2.6).
10. **Wayne tidied** → leave one unredeemed edge; give his version of the never-said lines (T-6, E-4).
