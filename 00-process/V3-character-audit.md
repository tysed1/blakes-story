# V3 — Character Audit (lead decisions)

*Version 3 of the project: the story after the character audit. Input: `critic-reports/critic-A-character-dialogue.md` (sections 1, 2, 4 and 6), read against the bible, the V2 macrostructure and the drafts of Chapters I–VI. The critic criticized; the lead decides. Every finding below is marked **Accept**, **Modify** or **Reject**. The repairs to I–VI are executed under work orders R1 and R2 in `V4-red-team.md`.*

## What the audit found, in one paragraph

The characters are right and the voices aren't separate enough yet. Ellis, Riley and Cal share one dry sitcom register ("Exactly." "Fair." "Strong recovery."), so with the names stripped off half their banter could belong to anyone. Ellis, the voice the player lives in, has lost the mountain speech and the gallows humor his bible gives him. The adults hand out aphorisms. Riley is used too often as the person who watches Ellis, and her own song is barely on screen. Cal notices things seven times and acts on none of them. Wayne's thaw is bunched into Chapter V. None of this needs new characters or a new plot. It needs a voice pass, some cuts, and four small new scenes.

## The voice sheet (binding from V3 on)

Each principal gets one comic engine. Stock replies have a single owner, and nobody else uses them.

| Character | Comic engine | Owns | Never says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ellis** | Mock-formality ("Allegedly." "That's a matter for the courts." "I'll take it under advisement.") and sudden specific images when he forgets to guard them. Mountain markers, light: *reckon, might could, fixing to, Hm.* Funniest at 3 a.m. and when things are going badly. | "Allegedly." | "Exactly." "Fair." |
| **Riley** | Argument form ("Define 'fine.'" "Name one. I'll wait."), words from the list inside her notebook covers, crossword precision. | "Fair." and the deadpan appraisal ("Strong recovery.") | "Okay." as a whole reply, more than once a chapter |
| **Cal** | Corrections and technical questions ("Be specific." "Which verse?"). "No." as a complete sentence. "I know." instead of "You're right." | "Be specific." | Jokes that aren't corrections |
| **Dean** | Nicknames (Dad, Professor, Blake), overstatement, bets, and "That's a tomorrow problem." | "Exactly." | "I'm tired." "Stop." (until X) |
| **Clara** | Big-sister orders in mountain idiom ("Hush." "Eat something." "Quit that."). Teasing. Calls Wayne **"your daddy,"** never "Wayne." | "Liar" (three times in the game) | "Go on," "go," or any send-off |
| **Wayne** | Flat sarcasm, few words. "Hm." "Son" only when he's frightened. | "Hm." | "It wasn't your fault." "I'm proud of you." "Lorraine." |

**Rules for the pass:**
- Cut two-thirds of the 29 whole-line "Nothing." replies. Replace each one in the speaker's own voice: Wayne "Hm."; Cal a technical question; Dean a joke; Ellis mock-formality; Riley a precise evasion.
- Replace at least half of the 72 name-only lines ("Ellis." "Ellis?") with a physical beat (Riley's knuckle to her glasses, a hand on an arm) or a specific question. Riley gets no more than two per chapter.
- Ellis gets at least three mountain markers, one mock-formal deflection and one unguarded image in dialogue per chapter. He says "Hm" in the same scene as Wayne in VI M12, without either of them noticing.

## Consequences for Chapters VII–X

Decided here and carried into the V4 plan:
- Riley's own work keeps growing. In VII she writes "Occasionally Astonishing" (her first solo record, 1979), and Nina Sorensen notices her at the New York showcase. In VIII, "Sunday Clothes" being taken from her is the chapter's second wound, not a footnote.
- Cal acts. VIII: he provokes first ("She isn't real, Ellis"), is hurt worse, leaves, and comes back on his own terms by naming Theo to the band as his. IX: he tells Ellis he has known about the Thursday since August '75.
- Dean's arithmetic and Lynette carry through VIII (the ER, the phone call) and IX (sober).
- Wayne's thaw stays one gesture per chapter until IX, when he talks.
- Clara stays warm. Her cruelty in VII–VIII lands because she is still funny in the same scenes.
