## 2. The premise, restated

V7's pitch was accurate but familiar: a band, a doomed frontman, a dead sister, an imaginary friend. The ingredients stay; the identity is sacred. What changes is how the premise is stated and what it promises the player:

> *A game where the camera decides who you are. For half of it, the camera has been showing you someone who isn't there, and she has been the only help the game ever gave you.*

Three things in that sentence are new:
1. **The camera as a character that can be wrong.** The switch is taught, then betrayed, then fails.
2. **The help as the hallucination** (§3).
3. **The player's hands as the myth's source.** The Observe lines become "genius" without consent.

The fourth new thing is structural: **the last playable character is the man the camera refused for fifty hours.**

## 3. Clara's help (the new system)

**The design.** There is no tutorial in this game, no hint text and no advice. The only voice that ever tells the player what to look at, where to go, how to sing the harmony, what day it is, or what they're forgetting is Clara's. She is the game's help.

**What she helps with.** All of it is in her voice, in her idiom, and never on a HUD.
- **Observe.** She points out things worth noticing. *"Look at that man's hat."* The line Ellis writes is his.
- **The Room.** Before Riley, she hums the high part, the part Grace sang, and the player can find the harmony by following her. When Riley first sings the high part on "No Name" (I), Clara stops humming. That's the first thing anyone takes from her.
- **Directions.** *"Not that way."* *"Left at the church."*
- **Money, time, the calendar.** *"Rent's Friday."* *"You've got eleven dollars."* *"It's Thursday."* Ellis always knows what day it is. The player may realize, later, that Clara was how.
- **Social reads.** *"She likes you. Quit it."* *"He's lying about the tubes."*

**What she never helps with.** Her help is always right about the world, because it's Ellis's own knowledge (Rule 6). It is always biased toward keeping him with her.
- She steers him away from Tolliver Road.
- She steers him away from doctors: *"Don't you let some doctor poke at you."*
- She steers him away from telling Riley about the car, and from going home early.

**How it plays.**
- **First playthrough.** The player learns to trust her.
- **Midpoint (V M16).** The objective camera shows the empty seat, and every piece of help the player has taken is recast.
- **After the midpoint.** The player keeps using the help, because it's still good. That's the point.
- **Second playthrough.** The player can see every place she steered.

**When there's no help.**
- **As Riley, Cal or Dean.** There's no help at all. Each has their own verbs, and the band's signals are the only guidance. The player learns the difference between playing with Clara and playing without her.
- **With Wayne in the room.** No help. She leaves.
- **On the medication (VIII M17 – IX M7).** No Clara, and no help. The Room has no hum. Observe is muted. Directions, money and the calendar come only from the HUD. The medicated chapters are quieter and harder, and the player feels, in their own hands, why Ellis stops. Nothing moral is attached. He sleeps, swims and finishes "New Skin" without her.
- **Flush, day four (IX M7).** She comes back, and so does the help. The player will feel relief. The game wants them to, and then shows them what it costs.
- **Chapter X.** She helps in the field, and she tells him to push his hair out of his eyes on the walk. At HOME she gives no help at all. It's the only decision in the game she doesn't help with. The player makes it alone.
- **The epilogue and the coda.** There's no help anywhere. The last objective, GO HOME, is the only instruction the game gives, and nobody says it.

**Tests (for the savvy player).** Before the midpoint, a player who suspects her can ask her to do something objective: pay, pick something up, hand Riley a pick. She refuses in idiom. *"I'm not your mama."* *"Do it yourself, you've got hands."* The game tracks it quietly. At V M16, if the player tested her three or more times, one Observe line appears in the notebook afterward, in Ellis's hand: *I kept asking her to prove it and she kept not.* The game never otherwise acknowledges it.

**Precedent and difference.** *Hellblade: Senua's Sacrifice* uses psychotic voices as hints. Here, the help is warm and funny, and the player doesn't know it's a hallucination until halfway. It's biased toward the wound rather than hostile. Medication takes it away as a playable cost. And the final choice is the one it won't help with.

**Rules.** The help obeys Rules 1–13, most of all 6 (nothing Ellis doesn't know) and 10 (no "go"). The bible gets §11.8.

## 4. The savvy guess, designed in

The player most likely to guess early guesses *"she's his dead sister's ghost."* That's also the fans' theory in VII–X ("It's his sister, dummy") and *Rave*'s implication. The second half corrects it:
- She isn't Grace. Grace was fourteen; Clara is twenty-one, with their mother's face and their grandmother's name.
- She's the older sister Grace never got to be, built by a boy who couldn't leave her in the car.
- "I couldn't be her." (IX M14)

The early guesser is put in the position of the tabloid reader, and the game shows them their guess was the myth's short version. No new scenes are needed. Add one sentence to V M16's design note and one to VIII M13's.

## 5. Characters

**Riley pass.** For every segment where Riley is playable in VII–X and the epilogue, make sure the segment is also about her own want: the song, her mother, school, her voice, her body, her friends, her record. Add a concrete personal stake where it's missing, about 100 words at most. Priority segments:
- VII: the seventh chair, and the phone in the rain.
- VIII: M11, M12, M15.
- IX: M6 (the dock), M10 (Count), M15.
- X: M2 (the hayfield) and M9 (the rope).
- The epilogue.

**Performance bible (new, 18).** Casting and direction for every principal:
- **Clara:** a bossy older sister at a county fair, never mysterious. Her help lines are delivered as nags, not whispers.
- **Wayne:** never cries on camera.
- **Ellis:** the "hotel room" line is Wayne's cruelty coming out of the son.
- **Dean:** jokes as load-bearing.
- **Riley:** thinking out loud.
- **Cal:** economy.
