# V4 — Red Team (lead decisions and repair work orders)

*Version 4: the story after both red teams. Inputs: `critic-reports/critic-A-character-dialogue.md` (sections 3, 5 and 7) and `critic-reports/critic-B-continuity-systems.md` (all of it). The critics reviewed the V2 macrostructure and the drafts of Chapters I–VI. Character and voice decisions are in `V3-character-audit.md`. This document records every other decision, the changes to the plan for Chapters VII–X, and the two work orders (R1, R2) that repair Chapters I–VI.*

**Process note.** The rebuild drafted Chapters I–VI before the red teams reported, to keep momentum. That meant the critics could test real scenes instead of outlines, and their findings could be applied as repairs to finished text rather than to a plan. Chapter VII was drafted while the reports were being written; it has been conformed to these decisions. Chapters VIII–X are written after this document and follow it.

## Part 1 — Decisions on the red-team findings

### 1.1 The Clara mystery (Critic A, C1–C6)

**C1. Too many tells before the midpoint. Accepted.** First-time players must be able to believe Clara is a person until V M16.
- **Keep one "Who're you talking to?" in IV:** Marlon's, with the door hiding her.
- At the EXIT sign, Cal's line becomes "Blake. Now."
- **Wayne's room scene in IV:** Clara leaves before Wayne comes in.
- **No third-party point-of-view shots before V M16.** Cal's view of the amp case becomes Ellis's view. Marlon's eyeline doesn't land on the empty space.
- **Clara never rides in a vehicle with a band member before V M16.** Where she does, she moves to a moment when Ellis is alone: the gas-station lot, the Valiant alone, the walk to the van.
- **The lyric in "Still Here" changes** from "Nobody here can see you" to **"Nobody here knows you."** It's the same line in every chapter and in the bible. On replay it still works: in VII, sixteen hundred strangers sing it to a man none of them know.
- **III:** cut "Ellis glances toward Dean" after "Psychotic." After the Milledgeville line, Ellis looks at his hands, not at the tree line.
- **Add social cover.** Roy in I M2: "That Clara girl find you? You said she might come by." In II M9, Ellis orders two coffees at the Starlite and drinks both.

**C2. The finale callbacks aren't planted. Accepted.**
- **"Liar."** Planted in I M9 and V M1 (Critic B's CL-1 text). The third use stays in VIII, so the X payoff has three to land on.
- **"El."** Planted in green felt-tip on the back of the crawdad snapshot in IV M9: *El caught it, I held it. G.*

**C3. Rule breaches. Accepted.**
- Clara is never in the house while Wayne is awake (I M9 and II M10 get sleep beats).
- III M11: the camera goes with Ellis.
- IV M8: "Go." becomes "They're waiting on you."
- **V "Where did we meet?"** She answers "Out back of Marlon's." Ellis: "When?" She can't answer. (Critic B's K-4 proposed keeping "Tolliver Road"; **rejected**. It gives away the crash connection three chapters early and contradicts the alley.)
- Horror grammar is cut: the mirror in II M10, the shop-window flicker in V M9, the store glass in VI M3, and the Lantern approach where she gets closer at every cut (IV M8).

**C4.** Pay off V M4's promise of the 45 played back "only Riley." **Accepted** (VI M17, one line).

**C5. Naming the diagnosis. Accepted.** Ellis knows only "he heard people." Wayne: "Doctor called it something long. I didn't write it down." The word "schizophrenia" is not spoken in the game until Dr. Lusk declines to use it in VIII.

**C6. Accepted.** At the Pasture switch, Riley hears Ellis say one line to nobody that only makes sense spoken to a child: "You don't have to sit up front."

### 1.2 The anti-AI filter (Critic A, section 3). Accepted as global line-edit rules.

- Banned phrases: "the house has a ghost now" (I) becomes "The house has a room the player hasn't been let into." Rewrite "sadder than a ghost" (V summary).
- **Negation pivots** ("Not X. Y." / "isn't X. It's Y."): cut two-thirds in every chapter.
- **"First time":** at most one per chapter.
- **"There it is":** two in the whole game (Frank in VI M6; Dean's "There it is. The invoice.").
- **Quip buttons:** no more than one section in three may end on a quip. End the others on action, a practical line, or an interruption.
- **Narrator verdicts** ("That's Ellis." "Perfect." "Another promise."): cut them.
- **Triadic anaphora** ("Nobody X. Nobody Y. Nobody Z."): at most one per chapter.
- The worst ten are repaired as the critic proposes, except #10, where VI M13 gets new lines (see R2).

## Part 2 — Changes to the plan for Chapters VII–X and the Epilogue (Critic A, section 7)

**Setups that must pay off (P1). All accepted and assigned:**
- Lorraine alive and gone: planted by IV. VII pays it off with the letter and her address.
- "Liar" and "El": see C2.
- Riley's "Sunday Clothes": VIII makes it the chapter's second wound.
- Lynette, Bobby and Dean's arithmetic: VII built them. VIII and IX pay them off.
- **Mrs. Pardue** gets one line in IX when the poems are printed ("I told you so, Ellis. In 1973.").
- **Carla Vickery's letter** is still taped to the kick drum at the festival (X, the kick-drum touch).
- **Opal Hensley:** in IX M5, Hollis says he ran to the Hensley place to call. The player may put it together; the game doesn't point.
- **Cal's knowledge:** in IX M12 (Bench), Cal tells Ellis he has known about the Thursday since August '75, and Marlon's name stays out of it.
- **"Mine's real":** Cal says it again in VIII, to the band, when he names Theo.
- **"What day is it?":** seeded in VII M17 ("I know what day it is"). Paid off in X.
- **The Engineers tickets:** Wayne and Roy use the other two in the epilogue.

**Duplicates removed (P2). Accepted:**
- **VIII "Payphone."** The player already knows Ellis was driving (IV, VI, and now VII's *Rave* article, where the whole country learns it). The scene is rebuilt so the new information is **Tolliver Bend itself**: the place, the oak with its crooked scar, the missing shoulder, the high beams in the player's memory from VII M15. "I was driving" is still said, as Ellis taking ownership of the fact, and it isn't staged as a revelation.
- **VIII, Wayne and Riley in the kitchen.** Wayne has known since August '75 that his son sees someone in Grace's jacket. The scene starts from that, not from the shock of the name.
- **IX *Late Hour*.** No second televised silence. On trifluoperazine, Ellis is polite, fluent, calm and wrong. He tells the peanut-stand story, laughs at the right places, and says what the host hopes he'll say. The country loves the medicated Ellis; the band watches from the lodge and doesn't recognize him. *Night Stage* (VIII) keeps the silence.

**The outing (P3). Accepted, all points:**
- Cal provokes first: "She isn't real, Ellis." Ellis retaliates with "At least mine isn't somebody I keep in a hotel room," and knows the instant he's said it that it's Wayne's move, and doesn't take it back.
- Tully is in the room. He says, "That's enough," and gets off the bus at the next stop and rides with the crew truck for a week.
- Dean's coffee ("For the record, I figured you were just boring") moves to **after Cal comes back**.
- Cal comes back on his own terms. At the band's next meeting he says Theo's name as his, not as the sound man's: "Theo's mine. That's real. You don't get to use it."

**The medication thread (P4). Accepted:**
- **"New Skin" is finished while Ellis is medicated** (IX M2), slowly, in daylight, at the lodge's kitchen table.
- The medicated free-roam flattens Clara and Observe, not the color palette. It also gives him things he's never had in the game: a full night's sleep, Tater's head on his boot, Riley laughing at something he said.
- Stopping the pills brings Clara back. It also brings back the 3 a.m. waking, the talking to the air, and Riley's pill count.
- **The Tabernacle cracks once.** Ellis loses a verse, and Cal carries him through it on the bass (the *follow* verb in reverse).
- The game never says stopping was right, and never says it was wrong.

**The finale (P5). Accepted, with one modification. This is the largest canon change in V4:**
- **Ellis does not take the LSD.** A tab is given to him at the motel on the morning of the festival (by a fan, tucked into a letter). He holds it at the sink and puts it back in the envelope, and the envelope goes into the green notebook. The camera doesn't comment. In the epilogue Dean finds it and flushes it, sober, so that nobody can ever say it. Ellis dies clean, and Nina Sorensen's column says so in one plain sentence.
- **"Go on" comes from the living.** At the foot of the stage stairs, Roy Cagle, who says "Go on" about everything, says "Go on, get up there." In the last verse of "Who Are You?" that ordinary phrase opens the memory of Grace's.
- **The press-tent line is mundane:** Ellis talks about the peanut-stand rankings. Dex will turn it into prophecy later.
- **Why he falls:** exhaustion, no sleep for two days, off his medication for seven weeks, and a memory arriving in the middle of a song. A 16mm crew's sun-gun hits his eyes as he turns home, and his body does what it did on Tolliver Bend and on US 19 in the rain (planted in VII M15): it swerves right. There's a gap between the stage deck and the PA wing. The death is an accident with causes, and none of the causes is a drug.

**The death mechanic (P6). Accepted:**
- **"Home" is withheld, not waited on.** While the player holds off pressing it, the band keeps following Ellis's improvisation (the "stay with me" loop) and visibly tires. Cal's hand cramps. Dean looks up at him. Riley's voice cracks on the high part. Clara stands at the lip of the stage. Holding off is *stay*; pressing is *go on*.
- There's no timer. The music keeps changing, so it never becomes a static screen.
- **Geometry:** "home" means turning to face Dean at the drum riser, upstage center. Riley has moved to the riser's stage-right corner during the last chorus, as she does on every ending, so when Ellis turns home he turns toward both of them. The camera crew is in the stage-left wing. The sun-gun hits him from his right at three-quarter angle as he comes around. His body swerves right, away from the light. The PA wing and the gap are stage right.

**The 1972 afternoon (P7). Accepted.** Ellis is **fifteen**, and doesn't drive (they bike, and Wayne drives them to the Tollivers'). Grace's line: "You're my brother. Unfortunately." Two chores and a joke for every tear.

**Hollis (P8). Accepted.** "She said something after. I couldn't make it out. I'm sorry." Only X gives the words.

**Epigrams (P9). Accepted.** The 1996 frame keeps "He was funny. Nobody writes that." (Dean) and "He wasn't so bad." (Cal). Riley talks about his maps, which were always wrong. Dex says something small and self-serving ("I sold a lot of magazines."). Wayne's door stays closed.

**The Epilogue (P10). Accepted.**
- **Clara is absent from the coda, and nobody remarks on it.**
- "Number One" stops being a relay mission. It becomes a two-minute sequence inside "The List" (Cal): the chart climbing on a radio behind Cal, who is doing something else, and the lawsuit over the footage.
- Total epilogue runtime drops to about 1.75 hours.

**P11. Accepted.**
- "Promise?" is cut from the X green room.
- **Kit Adair**, the 22-year-old holding the light, gets two humane beats: before the set, Tully shows her where to stand so she's out of the band's way; after, she sits alone on an equipment case and Tully sits down next to her.
